Misconception: enzymic browning and oxidation are the same process Both involve oxygen, but they are different reactions. Enzymic browning is enzyme-driven: it requires the polyphenol oxidase enzyme released from damaged plant cells. You can stop enzymic browning by inactivating the enzyme (heat, acid, or lack of oxygen). Oxidation does not require an enzyme — it is a direct chemical reaction between vitamin C (or fats) and atmospheric oxygen. Storing cut vegetables in an airtight container slows oxidative vitamin C loss, but it will not prevent enzymic browning once the enzymes have been released.
