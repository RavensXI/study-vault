UNIT STRUCTURE (15 units, 71 lessons)
--------------------------------------

Edexcel 1RA0 is a route-based qualification. Students study:
  - one Paper 1 religion (1A Catholic, 1B Christianity, or 1C Islam) — 50% of GCSE
  - one Paper 2 SECOND religion from 7 options (Catholic/Christianity/Islam/
    Buddhism/Hinduism/Judaism/Sikhism, must differ from Paper 1; if Paper 1
    was Catholic or Christianity, Paper 2 cannot be the other) — 25%
  - EITHER Paper 3 Philosophy & Ethics (3A/3B/3C matched to Paper 1) OR
    Paper 4 Textual Studies (4A Mark's Gospel matched to 1A/1B, or 4B Qur'an
    matched to 1C) — 25%

We ship every option as a separate browse-card unit. Students self-route via
the wizard (same pattern as history-ocr and history-aqa option units).

Paper 1 units (3 × 6 lessons = 18 lessons)
  1. paper-1-catholic-christianity          (6)  Beliefs + Practices + Sources + Forms
  2. paper-1-christianity                   (6)  Beliefs + Practices + Sources + Forms
  3. paper-1-islam                          (6)  Beliefs + Practices + Sources + Forms

Paper 2 units (7 × 4 lessons = 28 lessons) — only Beliefs + Practices
  4. paper-2-catholic-christianity          (4)

Note: Some content duplicates across Paper 1 vs Paper 2 (e.g. a student
studying Christianity for Paper 1 sees similar Beliefs/Practices content
as a Paper 2 Christianity student, but the Paper 1 version is denser and
adds Sources of Wisdom + Forms of Expression sections that Paper 2 lacks).
This is acceptable — parallel units are easier to wire through the wizard
than shared Supabase rows toggled by paper context.

QUESTION TYPES
--------------

Edexcel 1RA0 uses a strict four-part structure per topic:
  3 marks — Outline Three          (AO1, factual recall — three discrete points)
  3 marks — Describe               (AO1, used in some papers as Outline synonym)
  4 marks — Explain Two            (AO1, "beyond a habitual response" — develop two points)
  5 marks — Explain Two with Sources   (AO1, plus one named source/wisdom citation)
  12 marks — Discuss a Statement   (AO2, evaluative — two-sided + justified judgement)

The 12-mark Discuss is the highest-stakes question and consistently the
weakest area for students per Pearson exemplar commentary.

TEACHING BRIEF HIGHLIGHTS
-------------------------

Common misconceptions (8 catalogued):
  - Greater vs lesser Jihad (often reduced to "inner struggle vs holy war")
  - Aquinas's First Three Ways confused with Ontological or Kalam arguments
  - Augustinian (free will) vs Irenaean (soul-making) theodicies conflated
  - Catholic vs general Christian framing of Paper 3 content
  - Mark 16 longer ending (9-20) cited instead of spec-named Mark 16:1-8
  - Islamic teaching on Isa's crucifixion (Surah 4:157-158) misrepresented
  - 5-mark Explain question source citations omitted, paraphrased, or fabricated
  - 12-mark Discuss alternative point of view chronically underdeveloped

Question-type errors documented per type. Pedagogical guidance drawn from
EEF retrieval-practice + diagnostic assessment research, NATRE guidance on
teaching Islam, RE Today comparison-writing scaffolds, Theos Think Tank on
impartiality, and Pearson 2024 exemplar commentary.

ANTI-FABRICATION RISK NOTES
---------------------------

RS is fact-heavy. The plan flags specific named anchors that downstream
content agents MUST get right:

  Catholic — Catechism paragraph numbers (1210, 2360-2365, 2207, 1938 etc),
    papal documents (Humanae Vitae 1968 Paul VI; Familiaris Consortio 1981
    John Paul II; Evangelii Gaudium 2013 Francis; Not Just Good But
    Beautiful 2014 Francis), Vatican II documents (Dei Verbum, Lumen
    Gentium, Sacrosanctum Concilium, Gaudium et Spes).

  Christianity — biblical references must match Edexcel's named verses
    (Matthew 3:13-17, John 1:1-18, Luke 22-24, 1 Corinthians 13, etc).
    The 39 Articles of Religion is the Anglican confessional document.

  Islam — Surah:verse references are exact. Hadith references (Sahih
    al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim) are tightly specified. al-Ghazali's "Revival
    of Religious Sciences" is a real text (Ihya' Ulum al-Din, c. 1106-7).
    99 Beautiful Names selected by spec: Ar-Rahman, Ar-Rahim, As-Salam,
    Al-Khaliq, Al-Ghaffar, Ar-Razzaq.

  Buddhism — Pali Sutta names (Dhammacakkappavattana, Anapanasati, Kimsila,
    Mangala, Vakkali, Khandha) are real and verifiable.

  Hinduism — Upanishad/Vedic references (Katha 2.2.6-8, Mundaka 2.1,
    Taittiriya 2.7, Brihadaranyaka 4.4, Rig Veda 10.129, Bhagavad Gita
    citations) — get verse numbers exactly right.

  Judaism — Maimonides' Mishneh Torah (specifically Sefer Madda, the Book
    of Knowledge — first of 14 books, c. 1180 CE). Talmud Yoma 83-84 for
    Pikuach Nefesh.

  Sikhism — Guru Granth Sahib page-number citations (e.g. p1, p12, p94,
    p349, p1391, p1316 — these are real pages of the canonical 1,430-page
    text). Rahit Maryada chapter numbers. Mai Bhago (1666-1733) is a real
    historical Sikh woman warrior.

  Paper 4 — Mark passages by exact verse range (Mark 16:1-8 specifically,
    not the longer ending). Bonhoeffer (1906-1945) German Lutheran pastor;
    Romero (1917-1980) Salvadoran Archbishop assassinated saying Mass;
    Mother Teresa (1910-1997) founder of Missionaries of Charity. Qur'an
    surah names and verse ranges per spec.

Phase 6 fact-check (`scripts/_fact_check_subject.py`) is mandatory before
ship. Per the feedback_fact_check_built_in memory, scripture-citation
fabrication is a known failure mode in RE/RS — the cheapest fix is
getting it right first time.
