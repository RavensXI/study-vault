--- DESCRIPTION ---
Sexual and asexual reproduction, how meiosis produces gametes, and the structure of DNA.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Sexual and Asexual Reproduction</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">There are two types of reproduction. <dfn class="term" data-def="Reproduction involving the fusion of male and female gametes (sex cells). It produces genetically varied offspring.">Sexual reproduction</dfn> involves the fusion (joining) of male and female <dfn class="term" data-def="Sex cells — sperm in males and eggs (ova) in females. Each gamete contains 23 chromosomes (half the normal number).">gametes</dfn> (sperm and egg). The offspring are genetically different from each other and from both parents, because they receive a random mix of genetic material. This genetic variation is important for natural selection.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3"><dfn class="term" data-def="Reproduction involving only one parent. It does not involve gametes or fertilisation, so the offspring are genetically identical to the parent (clones).">Asexual reproduction</dfn> involves only one parent and no fusion of gametes. The offspring are genetically identical to the parent — they are <dfn class="term" data-def="Genetically identical organisms produced by asexual reproduction.">clones</dfn>. Asexual reproduction occurs through mitosis. Many plants and some animals (e.g. bacteria, starfish) can reproduce asexually.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. List the 3 key differences between sexual reproduction (two parents, gametes, varied offspring) and asexual reproduction.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Sexual reproduction: two parents, gametes fuse, offspring are genetically varied. Asexual reproduction: one parent, mitosis only, offspring are clones. Variation in sexual reproduction is important for natural selection and survival.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Meiosis</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n6"><dfn class="term" data-def="A type of cell division that produces four genetically different cells, each with half the number of chromosomes (haploid). It occurs in the reproductive organs to produce gametes.">Meiosis</dfn> is the type of cell division that produces gametes (sex cells). It takes place in the reproductive organs (ovaries and testes). Before meiosis, the DNA replicates. The cell then divides <strong>twice</strong>, producing four genetically different <dfn class="term" data-def="A cell that contains half the normal number of chromosomes — 23 in humans. Gametes are haploid.">haploid</dfn> cells, each with half the chromosome number (23 in humans).</p>

<p data-narration-id="n7">When two gametes fuse at <dfn class="term" data-def="The fusion of a male gamete (sperm) and a female gamete (egg) to form a new cell called a zygote with the full number of chromosomes.">fertilisation</dfn>, the full chromosome number is restored (46 in humans). The resulting cell is called a <dfn class="term" data-def="The cell formed when a sperm fertilises an egg. It contains 46 chromosomes and will develop into an embryo through mitosis.">zygote</dfn>, which then divides by mitosis to form an embryo.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">DNA Structure</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n10"><dfn class="term" data-def="Deoxyribonucleic acid — a large molecule that carries the genetic instructions for the development and functioning of living organisms. It is a double helix made of two strands.">DNA</dfn> (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a polymer made up of two strands twisted into a <dfn class="term" data-def="The twisted ladder shape of the DNA molecule, with two strands wound around each other.">double helix</dfn>. DNA is found in the nucleus of cells, arranged into structures called <dfn class="term" data-def="Thread-like structures made of DNA found in the nucleus. Humans have 23 pairs (46 total). Each chromosome carries many genes.">chromosomes</dfn>. Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes (46 total).</p>

<p data-narration-id="n11">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A short section of DNA on a chromosome that codes for a specific protein. Different versions of the same gene are called alleles.">gene</dfn> is a short section of DNA on a chromosome. Each gene codes for a particular sequence of amino acids, which are assembled into a specific <dfn class="term" data-def="Large biological molecules made of chains of amino acids. They carry out many functions in the body, including enzymes, structural proteins and hormones.">protein</dfn>. The entire set of genetic material in an organism is called its <dfn class="term" data-def="The entire set of genetic material (DNA) in an organism.">genome</dfn>.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n12" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Name the DNA structure, state how many chromosome pairs humans have, and recall what the Human Genome Project mapped.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>DNA is a double helix found in chromosomes in the nucleus. A gene is a section of DNA that codes for a protein. Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes. The Human Genome Project mapped all human DNA.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n15">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n16">Sexual reproduction produces genetically varied offspring via gamete fusion; asexual reproduction produces clones via mitosis.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n17">Meiosis: two divisions, four genetically different haploid cells. Mitosis: one division, two identical diploid cells.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n18">DNA is a double helix in chromosomes; genes are sections of DNA that code for proteins.</li>
</ul>
