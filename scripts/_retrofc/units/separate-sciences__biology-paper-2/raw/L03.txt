--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Hormones and the Menstrual Cycle</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">The menstrual cycle is a roughly 28-day cycle controlled by four key hormones that interact through feedback mechanisms:</p>

<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n3"><strong><dfn class="term" data-def="A hormone produced by the pituitary gland that stimulates the maturation of an egg in a follicle in the ovary.">FSH</dfn></strong> (follicle-stimulating hormone) — released by the pituitary gland. It causes an egg to mature in a follicle in the ovary and stimulates the ovaries to produce oestrogen.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n4"><strong><dfn class="term" data-def="A hormone produced by the ovaries that causes the uterus lining to thicken and stimulates the pituitary gland to release LH. It also inhibits further release of FSH.">Oestrogen</dfn></strong> — produced by the ovaries. It causes the uterus lining to thicken and build up with blood vessels. At high levels, it stimulates LH release and inhibits FSH.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n5"><strong><dfn class="term" data-def="A hormone released by the pituitary gland that triggers ovulation — the release of the mature egg from the ovary around day 14 of the cycle.">LH</dfn></strong> (luteinising hormone) — released by the pituitary gland. A surge of LH triggers <dfn class="term" data-def="The release of a mature egg from the ovary, typically around day 14 of the menstrual cycle.">ovulation</dfn> around day 14.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n6"><strong><dfn class="term" data-def="A hormone produced by the ovaries (from the empty follicle) after ovulation. It maintains the uterus lining and inhibits FSH and LH.">Progesterone</dfn></strong> — produced by the empty follicle after ovulation. It maintains the uterus lining. If no fertilisation occurs, progesterone levels drop, the lining breaks down, and menstruation begins.</li>
</ul>

<div class="higher-only">
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n7" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and list the 4 hormones in order: what FSH does, what triggers LH surge, when ovulation occurs, what maintains lining.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The menstrual cycle: FSH (matures egg + stimulates oestrogen) → Oestrogen (thickens lining + triggers LH surge) → LH (triggers ovulation on day 14) → Progesterone (maintains lining). All four hormones interact via feedback.</p>
</div>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n8">Contraception</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n9"><dfn class="term" data-def="Methods used to prevent pregnancy. They can be hormonal (e.g. the pill, implant, injection) or non-hormonal (e.g. condoms, diaphragm, IUD).">Contraception</dfn> can be hormonal or non-hormonal:</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Hormonal Methods</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Oral contraceptive pill</strong> — contains hormones (oestrogen and/or progesterone) that inhibit FSH production, preventing egg maturation and ovulation. The combined pill uses both hormones; the progesterone-only pill has fewer side effects.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Contraceptive injection, implant or skin patch</strong> — release progesterone slowly over weeks or months, providing long-term contraception without daily action.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Intrauterine device (IUD)</strong> — a device inserted into the uterus. Hormonal IUDs release progesterone; copper IUDs are non-hormonal and prevent implantation.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Non-Hormonal Methods</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Barrier methods</strong> — condoms (male and female) and diaphragms physically prevent sperm from reaching the egg. Condoms also protect against sexually transmitted infections (STIs).</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Spermicidal agents</strong> — chemicals that kill or disable sperm, often used with barrier methods.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Surgical methods</strong> — sterilisation (cutting or tying the fallopian tubes or vas deferens) is permanent and very effective.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Abstinence or natural family planning</strong> — avoiding intercourse during the fertile window. Less reliable than other methods.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall: which hormone do contraceptives inhibit, what this prevents, and which method protects against STIs.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Hormonal contraceptives work by inhibiting FSH (preventing egg maturation) and/or preventing ovulation. Only barrier methods (condoms) protect against both pregnancy and STIs.</p>
</div>

<div class="higher-only">
<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Fertility Treatments</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n19">Some couples have difficulty conceiving. Fertility treatments include:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n20"><strong>FSH and LH injections</strong> — given to women who do not produce enough FSH naturally, stimulating egg maturation and ovulation.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n21"><strong>IVF</strong> (<dfn class="term" data-def="In vitro fertilisation — eggs are collected from the ovaries after hormonal stimulation, fertilised with sperm in a laboratory, and one or two embryos are transferred to the uterus.">in vitro fertilisation</dfn>) — the mother is given FSH and LH to stimulate multiple eggs, which are collected and fertilised with sperm outside the body. One or two embryos are then transferred to the uterus.</p>
</ul>

<p data-narration-id="n22">IVF raises ethical issues: it is emotionally and physically demanding, expensive, has a low success rate, and can result in multiple pregnancies. Unused embryos may be destroyed, which some people object to on moral or religious grounds.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n24">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">Four hormones control the menstrual cycle: FSH (egg maturation), oestrogen (lining thickens, triggers LH), LH (ovulation), progesterone (maintains lining).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n26">Hormonal contraceptives inhibit FSH/prevent ovulation; barrier methods (condoms) also protect against STIs.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n27">IVF involves hormonal stimulation, egg collection, laboratory fertilisation and embryo transfer.</li>
</ul>
