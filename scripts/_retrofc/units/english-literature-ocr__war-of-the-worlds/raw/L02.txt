--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Landing on Horsell Common</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">The first Martian cylinder lands on Horsell Common in Surrey, attracting curious crowds who treat it as a spectacle rather than a threat. A <dfn class="term" data-def="A group of people sent to negotiate or make contact.">deputation</dfn> approaches the cylinder waving a white flag. The Martians respond with the Heat-Ray, killing them instantly. Wells uses the crowd’s complacency to critique Victorian arrogance — the assumption that they could negotiate with or control any force they encountered.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">The narrator describes the scene with scientific detachment, listing details as if compiling a report. This <dfn class="term" data-def="A style of writing that presents facts objectively, without emotional commentary.">clinical tone</dfn> contrasts with the horror of events, creating a disturbing sense that human suffering is being observed rather than felt.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: How do the crowd react to the first cylinder, and what does this reveal?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The crowd treats the cylinder as a spectacle, approaching with curiosity rather than fear. Wells critiques Victorian complacency — the assumption that Britain was invulnerable and could control any situation.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">The Heat-Ray — Technology as Terror</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n6">The Martian Heat-Ray is described as an invisible beam that sets everything it touches on fire. Wells never explains the science — it is simply beyond human understanding. The weapon represents the technological superiority that real imperial powers used against colonised peoples. Just as the Maxim gun devastated African warriors, the Heat-Ray devastates the Surrey militia.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n7">The British army responds with conventional weapons that prove completely ineffective. Wells systematically dismantles Victorian confidence in military power, showing that technological superiority makes bravery irrelevant.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">The Narrator’s Response</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n10">The unnamed <dfn class="term" data-def="A character who tells the story using the first person ‘I’, limiting the reader’s knowledge to their perspective.">first-person narrator</dfn> is an educated, middle-class man whose certainties collapse throughout the novel. His initial curiosity gives way to terror as he witnesses the Heat-Ray. Wells uses this everyman perspective to make the reader share the experience of having your entire worldview destroyed.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Scientific Detachment in the Narration</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">

    <p data-narration-id="n11">The narrator describes horrors with clinical precision: people are “swept out of existence” as if in a scientific experiment. This detached tone reflects Wells’s training as a scientist but also creates dramatic irony — the reader feels the horror that the narrator suppresses.</p>

  </div></div>
</div>


<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n12" data-revision-tip="Close this and explain how Wells uses the Heat-Ray as an analogy for imperial weapons.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The Heat-Ray devastates the Surrey militia just as European weapons devastated colonised peoples. Wells reverses the power dynamic: now it is the British who face overwhelming technological superiority, forcing Victorian readers to empathise with the colonised.</p>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n14">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n15">Book 1 Chapters 1–7 establish the invasion through escalating horror: from curiosity to the devastating Heat-Ray.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n16">Documentary realism and first-person narration create eyewitness immediacy.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n17">The narrator’s initial complacency mirrors Victorian faith in superiority.</li></ul>
