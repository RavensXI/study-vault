<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Comparison Skills: Youth & Age</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The Youth and Age comparison names one poem; you choose another. The collection has a natural advantage: poems about different life stages create contrast automatically — a childhood poem paired with a death poem spans the full arc of human experience.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: What are the four strongest comparison strategies for Youth and Age poems?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Four strategies: <strong>Same stage, different attitude</strong> (two childhood poems with different views), <strong>Different stages, same theme</strong> (childhood + death, both about loss), <strong>Same focus</strong> (parent-child bond across two poems), <strong>Across time</strong> (Romantic + contemporary for AO3 contrast).</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Structuring Your Comparison</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">Use the five-step paragraph structure: topic sentence, Poem A analysis, comparative connective, Poem B analysis, context link. For Youth and Age, strong topic sentences frame comparison around <strong>time</strong>: “Both poets explore how time transforms the relationship between parent and child, but they present this transformation differently.”</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n11" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: write topic sentences for three different comparison paragraphs.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The best Y&A comparisons focus on <strong>what time does</strong>: Does it destroy innocence? Deepen understanding? Create grief? Bring freedom? The answer differs for every poem — explaining <em>why</em> through methods and context earns top marks.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">

    <p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Two separate analyses:</strong> Write about both poems in every paragraph. <strong>Only similarities:</strong> Include differences too. <strong>Describing not analysing:</strong> Focus on how and why, not what happens.</p>
  </div></div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n14">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n15">Choose pairs that create rich contrast: same stage/different attitude, different stages/same theme, or across time.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n16">Frame topic sentences around time and change — the collection’s central concern.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n17">Use five-step paragraph structure consistently; aim for 4–5 developed paragraphs.</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n13">Youth and Age poems have a natural advantage: the life-arc theme means any two poems can be compared through the lens of time and change. Even if the question focuses on something specific, connect it back to time and its effects.</p>
