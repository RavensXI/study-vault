--- DESCRIPTION ---
Identify when an unfamiliar popular music extract was written by listening for the production, instruments and vocal treatment that belong to each period of Area of Study 2.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Why the date matters</h2><p data-narration-id="n2">Section A plays you music you have never heard. The question is not only &ldquo;what can you hear?&rdquo; but &ldquo;where does this belong?&rdquo; Your exam covers four areas of popular music, and each is tied to a period: Broadway from the 1950s to the 1990s, rock of the 1960s and 1970s, film and computer gaming music from the 1990s onwards, and pop from the 1990s onwards.</p><p data-narration-id="n3">You are not asked to guess the year. You are asked to hear the features that belong to a period and name them. This lesson gives you those features.</p><h2 data-narration-id="n4">Rock of the 1960s and 1970s</h2><figure class="sv-embed"><div class="sv-embed-frame"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/Yjyj8qnqkYI" title="The Beatles — A Hard Day's Night" loading="lazy" allow="fullscreen" allowfullscreen></iframe></div><figcaption class="sv-embed-cap">Listen: The Beatles &mdash; <em>A Hard Day&rsquo;s Night</em> (1964). Live kit, jangling guitars, close vocal harmony &mdash; the sixties sound.</figcaption></figure><p data-narration-id="n5">The clearest clue is that the band sounds like people playing together in a room, because that is usually how it was recorded. The tempo breathes slightly instead of staying machine-exact. Drums are acoustic and you can hear the room around them.</p><p data-narration-id="n6">Listen for electric guitar with <dfn class="term" data-def="A warm, gritty distortion made by pushing an amplifier or pedal hard.">overdrive</dfn> or <dfn class="term" data-def="A heavier, buzzier distortion effect popular in the 1960s.">fuzz</dfn>, the Hammond organ or electric piano, and a bass guitar playing a real line rather than a synthesised one. Vocals are often <dfn class="term" data-def="The same vocal recorded twice and layered, thickening the sound.">double-tracked</dfn>, but never pitch-corrected.</p><p data-narration-id="n7">Structures are verse and chorus with room for an instrumental solo. Later in the 1970s some rock grows more ambitious: longer forms, changes of metre and whole album sides given to one piece.</p><div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n8" data-revision-tip="Cover the box, then from memory list the specific clues you use to run The Timing Test on an unfamiliar track."><div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact &mdash; The Timing Test</div><p>Ask yourself whether the drums are perfectly even. Human drumming pushes and pulls very slightly. A grid-exact beat almost always means programming or a drum machine. Those have been common since the early 1980s, so an exact beat rules out the live-band rock of the 1960s and 1970s.</p></div><h2 data-narration-id="n9">Pop from the 1990s to now</h2><figure class="sv-embed"><div class="sv-embed-frame"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/C-u5WLJ9Yk4" title="Britney Spears — …Baby One More Time" loading="lazy" allow="fullscreen" allowfullscreen></iframe></div><figcaption class="sv-embed-cap">Listen: Britney Spears &mdash; <em>&hellip;Baby One More Time</em> (1998). Drum machine, synth bass, studio-processed vocals &mdash; the production IS the date.</figcaption></figure><p data-narration-id="n10">Modern pop is built rather than performed. Drums are programmed or sampled and <dfn class="term" data-def="Snapped exactly onto the beat by software, so the timing is perfect.">quantised</dfn>, so the beat does not drift. Underneath sit synthesiser pads and bass, and often a <dfn class="term" data-def="A short section of music repeated over and over as the foundation of a track.">loop</dfn> that runs through the whole track.</p><p data-narration-id="n11">Vocals are the giveaway. They are layered, close-recorded and heavily treated, and from the late 1990s onwards you may hear <dfn class="term" data-def="Software that pulls a sung note exactly in tune, sometimes deliberately audible.">pitch correction</dfn>. Everything is compressed so the whole track sits loud and level.</p><p data-narration-id="n12">Structure is tighter than in older rock: a short introduction, verse, a <dfn class="term" data-def="A short passage that builds tension between verse and chorus.">pre-chorus</dfn>, then the chorus, often with a stripped-back middle section before the last one.</p><h2 data-narration-id="n13">Broadway from the 1950s to the 1990s</h2><figure class="sv-embed"><div class="sv-embed-frame"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/cuI5ATKPvp0" title="West Side Story (1961) — key scenes" loading="lazy" allow="fullscreen" allowfullscreen></iframe></div><figcaption class="sv-embed-cap">Watch: <em>West Side Story</em> (1961). Full pit orchestra, brassy stabs, Latin percussion, trained theatre voices.</figcaption></figure><p data-narration-id="n14">Broadway spans fifty years, so listen for where in that span an extract sits. The 1950s bring a large pit orchestra with lush strings and a singing style closer to the classical tradition. Songs carry the plot: an opening number that sets the scene, and a song where the main character states what they want.</p><p data-narration-id="n15">From the 1960s and 1970s, rock and pop instruments arrive in the pit &mdash; drum kit, electric guitar, electric bass. By the 1980s and 1990s you meet the big sung-through musical: little or no spoken dialogue, amplified voices, synthesisers alongside the orchestra, and themes that return across the whole show.</p><h2 data-narration-id="n16">Film and gaming music from the 1990s</h2><figure class="sv-embed"><div class="sv-embed-frame"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/lDlU08RU7Tk" title="Theme From Jurassic Park" loading="lazy" allow="fullscreen" allowfullscreen></iframe></div><figcaption class="sv-embed-cap">Listen: John Williams &mdash; <em>Jurassic Park</em> (1993). A full orchestra scoring wonder &mdash; the sound of the modern blockbuster.</figcaption></figure><p data-narration-id="n17">Orchestral scoring never went away, and a full orchestra with <dfn class="term" data-def="Short themes attached to a character, place or idea, returning whenever it does.">leitmotifs</dfn> remains common. What marks out the modern period is the blend: orchestra layered with synthesisers, electronic percussion and sound design, and driving <dfn class="term" data-def="A short pattern repeated persistently.">ostinato</dfn> patterns under action scenes.</p><p data-narration-id="n18">Game music has one feature film music does not need. It cannot know how long a scene will last, so it is written to loop seamlessly, and it often changes in layers &mdash; instruments are added or removed as the action intensifies rather than the music simply stopping and starting.</p><h2 data-narration-id="n19">Putting it together in the exam</h2><p data-narration-id="n20">Name the feature, then let it place the extract. &ldquo;The drums are programmed and exactly on the grid, and the vocal is layered and pitch-corrected, so this is pop from the 1990s or later&rdquo; earns marks twice over: it describes the music and it supplies the context.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<p>Every strand in this Area of Study is tied to a period. Rock of the 1960s and 1970s sounds like a band playing live; pop from the 1990s sounds assembled from programmed and processed parts. Broadway shifts from pit orchestra to sung-through and amplified. Film and gaming music blends orchestra with electronics, and game music loops and layers because it cannot know what happens next.</p>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p>Give a feature and the period it belongs to. One sentence that names the production and places it in time is worth more than a confident date with no evidence behind it.</p>
