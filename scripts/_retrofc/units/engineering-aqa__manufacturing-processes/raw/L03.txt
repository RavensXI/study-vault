--- DESCRIPTION ---
Press forming, sand casting, die casting and injection moulding — stages and where each fits.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Four Distinct Families</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Shaping, casting and moulding all give material a new form without removing it as waste chips &mdash; but the mechanisms are completely different. A common exam error is mixing stages from different processes. Keep these four families separate from the start:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n3"><strong>Press forming</strong> &mdash; squeeze a sheet between two solid dies to change its shape in the solid state.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n4"><strong>Injection moulding</strong> &mdash; melt polymer granules and inject under pressure into a closed metal mould.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n5"><strong>Sand casting</strong> &mdash; pour liquid metal into a cavity formed by compressing sand around a pattern.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Pressure die casting</strong> &mdash; inject liquid metal under high pressure into a precision reusable steel die.</li>
</ul>

<h2 data-narration-id="n7">Press Forming (and Related Sheet-Metal Operations)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n8"><dfn class="term" data-def="A manufacturing process in which sheet metal is forced between a punch and a die under high press force to produce a 3D shape in the solid state.">Press forming</dfn> places a flat sheet of metal between a <strong>punch</strong> (upper male tool) and a <strong>die</strong> (lower female tool). The press ram drives the punch down with enormous force &mdash; hundreds of tonnes for structural pressings &mdash; deforming the sheet permanently into the die cavity. The car body panel on every modern car is made this way: the outer skin of a vehicle door takes less than a second to press from a flat steel blank. Related sheet-metal operations include:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Bending and folding</strong> &mdash; a press-brake bends sheet along a straight line; a box-and-pan folder creates enclosure cases and duct sections.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Punching and blanking</strong> &mdash; a punch shears a hole (punching) or cuts a shaped blank (blanking) from sheet stock in a single stroke.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Stamping</strong> &mdash; a high-speed press stamps (embosses or cuts) repeated features &mdash; such as the tabs on a battery connector &mdash; into sheet at production rates of thousands per hour.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Composite lay-up</strong> &mdash; individual layers of glass-fibre or carbon-fibre cloth pre-impregnated with resin (&ldquo;prepreg&rdquo;) are laid by hand into a mould, then cured under heat and pressure in an autoclave. Used for F1 monocoques and wind-turbine blades where weight is critical.</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n13" data-revision-tip="Without looking, explain in one sentence each how press forming differs from vacuum forming and injection moulding.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Press forming acts on solid sheet metal between a punch and die. Vacuum forming stretches a heated thermoplastic sheet over a single mould using atmospheric pressure. Injection moulding melts polymer and injects it under pressure into a closed mould. These are three distinct processes &mdash; mixing their stages in an answer loses marks.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Sand Casting</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15">Sand casting is one of the oldest manufacturing processes still used today. A replica of the desired part &mdash; the <dfn class="term" data-def="A replica of the finished part, slightly oversized to allow for metal shrinkage, used to form the cavity in a sand mould.">pattern</dfn> &mdash; is placed inside a two-part wooden or metal moulding box (the <em>cope</em> and <em>drag</em>). Sand mixed with a bonding agent is rammed tightly around the pattern. The pattern is then removed, leaving a cavity. If the part requires an internal void (such as a pipe bore), a separate <strong>core</strong> of sand is placed inside the cavity. The two halves are clamped together.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n16">Molten metal is poured in through the <strong>sprue</strong> (the vertical channel), flows along the <strong>runner</strong> (horizontal channel) and enters the mould cavity through the <strong>gate</strong>. A <strong>riser</strong> (also called a feeder) is an additional reservoir of molten metal that compensates for shrinkage as the casting cools. Once solidified and cooled, the sand is broken away and the casting is removed. A <dfn class="term" data-def="A tapered angle on the side walls of a mould pattern that allows it to be withdrawn from the sand without collapsing the mould cavity.">draft angle</dfn> on the pattern walls is essential to release the pattern cleanly from the sand without crumbling the cavity walls. The casting is then fettled (excess metal from sprues and runners is cut away) and may be machined to final dimensions.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n24">Pressure Die Casting</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n25"><dfn class="term" data-def="A casting process in which molten metal is injected under high pressure into a precision steel die that is reused many thousands of times.">Pressure die casting</dfn> injects molten metal &mdash; typically aluminium, zinc or magnesium alloy &mdash; under high pressure (10&ndash;200 MPa) into a hardened steel die. The die is precision-machined and can be reused for tens of thousands of cycles. The high pressure forces metal into fine detail and thin sections that gravity alone cannot fill. The cooling rate is much faster than sand casting (the steel die conducts heat quickly), giving a finer grain structure and better mechanical properties.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n26">Advantages over sand casting: better surface finish, tighter dimensional tolerance, faster cycle time (seconds per part vs minutes), reusable die. Limitations: the steel die costs tens of thousands of pounds to make, so die casting is only economical at high volumes. It is also limited to non-ferrous metals &mdash; the high melting point of steel would destroy a steel die. Gearbox housings, engine covers and tap bodies are typical die-cast products.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n27">Injection Moulding</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n28">Injection moulding is the dominant process for producing plastic parts at high volume. Polymer granules are fed from a hopper into a heated barrel where a reciprocating screw melts and homogenises them. When sufficient melt has accumulated, the screw acts as a plunger, injecting the melt under high pressure through the gate into the closed mould cavity. The mould is water-cooled, so the part solidifies in seconds. The mould then opens and ejector pins push the part free. Cycle times are typically 15&ndash;60 seconds for most consumer products.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n29">Critical design features for injection moulding: the mould must have a draft angle on all walls so the part releases cleanly; wall thickness should be uniform to avoid sink marks and warping; internal ribs increase stiffness without adding bulk. The mould tool costs &pound;20,000&ndash;&pound;200,000 but is amortised across millions of parts. This process produces everything from mobile phone cases to car dashboards.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n30" data-revision-tip="Cover this and state: which two casting processes use a reusable die, and which uses a disposable sand mould?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Injection moulding and pressure die casting both use a reusable precision metal die/mould; sand casting uses a single-use sand mould that is destroyed each cycle. The reusable die gives better surface finish and tighter tolerances but requires high capital investment.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n36">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n37">Press forming squeezes solid sheet metal between a punch and die. Injection moulding melts polymer and injects it into a closed metal mould. Sand casting pours liquid metal into a disposable sand mould. Pressure die casting injects liquid metal into a reusable precision steel die &mdash; keep these stages separate.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n38">The draft angle is essential on mould patterns and die walls so the solidified part or pattern releases cleanly without damaging the mould cavity.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n39">Sand casting suits ferrous metals and low volumes (cheap tooling); pressure die casting and injection moulding suit high volumes (expensive reusable tooling amortised across many parts).</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n35">The biggest mistake in shaping/moulding questions is mixing up process stages. For press forming: punch-and-die, solid sheet, high force. For injection moulding: melt-and-inject polymer into a closed mould. For sand casting: pattern, sand mould, pour liquid metal, break mould. For pressure die casting: inject liquid metal into reusable steel die under high pressure. If an exam question describes &ldquo;vacuum forming&rdquo; stages alongside injection moulding, recognise these are wrong &mdash; draw a mental line between the four families before writing your answer. Always include the draft angle as a key design feature for any process involving a mould or die.</p>
