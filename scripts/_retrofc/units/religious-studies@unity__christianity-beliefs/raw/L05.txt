--- DESCRIPTION ---
Original sin, the fall of humanity, grace, and the role of Christ in offering salvation.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Is Sin?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">In Christianity, <dfn class="term" data-def="Any action or thought that separates humans from God, going against God’s laws and wishes.">sin</dfn> means any action or thought that goes against God’s will. Sin damages the relationship between humans and God. It can refer to specific wrong actions — such as lying, stealing, or harming others — but it can also describe a deeper state of being separated from God.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Most Christians believe in <dfn class="term" data-def="When a person acts or thinks against God’s will, damaging their personal relationship with God.">personal sin</dfn> — when someone individually chooses to do something wrong, breaking God’s commandments. This is different from the concept of original sin, which affects all of humanity from birth.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down what you recall about sin separates humans from God.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Sin separates humans from God. The consequence of sin is believed to be death, but those who are saved receive eternal life.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Original Sin and the Fall</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6"><dfn class="term" data-def="The Christian doctrine, credited to St Augustine, that all humans are born with a built-in tendency to sin, inherited from Adam and Eve’s disobedience.">Original sin</dfn> is the belief that every human is born with a built-in tendency to do wrong. The concept is generally credited to St Augustine and is an important doctrine within the Catholic Church.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">According to the Book of Genesis, God placed Adam and Eve in the Garden of Eden and gave them freedom to enjoy everything — except the fruit from the Tree of Knowledge of Good and Evil. A serpent tempted Eve, and both Adam and Eve ate the forbidden fruit. This act of disobedience is known as “the Fall.”</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>The Consequences of the Fall</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n8">Eating the fruit made Adam and Eve aware of their own sinful act. They were cast out of paradise, and their disobedience had lasting consequences: separation from God and the introduction of death into the world. Christians believe that this damaged human nature was passed down through all their descendants. As a result, every person is born with an inherited tendency to sin.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9">Only Mary (Jesus’ mother) and Jesus himself are believed by Catholics to have been born without original sin. The doctrine teaches that because Adam and Eve sinned, all humans need saving — they cannot fix the problem on their own.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n10" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Can you recall this quote and explain its meaning?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>“Original sin means the first sin of Adam and Eve. They disobeyed God by eating fruit from the forbidden tree. Humans are now born with a built-in tendency to sin.”</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n11">What Is Salvation?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n12"><dfn class="term" data-def="Being saved from sin and its consequences, and being granted eternal life with God.">Salvation</dfn> means being saved from sin and its consequences and being granted eternal life with God. Because sin has separated humans from God, salvation is the way back — it enables humans to restore their relationship with God and receive the promise of heaven.</p>
<figure class="diagram"><img src="https://pub-aeb94e100e5a48f4a133be5bf206aecb.r2.dev/religious-education/christianity-beliefs/diagram_generated_da1acb44.png" alt="Generated diagram for Sin and Salvation"><figcaption>Generated diagram for Sin and Salvation</figcaption></figure>
<p data-narration-id="n13">Christians believe that they need saving from sin so they can have a good relationship with God and avoid hell. Most Christians believe that if they put their faith in Jesus, confess their sins and ask for forgiveness, they will be saved.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n14">How Is Salvation Achieved?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15">the specification identifies several means of salvation: law, <dfn class="term" data-def="A quality of God which provides love and support to humans that they do not need to earn.">grace</dfn>, and the Holy Spirit. Different Christian traditions place different emphasis on each of these.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Salvation through Law, Grace and Spirit</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Law:</strong> In the Old Testament, Jews tried to achieve salvation by following God’s laws, including the Ten Commandments. Christians believe they no longer need to follow all of the Jewish Law because Jesus fulfilled it through his life, death and resurrection. However, many Christians still see living a moral life and doing good works as part of their response to God’s gift of salvation.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Grace:</strong> Grace is God’s unconditional love and favour, given freely to humans without them needing to earn it. Many Christians — especially Protestants — believe that salvation comes through grace alone. Jesus coming to save humanity was the ultimate expression of God’s grace. As the Bible teaches: “God so loved the world that He gave his only Son, so that whoever believes in Him shall not perish but have eternal life” (John 3:16).</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n18"><strong>The Holy Spirit:</strong> Many Christians believe that they receive God’s grace through the <dfn class="term" data-def="The third person of the Trinity, believed to be God’s inspiring presence guiding and comforting Christians in the world.">Holy Spirit</dfn>, which guides, comforts and strengthens believers in their daily lives. Christians also receive grace through the Bible, prayer and the sacraments.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Contrasting Views: Salvation by Works vs Salvation by Faith</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n19"><strong>Salvation by works:</strong> Some Christians believe that doing good deeds and following God’s commandments is essential for salvation. The Old Testament emphasises having faith in God and following his rules. The Parable of the Sheep and the Goats (Matthew 25) suggests that people will be judged on their actions — those who helped others will enter heaven.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Salvation by faith (grace):</strong> Other Christians, particularly Protestants, believe that salvation comes through faith in Jesus Christ and God’s grace alone — not through good works. They point to passages such as “‘Very truly I tell you, the one who believes has eternal life’ (John 6:47, NIV). Prayer and asking for forgiveness when we make mistakes are also part of receiving God’s grace.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n21">The Role of Christ in Salvation: Atonement</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22"><dfn class="term" data-def="Restoring the relationship between people and God through the life, death and resurrection of Jesus.">Atonement</dfn> is at the heart of Christian belief about salvation. It means restoring the relationship between humans and God through the life, death and resurrection of Jesus. Christians believe that Jesus’ death makes up for the original sin committed by Adam and Eve, and so brings people back to God.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n23">As Son of God, Jesus could easily have avoided being crucified. While he was on the cross, some people shouted to him to use his power to get down. However, he knew his death was needed to restore the relationship between God and believers. This gave all people the opportunity for salvation.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n24" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Can you recall this quote and explain its meaning?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>“For since death came through a man, the resurrection of the dead also comes through a man. For as in Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive.” — 1 Corinthians 15:21–22</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n25">Jesus’ resurrection, three days after his death, showed Christians that death had been overcome and that the goodness of Jesus had defeated the power of evil. Atonement removes the effects of sin and allows people to restore their relationship with God. This is only possible because Jesus atoned for the sins of humanity when he died and rose again.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n26">As the Bible says: “If anybody does sin, we have an advocate with the Father — Jesus Christ, the Righteous One. He is the atoning sacrifice for our sins, and not only for ours but also for the sins of the whole world” (1 John 2:1–2).</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Why Atonement Matters to Christians Today</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n27">Atonement is important to Christians because it demonstrates God’s all-loving nature — he died for human beings so they could live with God in peace. It shows the power of good over evil and provides an example for Christians to follow. Atonement offers hope in a world full of suffering. It is remembered in the celebration of Easter and the practice of Holy Communion, and it remains the central message of Christian evangelism.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n29">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n30">Original sin entered the world through Adam and Eve’s disobedience in the Garden of Eden, leaving all humans with an inherited tendency to sin and separated from God.</li><li data-narration-id="n31">Salvation — being saved from sin and death — can be achieved through law, grace and the Holy Spirit, with Christians holding contrasting views on whether works or faith alone are required.</li><li data-narration-id="n32">Jesus’ death and resurrection provide atonement, restoring the broken relationship between humanity and God and offering the promise of eternal life.</li></ul>
