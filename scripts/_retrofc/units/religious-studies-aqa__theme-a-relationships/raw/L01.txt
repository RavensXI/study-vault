<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Human Sexuality</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="How people express themselves as sexual beings, including heterosexual, homosexual and bisexual attraction.">Human sexuality</dfn> refers to how people experience and express sexual attraction. People may be <dfn class="term" data-def="Being sexually attracted to members of the opposite sex.">heterosexual</dfn> (attracted to the opposite sex), homosexual (attracted to the same sex), or bisexual (attracted to both sexes). In contemporary British society, all three orientations are widely accepted and protected by law.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="From memory, write down the Catholic, liberal Christian, and Islamic views on homosexuality.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Both Christianity and Islam teach that sexual relationships should take place within marriage. However, there is a range of views within Christianity on homosexuality — from conservative opposition to liberal acceptance.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Sexual Relationships Before & Outside Marriage</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">In modern Britain, sex before marriage is common and widely accepted. However, most religious traditions teach that sexual intimacy belongs within the commitment of marriage.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n6">The Catholic Church teaches that sex must only take place within marriage. St Paul wrote: “Flee from sexual immorality” (1 Corinthians 6:18). Pope Pius XI declared in 1930 that “every sexual act must be within a framework of marriage.” The Church of England generally agrees but is more lenient towards committed couples. Liberal Protestants tend to accept sex in loving, stable relationships even without marriage.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n7">Islam is clear that sex must only happen within marriage. The Qur’an forbids <dfn class="term" data-def="Unlawful sexual activity in Islam, including adultery, sex before marriage and homosexual acts.">zinah</dfn> (unlawful sexual intercourse). Under Shari’ah law, sex before marriage is a serious offence. Abdul Wahid Hamid wrote: “The only way to protect all within society is to maintain a society where only a man and his wife share the act of sex.”</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n8">Both Christianity and Islam also condemn <dfn class="term" data-def="Voluntary sexual intercourse between a married person and someone who is not their spouse.">adultery</dfn>. The Ten Commandments state: “You shall not commit adultery” (Exodus 20:14). The Qur’an (17:32) warns: “Do not go anywhere near adultery: it is an outrage, and an evil path.”</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n9">The Nature & Purpose of Marriage</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">Marriage is a formal, legal union between two people. For religious believers, it is more than a legal contract — it is a sacred commitment before God. Christians call marriage a <dfn class="term" data-def="A visible sign of God’s grace, such as baptism or marriage, regarded as sacred by many Christians.">sacrament</dfn> (Catholics and some Anglicans) or a <dfn class="term" data-def="A solemn promise or commitment, especially one made before God during a religious ceremony.">covenant</dfn> before God.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n11" data-revision-tip="Without looking, write the key scripture reference on the purpose of marriage for Christianity and for Islam.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The purposes of marriage include: lifelong companionship, a stable environment for children, sexual intimacy within a committed relationship, and (for religious believers) following God’s plan. Genesis 2:24 says a man shall “be united to his wife, and they will become one flesh.” The Qur’an (30:21) describes marriage as a source of “love and mercy.”</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n12">Same-Sex Marriage & Cohabitation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">In a Christian marriage ceremony, couples exchange vows before God, promising to love and cherish each other “for better, for worse, for richer, for poorer, in sickness and in health, till death do us part.” In Islam, the marriage contract is called a <dfn class="term" data-def="The Islamic marriage contract, a formal agreement between the bride and groom that includes a financial gift (mahr) from the groom to the bride.">nikah</dfn>. It includes a mahr (financial gift from groom to bride), and both parties must freely consent. The ceremony is often simple, witnessed by family and an imam.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Same-sex marriage became legal in England and Wales in 2013. Many liberal Christians support it, arguing that love and commitment matter more than the gender of the partners. However, the Catholic Church and many evangelical Christians oppose same-sex marriage, teaching that marriage is between a man and a woman as God intended.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15">Islam does not recognise same-sex marriage. Traditional Islamic teaching holds that marriage can only be between a man and a woman, based on the Qur’an’s teachings about the natural order of creation.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n16"><dfn class="term" data-def="Living together in a sexual relationship without being married.">Cohabitation</dfn> (living together without marriage) is increasingly common in Britain. Most Christians prefer marriage but liberal denominations accept cohabitation in committed relationships. Islam does not accept cohabitation because sexual relationships outside marriage are forbidden.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Divorce & Remarriage</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Divorce is the legal ending of a marriage. In Britain, divorce is common — roughly 42% of marriages end in divorce. Common reasons include adultery, unreasonable behaviour, growing apart, financial problems and domestic abuse. Religious attitudes to divorce vary significantly, and the question of whether remarriage should be permitted after divorce is closely linked to beliefs about the permanence of marriage vows.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n19">The Catholic Church does not allow divorce because marriage is a sacrament. Jesus taught: “What God has joined together, let no one separate” (Mark 10:9). Catholics may obtain an <dfn class="term" data-def="A declaration by the Catholic Church that a marriage was never valid in the first place, allowing both parties to remarry.">annulment</dfn> if the marriage was not valid. The Church of England permits divorce and some clergy will remarry divorced people. Most Protestant churches accept divorce as a last resort, showing <dfn class="term" data-def="Sympathetic concern for the suffering of others, often cited as a reason for accepting divorce when a marriage causes harm.">compassion</dfn> for those in unhappy or abusive marriages.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Islamic Views on Divorce</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n20">Islam permits divorce but discourages it. The Prophet Muhammad said: “Of all the lawful things, divorce is the most hated by Allah.” There is a waiting period called <dfn class="term" data-def="A three-month waiting period after divorce in Islam, during which a woman cannot remarry, to ensure she is not pregnant.">iddah</dfn> (three months) to allow time for reconciliation and to check if the wife is pregnant. Both men and women can initiate divorce in Islam, and remarriage is permitted after the iddah period.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n21" data-revision-tip="Cover this and explain the Catholic, Protestant, and Islamic positions on divorce, with one teaching for each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The ethical tension around divorce centres on two principles: the <em>sanctity of marriage vows</em> (marriage is sacred and permanent) versus <em>compassion</em> (it is cruel to force people to stay in harmful or loveless marriages). Different religious traditions balance these principles differently.</p>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n23">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n24">Both Christianity and Islam teach that sexual relationships belong within marriage, though liberal Christians are more accepting of sex in committed relationships and same-sex partnerships.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n25">Marriage serves purposes of companionship, procreation and following God’s plan. Same-sex marriage is accepted by some Christians but rejected by Catholics, evangelicals and Islam.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n26">Divorce involves a tension between the sanctity of vows (Catholic view: no divorce) and compassion (Protestant and Islamic views: divorce as a last resort).</li></ul>
