<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Contraception & Family Planning</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="The deliberate use of methods or devices to prevent pregnancy, such as the contraceptive pill, condoms or implants.">Contraception</dfn> is the deliberate prevention of pregnancy. In modern Britain, contraception is widely available and accepted as a normal part of <dfn class="term" data-def="The practice of controlling the number of children in a family and the intervals between their births, often using contraception.">family planning</dfn>. However, religious views on contraception vary significantly.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="From memory, explain the Catholic and Protestant views on contraception. Name the relevant Catholic document.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The Catholic Church is the only major Christian denomination that opposes all <dfn class="term" data-def="Methods of preventing pregnancy that involve physical barriers, hormones or devices, such as condoms, the pill or IUDs.">artificial contraception</dfn>. Pope Paul VI’s encyclical <em>Humanae Vitae</em> (1968) taught that every sexual act must be open to the possibility of creating life. Catholics may only use <dfn class="term" data-def="A method of family planning that involves tracking a woman’s fertility cycle and avoiding sex during fertile periods, accepted by the Catholic Church.">natural family planning</dfn> (tracking fertility cycles).</p>
</div>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n4">Most Protestant denominations, including the Church of England, accept the use of artificial contraception. They argue that responsible family planning allows couples to provide better for the children they do have. The Methodist Church stated that “responsible contraception is a welcome means towards fulfilment in marriage.” Contraception can also help prevent the spread of sexually transmitted infections. Protestants see family planning as a practical way to show love and responsibility.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n5">Islamic views on contraception are divided. Many Muslim scholars accept contraception within marriage, particularly if pregnancy would harm the mother’s health, the family cannot afford more children, or it would protect existing children’s welfare. The <dfn class="term" data-def="A record of the sayings, actions and approval of the Prophet Muhammad, used alongside the Qur’an as a source of guidance in Islam.">Hadith</dfn> records that some companions of the Prophet practised withdrawal (coitus interruptus) and he did not forbid it. However, some scholars oppose contraception because children are a blessing from Allah and humans should not interfere with God’s will. Methods that are permanent (such as sterilisation) are generally considered haram unless medically necessary.</p>

</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Sexual Relationships Before Marriage</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">The specification requires you to explain contrasting beliefs about sexual relationships before marriage. In contemporary British society, <dfn class="term" data-def="Engaging in sexual activity before marriage, common in modern Britain but opposed by many religious traditions.">pre-marital sex</dfn> is widely accepted. However, most religious traditions teach that sex should be reserved for marriage.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Christianity:</strong> The Catholic Church teaches that sex must only take place within marriage. St Paul wrote: “Flee from sexual immorality” (1 Corinthians 6:18). However, liberal Protestants tend to accept sex within committed, loving relationships even without marriage.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Islam:</strong> Sex before marriage is strictly forbidden. The Qur’an prohibits <dfn class="term" data-def="Unlawful sexual activity in Islam, including adultery, sex before marriage and homosexual acts.">zinah</dfn> (unlawful sexual intercourse). Under Shari’ah law, sex outside marriage is a serious offence. There is no liberal/conservative divide on this issue in Islam — the teaching is clear and universal.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Same-Sex Relationships</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Same-sex relationships are legally protected in the UK. The Equality Act 2010 makes discrimination based on sexual orientation illegal. Same-sex marriage has been legal in England and Wales since 2013. However, religious views on <dfn class="term" data-def="Romantic and/or sexual relationships between two people of the same sex.">same-sex relationships</dfn> vary widely.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n12" data-revision-tip="Without looking, write the Christian range of views and the Islamic view on this topic, with one source for each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>AQA requires you to explain contrasting beliefs on same-sex relationships with reference to Christianity and at least one other religion. Christianity has a wide range of views, from strong opposition to full acceptance. Islam is more uniformly opposed to same-sex relationships.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Christian Views on Same-Sex Relationships</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Conservative Christians</strong> oppose same-sex relationships. They cite Leviticus 18:22: “Do not have sexual relations with a man as one does with a woman; that is detestable.” The Catholic Church teaches that <dfn class="term" data-def="Having a homosexual orientation, which the Catholic Church does not consider sinful in itself, though homosexual acts are considered sinful.">homosexual orientation</dfn> is not sinful, but homosexual acts are. The Catechism states that homosexual people “must be accepted with respect, compassion, and sensitivity” but should remain chaste.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Liberal Christians</strong> support same-sex relationships. They argue that the Bible must be interpreted in its historical context. Jesus never spoke about homosexuality but taught love for all people. Some denominations, such as the Quakers and United Reformed Church, perform same-sex marriages. They point to Jesus’s commandment: “Love your neighbour as yourself” (Mark 12:31).</p>

  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n16">The Church of England occupies a middle ground. It welcomes homosexual people and recognises civil partnerships and same-sex marriages performed in civil ceremonies. However, it does not currently allow same-sex marriages to take place in its churches. In 2023, the Church of England voted to allow blessings for same-sex couples but stopped short of allowing full church weddings. This ongoing debate shows how religious organisations can evolve on social issues.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Islamic Views on Same-Sex Relationships</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Traditional Islamic teaching opposes all same-sex relationships. The Qur’an (7:80–81) tells the story of the Prophet Lut (Lot), whose people were punished for homosexual behaviour. Homosexual acts are classified as zinah under Shari’ah law.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n19">However, some progressive Muslim organisations in Britain argue for greater acceptance. Groups like the Inclusive Mosque Initiative welcome LGBTQ+ Muslims. Many British Muslims also acknowledge that while they may personally disagree with homosexuality on religious grounds, they respect the laws of the country they live in and oppose discrimination against anyone.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Non-Religious Perspectives</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n20">Humanists and atheists generally support same-sex relationships and full equality for LGBTQ+ people. They argue that moral decisions should be based on human rights, happiness and the harm principle — not on ancient religious texts. Since same-sex relationships between consenting adults cause no harm, there is no ethical reason to oppose them. The British Humanist Association actively campaigns for LGBTQ+ rights.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The three contrasting beliefs on same-sex relationships required by AQA are: (1) conservative Christians oppose them (Leviticus 18:22), (2) liberal Christians accept them (love is most important), and (3) Islam opposes them (Qur’an 7:80–81, story of Lut). You must be able to explain <em>why</em> each group holds its view.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n23">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n24">The Catholic Church opposes artificial contraception (<em>Humanae Vitae</em>), while most Protestants and many Muslims accept it within marriage for responsible family planning.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n25">Both Christianity and Islam teach that sex belongs within marriage, though liberal Christians are more accepting of pre-marital sex in committed relationships.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n26">Christian views on same-sex relationships range from conservative opposition (Leviticus 18:22) to liberal acceptance (love is most important). Islam traditionally opposes same-sex relationships (Qur’an 7:80–81).</li></ul>
