--- DESCRIPTION ---
How Austen explores pride, prejudice, class, marriage and reputation across the novel.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Pride and Prejudice — The Central Duality</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">The title names the novel’s two central flaws. Darcy’s <dfn class="term" data-def="An excessively high opinion of one’s own importance or standing.">pride</dfn> makes him dismissive; Elizabeth’s <dfn class="term" data-def="A preconceived opinion not based on reason.">prejudice</dfn> makes her judge harshly while trusting Wickham’s charm. Both stem from judging by appearance rather than character.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">Mary Bennet observes that “vanity and pride are different things”: pride relates to self-opinion, vanity to others’ opinions. Darcy’s pride is eventually compatible with goodness; Mrs Bennet’s vanity is driven by social anxiety.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: How does Austen distinguish between pride and vanity?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Mary states “vanity and pride are different things” — pride is justified self-respect, vanity is concern for others’ opinion. Darcy has pride; Mrs Bennet has vanity. The novel asks whether pride can coexist with humility.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Class, Wealth and Social Mobility</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n6">Class operates at every level. Lady Catherine represents aristocratic snobbery: “Are the shades of Pemberley to be thus polluted?” Caroline Bingley’s contempt reflects new money’s insecurity. Even Elizabeth judges Charlotte’s choice, revealing her own class assumptions about what constitutes an acceptable life.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n7">Austen does not reject the class system entirely — she values taste, education and responsibility. But she argues that these qualities are not the exclusive property of any class. The Gardiners, despite their trade connections, are more refined than many gentlemen.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Lady Catherine’s Confrontation</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">

    <p data-narration-id="n8">Lady Catherine demands Elizabeth refuse Darcy: “Are the shades of Pemberley to be thus polluted?” The metaphor of “pollution” reveals her view of class as biological purity. Elizabeth’s refusal — “I am only resolved to act in that manner which will constitute my happiness” — is her finest moment.</p>

  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Marriage and Reputation</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n10">Marriage is simultaneously love story and economic transaction. Austen argues the best marriages combine mutual respect, intellectual equality and affection. <dfn class="term" data-def="The general opinion held about a person, which in Regency society determined social standing.">Reputation</dfn> is the mechanism through which society controls individuals — Lydia’s scandal shows how one person’s behaviour can destroy an entire family’s prospects.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Pemberley as Symbol</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">

    <p data-narration-id="n11">Pemberley is a physical manifestation of Darcy’s character. The grounds are beautiful but natural, showing taste over ostentation. Elizabeth’s thought — “to be mistress of Pemberley might be something!” — is comic, but Austen also suggests that appreciating Pemberley means appreciating Darcy’s true qualities.</p>

  </div></div>
</div>


<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n12" data-revision-tip="Close this and explain how reputation functions as a theme.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Reputation is social currency. Lydia’s elopement threatens all sisters. Lady Catherine sees Elizabeth as polluting Pemberley’s reputation. Austen shows reputation constrains women more than men — Wickham escapes while Lydia would be destroyed.</p>
</div>
