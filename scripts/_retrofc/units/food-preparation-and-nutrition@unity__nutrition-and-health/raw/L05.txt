<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Is the Eatwell Guide?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The <dfn class="term" data-def="A healthy eating model published by Public Health England showing the types and proportions of foods needed for a well-balanced diet.">Eatwell Guide</dfn> is the UK government’s official healthy eating model. It shows the proportions in which different food groups should be eaten over the course of a day or more. The guide is suitable for most people aged 2 and over, and it applies to people of all ethnic backgrounds and body weights.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">The Eatwell Guide is shaped like a plate, divided into five food groups of different sizes. The size of each segment shows how much of your overall diet should come from that group. It does not mean every single meal must look like the plate — it represents what you eat across a whole day.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Can you recall all 5 items listed here?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The Eatwell Guide comprises <strong>five main food groups</strong>. It shows the proportions of each group needed for a balanced diet and is suitable for <strong>most people over 2 years of age</strong>. The proportions are representative of food eaten over a day or more, not a single meal.</p>




</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">The Five Food Groups</h2>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Group 1: Fruit and Vegetables</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n6">This group should make up just over a third of the food eaten each day. The recommendation is to eat at least <strong>five portions of a variety</strong> each day. Sources include fresh, frozen, canned, dried, or juiced fruit and vegetables.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n7">A single portion is around <strong>80g</strong> (roughly three heaped tablespoons). However, 30g of dried fruit or a 150ml glass of fruit juice or smoothie can only count as a maximum of <strong>one portion per day</strong>, because drying and juicing concentrates the sugars and removes fibre.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Group 2: Potatoes, Bread, Rice, Pasta and Other Starchy Carbohydrates</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n8"><dfn class="term" data-def="Foods rich in complex carbohydrates that provide the body with a slow, steady release of energy. Examples include bread, rice, pasta, and potatoes.">Starchy carbohydrates</dfn> should form the base of meals and make up just over a third of the diet. These foods provide energy, fibre, B vitamins, and minerals.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9">The guidance is to choose higher-fibre, wholegrain varieties where possible. Wholegrain options include wholemeal bread, brown rice, and wholewheat pasta. These release energy more slowly and contain more <dfn class="term" data-def="A type of carbohydrate found in plant foods that the body cannot digest. It helps the digestive system work properly and reduces the risk of heart disease and some cancers.">dietary fibre</dfn> than their refined equivalents.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Group 3: Dairy and Alternatives</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n10">Dairy products such as milk, cheese, and yoghurt are good sources of protein, vitamins, and an important source of <strong>calcium</strong>, which helps to keep bones strong. The advice is to choose lower-fat and lower-sugar products where possible.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n11">Alternatives include calcium-fortified soya, oat, or almond drinks. These are particularly important for people who follow a <dfn class="term" data-def="A diet that excludes all animal products including meat, fish, dairy, eggs, and honey.">vegan</dfn> diet or who have <dfn class="term" data-def="The inability to digest lactose, a sugar found in milk and dairy products, causing symptoms such as bloating, cramps, and diarrhoea.">lactose intolerance</dfn>.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Group 4: Beans, Pulses, Fish, Eggs, Meat and Other Protein</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n12">This group provides protein, vitamins, and minerals. The recommendation is to aim for at least <strong>two portions of fish per week</strong>, including one portion of oily fish (such as salmon, mackerel, or sardines). Oily fish is rich in omega-3 fatty acids, which help to protect the heart.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n13">People who eat more than 90g per day of red or processed meat should cut down to no more than <strong>70g per day</strong>. Beans and pulses are excellent alternatives because they are high in fibre, low in fat, and count towards the 5-a-day target.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Group 5: Oils and Spreads</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n14"><dfn class="term" data-def="Fats that are usually liquid at room temperature and come from plant sources such as olive oil, rapeseed oil, and sunflower oil. They are considered healthier than saturated fats.">Unsaturated fats</dfn> are healthier fats, usually from plant sources and in liquid form as oil — for example, olive oil or rapeseed oil. These should be used in small amounts.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n15">Generally, people are eating too much <dfn class="term" data-def="A type of fat found mainly in animal products and some processed foods. Eating too much raises blood cholesterol and increases the risk of heart disease.">saturated fat</dfn> and need to reduce their consumption. Saturated fats are found in butter, lard, cheese, cakes, biscuits, and fatty cuts of meat.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Foods High in Fat, Salt and Sugar</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">Products such as chocolate, cakes, biscuits, full-sugar soft drinks, butter, and ice cream are high in fat, sugar, and energy. These foods are <strong>not</strong> needed in the diet. If they are included, they should be consumed infrequently and in small amounts. They sit outside the main five food groups on the Eatwell Guide.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Composite and Combination Foods</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19">Much of the food people eat is in the form of dishes or meals made from more than one food group. These are called <dfn class="term" data-def="A food or dish made with ingredients from more than one food group, such as pizza, lasagne, casseroles, or sandwiches.">composite foods</dfn> (or combination foods). Examples include pizza, casseroles, spaghetti bolognese, lasagne, and sandwiches.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n20">For example, a lasagne contains: pasta sheets (starchy carbohydrates), onions, garlic and chopped tomatoes (fruit and vegetables), lean minced meat (protein), cheese sauce made with milk and cheese (dairy), and olive oil used to cook the mince (oils and spreads). Being able to identify which food group each ingredient belongs to is a key exam skill.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n21" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. List the exam technique advice given here.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>A <strong>composite food</strong> contains ingredients from more than one food group. In the exam, you may be asked to break down a dish into its Eatwell Guide components — for example, identifying the starchy carbohydrate, protein, dairy, fruit and vegetable, and oil elements in a recipe.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n22">The 8 Tips for Healthier Eating</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n23">The government provides eight practical tips that cover the basics of healthy eating:</p>

<div class="timeline" data-narration-id="n24">
  <div class="timeline-event">
    <div class="timeline-date">Tip 1</div>
    <h4>Base Your Meals on Starchy Carbohydrates</h4><p>Starchy foods should make up just over a third of everything you eat. Choose wholegrain varieties for extra fibre.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="timeline-event">
    <div class="timeline-date">Tip 2</div>
    <h4>Eat Lots of Fruit and Veg</h4><p>Aim for at least 5 portions of a variety of fruit and vegetables every day. Fresh, frozen, canned, dried, and juiced all count.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="timeline-event">
    <div class="timeline-date">Tip 3</div>
    <h4>Eat More Fish</h4><p>Aim for at least 2 portions of fish a week, including 1 portion of oily fish such as salmon or mackerel.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="timeline-event">
    <div class="timeline-date">Tip 4</div>
    <h4>Cut Down on Saturated Fat and Sugar</h4><p>Too much saturated fat raises blood cholesterol. Too much sugar causes tooth decay and weight gain. Check food labels to make informed choices.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="timeline-event">
    <div class="timeline-date">Tip 5</div>
    <h4>Eat Less Salt</h4><p>Adults should eat no more than <strong>6g of salt per day</strong>. Too much salt raises blood pressure, which increases the risk of heart disease and stroke.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="timeline-event">
    <div class="timeline-date">Tip 6</div>
    <h4>Get Active and Be a Healthy Weight</h4><p>Regular physical activity helps maintain a healthy weight and reduces the risk of diet-related diseases.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="timeline-event">
    <div class="timeline-date">Tip 7</div>
    <h4>Don’t Get Thirsty</h4><p>Aim for 6–8 glasses of fluid every day. Water, lower-fat milk, and sugar-free drinks all count. Limit fruit juice and smoothies to a combined total of 150ml per day.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="timeline-event">
    <div class="timeline-date">Tip 8</div>
    <h4>Don’t Skip Breakfast</h4><p>A healthy breakfast provides energy and some of the vitamins and minerals needed for good health. Wholegrain cereals with fruit are a nutritious choice.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n25">Hydration</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n26"><dfn class="term" data-def="The process of replacing water in the body. The body needs around 6 to 8 glasses of fluid per day to stay properly hydrated.">Hydration</dfn> means replacing the water your body loses throughout the day. The recommendation is to drink <strong>6–8 glasses of fluid</strong> every day. Water, lower-fat milk, and sugar-free drinks including tea and coffee all count towards this total.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n27">Fruit juice and smoothies also count, but they should be limited to no more than a combined total of <strong>150ml per day</strong> because of their high sugar content. Staying hydrated helps concentration, digestion, and kidney function.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n28">Dietary Fibre</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n29">Dietary fibre is a type of carbohydrate found in plant foods. Good sources include wholegrain cereals and cereal products, oats, beans, lentils, fruit, vegetables, nuts, and seeds. The recommended average intake is <strong>30g per day</strong> for adults.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n30">Dietary fibre helps to reduce the risk of heart disease, diabetes, and some cancers. It also helps with weight control, bulks up stools, prevents constipation, and improves gut health.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n31" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down what you recall about adults need 30g of dietary fibre per day.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Adults need <strong>30g of dietary fibre per day</strong>. Good sources include wholegrain cereals, oats, beans, lentils, fruit, vegetables, nuts, and seeds. Fibre reduces the risk of heart disease, diabetes, and some cancers, and helps prevent constipation.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n32">Adapting Diets for Different Life Stages</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n33">Nutritional needs change throughout life. The Eatwell Guide principles apply to everyone aged 2 and over, but the amounts and emphasis differ by life stage.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Nutritional Needs by Life Stage</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n34"><strong>Children (2–11 years):</strong> Need energy-dense foods for growth and development. Require calcium and vitamin D for strong bones and teeth. Smaller portions than adults, but meals should still follow the Eatwell Guide proportions. Limit sugary snacks and drinks.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n35"><strong>Teenagers (12–18 years):</strong> High energy needs due to rapid growth spurts. Extra iron is important, especially for girls after the onset of menstruation. Calcium is critical as bones are still developing. Protein is needed for muscle growth.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n36"><strong>Adults (19–64 years):</strong> Energy needs depend on activity level and body size. A balanced diet following the Eatwell Guide helps maintain a healthy weight and reduce the risk of diet-related diseases such as type 2 diabetes and coronary heart disease.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n37"><strong>Elderly (65+ years):</strong> Energy needs decrease as activity levels drop, but nutrient needs remain high. Calcium and vitamin D are especially important to prevent osteoporosis. Fibre helps with digestion. Smaller, nutrient-dense meals are recommended.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n38">Adapting Diets for Dietary Groups</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n39">Some people need to modify the Eatwell Guide to suit their dietary requirements, whether for ethical, religious, health, or medical reasons.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Common Dietary Modifications</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n40"><strong>Vegetarian:</strong> Excludes meat and fish but includes dairy and eggs. Protein can come from beans, pulses, eggs, cheese, tofu, and Quorn. <dfn class="term" data-def="The practice of combining different plant-based protein sources in one meal to obtain all essential amino acids. Examples include beans on toast and rice with peas.">Protein complementation</dfn> — combining different plant proteins at the same meal — ensures all essential amino acids are consumed.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n41"><strong>Vegan:</strong> Excludes all animal products including dairy, eggs, and honey. Alternative calcium sources include fortified plant milks, tofu, and green leafy vegetables. Vitamin B12 must come from fortified foods or supplements as it is only found naturally in animal products.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n42"><strong>Coeliac disease:</strong> An autoimmune condition triggered by <dfn class="term" data-def="A protein found in wheat, barley, and rye. People with coeliac disease must avoid gluten as it damages the lining of the small intestine.">gluten</dfn>, a protein found in wheat, barley, and rye. People with coeliac disease must follow a strict gluten-free diet. Alternatives include rice, potatoes, corn, and gluten-free flour blends.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n43"><strong>Lactose intolerant:</strong> People who cannot digest lactose, the sugar found in milk, must avoid or limit dairy products. Alternatives include lactose-free milk, calcium-fortified plant milks, and hard cheeses (which are naturally lower in lactose).</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n44"><strong>High-fibre diet:</strong> Recommended for people at risk of constipation, bowel disease, or heart disease. Increase wholegrain cereals, beans, lentils, fruit, and vegetables. The target is 30g of fibre per day for adults.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n45">Portion Size and Costing</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n46">Understanding portion size helps people follow the Eatwell Guide without overeating. For example, a portion of fruit or vegetables is about 80g, a portion of meat or fish is roughly the size of a deck of cards, and a portion of cheese is about 30g (matchbox-sized).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n47">When planning meals, costing is also important. Dried pulses and frozen vegetables are often cheaper than fresh alternatives and can help families eat healthily on a budget. Batch cooking and using seasonal produce are practical strategies that reduce food costs while following the Eatwell Guide principles.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n49">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n50">The Eatwell Guide shows the proportions of five food groups needed for a balanced diet — fruit and vegetables and starchy carbohydrates should each make up about a third of your plate.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n51">The 8 tips for healthier eating provide practical guidance including eating 5 portions of fruit and veg daily, limiting salt to 6g per day, drinking 6–8 glasses of fluid, and choosing wholegrain and unsaturated fat options.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n52">Diets must be adapted for different life stages (children, teenagers, adults, elderly) and dietary groups (vegetarian, vegan, coeliac, lactose intolerant), while still following the Eatwell Guide principles.</li></ul>

For ‘explain’ questions, state the nutritional benefit — for example, “switching to wholegrain bread increases dietary fibre intake, which helps prevent constipation and reduces the risk of heart disease.” For ‘discuss’ questions, consider both sides and reach a justified conclusion.</p>
