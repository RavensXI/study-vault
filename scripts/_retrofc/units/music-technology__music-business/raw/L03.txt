--- DESCRIPTION ---
How music is marketed, promoted and distributed through physical and digital channels.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Getting Music to the Audience</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Creating great music is only half the story. Artists and labels must market, promote and distribute their work to reach listeners. This lesson covers the three key stages: marketing (building awareness), promotion (generating interest) and distribution (getting the product to consumers).</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Marketing</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4"><dfn class="term" data-def="The process of researching, planning and executing strategies to promote a product to a target audience.">Marketing</dfn> is about understanding your audience and creating a strategy to reach them. It begins with <dfn class="term" data-def="The process of gathering information about a target audience, including their age, location, tastes and habits.">market research</dfn> — gathering data about the target audience. This includes identifying the <dfn class="term" data-def="A specific group of consumers defined by characteristics such as age, gender, location or musical taste.">audience demographic</dfn>: their age, gender, location, income and musical preferences.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n5"><dfn class="term" data-def="The process of creating a recognisable identity for an artist or product through logos, colour schemes and consistent visual style.">Branding</dfn> is a central part of music marketing. It involves developing a recognisable identity through logos, colour schemes, typography and a consistent visual style. Strong branding makes an artist instantly recognisable — think of how certain artists have distinctive colour palettes and imagery across all their releases.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Marketing <dfn class="term" data-def="A coordinated series of promotional activities designed to increase an artist’s visibility and reach.">campaigns</dfn> are coordinated efforts to increase visibility. A campaign might include a countdown to release, teaser videos, social media activity and timed press coverage, all designed to build anticipation.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n7" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: what are the three elements of music marketing?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The three elements of music marketing are: market research (understanding the audience demographic), branding (creating a recognisable identity with logos and colour schemes) and campaigns (coordinated activities to increase visibility).</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n8">Promotion</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9">Promotion is how artists generate interest and excitement about their music. The spec divides promotion into two categories: physical and digital.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Physical Promotion</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n10">Physical promotion involves real-world, in-person activities. There are three main types:</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Appearances</strong> — launch parties, radio and TV show appearances, and press interviews all help artists connect directly with audiences and media. A well-timed TV interview before a release date can generate significant buzz.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Performances</strong> — gigs, tours and festival appearances put the artist in front of live audiences. Touring is one of the most effective promotional tools because it builds a loyal fan base and generates word-of-mouth.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Materials</strong> — physical promotional materials include posters, billboards and flyers. These are placed in strategic locations such as music venues, record shops and high-traffic areas to catch the attention of potential fans.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Digital Promotion</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n14">Digital promotion uses online platforms to reach audiences at scale.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Content</strong> — social media posts and stories, promotional videos, behind-the-scenes content and live streaming all keep fans engaged between releases. Regular, authentic content builds a relationship with the audience.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Platforms</strong> — official websites act as a central hub for news, tour dates and merchandise. Social media pages (Instagram, TikTok, X, YouTube) allow direct interaction with fans. Each platform has its own audience and content style — short-form video works well on TikTok, while longer content suits YouTube.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Selling & Distribution</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Once music is created and promoted, it needs to reach consumers. This involves choosing the right media products, formats and distribution channels.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n19">Media Products & Formats</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Artists can release music as a <strong>single</strong>, <strong>EP</strong> (extended play, typically 3–6 tracks), <strong>album</strong> (full-length collection) or <strong>music video</strong>. These are distributed in two main formats:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Physical formats</strong> — vinyl records, CDs, cassettes and DVDs. Physical formats still have a market, especially for collectors and fans who value tangible products.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Digital formats</strong> — compressed audio (e.g. MP3, AAC), uncompressed audio (e.g. WAV, FLAC), compressed video and uncompressed video. Digital formats can be downloaded or streamed.</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Merchandise</strong> (t-shirts, posters, limited editions) is also an important revenue stream, often sold alongside music at gigs and online.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n24" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: name two physical and two digital formats for distributing music.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Physical distribution formats include vinyl, CD, cassette and DVD. Digital distribution formats include compressed audio (MP3), uncompressed audio (WAV) and streaming. Artists also sell merchandise alongside their music.</p>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n25">Distribution Channels</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n26"><strong>Physical distribution</strong> involves manufacturing (pressing vinyl, printing CDs), shipping products to retailers and selling through physical shops. This requires significant upfront investment.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n27"><strong>Digital distribution</strong> uses <dfn class="term" data-def="Companies that deliver an artist’s music to online stores and streaming platforms on their behalf.">aggregators</dfn> (such as DistroKid or TuneCore) to deliver music to online stores (iTunes, Amazon Music) and streaming platforms (Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music). Aggregators handle the technical process of getting music onto platforms.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n28">Admin Services</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n29">Two important organisations support artists’ rights and income:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n30"><dfn class="term" data-def="Performing Right Society for Music — a UK organisation that collects royalties on behalf of songwriters, composers and publishers.">PRS for Music</dfn> — collects royalties every time a song is performed publicly, broadcast on radio/TV or streamed online. Songwriters and composers register their works with PRS to receive payment.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n31">The <dfn class="term" data-def="A trade union that represents and supports professional musicians in the UK.">Musicians’ Union</dfn> — a trade union that represents professional musicians. It offers legal advice, contract support, insurance and campaigns for fair pay and working conditions.</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n32" data-revision-tip="Close this and explain: what does PRS for Music do and who does it help?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>PRS for Music collects royalties for songwriters, composers and publishers whenever their music is performed, broadcast or streamed. The Musicians’ Union represents and supports professional musicians with legal advice and fair pay campaigns.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n34">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n35">Marketing involves market research, branding and campaigns to build awareness of an artist or release.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n36">Promotion is divided into physical (appearances, performances, materials) and digital (content, platforms).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n37">Distribution covers media products, physical and digital formats, aggregators and admin services like PRS for Music and the Musicians’ Union.</li>
</ul>
