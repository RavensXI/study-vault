<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Who Works in the Music Business?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The music industry relies on a wide range of professionals working together to create, produce, promote and distribute music. Each role has distinct responsibilities, and understanding them is essential for your NCFE exam.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Creative Roles</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">At the heart of the music business are the people who create and perform the music itself.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n5">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A person who creates and performs original music.">musical artist</dfn> is responsible for the creation and performance of music. This can include writing, singing, playing instruments and performing live. Artists are the public-facing talent that audiences connect with.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A person who writes the musical composition, including melody and harmony.">composer</dfn> or songwriter writes the musical composition — the melody, harmony and overall structure of a piece. A <dfn class="term" data-def="A person who writes the words for a song.">lyricist</dfn> focuses specifically on writing the words. These roles can overlap: many artists write their own songs, but some work with dedicated songwriters and lyricists.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">An <dfn class="term" data-def="A person who adapts a musical composition for specific instruments or voices.">arranger</dfn> takes an existing composition and adapts it for specific instruments, voices or ensembles. For example, an arranger might take a pop song and create a version for a string quartet.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n8" data-revision-tip="Cover this and list: what are the four main creative roles in the music business?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The main creative roles are: musical artist (creation & performance), composer/songwriter (melody & harmony), lyricist (words), and arranger (adapting compositions for different instruments or voices).</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Session Musicians & Sound Designers</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n9"><dfn class="term" data-def="Skilled musicians hired to perform on recordings or at live events, rather than being a permanent band member.">Session musicians</dfn> are skilled performers hired to play on recordings or at live events. They are not permanent members of a band but are brought in for their expertise on a particular instrument. Studios rely on session musicians to add professional-quality parts to tracks.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n10">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A professional who creates and manipulates audio elements such as effects, atmospheres and textures.">sound designer</dfn> creates and manipulates audio elements — effects, atmospheres and textures. Sound designers work across music production, film, television and gaming. They might create unique synthesiser patches, design ambient soundscapes or produce Foley effects.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n11">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A performer who selects and mixes pre-recorded music for a live audience or broadcast.">DJ</dfn> selects, mixes and plays pre-recorded music for live audiences or broadcasts. DJs use turntables, CDJs, mixers and software to create seamless transitions between tracks. Some DJs also produce original music.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n12">Production & Technical Roles</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">A <dfn class="term" data-def="The person who oversees the creative and technical aspects of a recording, shaping the final sound.">producer</dfn> oversees the creative and technical direction of a recording. They shape the overall sound, make decisions about arrangement, choose the right studio and guide artists during sessions. A producer might also compose beats, program instruments and mix tracks.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A person who sets up, operates and maintains technical equipment for live events or studio sessions.">technician</dfn> is responsible for setting up, operating and maintaining equipment. In a live setting, technicians handle stage setup, monitor systems and troubleshoot technical issues. In the studio, they ensure microphones, cables and hardware are functioning correctly.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15">An <dfn class="term" data-def="A professional who operates recording equipment and mixing consoles to capture and shape sound.">engineer</dfn> operates the recording equipment and mixing console. They capture sound during sessions, adjust levels, apply effects and create the final mix. Recording engineers need strong technical knowledge of signal flow, microphone placement and acoustic principles.</p>



<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n16" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: name the three technical roles and one responsibility for each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The three key technical roles are: producer (creative & technical direction), technician (equipment setup & maintenance), and engineer (recording equipment & mixing).</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Business & Management Roles</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Behind the scenes, business professionals ensure that artists’ careers are managed effectively and their music reaches the right audience.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Manager, Publicist & A&R</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n19">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A professional who handles an artist’s contracts, career opportunities and long-term development.">manager</dfn> handles an artist’s contracts, secures career opportunities and plans long-term career development. Managers negotiate deals with record labels, book tours and make strategic decisions about an artist’s direction. They act as the main point of contact between the artist and the wider industry.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n20">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A professional responsible for marketing, promotions and maintaining an artist’s public image.">publicist</dfn> is responsible for marketing, promotions and maintaining an artist’s public image. They write press releases, arrange interviews, manage social media campaigns and handle crisis communications. A strong publicist can make or break an artist’s reputation.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n21"><dfn class="term" data-def="Artists and Repertoire — the department responsible for finding new talent and developing artists within a record label.">A&R</dfn> stands for Artists and Repertoire. A&R professionals work within record labels to source new talent and oversee artist development. They attend gigs, review demos, sign artists and guide them through the recording process. A&R is the bridge between the creative and commercial sides of a record label.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n22" data-revision-tip="Close this and recall: what does A&R stand for and what are its two main functions?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>A&R stands for Artists and Repertoire. Its two main functions are sourcing new talent and artist development within a record label.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n23">All of these roles work together to take music from an idea in an artist’s head to a finished product that reaches listeners worldwide. In your exam, you may be asked to identify specific roles and explain their responsibilities.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n25">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n26">Creative roles include musical artist, composer/songwriter, lyricist, arranger, session musician, sound designer and DJ.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n27">Technical roles include producer, technician and engineer — each with distinct responsibilities.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n28">Business roles include manager (contracts & career), publicist (marketing & image) and A&R (talent sourcing & development).</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n24">In the exam, you may be asked to identify a role and describe its responsibilities. Be precise — don’t confuse a producer (creative direction) with an engineer (technical recording). For extended response questions, explain how different roles depend on each other. For example, an artist relies on a manager to secure opportunities and a publicist to build their public image.</p>
