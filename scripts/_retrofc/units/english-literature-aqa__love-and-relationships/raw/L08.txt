<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Key Quotations to Memorise</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">In the anthology comparison, you will need quotations at your fingertips. Some papers print one of the poems in the question booklet while your comparison poem is from memory — others may print neither. Either way, aim to memorise 2–3 quotations per poem for your “go-to” poems. Short, punchy quotations are easier to remember and more effective to analyse than long ones. Check your exam board’s specimen paper for the exact format.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n4"><strong>Shelley (“Love’s Philosophy”):</strong> “What is all this sweet work worth / If thou kiss not me?” — rhetorical question: nature’s unity is meaningless without love.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n5"><strong>Barrett Browning (Sonnet 29):</strong> “I do not think of thee — I am too near thee” — volta rejecting fantasy for reality.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Browning (“Porphyria’s Lover”):</strong> “That moment she was mine, mine” — possessive repetition. “And yet God has not said a word!” — delusional justification.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Nagra (“Singh Song!”):</strong> “my bride she effing at my mum / in all di colours of Punjabi” — joyful, vibrant, culturally hybrid.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Sheers (“Winter Swans”):</strong> “our hands … folded, one over the other, / like a pair of wings settling after flight” — reconciliation through nature.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Cover this and write down two quotations for each of your three go-to poems from memory.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Memorise 2–3 short quotations per poem for your go-to choices. Short is better: “mine, mine” (Browning) is more analysable than a full sentence. Practise writing quotations from memory regularly — flashcards, look-cover-write, or testing with a friend.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n15">Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>1. Writing about one poem then the other.</strong> This loses comparison marks. Both poems must be in every paragraph. <strong>2. Retelling the poem.</strong> Do not describe what happens — analyse what it means and how the poet achieves it. <strong>3. Identifying techniques without explaining their effect.</strong> “Shelley uses personification” earns nothing. “Shelley personifies the sunlight as ‘clasping’ the earth, giving nature the tenderness of a lover and making his argument for physical union feel instinctive and natural” earns real credit for methods analysis. <strong>4. Bolting on context.</strong> Do not write a separate context paragraph — embed it within your analysis.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Timing under exam conditions:</strong> whatever time your exam board gives you, aim to spend roughly the first ten per cent planning (identify theme, choose poem, plan three paragraphs), the bulk of it writing (introduction, three comparative paragraphs, conclusion), and the last few minutes checking spelling, punctuation, grammar, quotation accuracy, and comparative connectives. Check your exam board’s specimen paper for the specific time allocation.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n18"><strong>Planning approach:</strong> Draw a quick table: left column = first poem, right column = second poem. For each paragraph, write one comparison point that links both. Identify one quotation per poem per paragraph. Planning takes a few minutes but saves time and prevents the poem-by-poem trap.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n19">Revision Plan for Poetry</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Week 1:</strong> Re-read all 15 poems. For each, write a one-sentence summary and note the form, key technique, and key quotation. <strong>Week 2:</strong> Practise pairings — write comparison plans (not full essays) for 6 different question types. <strong>Week 3:</strong> Memorise quotations using flashcards. Test yourself daily. <strong>Week 4:</strong> Write 2–3 full timed essays under exam conditions. Check them against your exam board’s published assessment criteria.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n21" data-revision-tip="Close this and write a timed exam plan for: 'Compare how desire is presented in two poems.'">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The best revision strategy combines: (1) knowing all 15 poems well enough to summarise, (2) having 3–4 “go-to” poems memorised in depth, (3) practising the comparison structure under timed conditions. Do not try to memorise everything — depth on 4 poems is better than surface knowledge of 15.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n22">Remember: the comparison essay rewards <dfn class="term" data-def="An original, well-supported interpretation of a text, showing genuine engagement and thought rather than repeating standard points.">personal response</dfn>. Do not repeat generic revision-guide points — develop your own interpretations. The examiner wants to see <em>your</em> thinking about the poems, supported by evidence and method analysis. Be bold, be specific, and always link back to the question.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n24">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n25">Memorise 2–3 short quotations per poem for your go-to choices. Short quotations are easier to remember and more effective to analyse.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n26">Avoid the four common mistakes: writing poem-by-poem, retelling, identifying without analysing, and bolting on context.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n27">Plan for 5 minutes, write for 35 minutes, check for 5 minutes. Practise under timed conditions in the weeks before the exam.</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n23">In the final week before the exam, focus on <strong>speed and structure</strong>, not new content. Write one full timed essay per day for three days. Check each against the mark scheme: Did you compare in every paragraph? Did you analyse methods (AO2)? Did you embed context (AO3)? These three checks will push your mark into the top band.</p>
