--- DESCRIPTION ---
War Communism, the Kronstadt sailors’ revolt and Lenin’s tactical retreat to the NEP.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n1">The Civil War left the Soviet economy in collapse. Industrial output stood at a fifth of pre-war levels, the cities had emptied as workers fled to the countryside in search of food, and a famine in 1921-22 killed perhaps five million people. The policy that fed the Red Army &mdash; <dfn class="term" data-def="The harsh wartime economic system used by the Bolsheviks 1918-21, including grain requisitioning, banned private trade and militarised labour.">War Communism</dfn> &mdash; held the regime together but produced famine, peasant revolts and finally an uprising of Kronstadt sailors who had once been the &lsquo;pride of the revolution&rsquo;.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n2">War Communism, 1918-1921</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n3">War Communism was a set of emergency measures designed to win the Civil War, not a planned economy. The state nationalised all industry above the smallest workshops. Private trade was outlawed; the few who broke the rules were called &lsquo;bagmen&rsquo; and shot. Workers were drafted into labour battalions and paid in rations. The most damaging element was <dfn class="term" data-def="The forced seizure of grain from peasants by armed Soviet detachments during War Communism, with no fair payment.">grain requisitioning</dfn> &mdash; armed detachments seized peasants&rsquo; harvests at fixed low prices, often taking the seed grain too.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n4">Predictably, peasants stopped growing surplus grain. Why grow what would only be confiscated? Sown area shrank year on year. Combined with civil-war disruption and the loss of Ukraine&rsquo;s grain belt under Brest-Litovsk, this triggered a catastrophic famine in the Volga region in 1921-22. The Bolsheviks, who had banned all private charity, eventually accepted aid from Herbert Hoover&rsquo;s American Relief Administration to keep millions alive.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: name three core measures of War Communism, plus the famine year and the death toll.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>War Communism (1918-21) used grain requisitioning, banned private trade, and militarised labour to keep the Red Army fed. The cost was a collapsed economy and the Volga famine of 1921-22, which killed roughly five million people.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">The Tambov Rising and Rural Revolt</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">By 1920 peasant rebellions covered large parts of European Russia. The most serious was the Tambov Rising under Alexander Antonov, where some 50,000 armed peasants defied the regime for over a year. Lenin sent the Red Army with chemical weapons and concentration camps for hostage families. The countryside was being reconquered.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n8">The Kronstadt Rising, March 1921</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9">The shock came from a different direction. The sailors of the <dfn class="term" data-def="The naval base in the Gulf of Finland whose sailors led a 1921 uprising against the Bolsheviks they had helped bring to power in 1917.">Kronstadt</dfn> naval base, just outside Petrograd, had been Bolshevik heroes in 1917. Trotsky had called them the &lsquo;pride and glory of the Russian revolution&rsquo;. In March 1921 they issued fifteen demands: free elections to the Soviets, freedom of speech for socialist parties, freedom of trade unions, an end to grain requisitioning, and the release of socialist political prisoners.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n10">It was these demands &mdash; from revolutionary insiders, not Tsarist enemies &mdash; that frightened Lenin most. He branded the Kronstadters as White-led mutineers. Trotsky ordered the Red Army across the frozen ice to storm the fortress. Several thousand sailors were killed; many more were arrested and shot in the weeks afterwards. The rising was crushed, but the message had reached Moscow: the regime&rsquo;s own base was in revolt against War Communism.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11">The Tenth Party Congress met in Moscow as Kronstadt was being crushed. It made two huge decisions. First, it announced the New Economic Policy, replacing requisitioning with a tax in kind. Second, it banned factions inside the party &mdash; the Workers&rsquo; Opposition led by Alexandra Kollontai and Alexander Shlyapnikov, which had argued for more trade-union power, was outlawed. The price of economic relaxation was tighter political control. From 1921 on, dissent was channelled inside one party, and that party would not allow internal disagreement to surface.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n12">The New Economic Policy, 1921</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">The <dfn class="term" data-def="Lenin&rsquo;s 1921 economic policy that replaced grain requisitioning with a tax in kind and allowed limited private trade and small businesses.">New Economic Policy</dfn> (NEP) replaced grain requisitioning with a tax in kind: peasants paid a fixed percentage of their crop and could sell the surplus. Small private workshops and shops were legalised. Foreign concessions were granted to bring in capital. The state kept the &lsquo;commanding heights&rsquo; &mdash; heavy industry, banking, foreign trade, transport &mdash; but the rest reverted to a market.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14">The economic results were rapid. By 1926 industrial and agricultural output had recovered to roughly 1913 levels. Peasants planted more, queued less. A new class of small traders, the &lsquo;<dfn class="term" data-def="Small private traders and shopkeepers who flourished under the NEP and were widely resented by hardline Bolsheviks.">Nepmen</dfn>&rsquo;, ran shops in the cities. Hardliners called the NEP a betrayal of socialism &mdash; the worst Bolshevik insult was to be called a &lsquo;NEP-man&rsquo;.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n15">Lenin defended the NEP as a temporary retreat &mdash; &lsquo;one step backwards, two steps forward&rsquo;. The political dictatorship of the party was reinforced even as economic life relaxed. Bukharin embraced it and argued it should run for &lsquo;decades&rsquo;. Left critics, including Trotsky, warned it was empowering rural capitalists and small traders at socialism&rsquo;s expense. The unresolved tension &mdash; abandon the NEP and force industrialisation, or extend it and accept slower growth &mdash; became the central question of the post-Lenin power struggle.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n16" data-revision-tip="Close this and explain in two sentences how Kronstadt connects to the NEP.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The chain runs Kronstadt &rarr; Tenth Party Congress &rarr; NEP. The sailors&rsquo; revolt convinced Lenin that War Communism could not survive peace; the same Congress that crushed Kronstadt also abandoned grain requisitioning and banned factions, trading economic relaxation for tighter party control.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Lenin&rsquo;s Achievements by 1924</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">When Lenin died in January 1924, the Soviet state had survived a civil war, ended a famine, and recovered roughly to 1913 economic levels. The political settlement, however, was a one-party dictatorship with a militarised secret police and no permitted opposition either outside or inside the party. Trotsky as War Commissar had built the Red Army that won the war; Lenin&rsquo;s tactical flexibility had produced both War Communism and the NEP. The price had been paid in lives, principles and political pluralism.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n20">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n21">War Communism (1918-21) won the Civil War but produced famine in 1921-22 (about five million dead) and triggered widespread peasant and worker revolt.</li><li data-narration-id="n22">The Kronstadt Rising of March 1921 was crushed by Trotsky&rsquo;s Red Army but proved that even Bolshevik insiders rejected War Communism.</li><li data-narration-id="n23">The Tenth Party Congress launched the NEP &mdash; tax in kind plus legalised small trade &mdash; while banning factions; economic relaxation came with tighter political control.</li></ul>
