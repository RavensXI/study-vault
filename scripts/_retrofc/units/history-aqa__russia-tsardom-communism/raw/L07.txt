<h2 data-narration-id="n1">A Sealed Train and a Slogan</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">When the Tsar fell in March 1917, Lenin was in Z&uuml;rich. Cut off from Russia by the war, he reached the Finland Station in Petrograd on 3 April thanks to the German government, who paid for a sealed railway carriage to take him through Germany. They wanted Russia knocked out of the war; they expected Lenin to oblige. He delivered. The next morning he stunned even his own Bolshevik colleagues by reading out the <dfn class="term" data-def="Lenin&rsquo;s ten-point programme of April 1917 demanding no support for the Provisional Government, an end to the war, all power to the soviets and immediate land redistribution.">April Theses</dfn>: no cooperation with the Provisional Government; immediate end to the war; all power to the soviets; nationalisation of land; the slogan &lsquo;Peace, Land, Bread&rsquo;.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and write down what each word of the slogan &lsquo;Peace, Land, Bread&rsquo; meant in 1917, with one group it appealed to.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Lenin&rsquo;s slogan &lsquo;Peace, Land, Bread&rsquo; was the political masterstroke of 1917. &lsquo;Peace&rsquo; appealed to soldiers and to war-weary cities; &lsquo;Land&rsquo; appealed to peasants who wanted the noble estates broken up; &lsquo;Bread&rsquo; appealed to workers facing inflation and food queues. No other party offered all three.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Trotsky Joins</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Lenin&rsquo;s most important new ally was Leon Trotsky, who returned to Petrograd from New York in May. Trotsky had spent fifteen years as an independent Marxist outside both factions of the <dfn class="term" data-def="&lsquo;Minority&rsquo; faction of the Russian Social Democrats from 1903; favoured a mass party along Western European lines and split decisively from the Bolsheviks during 1917.">Mensheviks</dfn> and Bolsheviks; he had been close to the former at the 1903 split. Now he formally joined the Bolsheviks. He was arguably their best public speaker, a battle-hardened veteran of the 1905 St Petersburg Soviet, and a brilliant organiser. With Lenin in hiding for parts of the year and other senior Bolsheviks (Stalin, Kamenev, Zinoviev) more cautious, Trotsky&rsquo;s combination of theory, oratory and military instinct would matter decisively.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">A Disciplined Party</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">The Bolsheviks were not large in 1917. In April they had perhaps 24,000 members; by October maybe 350,000. What made them effective was not size but discipline. Lenin&rsquo;s 1903 demand for a small party of professional revolutionaries had produced a body where the central committee&rsquo;s decisions were binding on every cell. Their newspaper <em>Pravda</em> repeated the same slogans week after week. Their agitators were trained to give the same answers to the same questions in any factory or barracks. While other socialist groups debated, the Bolsheviks acted.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8">After the Kornilov Affair in August 1917 (covered in the previous lesson), the Bolsheviks emerged with arms, prestige, and growing soviet majorities. By 25 September they controlled the Petrograd Soviet under Trotsky as chairman. By early October Lenin, still hiding in Finland, was writing letters to the central committee demanding immediate insurrection.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n9">On 10 October (OS) the Bolshevik central committee met in secret in Petrograd. Lenin had returned in disguise. He demanded an immediate seizure of power before the Constituent Assembly elections, which he expected the Bolsheviks to lose. Two senior Bolsheviks &mdash; Kamenev and Zinoviev &mdash; opposed insurrection and were so worried they leaked the plan to the press. Most of the committee voted with Lenin. Practical preparation was handed to Trotsky&rsquo;s newly created Military Revolutionary Committee of the Petrograd Soviet, which would coordinate the seizure under the cover of defending the Soviet from a supposed government coup.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">The October Insurrection</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">The seizure of power began on the night of 24&ndash;25 October (6&ndash;7 November, NS). Sailors from the Kronstadt naval base, Bolshevik Red Guards from the factories and pro-Bolshevik units of the Petrograd garrison occupied the central railway stations, telegraph office, post office, state bank and bridges. Resistance was minimal; many Provisional Government supporters had already fled or simply refused to fight. Kerensky escaped Petrograd that morning to look for loyal troops, leaving his cabinet to be arrested in the <dfn class="term" data-def="The Tsar&rsquo;s former St Petersburg residence, headquarters of the Provisional Government in 1917 and stormed by Bolshevik forces on the night of 25 October (Old Style).">Winter Palace</dfn>.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12">The Winter Palace fell in the early hours of 26 October. The cruiser <em>Aurora</em> fired blank shells from the Neva to signal the assault. Inside, the cabinet was guarded by a few hundred officer cadets and the Women&rsquo;s Battalion of Death; resistance was brief and casualties light. Soviet propaganda would later film a stylised &lsquo;storming&rsquo;, but in reality the takeover was closer to a tightly organised coup than a popular uprising.</p>

    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n13">As the Winter Palace fell, the Second All-Russian Congress of Soviets opened in the Smolny Institute. The Bolsheviks held a narrow majority. Mensheviks and right-wing Socialist Revolutionaries walked out in protest at the seizure of power, leaving the floor to Lenin&rsquo;s party and the Left Socialist Revolutionaries. Trotsky famously dismissed the departing Mensheviks: &lsquo;You are pitiful isolated individuals; you are bankrupts; your role is played out. Go where you belong from now on &mdash; into the dustbin of history!&rsquo; The Congress passed the Decree on Peace (calling for an immediate end to the war) and the Decree on Land (abolishing private ownership). A new government &mdash; the Council of People&rsquo;s Commissars (Sovnarkom) &mdash; was named, with Lenin as chairman.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Why the Bolsheviks Won in 1917</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15">The Bolshevik victory was not predetermined. It came from the combination of two long-running weaknesses in their opponents and three Bolshevik strengths. Their opponents&rsquo; weaknesses: the Provisional Government refused to end the war or distribute land, and Dual Power meant it could not coerce its own population. Their strengths: a clear, popular slogan that no rival could match; a tightly disciplined party that could act when others debated; and Trotsky&rsquo;s organisational skill in turning the Petrograd Soviet&rsquo;s Military Revolutionary Committee into the instrument of the seizure.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n16">It is fashionable to argue that the Bolsheviks were lucky &mdash; that in any of a dozen moments between April and October the situation could have gone the other way. That is true. It is also true that they were the only party who had organised themselves to take advantage of luck when it came.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Close this and list the four steps from the 10 October vote to the fall of the Winter Palace, with one date or detail each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The October Revolution was a tightly planned coup, not a mass uprising. Trotsky&rsquo;s Military Revolutionary Committee coordinated armed workers, sailors and pro-Bolshevik soldiers to seize key buildings on the night of 24&ndash;25 October. The Winter Palace fell in the early hours of 26 October with light casualties. The Second Congress of Soviets ratified the new government the next morning.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n19">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n20">Lenin&rsquo;s April Theses and the slogan &lsquo;Peace, Land, Bread&rsquo; gave the Bolsheviks a clear, popular programme that no rival party could match.</li><li data-narration-id="n21">Bolshevik organisational discipline, combined with Trotsky&rsquo;s recruitment in May 1917 and the political opening created by the Kornilov Affair in August, transformed the party from a weak minority into the strongest force in the Petrograd Soviet by September.</li><li data-narration-id="n22">The October seizure of power on 24&ndash;26 October 1917 was a tightly planned coup, not a mass uprising; the Second Congress of Soviets ratified the Bolshevik government the next morning under Lenin as chairman of Sovnarkom.</li></ul>
