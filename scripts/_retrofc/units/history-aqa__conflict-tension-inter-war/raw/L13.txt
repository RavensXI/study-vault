--- DESCRIPTION ---
How the March 1939 occupation of Czechoslovakia and the Polish guarantee changed British policy.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Hinge Moment</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Munich had transferred the Sudetenland in October 1938. Six months later, on 15 March 1939, German troops marched into Prague and seized what was left of Czechoslovakia. Bohemia and Moravia became a German &lsquo;<dfn class="term" data-def="A Nazi-controlled territory directly administered by Germany; in March 1939 Bohemia and Moravia became the &lsquo;Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia&rsquo; under Reich control.">protectorate</dfn>&rsquo; under Reich administration; Slovakia became a German puppet state under the priest-politician Jozef Tiso. Hitler personally toured Prague Castle that night. The action was unmistakably different from what had gone before. The seized populations were Czech, not German. The principle of self-determination &mdash; Hitler&rsquo;s constant pretext since 1933 &mdash; could not be made to fit. Within two weeks British policy had changed.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: list the four key dates between 15 March and 31 March 1939, with what happened on each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The end of appeasement came in two weeks. On 15 March 1939 German troops entered Prague. On 17 March Chamberlain&rsquo;s Birmingham speech publicly turned. On 31 March Britain announced the Polish guarantee. France joined within days, and on 7 April Mussolini invaded Albania.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">15 March 1939: Prague</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">The Czech President <dfn class="term" data-def="Czechoslovak President from late 1938, summoned to Berlin in March 1939 and bullied into accepting German occupation; reportedly suffered a heart attack during the meeting.">Emil H&aacute;cha</dfn> &mdash; who had replaced Bene&scaron; after Munich &mdash; was summoned to Berlin overnight on 14-15 March. Hitler kept him waiting until 1 a.m. and then presented him with the choice: sign acceptance of a German &lsquo;protectorate&rsquo; or watch Prague be bombed at dawn. H&aacute;cha reportedly suffered a heart attack during the meeting and was kept conscious by injections from Hitler&rsquo;s personal physician. He signed. German troops entered Prague at 6 a.m. on 15 March. Slovakia, encouraged by Berlin to declare independence the day before, became a satellite state. The whole of Czechoslovakia was gone.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">The British Public Turn</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">In Britain, the public reaction was sharper than anything since 1935. Munich had been welcomed because it had appealed to a principle &mdash; self-determination &mdash; and avoided war. Prague could not be defended on either ground. The Times, which had backed Munich, turned. Conservative MPs who had cheered Chamberlain in October 1938 now openly questioned the policy. Lord Halifax, who as Foreign Secretary had accepted Anschluss with regret in March 1938, told Chamberlain bluntly that the policy had run out. Chamberlain&rsquo;s personal credibility was on the line. On 17 March, in a speech at Birmingham marking his seventieth birthday, he made the public turn. Britain, he said, would resist any further German moves toward European domination.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n8">The Birmingham speech of 17 March 1939 is sometimes called the moment Chamberlain &lsquo;abandoned appeasement&rsquo;. The reality is more nuanced. Chamberlain still hoped to avoid war, but he now publicly accepted that Hitler could not be trusted to honour an agreement &mdash; the central premise of Munich. The shift from negotiation to <dfn class="term" data-def="A foreign-policy doctrine that aims to discourage an aggressor by making clear in advance that an attack will be met with overwhelming force.">deterrence</dfn> followed within two weeks. The policy that had defined British foreign policy since 1937 was over in everything but name.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">31 March 1939: The Polish Guarantee</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">Hitler had begun openly pressing Poland for the return of <dfn class="term" data-def="The mainly German-speaking Baltic city established as a Free City under League administration in 1919; demanded by Hitler in 1939.">Danzig</dfn> &mdash; the Free City under League administration &mdash; and for an extraterritorial corridor across Polish territory linking Germany to East Prussia. Poland refused. On 31 March 1939 Chamberlain told the House of Commons that Britain would lend Poland &lsquo;all support in their power&rsquo; in the event of any threat to Polish independence. France joined the <dfn class="term" data-def="A formal promise by one country to defend another in the event of attack; Britain and France gave such a promise to Poland on 31 March 1939.">guarantee</dfn> within days. Greece and Romania received similar guarantees in April. The strategic shift was complete: Britain and France had moved from negotiation to deterrence.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Was the Polish Guarantee Credible?</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11">Historians have long argued over whether the guarantee was militarily credible. Britain and France had no direct way of defending Poland; their best hope of relieving German pressure was a Western Front offensive that neither was militarily ready to launch. The guarantee made political sense as a deterrent &mdash; signalling to Hitler that any Polish move would mean war &mdash; but it could not stop a German invasion in practice. The guarantee&rsquo;s real purpose was therefore to deter, not to defend. When Hitler called the bluff in September 1939, Britain and France honoured their promise but could not save Poland.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n12">Conscription and Mussolini in Albania</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">In April 1939 Britain introduced peacetime conscription &mdash; the first time outside a major war &mdash; signalling to Berlin that the rearmament effort had moved into a new phase, with men as well as money committed to the coming confrontation. On 7 April 1939 Mussolini opportunistically invaded Albania, taking advantage of European attention focused on Poland. The Italian move did not provoke a major Allied response &mdash; Britain and France were focused on the German threat &mdash; but it confirmed that the Axis powers were now acting in coordination, and prompted further British guarantees to Greece and Romania later in the month to deter any Italian move toward the Balkans.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: explain in two sentences why March 1939 mattered more for British policy than September 1938.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>March 1939 was the hinge moment. Munich had been defensible on grounds of self-determination; Prague could not be. The seizure of non-German Czech lands ended the policy of appeasement and produced the Polish guarantee, peacetime conscription and a public shift to deterrence.</p>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n16">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n17">On 15 March 1939 German troops occupied Prague; Bohemia and Moravia became a Reich protectorate, Slovakia a German puppet, and the principle of self-determination could no longer cover Hitler&rsquo;s actions.</li><li data-narration-id="n18">British public opinion turned within days; Chamberlain&rsquo;s Birmingham speech of 17 March publicly abandoned the trust on which Munich had rested.</li><li data-narration-id="n19">On 31 March Britain guaranteed Poland; France followed; peacetime conscription was introduced in April; Mussolini invaded Albania the same month. Appeasement was over.</li></ul>
