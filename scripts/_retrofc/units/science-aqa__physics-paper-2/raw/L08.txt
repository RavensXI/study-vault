--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Permanent and Induced Magnets</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A material that produces its own magnetic field and always has a north and south pole.">permanent magnet</dfn> produces its own magnetic field. It always has a north pole and a south pole. Like poles repel; unlike poles attract. Common permanent magnets are made from iron, steel, nickel, or cobalt.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">An <dfn class="term" data-def="A material that becomes magnetic only when placed in a magnetic field; it loses its magnetism when removed.">induced magnet</dfn> is a material that becomes magnetic only when placed inside a magnetic field. When removed from the field, it quickly loses its magnetism. An induced magnet is always attracted to the permanent magnet, never repelled.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Name the direction magnetic field lines travel outside a magnet and what closer field lines indicate."><div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>A <dfn class="term" data-def="The region around a magnet where other magnetic materials experience a force.">magnetic field</dfn> is the region around a magnet where a force acts on another magnet or magnetic material. Field lines go from north to south outside the magnet. The closer together the field lines, the stronger the field.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Electromagnetism</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">When a current flows through a wire, it produces a <dfn class="term" data-def="A magnetic field created by an electric current flowing through a conductor.">magnetic field</dfn> around the wire. The field lines form concentric circles around the wire. The direction of the field can be found using the right-hand rule: point your thumb in the direction of current, and your fingers curl in the direction of the field.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n8">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A coil of wire that produces a strong magnetic field when current flows through it; the field pattern is similar to a bar magnet.">solenoid</dfn> is a coil of wire. When current flows through it, the magnetic field inside is strong and uniform (like a bar magnet). The strength of the field can be increased by: increasing the current, increasing the number of turns, or placing an iron core inside the solenoid.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n9" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. List four practical applications of electromagnets and explain what makes them switchable on and off."><div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>An <dfn class="term" data-def="A solenoid with a soft iron core inside, creating a magnet that can be switched on and off.">electromagnet</dfn> is a solenoid with a soft iron core. It can be switched on and off by controlling the current. This makes electromagnets useful in scrapyard cranes, circuit breakers, electric bells, and MRI scanners.</p>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n10">The Motor Effect</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">When a current-carrying wire is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences a force. This is called the <dfn class="term" data-def="The force experienced by a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field.">motor effect</dfn>. The force is at its maximum when the wire is perpendicular to the magnetic field and zero when the wire is parallel to the field.</p>

<div class="higher-only">
<p data-narration-id="n12">The size of the force is given by:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n13">$$F = BIl$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Where \(F\) is force in newtons, \(B\) is <dfn class="term" data-def="A measure of the strength of a magnetic field, measured in tesla (T).">magnetic flux density</dfn> in tesla (T), \(I\) is current in amps, and \(l\) is the length of wire in the field in metres.</p>

<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n15"><dfn class="term" data-def="A rule for predicting the direction of force on a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field: thumb = force, first finger = field, second finger = current.">Fleming’s left-hand rule</dfn> predicts the direction of the force. Hold your left hand with the thumb, first finger, and second finger all at right angles. The first finger points in the direction of the magnetic field (north to south), the second finger points in the direction of conventional current, and the thumb shows the direction of the force (motion).</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Electric Motors</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">A simple d.c. motor uses the motor effect to produce rotation. A coil of wire is placed between the poles of a magnet. When current flows, one side of the coil is pushed up and the other is pushed down (by the motor effect), creating a turning force.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>The Role of the Split-Ring Commutator</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n18">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A device in a d.c. motor that reverses the direction of current every half turn, ensuring continuous rotation.">split-ring commutator</dfn> reverses the direction of current in the coil every half turn. Without it, the coil would rotate 180° and then stop. The commutator ensures the forces always act in the right direction to keep the coil spinning continuously.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n19">The speed of a motor can be increased by: increasing the current, using a stronger magnet, or adding more turns to the coil.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n21">Key Takeaways</h3><ul><li data-narration-id="n22">Permanent magnets always have north and south poles; induced magnets only work inside a magnetic field</li><li data-narration-id="n23">A solenoid with an iron core is an electromagnet; field strength increased by more current, more turns, or an iron core</li><li data-narration-id="n24">Motor effect: \(F = BIl\); direction found by Fleming’s left-hand rule; split-ring commutator enables continuous rotation</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n20">For the motor effect, always state the equation \(F = BIl\) and use Fleming’s left-hand rule to find the direction. In questions about electromagnets, list the three ways to increase the field strength (more current, more turns, iron core). Remember that the split-ring commutator reverses the current direction every half turn to keep the motor spinning.</p>
