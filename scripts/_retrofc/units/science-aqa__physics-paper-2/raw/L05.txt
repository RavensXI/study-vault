<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Are Stopping Distances?</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">When a vehicle needs to stop, it doesn't happen instantly. The <dfn class="term" data-def="The total distance needed to bring a moving vehicle to a complete stop.">stopping distance</dfn> is made up of two parts: thinking distance and braking distance.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write the equation for stopping distance using the two component distances.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n4">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The distance travelled between seeing a hazard and applying the brakes.">thinking distance</dfn> is how far the car travels while the driver reacts to a hazard. During this time, the vehicle continues at constant speed.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n5">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The distance travelled from when the brakes are applied until the vehicle stops.">braking distance</dfn> is how far the car travels while the brakes are being applied and the vehicle is decelerating.</p>

  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Factors Affecting Thinking Distance</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n6">Several factors can increase thinking distance:</p>
    <ul>
      <li data-narration-id="n7">Tiredness or fatigue reduces reaction speed</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n8">Drugs and alcohol slow down the nervous system</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n9">Distractions like mobile phones delay reactions</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n10">Higher speeds mean more distance covered during reaction time</li>
    </ul>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n11">Braking Forces and Energy</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n12">When brakes are applied, friction between the brake pads and wheels creates a force that opposes motion. This <dfn class="term" data-def="The force that opposes motion when brakes are applied.">braking force</dfn> does work against the vehicle's kinetic energy.</p>



<p data-narration-id="n13">The work done by braking forces transfers the vehicle's kinetic energy into heat energy at the brakes. We can calculate this using:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n14">$$\text{Work done by brakes} = \text{Braking force} \times \text{Braking distance}$$</p>

<p data-narration-id="n15">This work done equals the kinetic energy that was lost: \(\frac{1}{2}mv^2\)</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n16" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Calculate the braking distance multiplier when speed doubles from the proportional relationship.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Braking distance is proportional to the square of speed. Double the speed means four times the braking distance.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Factors Affecting Braking Distance</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n17">Braking distance increases when:</p>
    <ul>
      <li data-narration-id="n18">Vehicle speed is higher — kinetic energy increases with speed squared</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n19">Vehicle mass is greater — more kinetic energy to remove</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n20">Road conditions are poor — wet or icy roads reduce friction</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n21">Brake or tyre condition is poor — worn components reduce braking force</li>
    </ul>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n22">Understanding Momentum</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n23">Every moving object has <dfn class="term" data-def="The product of an object's mass and velocity.">momentum</dfn>. Momentum is calculated using:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n24">$$\text{momentum} = \text{mass} \times \text{velocity}$$</p>

<p data-narration-id="n25">$$p = m \times v$$</p>

<p data-narration-id="n26">Momentum is measured in kg⋅m/s and is a vector quantity — it has both size and direction.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Conservation of Momentum</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n27">In any collision or explosion, the total momentum before equals the total momentum after. This is the <dfn class="term" data-def="In a closed system, total momentum remains constant unless acted upon by external forces.">conservation of momentum</dfn>.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n28">For two objects colliding:</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n29">$$m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$$</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n30">Where \(u\) represents initial velocities and \(v\) represents final velocities.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n31">Vehicle Safety Features</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n32">Modern vehicles use momentum principles to improve safety during collisions. The key is to increase the time over which momentum changes occur.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n33">Using \(F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}\), we can see that increasing the time (\(\Delta t\)) for a momentum change reduces the force experienced.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n34" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Explain how increasing collision time affects the forces on passengers in safety features.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Safety features work by increasing collision time, which reduces the forces acting on passengers.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n35">Examples of safety features include:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n36">Crumple zones — designed to deform and extend collision time</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n37">Airbags — inflate to cushion impact and increase stopping time</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n38">Seat belts — stretch slightly to reduce peak forces on the body</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n39">ABS brakes — prevent wheel locking to maintain steering control</li>
</ul>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n41">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n42">Stopping distance combines thinking distance and braking distance, both affected by different factors</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n43">Braking transfers kinetic energy to heat, with braking distance proportional to speed squared</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n44">Momentum equals mass times velocity and is conserved in collisions</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n45">Vehicle safety features reduce collision forces by increasing the time over which momentum changes</li>
</ul>
