--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Electromagnetic Spectrum</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The continuous range of electromagnetic waves ordered by wavelength or frequency.">electromagnetic (EM) spectrum</dfn> is a continuous range of waves, all of which travel at the speed of light in a vacuum (\(3 \times 10^8\) m/s). All EM waves are transverse and can travel through a vacuum — they do not need a medium.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. List the 7 EM waves in order of decreasing wavelength and recite the mnemonic about Running Men In Vests."><div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The seven types of EM wave in order of decreasing wavelength (increasing frequency) are: radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays. Use the mnemonic: <strong>R</strong>unning <strong>M</strong>en <strong>I</strong>n <strong>V</strong>ests <strong>U</strong>sually <strong>X</strong>-ray <strong>G</strong>iraffes.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n4">All EM waves share the same basic properties: they transfer energy, travel at \(3 \times 10^8\) m/s in a vacuum, and obey the wave equation \(v = f\lambda\). Since speed is constant, waves with higher frequency have shorter wavelength.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Uses and Dangers</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Each type of EM wave has specific uses that depend on its properties.</p>

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n7"><dfn class="term" data-def="EM waves with the longest wavelength, used for TV, radio broadcasts and communication.">Radio waves</dfn> have the longest wavelengths and are used for television, radio broadcasts, and communication. Different wavelengths are used for different purposes — long wave can diffract around hills, while short wave can be reflected off the ionosphere for long-distance communication.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8"><dfn class="term" data-def="EM waves used for satellite communication, mobile phones, and heating food.">Microwaves</dfn> are used in satellite communication, mobile phone signals, and microwave ovens. In ovens, microwaves cause water molecules to vibrate, heating the food.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Infrared, Visible Light and Ultraviolet</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n9"><dfn class="term" data-def="EM radiation felt as heat, used in remote controls, thermal imaging, and optical fibre communication.">Infrared</dfn> radiation is emitted by all warm objects. It is used in remote controls, thermal imaging cameras, cooking, and optical fibre communication. Too much infrared can cause skin burns.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n10"><dfn class="term" data-def="The narrow range of EM radiation that the human eye can detect, from red to violet.">Visible light</dfn> is the only part of the EM spectrum we can see. It is used in photography, fibre optic communication, and vision. It ranges from red (longest wavelength) to violet (shortest).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><dfn class="term" data-def="EM radiation beyond violet light, used in fluorescent lamps and security marking; can cause skin damage.">Ultraviolet</dfn> (UV) causes skin to tan and is used in fluorescent lamps, security marking, and detecting forged banknotes. Overexposure causes sunburn and increases the risk of skin cancer.</p>
</div></div>
</div>



<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>X-rays and Gamma Rays</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n12"><dfn class="term" data-def="High-energy EM waves used in medical imaging to see inside the body; can damage cells with overexposure.">X-rays</dfn> can pass through soft tissue but are absorbed by bone and metal. They are used in medical imaging and airport security scanners. Overexposure can cause cell damage and cancer.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n13"><dfn class="term" data-def="The highest-frequency EM waves, emitted by radioactive nuclei, used in cancer treatment and sterilisation.">Gamma rays</dfn> have the highest frequency and shortest wavelength. They are emitted by radioactive nuclei and used in cancer treatment (radiotherapy), sterilising medical equipment, and as tracers in medical imaging. They are the most penetrating and can cause serious cell damage.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Explain why UV causes sunburn while X-rays and gamma rays damage DNA, and which end of the spectrum is most dangerous."><div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The danger of EM radiation generally increases with frequency. Radio waves and microwaves pose low risk. UV causes sunburn and skin cancer. X-rays and gamma rays can damage cells and DNA, potentially causing cancer and mutations.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n15">Emission and Absorption of Infrared</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n16">All objects emit and absorb infrared radiation. The hotter an object, the more infrared it emits. Dark, matt surfaces are the best emitters and absorbers of infrared. Light, shiny surfaces are the worst emitters and absorbers (they are good reflectors instead).</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n17">Fill a Leslie cube (a metal cube with four different surfaces: shiny black, matt black, shiny white, matt white) with hot water. Use an infrared detector at the same distance from each face. The matt black surface emits the most infrared; the shiny white surface emits the least.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

Link dangers to frequency — higher frequency waves carry more energy and cause more cell damage. If a question asks you to explain why a particular wave type is used for a specific purpose, relate it to the wave’s properties (penetrating power, wavelength, energy).</p>
