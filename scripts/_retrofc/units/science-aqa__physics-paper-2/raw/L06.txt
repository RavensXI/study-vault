--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Are Waves?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="Oscillations that transfer energy from one place to another without transferring matter.">Waves</dfn> transfer energy from one place to another without transferring matter. The particles in the medium vibrate back and forth, but they do not travel with the wave.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">There are two types of wave. In a <dfn class="term" data-def="A wave where the oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer, such as light and water waves.">transverse wave</dfn>, the oscillations are perpendicular (at right angles) to the direction of energy transfer. Examples include light, water waves, and all electromagnetic waves.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n4">In a <dfn class="term" data-def="A wave where the oscillations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer, such as sound waves.">longitudinal wave</dfn>, the oscillations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer. Sound is the most common example. Longitudinal waves have areas of <dfn class="term" data-def="A region in a longitudinal wave where particles are pushed close together.">compression</dfn> (particles squashed together) and <dfn class="term" data-def="A region in a longitudinal wave where particles are spread apart.">rarefaction</dfn> (particles spread apart).</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down the four wave properties and the wave equation that links three of them using v, f, and λ."><div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>All waves can be described using four properties: amplitude, wavelength, frequency, and wave speed. The wave equation links three of these: \(v = f \lambda\).</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Wave Properties</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7"><dfn class="term" data-def="The maximum displacement of a point on a wave from its rest position, measured in metres.">Amplitude</dfn> is the maximum displacement from the rest position. Larger amplitude means more energy. <dfn class="term" data-def="The distance from one point on a wave to the equivalent point on the next wave, measured in metres.">Wavelength</dfn> (\(\lambda\)) is the distance from one point on a wave to the same point on the next wave (e.g. crest to crest).</p>

<p data-narration-id="n8"><dfn class="term" data-def="The number of waves passing a point per second, measured in hertz (Hz).">Frequency</dfn> is the number of complete waves passing a point per second, measured in hertz (Hz). The <dfn class="term" data-def="The time taken for one complete wave to pass a point, measured in seconds.">period</dfn> is the time for one complete wave and is related to frequency by: \(T = \frac{1}{f}\).</p>

<p data-narration-id="n9">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The speed at which a wave travels through a medium, calculated using v = fλ.">wave speed</dfn> equation is:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n10">$$v = f \lambda$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Where \(v\) is wave speed in m/s, \(f\) is frequency in Hz, and \(\lambda\) is wavelength in metres.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Reflection, Refraction and Absorption</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15">When waves meet a boundary between different materials, they can be reflected, refracted, or absorbed. Often a combination of all three occurs.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n16"><dfn class="term" data-def="When a wave bounces off a surface; the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.">Reflection</dfn> occurs when a wave bounces off a surface. The law of reflection states that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection (both measured from the normal).</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Explain what happens to bending direction when waves slow down vs speed up, and which property stays constant during refraction."><div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p><dfn class="term" data-def="The change in direction of a wave when it passes from one medium to another due to a change in speed.">Refraction</dfn> occurs when a wave changes speed as it passes from one medium to another. If the wave slows down, it bends towards the normal. If it speeds up, it bends away from the normal. The frequency stays the same; only the speed and wavelength change.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n20">Diffraction</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n21"><dfn class="term" data-def="The spreading of waves when they pass through a gap or around an obstacle.">Diffraction</dfn> is the spreading out of waves when they pass through a gap or around an obstacle. The effect is greatest when the gap width is about the same as the wavelength. If the gap is much larger than the wavelength, very little diffraction occurs.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Diffraction Examples</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n22">Sound waves have long wavelengths (up to several metres), so they diffract easily around doors and buildings — you can hear someone around a corner. Light has very short wavelengths (around 500 nm), so it only diffracts noticeably through extremely narrow slits.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n24">Key Takeaways</h3><ul><li data-narration-id="n25">Transverse waves oscillate perpendicular to energy transfer; longitudinal waves oscillate parallel (compressions and rarefactions)</li><li data-narration-id="n26">Wave equation: \(v = f\lambda\); period \(T = 1/f\); amplitude relates to energy</li><li data-narration-id="n27">Waves can be reflected (angle i = angle r), refracted (speed change causes direction change), and diffracted (spreading through gaps)</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n23">When describing refraction, always state that the wave changes speed and therefore changes direction. Remember that frequency stays the same during refraction — only speed and wavelength change. For diffraction questions, relate the gap width to the wavelength: maximum diffraction occurs when gap ≈ wavelength.</p>
