Christian beliefs about how and why God created the world, and what happens after death.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Christian Beliefs About Creation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Christians believe God created the entire universe. The two main biblical accounts of creation are found in <strong>Genesis 1</strong> and <strong>John 1:1–3</strong>. Both are important For the exam , and students must understand different Christian interpretations of these texts.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Genesis 1:1–3 — The Creation Account</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">The book of Genesis describes God creating the world in six days and resting on the seventh. “In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. Now the earth was formless and empty, darkness was over the surface of the deep, and the <dfn class="term" data-def="The third person of the Trinity; God's active presence in the world.">Spirit of God</dfn> was hovering over the waters. And God said, ‘Let there be light,’ and there was light.” (Genesis 1:1–3)</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Cover this and explain from memory the roles of the Word and the Spirit in Genesis 1.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Genesis 1 shows the role of both the <strong>Word</strong> and the <strong>Spirit</strong> in creation. God’s Spirit hovered over the waters (the Holy Spirit was present), and God <strong>spoke</strong> creation into existence (“God said”) — this is the Word (Logos) at work. Christians see this as evidence that the Trinity was active at creation.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">John 1:1–3 — The Word (Logos)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">The Gospel of John opens with a different perspective on creation: “In the beginning was the <dfn class="term" data-def="Greek for 'Word'; used in John's Gospel to refer to Jesus Christ, who existed with God from the beginning.">Word</dfn>, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was with God in the beginning. Through him all things were made; without him nothing was made that has been made.” (John 1:1–3)</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8">Christians understand “the Word” as referring to Jesus Christ (God the Son). This passage teaches that Jesus was not just a human teacher — he existed with God from the very beginning and played an active role in creating everything. Nothing that exists was made without him.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>The Role of Word and Spirit in Creation</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n9">Taken together, Genesis 1 and John 1 show that all three persons of the Trinity were involved in creation. <strong>God the Father</strong> initiated creation. <strong>God the Son</strong> (the Word) was the agent through whom everything was made. <strong>God the Holy Spirit</strong> was present, hovering over the waters. This is significant because it shows the Trinity working together from the very beginning of time.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Different Interpretations of Creation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Christians disagree about how to interpret the creation accounts. The AQA exam expects you to understand these different views:</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Literalist vs Liberal Interpretations</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Literalist (fundamentalist) view:</strong> Some Christians believe the Genesis account is literally true — God created the world in six 24-hour days exactly as described. They reject the theory of evolution and believe the Earth is only a few thousand years old. They see the Bible as the direct word of God, without error.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Liberal (non-literal) view:</strong> Many Christians interpret the creation story as a myth or metaphor — it communicates important truths (that God is the creator, that creation is good, that humans are special) without being a scientific account. They accept the theory of evolution and the Big Bang as the mechanism God used. The “six days” may represent long periods of time.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Catholic view:</strong> The Catholic Church teaches that faith and science are not in conflict. Pope Francis has said that the Big Bang and evolution are real, but God is behind them. The creation stories reveal <strong>why</strong> God created (out of love), not <strong>how</strong> the physical process worked.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="Without looking, explain the literal and non-literal interpretations of the Genesis creation account.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>All Christians agree that God is the ultimate creator of everything. They disagree about <strong>how</strong> to interpret the Genesis account — literally (six 24-hour days) or non-literally (metaphorical, compatible with science). The exam often tests these “common and divergent views.”</p>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Christian Beliefs About the Afterlife</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">Christians believe that physical death is not the end. What happens after death is a central part of Christian faith, and different Christians hold different views about the details.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Resurrection and Life After Death</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19"><dfn class="term" data-def="Rising from the dead; Christians believe Jesus was resurrected and that believers will also be raised to new life.">Resurrection</dfn> is the belief that the dead will be raised to new life. Christians believe this because Jesus himself was resurrected three days after his crucifixion. St Paul wrote: “If Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile” (1 Corinthians 15:17) — showing that resurrection is absolutely central to Christianity.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Some Christians believe in <strong>bodily resurrection</strong> — that the physical body will be raised and transformed. Others believe in <strong>spiritual resurrection</strong> — that the soul lives on in a new spiritual form. Catholics believe the body and soul are reunited at the resurrection on the last day.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n21">Judgement</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22">Christians believe God will <dfn class="term" data-def="God's assessment of a person's life to determine their eternal destiny in heaven or hell.">judge</dfn> every person after death. Some Christians distinguish between two types of judgement:</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Types of Judgement</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Particular judgement:</strong> This happens immediately after a person dies. Their soul is judged by God and sent to heaven, hell or (in Catholic belief) purgatory.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Final (Last) judgement:</strong> At the end of time, Jesus will return and judge all people — both the living and the dead. This is described in Matthew 25:31–46, the parable of the sheep and the goats, where those who helped others are welcomed into God’s kingdom and those who did not are sent away.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n25">Heaven and Hell</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n26"><dfn class="term" data-def="The state of eternal happiness in the presence of God, the ultimate reward for the faithful.">Heaven</dfn> is understood as a state of eternal happiness and peace in the presence of God. It is the ultimate reward for those who have lived according to God’s will. Jesus told the criminal crucified beside him: “Today you will be with me in paradise” (Luke 23:43).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n27"><dfn class="term" data-def="The state of eternal separation from God, understood as punishment for those who reject God.">Hell</dfn> is understood as eternal separation from God. Some Christians believe hell is a literal place of fire and torment. Others interpret it as the absence of God’s love and presence — a spiritual suffering rather than a physical one. Some liberal Christians question whether a loving God would condemn anyone to eternal suffering.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n28" data-revision-tip="From memory, explain two ways belief in the afterlife influences how Christians live their daily lives.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Beliefs about the afterlife influence how Christians live. Because they believe in judgement, they try to live morally, follow Jesus’s teachings, help others and avoid sin. The hope of heaven brings comfort in times of suffering, and the possibility of hell motivates them to stay on the right path.</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n29">Catholics also believe in <strong>purgatory</strong> — a state of purification after death where souls are cleansed of their sins before entering heaven. This is not a Protestant belief. Catholics pray for the dead, asking God to release souls from purgatory sooner.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n31">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n32">Genesis 1 and John 1:1–3 show all three persons of the Trinity were involved in creation — the Father initiated, the Word (Son) was the agent, and the Spirit was present.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n33">Christians disagree about whether Genesis should be taken literally (six days) or non-literally (metaphor compatible with science), but all agree God is the ultimate creator.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n34">Christians believe in resurrection, judgement, heaven and hell — these beliefs shape how they live by motivating moral behaviour and offering hope beyond death.</li>
</ul>
