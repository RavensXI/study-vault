--- DESCRIPTION ---
Why Christians believe Jesus is the Son of God, and the significance of his death and rising.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Incarnation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The belief that God became human in the person of Jesus Christ; literally 'made flesh'.">incarnation</dfn> is the Christian belief that God became human in the person of Jesus Christ. The word literally means “made flesh.” Christians believe that Jesus was not just a good teacher or prophet — he was God the Son taking on a human body to live among people on Earth.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">John 1:14 states: “The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us.” This connects directly to John 1:1, which says the Word was God. So the Word (God the Son) who created all things became a human being — Jesus of Nazareth. This is one of the most important beliefs in Christianity.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this and define incarnation from memory. Write the key quote from John 1:14.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Christians believe Jesus is both <strong>fully God</strong> and <strong>fully human</strong>. He was not half-God and half-human, nor God pretending to be human. He experienced everything humans experience — hunger, tiredness, pain, temptation — while also being divine. This is affirmed in the Nicene Creed: “God from God, Light from Light, true God from true God.”</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Jesus as the Son of God</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Christians call Jesus the <dfn class="term" data-def="A title for Jesus meaning he has a unique relationship with God the Father and shares God's divine nature.">Son of God</dfn>, meaning he has a unique relationship with God the Father. This is not meant literally — God did not have a child in the human sense. Rather, it means Jesus shares God’s divine nature and has existed with the Father for all eternity.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Several events in the Gospels confirm Jesus as the Son of God:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n8">At Jesus’s <strong>baptism</strong>, a voice from heaven said: “This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well pleased” (Matthew 3:17).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n9">At the <strong>transfiguration</strong>, God’s voice again declared: “This is my Son, whom I love. Listen to him!” (Mark 9:7).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n10">Jesus performed <strong>miracles</strong> — healing the sick, calming storms, raising the dead — demonstrating divine power.</li>
</ul>

  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Why the Incarnation Matters to Christians</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11">The incarnation is significant for several reasons. It shows that God loves humanity enough to become one of us. It means God understands human suffering from personal experience. It also means that Jesus’s teachings carry the full authority of God — when Jesus speaks, God speaks. Finally, it was necessary for salvation: only someone who was both God and human could bridge the gap between a holy God and sinful humanity.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n12">The Crucifixion</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The execution of Jesus by being nailed to a cross; central to Christian belief about salvation.">crucifixion</dfn> refers to the death of Jesus on a Roman cross. Jesus was arrested, tried, and sentenced to death by the Roman governor Pontius Pilate. He was nailed to a cross and died on a Friday, now commemorated as Good Friday.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Christians believe the crucifixion was not simply a tragic execution — it was part of God’s plan to save humanity from sin. Jesus willingly gave up his life as a sacrifice. Before his arrest, Jesus prayed: “Not my will, but yours be done” (Luke 22:42), showing he accepted his suffering as the Father’s will.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="Without looking, explain why the crucifixion is central to Christian belief about salvation.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Christians believe Jesus’s death on the cross paid the price for human sin. Because Jesus was sinless, his death was a perfect sacrifice that restored the relationship between God and humanity. This is why the cross is the most important symbol in Christianity.</p>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n16">The Resurrection</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The rising of Jesus from the dead on the third day after his crucifixion; the foundation of Christian faith.">resurrection</dfn> is the belief that Jesus rose from the dead three days after his crucifixion. On Easter Sunday, women visiting Jesus’s tomb found it empty. An angel told them: “He is not here; he has risen!” (Matthew 28:6). Jesus then appeared to his disciples over 40 days.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n18">The resurrection is the most important event in Christianity. St Paul wrote: “If Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile” (1 Corinthians 15:17). Without the resurrection, Christianity falls apart.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Why the Resurrection Matters</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n19"><strong>It proves Jesus is the Son of God.</strong> Only God could conquer death. The resurrection confirms that Jesus truly was divine.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>It shows God’s power over death.</strong> Death is not the end — God has the power to raise the dead. This gives Christians hope that they too will be resurrected.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n21"><strong>It confirms the promise of eternal life.</strong> Jesus said: “I am the resurrection and the life. The one who believes in me will live, even though they die” (John 11:25).</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n22"><strong>It is the basis of Christian worship.</strong> Christians worship on Sunday because it is the day Jesus rose. Easter is the most important festival in the Christian calendar.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n23">The Ascension</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n24">Forty days after the resurrection, Jesus <dfn class="term" data-def="Jesus's return to heaven after his resurrection, witnessed by his disciples.">ascended</dfn> (went up) to heaven. Acts 1:9 describes how “he was taken up before their very eyes, and a cloud hid him from their sight.” Two angels then told the disciples that Jesus would return in the same way.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Significance of the Ascension</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n25">The ascension is significant because it shows Jesus’s mission on Earth was complete. He returned to the Father, where Christians believe he sits “at the right hand of God” (the Nicene Creed) — a position of honour and authority. The ascension also prepared the way for the Holy Spirit to come at Pentecost, empowering the disciples to spread the Christian message. Christians believe Jesus will return from heaven at the end of time to judge the living and the dead.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n26" data-revision-tip="From memory, list the four key events of Jesus's life in order and write one sentence on the significance of each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The events of Jesus’s life form a connected sequence: the <strong>incarnation</strong> (God becomes human), the <strong>crucifixion</strong> (Jesus dies for sin), the <strong>resurrection</strong> (Jesus conquers death), and the <strong>ascension</strong> (Jesus returns to heaven). Each event is essential to the Christian understanding of salvation.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n28">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n29">The incarnation means God became human in Jesus Christ. Jesus is both fully God and fully human, as affirmed in John 1:14 and the Nicene Creed.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n30">The crucifixion was not just a tragic death but a sacrifice for human sin. The resurrection proved Jesus was the Son of God and gives Christians hope of eternal life.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n31">The ascension completed Jesus’s earthly mission and prepared the way for the Holy Spirit to guide believers.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n27">When answering questions about the crucifixion and resurrection, always explain the <strong>significance</strong> — not just what happened, but <strong>why it matters</strong> to Christians. For example, don’t just say “Jesus died on the cross” — explain that his death paid the price for human sin and restored the relationship between God and humanity. The exam rewards understanding of meaning and importance, not just retelling the story.</p>
