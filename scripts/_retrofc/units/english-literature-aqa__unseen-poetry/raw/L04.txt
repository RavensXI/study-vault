--- DESCRIPTION ---
How to write a focused 8-mark comparison of two unseen poems, linking methods to shared or contrasting themes.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Comparison Task</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The unseen comparison asks you to compare the poem you have just analysed with a second unseen poem, focusing on how each poet uses their methods. It carries fewer marks than the longer single-poem analysis that precedes it, so your response should be focused and proportionate — two tight paragraphs are usually enough. The task typically invites you to discuss similarities and/or differences in <em>how</em> the poets present a particular idea or subject. Check your exam board’s specimen paper for the exact timing and wording.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall: what skill does this comparison task test above all, and why is 'both poems are about nature' not enough?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The comparison rewards <strong>analysis of methods</strong> above all. You <strong>must</strong> compare the <em>methods</em> (language, structure, form) the poets use, not just the content of the poems. Writing “Both poems are about nature” is content; writing “Both poets use personification to present nature as a living force, but Poet A creates awe while Poet B creates menace” is methods.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Reading the Second Poem</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">You need to read the second poem quickly and efficiently. Spend a few minutes reading it twice and making brief annotations. Focus on three things:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6"><strong>1. The theme</strong> — What is this poem about? How does its subject connect to the first poem?</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>2. The methods</strong> — What techniques stand out? Identify 2-3 methods you can compare with the first poem.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>3. Similarities and differences</strong> — Comparison tasks usually invite you to discuss both. The strongest responses will consider <em>both</em>, showing how the poets approach the same theme in different ways.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>How to Structure Your Comparison</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n9">Aim for <strong>two focused paragraphs</strong>. Each paragraph should compare one specific method used by both poets.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Paragraph 1:</strong> A similarity or difference in how the poets use <em>language</em>. Quote from both poems. Analyse the effect of each poet’s word choices and explain how they create similar or contrasting effects.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Paragraph 2:</strong> A similarity or difference in how the poets use <em>structure or form</em>. Compare how the poems are organised, how they develop, or how the poets use line breaks, rhythm, or stanza structure to create effects.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n12">Use <dfn class="term" data-def="Words and phrases used to signal comparison, such as ‘similarly,’ ‘in contrast,’ ‘whereas,’ ‘however,’ ‘both poets,’ ‘while Poet A... Poet B.’">comparative connectives</dfn> throughout: “Similarly,” “In contrast,” “Whereas,” “However,” “Both poets,” “While Poet A... Poet B.”</p>
  </div></div>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n13">What Markers Reward</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Examiner reports consistently point to a clear hierarchy of what moves answers up the bands:</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall: what three qualities characterise the strongest comparison answers, and what separates them from merely 'clear' work?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The strongest comparison responses are <strong>perceptive and detailed</strong>: they compare both poems’ methods throughout, use well-chosen references from both, and offer sophisticated analysis of the effects of language, form and structure. Clearer — but less insightful — work still explains comparisons with relevant references. Weaker work describes the poems separately or attempts only surface-level comparison. The step up is always from <em>describing</em> to <em>analysing effect</em>.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Common Mistakes</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Writing about one poem then the other:</strong> This is the biggest mistake. Write about <em>both poems in every paragraph</em>. A comparison response that analyses Poem 1 for ten lines and then Poem 2 for ten lines will not access the top band, even if the individual analysis is good.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n18"><strong>Comparing content, not methods:</strong> “Both poems are about loss” is content. “Both poets use <dfn class="term" data-def="A repeated word, phrase, or image that recurs throughout a poem, creating a pattern of meaning and connecting different parts of the text.">motifs</dfn> of darkness to represent loss, but Poet A creates quiet sadness while Poet B creates angry despair” is methods. The marks here are entirely for analysis of methods, so this distinction is critical.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n19"><strong>Spending too long:</strong> This comparison task is shorter than the single-poem analysis that comes before it. Don’t spend more time on the comparison than on the main unseen question. Two focused paragraphs are enough.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Model Comparison Paragraph</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Model:</strong> Both poets use natural imagery to present the passing of time, but with contrasting effects. In Poem 1, the metaphor “the river carried our laughter to the sea” presents time as a gentle, natural process — the river flows steadily, carrying memories with it. The verb “carried” suggests care and tenderness. In contrast, Poet 2 uses the image of “leaves / torn from the branch by winter’s fist” to present time as violent and destructive. The personification of winter as having a “fist” makes time feel aggressive and forceful, while the enjambment between “leaves” and “torn” mirrors the sudden tearing away. Where Poet 1 accepts time peacefully, Poet 2 resists it with anger.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n21">Useful Comparative Phrases</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22">These phrases will help you compare <em>throughout</em> your paragraphs:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n23"><strong>For similarities:</strong> “Both poets use [method] to [effect].” “Similarly, Poet 2 employs [technique] to create the same sense of...” “This is echoed in Poem 2, where...”</p>
<p data-narration-id="n24"><strong>For differences:</strong> “In contrast, Poet 2...” “Whereas Poet 1 uses [method] to create [effect], Poet 2 instead...” “However, the effect in Poem 2 is quite different:...” “Where Poet 1 [approach], Poet 2 [contrasting approach].”

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n26">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n27">Q28 is worth 8 marks and is assessed on AO2 only (methods). Compare how the poets use language, structure, and form — not just what the poems are about.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n28">Write two focused comparative paragraphs, each discussing both poems together. Never write about one poem and then the other.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n29">Use comparative connectives throughout: “Similarly,” “In contrast,” “Whereas,” “Both poets.” These signal to the examiner that you are comparing, not just analysing.</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n25">Q28 is the last question on the entire paper. Many students rush it or run out of time. To avoid this, keep strict time: 25 minutes for Q27, then 20 minutes for Q28. Two well-developed comparative paragraphs will score higher than four rushed ones. Focus on <strong>methods</strong> (language, structure, form), not just content — every mark is for AO2.</p>
