--- DESCRIPTION ---
The purpose, components and benefits of a warm up — pulse raiser, mobility, stretching (static, dynamic, PNF) and sport-specific movement.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---

<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Why Warm Up?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A series of exercises performed before physical activity to prepare the body, improve performance and reduce injury risk.">warm up</dfn> is a series of exercises performed before a workout or sporting activity. Its purpose is to prepare the body for the demands of exercise, improve the quality of performance and lower the risk of injury.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down what you recall about thorough warm up should last at least 15–20.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>A thorough warm up should last at least <strong>15–20 minutes</strong>. Skipping or rushing a warm up significantly increases the chance of muscle strains, ligament sprains and other soft-tissue injuries during physical activity.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">The Four Key Components of a Warm Up (PRMS)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Every effective warm up follows four stages, often remembered using the acronym <strong>PRMS</strong>: Pulse raiser, Range of motion (mobility), Muscle stretching and Sport-specific skill rehearsal. Each stage builds on the last to bring the body from rest to full readiness.</p>
<h3 data-narration-id="n6">1. Pulse Raiser</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n7">A <dfn class="term" data-def="Light cardiovascular activity such as jogging or cycling that gradually increases heart rate at the start of a warm up.">pulse raiser</dfn> is light cardiovascular activity — such as steady jogging, cycling, skipping or side-stepping — designed to gradually increase heart rate. As heart rate rises, more oxygenated blood is pumped to the working muscles, providing the energy they need for more intense exercise ahead.</p>
<h3 data-narration-id="n8">2. Mobility</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n9"><dfn class="term" data-def="Exercises that move joints through their full range of motion, such as arm swings, ankle rolls and hip circles.">Mobility</dfn> exercises take joints through their full range of motion. Examples include arm swings, ankle rolls and hip circles. These movements increase the <dfn class="term" data-def="The ability of ligaments and tendons to stretch and flex without tearing.">pliability</dfn> of the ligaments and tendons around each joint, so the joint can move further during activity without causing damage.</p>
<h3 data-narration-id="n10">3. Stretching</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Stretching prepares the muscles to work through a full range of motion. There are two main types used in warm ups:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n12">
<li><strong><dfn class="term" data-def="A stretch held in a fixed position for 15 to 30 seconds to lengthen a specific muscle.">Static stretching</dfn></strong> — holding a stretch in a fixed position for 15–30 seconds to lengthen the muscle gradually.</li>
<li><strong><dfn class="term" data-def="Stretching through controlled, repeated movements such as lunges or leg swings.">Dynamic stretching</dfn></strong> — controlled movements through the range of motion, such as lunges, leg swings or high knees. Dynamic stretches keep the body moving and are more commonly used during warm ups.</li>
</ul>

<div class="collapsible-content">
<div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Static stretching</strong> involves holding a position for 15–30 seconds to elongate a specific muscle. It is useful for improving flexibility but is best performed after the pulse raiser, when muscles are warm. <strong>Dynamic stretching</strong> uses controlled, repeated movements — such as walking lunges, leg swings and arm circles — to stretch muscles while keeping the body active. <strong><dfn class="term" data-def="Proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation — an advanced stretching technique involving contraction and relaxation of a muscle with a partner, considered the most effective method for increasing flexibility.">PNF stretching</dfn></strong> (proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation) is an advanced technique where the performer contracts, then relaxes, a muscle while a trained partner pushes the limb further into the stretch. PNF is considered the most effective stretching method, but requires a knowledgeable partner to perform safely.</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>
<h3 data-narration-id="n14">4. Skill Rehearsal / Sport-Specific</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n15"><dfn class="term" data-def="Practising movements and techniques from the actual sport during the warm up, such as passing drills or shooting practice.">Skill rehearsal</dfn> is the final stage. The performer practises movements and techniques from the actual sport they are about to play — for example, passing and shooting in football, rallies in badminton, or lineout throws in rugby. This means the body has completed most of the possible movements it will need in a safe, controlled environment before the activity begins.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Physiological Benefits of a Warm Up</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">A proper warm up triggers several important changes in the body that improve performance and reduce the risk of injury:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n18">
<li><strong>Increase in muscle temperature</strong> — warmer muscles release energy more readily, become more flexible and are less likely to tear.</li>
<li><strong>Increase in heart rate</strong> — more oxygenated blood is pumped to the working muscles, providing the fuel needed for exercise.</li>
<li><strong>Increase in flexibility of muscles and joints</strong> — muscles can stretch further through their range of motion without damage.</li>
<li><strong>Increase in pliability of ligaments and tendons</strong> — connective tissues become more elastic and less prone to sprains.</li>
<li><strong>Increase in blood flow and oxygen delivery to muscles</strong> — capillaries dilate, allowing a greater volume of blood to reach muscle fibres.</li>
<li><strong>Increase in speed of muscle contraction</strong> — nerve impulses travel faster when muscles are warm, improving reaction time and power.</li>
</ul>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n19" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Explain the cause or effect related to physiological benefits of warming.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The physiological benefits of warming up all link together: higher muscle temperature leads to greater flexibility and faster contractions, while increased heart rate drives more oxygen to the muscles. Together, these changes prepare the body for high-intensity work and make injuries far less likely.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n20">Psychological Benefits of a Warm Up</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n21">Warming up does not just prepare the body — it also prepares the mind. The psychological benefits include:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n22">
<li><strong>Increased focus and concentration</strong> — the warm up gives the performer time to switch attention away from distractions and focus entirely on the activity ahead.</li>
<li><strong>Opportunity for mental rehearsal</strong> — the performer can visualise key movements, tactics and scenarios before the game or event begins.</li>
<li><strong>Reduced anxiety</strong> — following a familiar warm up routine creates a sense of structure and control, which helps calm nerves before competition.</li>
<li><strong>Increased confidence</strong> — completing a thorough warm up reassures the performer that their body is ready, boosting self-belief going into the activity.</li>
</ul>
<h2 data-narration-id="n23">Example Warm Up: Rugby</h2>
<div class="collapsible">
<button aria-expanded="false" class="collapsible-toggle">
<span>Worked Example — 15-Year-Old Rugby Player</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" viewbox="0 0 24 24"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"></polyline></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content">
<div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n24">A 15-year-old rugby player preparing for a match might follow this warm up routine:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n25">
<li><strong>Pulse raiser (5 minutes)</strong> — light jog around the pitch, gradually increasing speed. Side-stepping and backwards jogging to raise heart rate and warm up leg muscles.</li>
<li><strong>Mobility and dynamic movements (3–4 minutes)</strong> — hip circles, ankle rolls, arm swings. Walking lunges across the width of the pitch. High knees and heel flicks to take joints through their full range of motion.</li>
<li><strong>Stretches (3–4 minutes)</strong> — dynamic stretches such as leg swings (forward and side-to-side), hamstring sweeps and calf raises. Brief static holds (15–20 seconds) for the quadriceps and groin.</li>
<li><strong>Skill rehearsal (5 minutes)</strong> — passing drills in pairs at walking pace, building to game-speed passes. Short sprints simulating breakaways. Lineout throwing and catching practice.</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n26">This routine covers all four PRMS components, lasts around 15–20 minutes, and ensures the player’s cardiovascular, muscular and nervous systems are all prepared for the demands of a competitive rugby match.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>A warm up is a structured routine lasting 15–20 minutes that prepares the body and mind for physical activity. Its four components — pulse raiser, mobility, stretching and skill rehearsal — work together to reduce injury risk and improve performance.</p>
<h3>Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Four components (PRMS)</strong> — every warm up should include a pulse raiser, mobility exercises, stretching and sport-specific skill rehearsal, performed in that order.</li>
<li><strong>Physiological benefits</strong> — warming up raises muscle temperature, increases heart rate, improves flexibility and makes ligaments and tendons more pliable, all of which reduce injury risk.</li>
<li><strong>Psychological benefits</strong> — a warm up increases focus, allows mental rehearsal, reduces anxiety and builds confidence before competition.</li>
</ul>
