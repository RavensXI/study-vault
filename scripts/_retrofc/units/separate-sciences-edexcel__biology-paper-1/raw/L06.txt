<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Natural Selection</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="The gradual change in the inherited characteristics of a biological population over successive generations, driven by natural selection.">Evolution</dfn> is the gradual change in the inherited characteristics of a population over many generations. The mechanism that drives evolution is <dfn class="term" data-def="The process by which organisms with characteristics better suited to their environment are more likely to survive, reproduce and pass on advantageous alleles to offspring. Over time, this shifts the characteristics of the population.">natural selection</dfn>, independently proposed by <strong>Charles Darwin</strong> (1809&ndash;1882) and <strong>Alfred Wallace</strong> (1823&ndash;1913) in the mid-nineteenth century.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">The four-step process of natural selection:</p>

<ol>
  <li data-narration-id="n4">Within a population there is <strong>variation</strong> in heritable characteristics, arising from random mutations and sexual reproduction.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n5">Individuals with characteristics better suited to the environment are more likely to <strong>survive</strong> long enough to reproduce (&lsquo;survival of the fittest&rsquo;).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n6">These successful individuals <strong>reproduce</strong> and pass on their advantageous alleles to offspring.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n7">Over many generations the frequency of the advantageous allele <strong>increases</strong> in the population, gradually changing its characteristics.</li>
</ol>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n8" data-revision-tip="Cover this and write the four steps of natural selection in order, using the words variation, survive, reproduce and frequency.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Natural selection: variation exists &rarr; individuals with advantageous traits survive and reproduce &rarr; advantageous alleles passed to offspring &rarr; frequency of the advantageous allele increases over generations. This process never acts on an individual during its lifetime &mdash; only populations evolve.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n9">A critical misconception to avoid: evolution does not happen in an individual&rsquo;s lifetime. Giraffes did not &lsquo;grow&rsquo; longer necks to reach leaves. Rather, variation in neck length already existed in the population; individuals with longer necks survived and reproduced more successfully, passing on the alleles for longer necks. Over many generations, the average neck length increased.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Evidence for Evolution</h2>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>The Fossil Record and Human Evolution</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11">Your exam requires knowledge of three named human fossil finds and the use of stone tools as evidence for human evolution:</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Ardi</strong> (<em>Ardipithecus ramidus</em>) &mdash; approximately 4.4 million years old. Discovered in Ethiopia by Tim White&rsquo;s team (fossils recovered 1992&ndash;1994, formally announced 2009). One of the earliest known hominid fossils, showing features of upright walking alongside features suited to tree living.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Lucy</strong> (<em>Australopithecus afarensis</em>) &mdash; approximately 3.2 million years old. Discovered in 1974 by Donald Johanson in Ethiopia. Lucy&rsquo;s skeletal remains show clear evidence of bipedalism (walking upright on two legs), yet a small braincase closer in size to that of a chimpanzee.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Richard Leakey</strong> &mdash; palaeoanthropologist who led fossil discoveries in East Africa attributed to hominin species living approximately 1.6 million years ago, including specimens of <em>Homo erectus</em>. These fossils show a larger brain and more sophisticated stone tools than earlier hominids.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Stone tools</strong> provide indirect evidence for human cognitive evolution. Increasingly complex tool-making (from basic Oldowan flakes to more refined Acheulean hand axes) correlates with increasing brain size over time.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Pentadactyl Limb and Antibiotic Resistance</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <div class="higher-only">
    <p data-narration-id="n16">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The five-digit limb structure shared by all vertebrates (amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals), modified in different ways for different functions but showing the same underlying bone arrangement.">pentadactyl limb</dfn> is the five-digit limb structure shared by all vertebrates, though modified for different functions &mdash; the human hand, bat wing, dolphin flipper and horse leg all contain the same set of homologous bones. This is strong evidence for common ancestry.</p>
    </div>
    <p data-narration-id="n17"><dfn class="term" data-def="The evolution of bacteria that are no longer killed by antibiotics that once worked against them, caused by natural selection acting on mutations that arise during reproduction.">Antibiotic resistance</dfn> is natural selection observed in action on a short timescale. When a bacterium acquires a random mutation that allows it to survive exposure to an antibiotic, it survives while others die. It reproduces rapidly, passing on the resistance gene. Over many generations, the resistant strain dominates. This is why completing a course of antibiotics and not overusing them is essential to slow resistance.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Classification: Three Domains</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n19">Living organisms are classified using a hierarchical system. This exam uses the <strong>three-domain classification</strong> proposed by Carl Woese, which divides life into: <strong>Archaea</strong> (ancient prokaryotes), <strong>Bacteria</strong> (true bacteria) and <strong>Eukarya</strong> (all eukaryotes including plants, animals, fungi and protists). Within each domain, organisms are further classified into kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus and species. The species is the most precise level: members of the same species can breed and produce fertile offspring.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n20">Selective Breeding</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n21"><dfn class="term" data-def="The deliberate process by which humans choose organisms with desired characteristics to breed together, producing offspring with those traits over successive generations. Also called artificial selection.">Selective breeding</dfn> (artificial selection) is the human-directed version of natural selection. Humans select organisms with desirable traits and breed them together. Repeating this over generations concentrates the desired characteristics.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n22">Examples: selectively breeding cows for higher milk yield, wheat for disease resistance, dogs for specific sizes or temperament. A key disadvantage is that it reduces <strong>genetic variation</strong> within a population (inbreeding), making the population more vulnerable to disease or environmental change. <strong>Tissue culture</strong> allows selective breeding to be extended to plants: identical plants can be produced from meristem cells and selected for desirable traits.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n23">Genetic Engineering and GM Crops (Triple Only)</h2>

<div class="higher-only">
<p data-narration-id="n24"><dfn class="term" data-def="The process of inserting a gene from one organism into the DNA of another, giving the recipient organism a new characteristic.">Genetic engineering</dfn> involves inserting a gene from one organism into the DNA of another. The general process:</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Steps in Genetic Engineering</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n25"><strong>Step 1 &mdash; Identify and isolate the gene:</strong> The desired gene (e.g. the human insulin gene) is identified. <dfn class="term" data-def="Enzymes that cut DNA at specific base sequences, producing fragments with sticky ends that can be joined to other DNA sequences.">Restriction enzymes</dfn> cut the gene from the donor organism&rsquo;s DNA at specific base sequences, leaving &lsquo;sticky ends&rsquo;.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n26"><strong>Step 2 &mdash; Insert into a vector:</strong> A <dfn class="term" data-def="A vehicle used to transfer a gene into a host cell. Common vectors include plasmids (bacterial rings of DNA) and viruses.">vector</dfn> (commonly a bacterial plasmid) is cut with the same restriction enzyme, leaving complementary sticky ends. The gene is inserted; <dfn class="term" data-def="An enzyme that joins the sticky ends of DNA fragments together, forming a recombinant DNA molecule.">ligase</dfn> enzyme joins the sticky ends, sealing the gene into the plasmid.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n27"><strong>Step 3 &mdash; Transform the host:</strong> The recombinant plasmid is introduced into a host cell (e.g. a bacterium such as <em>E. coli</em>). The bacterium then replicates, with each daughter cell containing the new gene and expressing the desired protein.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n28"><strong>GM crop example &mdash; Bt crops:</strong> The bacterium <em>Bacillus thuringiensis</em> naturally produces a protein (Bt toxin) that kills insect pests. The gene for Bt toxin has been inserted into crop plants (e.g. cotton, maize), making the crops resistant to pest damage without needing as many insecticide sprays.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n29" data-revision-tip="Cover this and name the three tools used in genetic engineering (one enzyme that cuts, one enzyme that joins, one vehicle that carries) and a GM crop example.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Genetic engineering uses restriction enzymes (cut DNA), ligase (joins sticky ends) and vectors such as plasmids (carry the gene into the host cell). Example: <em>Bacillus thuringiensis</em> (Bt) gene inserted into crops gives pest resistance without insecticides.</p>
</div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n31">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n32">Natural selection: variation &rarr; survival of the fittest &rarr; advantageous alleles inherited &rarr; frequency increases over generations. Proposed independently by Charles Darwin (1809&ndash;1882) and Alfred Wallace (1823&ndash;1913).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n33">Evidence for human evolution: Ardi (~4.4 mya), Lucy (~3.2 mya, bipedal), Leakey&rsquo;s finds (~1.6 mya, larger brain, stone tools); pentadactyl limb shows common ancestry; antibiotic resistance demonstrates natural selection in action.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n34">Genetic engineering uses restriction enzymes (cut), ligase (join) and plasmid vectors to insert genes. Selective breeding concentrates desired traits but reduces genetic variation. Three-domain classification: Archaea, Bacteria, Eukarya.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n30">The most common error on natural selection questions is writing &lsquo;organisms adapted to their environment&rsquo; as if individuals change in their own lifetime. Always write that variation <em>already exists</em> in the population, and that individuals with advantageous characteristics <em>survive and reproduce</em> more, passing alleles to offspring. For genetic engineering questions, name the specific enzymes (restriction enzymes and ligase) and the vector &mdash; vague answers about &lsquo;putting genes into bacteria&rsquo; rarely score full marks. For the named human fossils (Ardi, Lucy, Leakey), state the approximate age and one feature that provides evidence of human evolution.</p>
