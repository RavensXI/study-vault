<h2 data-narration-id="n1">DNA Structure and the Genome</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="Deoxyribonucleic acid: the molecule that carries the genetic instructions for all living organisms. It consists of two strands wound into a double helix, made of nucleotide units.">DNA</dfn> (deoxyribonucleic acid) is the molecule that carries genetic information. Each molecule consists of two strands wound around each other to form a <strong>double helix</strong>. Each strand is made of repeating units called <dfn class="term" data-def="The monomer unit of DNA. Each nucleotide consists of a deoxyribose sugar, a phosphate group and one of four nitrogenous bases: adenine, thymine, cytosine or guanine.">nucleotides</dfn>. Each nucleotide contains a deoxyribose sugar, a phosphate group and one of four nitrogenous bases: <strong>adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C)</strong> and <strong>guanine (G)</strong>.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">The two strands are held together by <strong>complementary base pairing</strong>: A pairs only with T (A&ndash;T), and C pairs only with G (C&ndash;G). This complementarity is central to how DNA replicates and how genetic information is read. A <dfn class="term" data-def="A section of DNA that codes for a specific protein. Each gene is made of a sequence of base pairs that act as instructions for building one polypeptide.">gene</dfn> is a section of DNA that codes for a specific protein. The complete set of genetic information in an organism is called its <dfn class="term" data-def="The complete set of genetic information (DNA) of an organism, including all its genes and non-coding sequences.">genome</dfn>. The Human Genome Project, completed in 2003, sequenced the entire human genome and identified the location of around 20,000 protein-coding genes.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this and write the complementary base pairs for DNA from memory, then define the terms gene and genome.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>DNA base pairing: A pairs with T; C pairs with G. A gene is a section of DNA that codes for one protein. The genome is the complete set of an organism&rsquo;s DNA. The Human Genome Project mapped all human genes.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Protein Synthesis: Transcription and Translation (Triple Only)</h2>

<div class="higher-only">
<p data-narration-id="n6">The information stored in a gene is used to build a specific protein through a two-stage process: <dfn class="term" data-def="The first stage of protein synthesis. An enzyme called RNA polymerase reads the DNA template strand and builds a complementary mRNA strand, which then leaves the nucleus.">transcription</dfn> and <dfn class="term" data-def="The second stage of protein synthesis. Ribosomes read the mRNA sequence in triplets (codons) and use tRNA to assemble amino acids in the correct order to build a polypeptide.">translation</dfn>.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Transcription and Translation &mdash; Step by Step</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Transcription (in the nucleus):</strong> An enzyme called <dfn class="term" data-def="The enzyme that catalyses transcription by reading the DNA template strand and building a complementary mRNA molecule.">RNA polymerase</dfn> binds to the gene and reads the template strand of DNA. It builds a complementary strand of messenger RNA (<strong>mRNA</strong>) using RNA bases (U replaces T in RNA; uracil pairs with adenine). The mRNA strand leaves the nucleus through pores in the nuclear envelope.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Translation (at the ribosome):</strong> The mRNA strand attaches to a ribosome. Each set of three bases on the mRNA (called a codon) codes for one specific amino acid. Transfer RNA (<strong>tRNA</strong>) molecules carry amino acids to the ribosome; each tRNA has an anticodon complementary to the mRNA codon. Amino acids are joined together in the correct sequence to build a polypeptide chain, which folds into the final protein.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9">A mutation in the DNA sequence can change the base sequence of the mRNA, which may alter the amino acid sequence of the protein. If the protein&rsquo;s shape changes, it may no longer function correctly.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Mendel&rsquo;s Work and the Principles of Inheritance</h2>

<div class="higher-only">
<p data-narration-id="n11">Gregor Mendel (1822&ndash;1884) was a monk working in Brno who carried out carefully controlled breeding experiments with pea plants between 1856 and 1863. He crossed plants with different traits (e.g. tall vs short, round vs wrinkled peas) and recorded the ratios of traits in offspring across thousands of plants. His results led him to propose two key principles: that hereditary &lsquo;factors&rsquo; (what we now call alleles) come in pairs, and that one factor can mask the other.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n12">Mendel&rsquo;s work was largely ignored during his lifetime, partly because he lacked a mechanism to explain his results. When chromosomes and cell division were discovered after his death, scientists realised that Mendel&rsquo;s hereditary factors were genes on chromosomes. His work was then recognised as the foundation of genetics.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n13">The key terminology you need:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n14"><dfn class="term" data-def="Different versions of the same gene. Each individual inherits two alleles of each gene, one from each parent.">Allele</dfn> &mdash; a different version of a gene. We inherit two alleles of each gene, one from each parent.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n15"><dfn class="term" data-def="An allele that is expressed in the phenotype whenever it is present, even if only one copy is inherited. Represented by a capital letter.">Dominant</dfn> &mdash; expressed whenever present (one or two copies). Capital letter.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n16"><dfn class="term" data-def="An allele that is only expressed in the phenotype when two copies are present (i.e. the individual is homozygous recessive). Represented by a lower-case letter.">Recessive</dfn> &mdash; only expressed when two copies are present. Lower-case letter.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n17"><dfn class="term" data-def="Having two identical alleles for a particular gene (e.g. BB or bb).">Homozygous</dfn> &mdash; both alleles the same (BB or bb).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n18"><dfn class="term" data-def="Having two different alleles for a particular gene (e.g. Bb).">Heterozygous</dfn> &mdash; two different alleles (Bb).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n19"><strong>Genotype</strong> &mdash; the allele combination (e.g. Bb). <strong>Phenotype</strong> &mdash; the expressed characteristic (e.g. brown eyes).</li>
</ul>

<h2 data-narration-id="n20">Punnett Squares and Genetic Crosses</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n21">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A grid used to predict the possible genotypes and phenotypes of offspring from a genetic cross. Parent alleles are written along the top and side; the cells show all possible offspring combinations.">Punnett square</dfn> is a grid that shows all possible allele combinations in offspring from a genetic cross. Parent alleles go along the top and down the side; each cell shows the genotype of a possible offspring.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Worked Example: Monohybrid Cross and Sex Determination</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n22">Both parents are carriers of cystic fibrosis (genotype Ff, where f is recessive). What are the possible genotypes and phenotypes of their offspring?</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n23">Punnett square: cross Ff &times; Ff gives FF, Ff, Ff, ff. Genotype ratio: 1 FF : 2 Ff : 1 ff. Phenotype ratio: 3 unaffected (FF or Ff) : 1 affected (ff). Probability of an affected child = \(\frac{1}{4}\) or 25%. Each pregnancy carries the same probability.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Sex determination:</strong> Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes; the 23rd pair determines sex. Females are XX; males are XY. A Punnett cross shows a 50% chance of XX (female) and 50% chance of XY (male) in any pregnancy.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n25">Codominance and ABO Blood Groups (Triple Only)</h2>

<div class="higher-only">
<p data-narration-id="n26"><dfn class="term" data-def="A pattern of inheritance where both alleles are expressed equally in the phenotype of a heterozygous individual. Neither allele is dominant over the other.">Codominance</dfn> occurs when both alleles are equally expressed in the heterozygote, so the phenotype is a blend or shows both traits simultaneously. The ABO blood group system illustrates codominance.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n27">Blood group is controlled by one gene with three alleles: I<sup>A</sup>, I<sup>B</sup> and I<sup>O</sup>. I<sup>A</sup> and I<sup>B</sup> are codominant with each other; I<sup>O</sup> is recessive to both.</p>

<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n28">Blood group A: I<sup>A</sup>I<sup>A</sup> or I<sup>A</sup>I<sup>O</sup></li>
  <li data-narration-id="n29">Blood group B: I<sup>B</sup>I<sup>B</sup> or I<sup>B</sup>I<sup>O</sup></li>
  <li data-narration-id="n30">Blood group AB: I<sup>A</sup>I<sup>B</sup> (both A and B antigens expressed &mdash; codominance)</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n31">Blood group O: I<sup>O</sup>I<sup>O</sup></li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n32" data-revision-tip="Cover this and write the genotype for blood group AB, then explain why AB is an example of codominance.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>ABO blood groups use codominance: I<sup>A</sup> and I<sup>B</sup> alleles are both expressed when present together, giving blood group AB (genotype I<sup>A</sup>I<sup>B</sup>). Blood group O requires two copies of the recessive I<sup>O</sup> allele.</p>
</div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n34">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n35">DNA is a double helix of complementary base pairs (A&ndash;T, C&ndash;G). A gene is a DNA section coding for a protein; the genome is the full set of DNA. The Human Genome Project mapped all human genes.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n36">Protein synthesis: transcription (DNA &rarr; mRNA in nucleus via RNA polymerase); translation (mRNA &rarr; polypeptide at ribosomes, using tRNA).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n37">Gregor Mendel&rsquo;s pea plant experiments established the principles of inheritance. Punnett squares predict offspring ratios. ABO blood groups demonstrate codominance: I<sup>A</sup>I<sup>B</sup> gives group AB because both alleles are expressed equally.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n33">In genetics questions, always set out your Punnett square clearly &mdash; write the parent genotypes, the gametes (one letter per gamete), and label the offspring genotypes and phenotypes with their ratios. For codominance questions such as ABO blood groups, use superscript notation (I<sup>A</sup>, I<sup>B</sup>, I<sup>O</sup>) and state clearly that <em>neither</em> allele masks the other in the heterozygote. In protein synthesis questions, distinguish between the two stages: transcription happens in the nucleus (DNA &rarr; mRNA); translation happens at the ribosome (mRNA &rarr; protein). Don&rsquo;t confuse mRNA with tRNA.</p>
