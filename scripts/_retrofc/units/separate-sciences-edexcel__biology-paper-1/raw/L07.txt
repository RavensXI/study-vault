<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Communicable and Non-Communicable Diseases</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="A disease caused by a pathogen that can spread from one organism to another.">Communicable diseases</dfn> are illnesses spread between organisms by <dfn class="term" data-def="Microorganisms that cause disease, including bacteria, viruses, fungi and protists.">pathogens</dfn>. <dfn class="term" data-def="A disease not caused by a pathogen and that cannot be passed from person to person — examples include cardiovascular disease and cancer.">Non-communicable diseases</dfn> are not caused by pathogens and cannot be spread. The two categories can interact: a weakened immune system (from a non-communicable condition) makes a person more vulnerable to infections, while chronic infection can trigger inflammatory damage that leads to non-communicable conditions.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall the four types of pathogen. For each type, name one disease it causes.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>There are four main types of pathogen: bacteria, viruses, fungi, and protists. Bacteria cause disease by producing toxins; viruses invade host cells to replicate; fungi and protists use other mechanisms. Each type requires a different treatment approach.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Named Pathogens You Need to Know</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n5">Your specification requires you to know a range of named diseases caused by specific pathogens. The table below groups them by pathogen type.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Bacterial Diseases: Cholera, Tuberculosis and Chlamydia</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Cholera</strong> is caused by the bacterium <em>Vibrio cholerae</em>, spread through contaminated water. It produces toxins that cause severe diarrhoea and rapid dehydration. Treatment is rehydration; clean water supplies prevent spread.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Tuberculosis (TB)</strong> is caused by <em>Mycobacterium tuberculosis</em>, spread through airborne droplets. It primarily infects the lungs, causing persistent cough, weight loss and fever. TB is treated with a long course of antibiotics and remains a leading infectious disease globally.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Helicobacter pylori</strong> is a bacterium that colonises the stomach lining. It is a major cause of stomach ulcers and is treated with antibiotics combined with acid-reducing medicines.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Chlamydia</strong> is a sexually transmitted bacterial infection. It often has no symptoms but can cause reproductive damage if untreated. It is treated with antibiotics and prevented through safe-sex practices.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Viral Diseases: HIV, Ebola and Lytic vs Lysogenic Cycles</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>HIV</strong> attacks the immune system&rsquo;s helper T-cells, destroying the body&rsquo;s ability to fight infection. Advanced HIV infection, when the immune system is severely compromised, is called <dfn class="term" data-def="Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome: the late stage of HIV infection, when the immune system is so weakened that life-threatening infections take hold.">AIDS</dfn>. HIV is spread through unprotected sexual contact, sharing needles, or mother to child. Antiretroviral drugs prevent progression but cannot cure the virus.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Ebola</strong> is a viral haemorrhagic fever spread through direct contact with bodily fluids of infected individuals. It causes fever, bleeding and multi-organ failure. Outbreaks are controlled by quarantine, contact tracing and barrier nursing.</p>
    <div class="higher-only">
    <p data-narration-id="n12">Viruses replicate by two cycles. In the <dfn class="term" data-def="A viral replication cycle in which the virus immediately hijacks the host cell&apos;s machinery to make copies, then bursts (lyses) the cell, releasing new viruses.">lytic cycle</dfn>, the virus immediately takes over the host cell, replicates rapidly, and bursts the cell to release new viral particles. In the <dfn class="term" data-def="A viral replication cycle in which viral DNA is incorporated into the host cell&apos;s genome and replicated quietly with the cell, potentially entering the lytic cycle later.">lysogenic cycle</dfn>, viral DNA integrates into the host cell&rsquo;s genome and is replicated passively each time the cell divides &mdash; it can remain dormant for years before switching to lytic replication.</p>
    </div>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Fungal and Protist Diseases: Chalara and Malaria</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Chalara ash dieback</strong> is caused by the fungus <em>Hymenoscyphus fraxineus</em>. It spreads via windborne spores and has devastated ash tree populations across Europe. There is no effective treatment; management involves removing infected trees to slow spread.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Malaria</strong> is caused by the protist <em>Plasmodium</em>, spread by the female <em>Anopheles</em> mosquito acting as a <dfn class="term" data-def="An organism that carries and transmits a pathogen from one host to another without necessarily becoming ill itself.">vector</dfn>. The protist infects and destroys red blood cells, causing fever, chills and anaemia. Prevention focuses on mosquito control (insecticides, bed nets, draining stagnant water); antimalarial drugs reduce severity.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n15">Plant Defences and Plant Pathogens</h2>

<div class="higher-only">
<p data-narration-id="n16">Plants also suffer from communicable diseases. Pathogens can spread through wind, water, soil and vectors such as insects. Plants defend themselves through physical barriers (the waxy cuticle, cell walls) and chemical defences (antibacterial compounds, toxic secondary metabolites). They also respond to infection with localised cell death to limit the spread of pathogens &mdash; a process called the hypersensitive response.</p>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall the three categories of non-specific defence in humans, giving one example structure or chemical for each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Human non-specific defences include: (1) physical barriers &mdash; skin and the mucus-cilia escalator in the airways; (2) chemical barriers &mdash; <dfn class="term" data-def="An enzyme found in tears, saliva and mucus that breaks down the cell walls of many bacteria.">lysozyme</dfn> in tears and saliva; (3) the inflammatory response &mdash; damaged tissues release signals that attract white blood cells to the site of infection.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">The Immune Response</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n19">If pathogens breach the non-specific barriers, the specific immune system responds. Pathogens carry molecules on their surfaces called <dfn class="term" data-def="Molecules on the surface of a pathogen that trigger the immune system to produce specific antibodies.">antigens</dfn>. These antigens are recognised as foreign, triggering white blood cells called lymphocytes to produce <dfn class="term" data-def="Proteins produced by lymphocytes that bind specifically to a pathogen&apos;s antigens, marking it for destruction.">antibodies</dfn>. Each antibody is complementary in shape to one specific antigen.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n20">Antibodies work by: clumping pathogens together so phagocytes can engulf them more easily; neutralising bacterial toxins; and marking pathogens for destruction by other immune cells. This specificity &mdash; one antibody shape for one antigen &mdash; is the key to the adaptive immune response.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Memory Lymphocytes and Immunity</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n21">After defeating a pathogen, some lymphocytes persist as <dfn class="term" data-def="Long-lived lymphocytes that remain in the blood after an infection, enabling a much faster secondary immune response if the same pathogen is encountered again.">memory lymphocytes</dfn>. These can remain for decades. If the same pathogen enters the body again, memory lymphocytes divide and differentiate rapidly, producing large quantities of the correct antibody far faster than the primary response. This rapid secondary response usually prevents symptoms from developing &mdash; the person is immune.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n22">Vaccination and Herd Immunity</h2>

<div class="higher-only">
<p data-narration-id="n23">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A preparation containing dead, weakened or fragmented pathogens (or their antigens) given to stimulate the immune system without causing disease.">vaccination</dfn> introduces antigens into the body without causing the full disease. The immune system responds by producing antibodies and memory lymphocytes. If the vaccinated person later encounters the real pathogen, their memory cells respond so rapidly that infection is prevented.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n24">When enough people in a community are vaccinated, the pathogen cannot spread easily &mdash; each infected person is likely to encounter immune individuals who block onward transmission. This is called <dfn class="term" data-def="When a large proportion of a population is immune to a disease, reducing its spread and protecting those who cannot be vaccinated, such as newborns or immunocompromised individuals.">herd immunity</dfn>. It protects vulnerable people who cannot receive vaccines, including those with certain medical conditions.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n25">Antibiotics and Aseptic Technique</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n26"><dfn class="term" data-def="Medicines that kill bacteria or inhibit their growth. They have no effect on viruses because viruses replicate inside host cells, not independently.">Antibiotics</dfn> kill bacteria or prevent their reproduction. They have no effect on viral infections because viruses replicate inside the host&rsquo;s own cells &mdash; an antibiotic that damaged viral replication would damage the host cell too. Antibiotic resistance arises through natural selection: bacteria with random mutations that confer resistance survive, reproduce and pass on resistance genes, especially when courses are not completed or antibiotics are used inappropriately.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Aseptic Technique and the Autoclave</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <div class="higher-only">
    <p data-narration-id="n27"><dfn class="term" data-def="A set of laboratory techniques used to prevent contamination of cultures by unwanted microorganisms, and to prevent laboratory strains from escaping into the environment.">Aseptic technique</dfn> is essential when culturing microorganisms. Key steps include: sterilising equipment before use (using an <dfn class="term" data-def="A pressurised chamber that uses high-pressure steam to kill all microorganisms and their spores on equipment and growth media.">autoclave</dfn> to kill all microorganisms at high pressure and temperature); working near a Bunsen flame to create an upward air current that carries contaminants away; flaming the necks of culture vessels before and after opening; and using sterile pipettes and loops. Agar plates must be incubated at no more than 25°C in school settings to prevent growth of dangerous human pathogens.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n28">In the core practical for investigating the effectiveness of antibiotics, antibiotic-soaked discs are placed on bacterial culture plates. The zone of inhibition &mdash; the clear area around the disc where bacteria cannot grow &mdash; measures the antibiotic&rsquo;s effectiveness. A larger zone indicates greater effectiveness against that strain.</p>
    </div>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n30">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n31">Communicable diseases are caused by pathogens (bacteria, viruses, fungi, protists) and can spread; non-communicable diseases cannot. Named pathogens include <em>Vibrio cholerae</em> (cholera), <em>Mycobacterium tuberculosis</em> (TB), HIV, Ebola, <em>Plasmodium</em> (malaria) and <em>Hymenoscyphus fraxineus</em> (Chalara ash dieback).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n32">The specific immune response produces antibodies complementary to antigens; memory lymphocytes remain to enable a faster secondary response that confers immunity.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n33">Vaccination stimulates immunity without causing disease and, at high population coverage, creates herd immunity. Antibiotics kill bacteria but not viruses; aseptic technique and the autoclave prevent contamination in microbiology practicals.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n29">When asked to &lsquo;explain&rsquo; how vaccination provides immunity, many students describe only the first exposure. Examiners also want the secondary response: memory lymphocytes enable a rapid antibody surge that prevents symptoms if the pathogen is encountered again &mdash; this is where the marks lie. For 6-mark method questions on culturing bacteria, include independent variable, dependent variable, control variables, repeats, aseptic precautions <em>and</em> how you would analyse the results. Missing any of these caps your mark.</p>
