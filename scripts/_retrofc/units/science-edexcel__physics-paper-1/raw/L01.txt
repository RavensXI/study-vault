--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Energy Stores</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Everything in the universe involves <dfn class="term" data-def="The capacity to do work or cause a change — measured in joules (J).">energy</dfn>. Energy is not a substance you can hold; it is a quantity that describes the capacity of a system to do work. In GCSE physics, we describe energy using the idea of <dfn class="term" data-def="A way of describing the energy of a system — examples include kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal, chemical, magnetic, electrostatic, and nuclear stores.">energy stores</dfn>. There are eight energy stores you need to know:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3"><strong>Kinetic</strong> — energy of a moving object. <strong>Gravitational potential</strong> — energy stored when an object is raised above the ground. <strong>Elastic potential</strong> — energy stored when an object is stretched or compressed. <strong>Thermal</strong> — energy related to the temperature of an object. <strong>Chemical</strong> — energy stored in bonds between atoms (fuels, food, batteries). <strong>Magnetic</strong> — energy of magnets attracting or repelling. <strong>Electrostatic</strong> — energy of charged particles attracting or repelling. <strong>Nuclear</strong> — energy stored in the nucleus of an atom.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this and write down all eight energy stores from memory, then check.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The eight energy stores are: kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal, chemical, magnetic, electrostatic, and nuclear.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Energy Transfers</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Energy can be <dfn class="term" data-def="A process by which energy moves between stores — the four main pathways are mechanical work, electrical work, heating, and radiation.">transferred</dfn> between stores by four main pathways:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Mechanically</strong> — a force acting on an object does work and transfers energy (e.g. pushing a trolley transfers energy from chemical stores in your muscles to kinetic stores in the trolley). <strong>Electrically</strong> — charges flowing through a circuit transfer energy (e.g. from the chemical store of a battery to the thermal store of a filament lamp). <strong>By heating</strong> — energy transfers from a hotter object to a cooler one by conduction, convection, or evaporation. <strong>By radiation</strong> — energy is transferred by electromagnetic waves, such as light or infrared radiation from the Sun.</p>

<!-- DIAGRAM -->

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n8" data-revision-tip="Close this and list the four energy transfer pathways with one example for each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The four energy transfer pathways are: mechanically (by forces doing work), electrically (by charges in a circuit), by heating (conduction, convection, evaporation), and by radiation (electromagnetic waves).</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Energy Transfers in Systems</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">A <dfn class="term" data-def="An object or group of objects considered together for the purpose of analysing energy changes.">system</dfn> is an object or group of objects. When a system changes, energy is transferred between stores. Here are some important examples from the specification:</p>

  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">

  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11">When a ball is thrown upwards, energy is transferred from the <dfn class="term" data-def="The energy stored in a moving object — depends on mass and speed.">kinetic store</dfn> to the <dfn class="term" data-def="Energy stored in an object due to its position above the ground — depends on mass, gravitational field strength, and height.">gravitational potential energy</dfn> store. As the ball rises, it slows down: kinetic energy decreases while gravitational potential energy increases. At the highest point, all the kinetic energy has been transferred to gravitational potential energy (ignoring air resistance). On the way down, the reverse happens.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">

    <p data-narration-id="n12">When a car crashes into a barrier, energy is transferred from the kinetic store to the thermal store (the car and barrier heat up) and to the elastic potential store (the car body deforms). Some energy is also transferred by sound waves to the thermal store of the surroundings.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Drawing Energy Transfer Diagrams</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15">Energy transfer diagrams use boxes to represent energy stores and arrows to show the transfer pathway. The width of each arrow can show the relative amount of energy transferred along that pathway. For example, boiling water in a kettle: the chemical store of the electricity supply transfers energy electrically to the thermal store of the water (useful) and to the thermal store of the surroundings (wasted, through heating of the kettle body and sound).</p>

<p data-narration-id="n16">When drawing these diagrams, always label each store clearly and show the transfer pathway on or beside the arrow. The total energy in must equal the total energy out — this links directly to the principle of conservation of energy.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Sketch an energy transfer diagram for a battery-powered torch, labelling each store and pathway.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Energy transfer diagrams show energy stores as labelled boxes and transfer pathways as arrows. The total energy in always equals the total energy out.</p>
</div>

<div class="exam-tip" data-narration-id="n18">
  <p><strong>Exam Tip:</strong> When describing energy changes, always name the <em>store</em> energy is transferred from, the <em>store</em> it goes to, and the <em>pathway</em> (mechanically, electrically, by heating, or by radiation). Vague answers like “energy is lost” lose marks — say “energy is dissipated to the thermal store of the surroundings by heating.”</p>
</div>

<div class="conclusion" data-narration-id="n19">
  <p>Energy is stored in eight different stores and transferred between them by four pathways. Being able to describe these changes precisely — naming stores and pathways — is essential for almost every energy question on the exam.</p>
</div>
