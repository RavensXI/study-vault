--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Conservation of Energy</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The <dfn class="term" data-def="A fundamental principle stating that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred between stores.">conservation of energy</dfn> is one of the most important ideas in physics. It states that energy cannot be created or destroyed — only transferred from one store to another. The total energy in a <dfn class="term" data-def="A system where no energy can enter or leave — the total energy stays the same.">closed system</dfn> always stays the same.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and state the principle of conservation of energy in your own words.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Energy cannot be created or destroyed. In a closed system, the total energy before a change is always equal to the total energy after the change.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Calculating Gravitational Potential Energy</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">When an object is raised above the ground, energy is transferred to its gravitational potential energy store. The amount of energy stored depends on the mass, the gravitational field strength, and the height gained:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n6"><strong>\(\Delta GPE = m \times g \times \Delta h\)</strong></p>

<p data-narration-id="n7">Where \(\Delta GPE\) is the change in gravitational potential energy in joules (J), \(m\) is mass in kilograms (kg), \(g\) is <dfn class="term" data-def="The force of gravity acting on each kilogram of mass — on Earth this is approximately 9.8 N/kg.">gravitational field strength</dfn> in newtons per kilogram (N/kg), and \(\Delta h\) is the change in vertical height in metres (m). On Earth, \(g \approx 9.8\) N/kg.</p>

</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Calculating Kinetic Energy</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">The energy in the <dfn class="term" data-def="The energy stored in a moving object — depends on mass and the square of its speed.">kinetic energy</dfn> store of a moving object depends on its mass and the square of its speed:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>\(KE = \frac{1}{2} \times m \times v^2\)</strong></p>

<p data-narration-id="n13">Where \(KE\) is kinetic energy in joules (J), \(m\) is mass in kilograms (kg), and \(v\) is speed in metres per second (m/s). Because speed is squared, doubling the speed quadruples the kinetic energy.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Wasted Energy and Efficiency</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">No energy transfer is perfectly efficient. Some energy is always <dfn class="term" data-def="Energy that has been transferred to the thermal store of the surroundings and is no longer in a useful form.">dissipated</dfn> — spread out to the thermal store of the surroundings. This wasted energy cannot easily be recovered. Mechanical processes become wasteful when friction causes a rise in temperature, heating the surroundings.</p>

<div class="higher-only">
<p data-narration-id="n18">We can reduce unwanted energy transfers by <dfn class="term" data-def="Using a substance such as oil or grease to reduce friction between moving surfaces.">lubrication</dfn> (reducing friction between moving parts) and <dfn class="term" data-def="Using materials with low thermal conductivity to reduce the rate of energy transfer by heating.">thermal insulation</dfn> (reducing energy transfer by heating). For example, loft insulation and cavity wall insulation reduce the rate at which a building cools by trapping air, which is a poor thermal conductor.</p>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n19" data-revision-tip="Write down the efficiency equation and explain what each term means.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Efficiency measures how much of the input energy is usefully transferred: \(\text{efficiency} = \frac{\text{useful energy output}}{\text{total energy input}}\). Efficiency has no units and is always between 0 and 1 (or 0% and 100%).</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n20">Energy Resources</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n21">The main energy resources available on Earth are: <dfn class="term" data-def="Energy resources that will eventually run out because they are used faster than they form — includes coal, oil, natural gas, and nuclear fuel.">non-renewable</dfn> sources (fossil fuels — coal, oil, natural gas — and nuclear fuel) and <dfn class="term" data-def="Energy resources that will not run out because they are replenished naturally — includes wind, solar, hydroelectric, tidal, geothermal, and bio-fuel.">renewable</dfn> sources (wind, solar, hydroelectric, tidal, geothermal, wave, and bio-fuel).</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Comparing renewable and non-renewable resources</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Non-renewable resources</strong> release large amounts of energy per kilogram and provide a reliable supply. However, burning fossil fuels produces carbon dioxide (contributing to climate change) and they will eventually run out. Nuclear fuel produces radioactive waste that must be stored safely.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Renewable resources</strong> will not run out and most produce no greenhouse gases during operation. However, many are intermittent (wind and solar depend on weather conditions), and some require large land areas or have visual/noise impacts. Bio-fuels are carbon neutral in theory because the plants absorb CO\(_2\) as they grow.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n24">The global trend is a shift towards renewable energy sources. This is driven by concerns about climate change, energy security (reducing dependence on imported fossil fuels), and the falling cost of renewable technologies such as solar panels and wind turbines.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n25" data-revision-tip="Close this and list three renewable and three non-renewable energy resources from memory.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Non-renewable resources (fossil fuels and nuclear) provide reliable, high-energy output but produce waste and will run out. Renewable resources (wind, solar, tidal, hydroelectric, geothermal, bio-fuel, wave) are replenished naturally but many are intermittent.</p>
</div>

<div class="exam-tip" data-narration-id="n26">
  <p><strong>Exam Tip:</strong> Calculation questions on energy are very common. Always write the equation, substitute the values with correct units, then calculate. Show every step — you can pick up method marks even if you make an arithmetic error. Remember that \(v\) in the kinetic energy equation is squared, so always square the speed before multiplying.</p>
</div>

<div class="conclusion" data-narration-id="n27">
  <p>The conservation of energy principle underpins all energy calculations. You need to recall and use the equations for gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy, understand efficiency, and be able to compare renewable and non-renewable energy resources.</p>
</div>
