--- DESCRIPTION ---
The ethics of nuclear deterrence, WMD, and religious responses to modern weapons.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Are Weapons of Mass Destruction?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="Weapons which cause widespread, indiscriminate damage, including nuclear, chemical, and biological weapons.">Weapons of mass destruction</dfn> (WMD) are weapons that have the power to kill and harm huge numbers of people. They cause large-scale damage to people, infrastructure, and the environment. There are three main types: nuclear, biological, and chemical.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. What does WMD stand for? List each part from memory.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The three types of WMD are: <strong>nuclear weapons</strong> (use nuclear reactions to devastate huge areas), <strong>biological weapons</strong> (use living organisms or viruses to cause disease and death), and <strong>chemical weapons</strong> (use toxic chemicals to burn, poison, or paralyse humans and destroy the environment).</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n4">It is impossible to use a weapon of mass destruction only on military targets — there will always be civilian victims. The weapons are controlled from a distance as missiles or bombs, meaning those who launch them do not directly see the destruction they cause. Biological and chemical weapons are illegal under international law, but nuclear weapons remain legal for some countries.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Nuclear Weapons and Nuclear Deterrence</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6"><dfn class="term" data-def="Weapons that work by nuclear reaction, devastating huge areas and killing large numbers of people.">Nuclear weapons</dfn> use the power of atoms, and sometimes radioactive substances, to achieve massive destruction. The atom bombs dropped on Japan at the end of World War II were so devastating that nuclear weapons have not been used in standard military conflict since.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Some countries keep nuclear weapons as a form of <dfn class="term" data-def="Having nuclear weapons with the aim of preventing other states from attacking, for fear of retaliation and mutually assured destruction.">nuclear deterrence</dfn>. The idea is that if a country has nuclear weapons, no other country will dare attack it because of the fear of devastating retaliation. This could lead to <dfn class="term" data-def="A scenario in which both sides in a nuclear conflict would be completely destroyed, making nuclear war unwinnable.">Mutually Assured Destruction</dfn> (MAD), where both sides would be completely destroyed.</p>
<figure class="diagram"><img src="https://pub-aeb94e100e5a48f4a133be5bf206aecb.r2.dev/religious-education/theme-d-peace-conflict/diagram_generated_da828055.png" alt="Generated diagram for Nuclear Weapons and Weapons of Mass Destruction"><figcaption>Generated diagram for Nuclear Weapons and Weapons of Mass Destruction</figcaption></figure>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n8" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. State the key difference described about countries that keep nuclear.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Countries that keep nuclear weapons include the UK (Christian majority), Pakistan (Muslim majority), and possibly Israel (Jewish majority). They argue these weapons keep the world safe through deterrence. However, critics say this is a huge waste of money and increases the risk of terrorists obtaining them.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Arguments for keeping nuclear weapons:</strong> They deter war due to fear of consequences. They provide security for countries. In an extreme emergency, they could save more lives overall. Some people who generally oppose nuclear weapons may accept their use to prevent a greater evil. <dfn class="term" data-def="An ethical theory that judges actions by whether they produce the greatest happiness for the greatest number of people.">Utilitarians</dfn> may accept nuclear weapons if they result in more pleasure than pain in the long run.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Arguments against nuclear weapons:</strong> They can kill many people, going against the <dfn class="term" data-def="The belief that all human life is holy and belongs to God, so only God has the right to take it.">sanctity of life</dfn> and religious commands not to murder. They will almost certainly kill innocent civilians. Radioactive fallout harms future generations. They damage animals and the environment. They do not give victims any chance to survive. Nuclear accidents and tests can be very damaging. They do not fulfil just war criteria.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n11">Christian Teachings on Nuclear Weapons and WMD</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Christians hold a range of views on nuclear weapons and WMD. There are no specific teachings in the Bible about nuclear weapons, as they did not exist in biblical times. Instead, Christians apply broader principles about peace, violence, and the value of human life.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Christian Views For and Against WMD</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Christians who oppose WMD</strong> point to Jesus’s teaching: “Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called children of God” (Matthew 5:9). They argue that WMD cause indiscriminate harm to innocent people, which contradicts the command to “love your neighbour as yourself” (Matthew 22:39). Several popes have spoken out against WMD because of the direct harm to people and the environment, and the indirect harm caused by spending money on weapons that could be used to help the poor. The Christian Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament is committed to the permanent elimination of nuclear weapons. Quakers are pacifists and oppose all WMD.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Christians who accept nuclear deterrence</strong> argue that the Bible allows violence in some circumstances, such as self-defence. They may see nuclear weapons as a necessary evil to keep the peace and protect innocent lives from aggression. Some Christians believe that keeping nuclear weapons as a deterrent — without intending to use them — is morally acceptable if it prevents greater suffering.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n15">Islamic Teachings on Nuclear Weapons and WMD</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n16">Islam teaches that violence should only be used in self-defence and must be proportionate. The Qur’an states: “If you have to respond to an attack, make your response proportionate” (Qur’an 16:126). Many Muslims believe WMD are not approved of by Allah because they go beyond defence and do not give victims a fair chance.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Islamic Views on WMD</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n17">The Prophet Muhammad forbade the killing of innocent civilians during conflict. WMD kill indiscriminately, meaning they cannot distinguish between soldiers and civilians. This makes their use incompatible with Islamic rules of warfare. Muslims also greet each other with “salaam alaikum” (“peace be upon you”), reflecting Islam’s emphasis on peace.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n18">However, some Muslims may argue that nuclear deterrence is acceptable to protect a Muslim country from invasion. Pakistan, a Muslim-majority country, possesses nuclear weapons for this reason. The Qur’an states: “Those who have been attacked are permitted to take up arms” (Qur’an 22:39). Some interpret this as allowing the possession of powerful weapons for defence, though not their offensive use against civilians.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n19" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. What does WMD stand for? List each part from memory.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Both Christianity and Islam teach the sanctity of life and the importance of protecting innocent people. The key ethical question is whether keeping WMD as a deterrent — without intending to use them — can be justified if it prevents war, or whether their very existence is immoral because of the risk of accidental or deliberate use.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n21">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n22">WMD include nuclear, biological, and chemical weapons — all cause indiscriminate, large-scale harm to civilians and the environment.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n23">Nuclear deterrence is the idea that keeping nuclear weapons prevents war, but many religious believers argue this contradicts the sanctity of life and teachings about peace.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n24">Both Christianity and Islam broadly oppose the use of WMD, but some believers accept nuclear deterrence as a necessary measure for national security.</li></ul>
