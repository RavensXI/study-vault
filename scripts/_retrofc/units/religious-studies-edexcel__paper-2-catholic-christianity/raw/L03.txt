--- DESCRIPTION ---
The seven sacraments, the Mass as source and summit, and forms of Catholic prayer.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Sacramental Vision of Reality</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Catholic theology teaches that all of creation can mediate the presence of God &mdash; the world is not a barrier between humans and God but a channel through which God communicates. This is the &lsquo;sacramental nature of reality.&rsquo; At its core are the seven <dfn class="term" data-def="In Catholic teaching, seven sacred rites instituted by Christ that confer God&rsquo;s grace on the recipient; they are signs that effect what they signify.">sacraments</dfn>: visible, physical actions through which God&rsquo;s invisible grace is communicated.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The official compendium of Catholic doctrine published in 1992, containing numbered paragraphs covering all aspects of faith and moral teaching.">Catechism of the Catholic Church</dfn> (CCC) 1210&ndash;1211 lists the seven sacraments: Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist, Penance (Reconciliation), Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders, and Matrimony. Each sacrament uses a physical sign (water, oil, bread, wine, the laying on of hands) and spoken words to effect what it signifies &mdash; the grace it conveys.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: name all seven Catholic sacraments from memory.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The seven sacraments (CCC 1210&ndash;1211) are: Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist, Penance/Reconciliation, Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders, and Matrimony. They are the primary channels of God&rsquo;s grace in Catholic life.</p>
</div>

    <p data-narration-id="n5">Orthodox Christianity shares the Catholic understanding of seven sacraments (though Orthodox may use the term &lsquo;mysteries&rsquo;). Most Protestant churches recognise only two sacraments: baptism and the Lord&rsquo;s Supper (Eucharist), on the grounds that only these two were explicitly commanded by Jesus with a physical element. Some Protestant traditions use the term &lsquo;ordinance&rsquo; instead of &lsquo;sacrament&rsquo;, emphasising that these are acts of obedience to Christ rather than channels of grace in themselves. Quakers and the Salvation Army practise no sacraments at all, arguing that all of life is sacred and no special rite is needed.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">The Mass: Source and Summit</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">The Mass (also called the <dfn class="term" data-def="The central sacrament of the Catholic Church in which bread and wine are consecrated and become the body and blood of Christ; the Mass makes present again the sacrifice of the cross.">Eucharist</dfn>) is the most important Catholic act of worship. The Second Vatican Council document <em>Lumen Gentium</em> paragraph 7 describes the Eucharist as &ldquo;the source and summit of the Christian life.&rdquo; &lsquo;Source&rsquo; means everything in Catholic spiritual life flows from the Mass; &lsquo;summit&rsquo; means everything is directed back towards it.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8">The Mass has two main parts. The <strong>Liturgy of the Word</strong> consists of readings from Scripture, a psalm, the Gospel, and a homily (sermon). The <strong>Liturgy of the Eucharist</strong> includes the Offertory (bringing bread and wine), the Eucharistic Prayer (the priest consecrates the bread and wine), and Holy Communion (the congregation receives). Catholics believe that at the consecration the bread and wine become truly Christ&rsquo;s body and blood &mdash; this is called <dfn class="term" data-def="The Catholic doctrine that at the consecration of the Mass the bread and wine are wholly changed into the body and blood of Christ, though the outward appearances remain.">transubstantiation</dfn>.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n9">The contrast examiners expect:</p>
    <ul>
      <li data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Catholic liturgical worship</strong> follows a fixed structure (the Order of Mass), uses set prayers, is led by an ordained priest, and centres on the Eucharist. <em>Lumen Gentium</em> 7 calls the Eucharist &lsquo;source and summit&rsquo; &mdash; nothing is more central.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Evangelical/Pentecostal worship</strong> is less-structured: spontaneous prayer, contemporary music, informal praise, emphasis on the Holy Spirit moving freely in the gathered congregation. There may be no set liturgy. The sermon (preaching the Word) often replaces the Eucharist as the centrepiece.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Protestant Reformed</strong> worship (Anglican, Methodist, Baptist) sits between these poles: some follow a liturgy (Book of Common Prayer in Anglicanism) while others are free-form. Only two sacraments are observed, and the Eucharist may be celebrated monthly rather than at every service.</li>
    </ul>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Catholic Prayer</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Prayer is described in Catholic tradition as &lsquo;the raising of hearts and minds to God.&rsquo; The primary prayer taught by Jesus is the <dfn class="term" data-def="The model prayer taught by Jesus to his disciples, recorded in Matthew 6:9&ndash;13, beginning &lsquo;Our Father, who art in heaven.&rsquo;">Lord&rsquo;s Prayer</dfn> (Matthew 6:5&ndash;14), which combines adoration, petition and forgiveness.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15">Catholics also practise <strong>set (formulaic) prayer</strong> &mdash; fixed words said at certain times (e.g. the Hail Mary, the Rosary, the Angelus) &mdash; and <strong>informal (extempore) prayer</strong>, spoken in the believer&rsquo;s own words.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Popular piety</strong> refers to devotional practices outside the official liturgy that are deeply embedded in Catholic culture. CCC 1674&ndash;1676 recognises and regulates popular piety. The three main forms are:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n17"><strong>The Rosary:</strong> A set form of meditative prayer using beads, combining the Lord&rsquo;s Prayer, the Hail Mary and the Glory Be while meditating on twenty &lsquo;mysteries&rsquo; (events in Christ&rsquo;s and Mary&rsquo;s lives). It is one of the most widely practised Catholic devotions.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n18"><strong>Eucharistic adoration:</strong> Prayer before the consecrated host (a large wafer) displayed in a <em>monstrance</em>. Catholics believe Christ is truly present in the consecrated host, so adoration is direct prayer before the Lord himself.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n19"><strong>Stations of the Cross:</strong> Fourteen pictorial stations placed around a church, representing moments of Christ&rsquo;s passion from condemnation to burial. The faithful move from station to station in prayer, entering imaginatively into Christ&rsquo;s suffering. Often used during Lent.</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n20" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: explain why Lumen Gentium paragraph 7 calls the Eucharist the &lsquo;source and summit&rsquo; of Catholic life &mdash; what does each word mean?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p><em>Lumen Gentium</em> paragraph 7 (Second Vatican Council) calls the Eucharist the &ldquo;source and summit of the Christian life.&rdquo; Source: all Catholic grace and identity flows from it. Summit: all Catholic life is ordered towards it. This is the single most important named source for the Mass in this paper.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n22">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n23">The seven sacraments (CCC 1210&ndash;1211) are the primary channels of grace in Catholic life: Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist, Penance, Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders, Matrimony.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n24">The Mass is the &lsquo;source and summit&rsquo; of Catholic life (<em>Lumen Gentium</em> 7), centring on the Eucharist in which Catholics believe bread and wine become Christ&rsquo;s body and blood (transubstantiation).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">Catholic prayer includes the Lord&rsquo;s Prayer (Matthew 6:5&ndash;14), formulaic and extempore prayer, and popular piety &mdash; the Rosary, Eucharistic adoration and Stations of the Cross (CCC 1674&ndash;1676).</li>
</ul>
