<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Your Site Question for 2028</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Kenilworth Castle is the named historic environment for your 2028 exam, which guarantees Paper 2 a 16-mark question testing a statement against the site and your contextual knowledge. The best way in is through one event: Elizabeth&rsquo;s visit of <strong>9 to 27 July 1575</strong> &mdash; nineteen days, the longest she ever stayed at a courtier&rsquo;s house, and the most extravagant entertainment of the reign. This lesson follows those days, then steps back to what Robert Dudley, Earl of Leicester, had built for them and what the whole performance was for. It connects directly to your work on the court (Lesson 3), the marriage question (Lesson 4) and Elizabeth&rsquo;s image.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall: the dates, the length and the host of the great Kenilworth visit.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact &mdash; The Visit</div>
  <p>Elizabeth stayed at Kenilworth from <strong>9 to 27 July 1575</strong> &mdash; nineteen days as the guest of Robert Dudley, Earl of Leicester, her favourite since the start of the reign and the owner of the castle by her own gift of 1563.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">The Welcome: A Castle Out of Legend</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Leicester designed the arrival to dissolve the border between his castle and the world of romance. As the queen approached across the great <dfn class="term" data-def="Kenilworth's vast artificial lake — a medieval defence turned Elizabethan stage for water pageants.">mere</dfn> at dusk, a floating &lsquo;Lady of the Lake&rsquo; glided out to greet her, welcoming her to a fortress of Arthurian legend; guns and fireworks answered from the walls. The message of the staging was itself an argument: this castle, this host and this magnificence all existed for Elizabeth &mdash; and had been waiting, like the Lady, for her alone.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Nineteen Days of Spectacle</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">The programme filled almost three weeks. There was hunting in the chase by day and masques and dancing by night; bear-baiting; an Italian acrobat; country sports and a rustic wedding staged for the court&rsquo;s amusement; and fireworks so vast that report claimed they were heard twenty miles off. The set-piece was the water pageant: a performer as <dfn class="term" data-def="A legendary Greek musician saved from the sea by a dolphin — impersonated on Kenilworth's mere in a pageant for Elizabeth in 1575.">Arion</dfn>, riding a mechanical dolphin on the mere, serenading the queen with music floating across the water. Gossip put Leicester&rsquo;s costs at a thousand pounds a day &mdash; loose talk, no doubt, but the truth was ruinous enough: no other subject in the reign attempted anything on this scale.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner"><p>Two eyewitnesses wrote it down: a long letter attributed to Robert Langham, a minor official of the household, all gossip and wonder; and the poet George Gascoigne, who helped devise the entertainments and published <em>The Princely Pleasures at Kenilworth</em>. Treat them as you would any source in this section &mdash; both men had reasons to magnify Leicester&rsquo;s magnificence, which is itself evidence of what the show was for. The Langham letter is so detailed that English Heritage used it to rebuild the Elizabethan garden in 2009.</p></div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n8">What Leicester Had Built for Her</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9">The nineteen days rested on a decade of building. For the queen&rsquo;s lodging Leicester raised a four-storey tower of state apartments &mdash; <strong>Leicester&rsquo;s Building</strong> &mdash; beside the medieval great hall. He drove great windows through the Norman keep so that ancient strength read as modern splendour, built a fashionable gatehouse for arrivals, and laid out a private garden below the walls with terraces, obelisks, a fountain and an aviary &mdash; created for her eyes. None of it was military. Kenilworth&rsquo;s real medieval defences, above all the mere that had once made the castle nearly untakeable, were repurposed as scenery. A fortress had been converted, floor by floor, into an instrument of persuasion.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n10" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall: the four main things Leicester built or remade for Elizabeth's visits.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact &mdash; Leicester&rsquo;s Works</div>
  <p>Leicester&rsquo;s Building (state apartments for the queen), the new gatehouse, great windows cut into the Norman keep, and the privy garden &mdash; rebuilt by English Heritage in 2009 from the Langham letter&rsquo;s description.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n11">What Was He Asking For?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Historians read 1575 as the climax of the longest courtship in English politics. Leicester had been spoken of as a husband for Elizabeth since the 1560s (Lesson 4); now he was in his forties and, in the eyes of the court, the window was closing. The entertainments pressed the theme in allegory &mdash; ladies delivered from lakes, masques of marriage &mdash; and one further entertainment on the theme was prepared but never performed, which many historians read as the queen declining to be lectured. She took the pleasure, admired the garden, and left on 27 July exactly as she had arrived: unmarried, unpromised, and in sole possession of the question. Leicester never asked on this scale again.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Building Your 16-Mark Answer</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Whatever the statement &mdash; about courtship, display, favour or royal control &mdash; argue through feature-and-consequence chains. Leicester&rsquo;s Building &rarr; what hosting the queen demanded of a courtier; the gift of 1563 &rarr; how Elizabeth bound men with favour; the pageants and their allegories &rarr; the marriage question performed; the unperformed entertainment &rarr; her control of the script; the cost &rarr; loyalty priced in fortunes; the printed accounts &rarr; propaganda reaching the whole realm. Then weigh with context from Lessons 3 and 4 and reach a judgement. Site feature, purpose, consequence &mdash; that chain, every paragraph.</p>

<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n17">The 2028 site question centres on Elizabeth's nineteen-day stay at Kenilworth, 9-27 July 1575 — the longest and most lavish courtier visit of the reign.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n18">Leicester rebuilt the castle as an instrument of persuasion: state apartments, gatehouse, great windows in the keep and a queen's garden, with the medieval mere turned into a stage.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n19">The entertainments argued in allegory for the marriage Leicester had pursued for a decade; Elizabeth enjoyed the show, silenced one entertainment, and left with the question still hers alone.</li>

</ul>
