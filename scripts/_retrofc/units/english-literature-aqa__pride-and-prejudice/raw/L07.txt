<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Five Major Themes</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Every essay on <em>Pride and Prejudice</em> connects to key themes. The five most tested are: <strong>pride and prejudice (self-knowledge)</strong>, <strong>marriage and money</strong>, <strong>class and social hierarchy</strong>, <strong>appearance versus reality</strong>, and <strong>women and independence</strong>.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: list the five themes and one quotation for each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p><strong>Pride/Prejudice:</strong> “How despicably I have acted!” (Elizabeth’s self-recognition). <strong>Marriage:</strong> “It is a truth universally acknowledged…” (ironic opening on the marriage market). <strong>Class:</strong> “He is a gentleman; I am a gentleman’s daughter; so far we are equal” (Elizabeth defying Lady Catherine). <strong>Appearance vs Reality:</strong> “She is tolerable, but not handsome enough to tempt me” (Darcy’s misleading first impression). <strong>Women:</strong> “I am only resolved to act in that manner which will constitute my happiness” (Elizabeth’s declaration of independence).</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Exam Approach: Extract + Whole Novel</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15">The nineteenth-century novel question typically gives you a printed extract (around 400 words) and asks you to write about both the extract and the novel as a whole. Plan around <strong>50 minutes</strong> for this response and check your exam board’s specimen paper for the exact mark weightings that apply to you.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n16" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: list what each AO rewards and give one example sentence that hits it.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p><strong>AO1:</strong> a sustained critical response supported by references from the extract AND the whole novel. <strong>AO2:</strong> analyse Austen’s methods — irony, free indirect discourse, dialogue, structure. <strong>AO3:</strong> embed context (Regency marriage, class, entailment, women’s roles). Never bolt context on as a separate paragraph.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Essay Structure and Method</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Use <dfn class="term" data-def="Point, Evidence, Analyse Language, Link to Context, Evaluate — a paragraph structure for literature essays.">PEALCE</dfn>. Balance extract (2–3 paragraphs) with whole-novel (2–3 paragraphs). The biggest mark differentiator is close analysis of Austen’s methods — analyse them, don’t just identify them. Austen’s key method is <dfn class="term" data-def="Austen’s signature method of using wit and humour to expose social failings while maintaining narrative elegance.">irony</dfn>: verbal irony (the opening line), dramatic irony (the reader sees Darcy’s admiration before Elizabeth), and structural irony (the title applies to both protagonists).</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Example Paragraph: The Opening Line</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n19"><strong>Point:</strong> Austen uses the opening line to satirise the Regency obsession with marriage and wealth. <strong>Evidence:</strong> “It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife.” <strong>Analysis:</strong> The phrase “universally acknowledged” claims objectivity, but the “truth” is actually the perspective of families desperate to marry their daughters. The passive voice (“it is… acknowledged”) makes the obsession sound like natural law rather than social pressure. The word “must” implies obligation, as if wealthy men have no choice. <strong>Context:</strong> In Regency England, women could not inherit property or pursue most careers; marriage was their primary route to financial security. Austen’s irony exposes a system that reduces both men and women to financial instruments. <strong>Evaluate:</strong> The sentence establishes the ironic tone that defines the entire novel: comedy on the surface, serious moral critique beneath.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n21">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n22">Prepare quotations for five themes: pride/prejudice, marriage/money, class, appearance/reality, women/independence. 2–3 per theme from different parts of the novel.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n23">The 30-mark question needs balanced extract + whole-novel analysis. Embed context; never bolt it on.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n24">Use PEALCE. Focus on AO2: analyse Austen’s irony, free indirect discourse, and dialogue — these are her signature methods.</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n20">The highest marks go to responses that analyse Austen’s irony at word level. Instead of “Austen uses irony,” write “Austen’s verbal irony in ‘universally acknowledged’ presents a social obsession as objective truth, satirising a marriage market that reduces both sexes to financial commodities.” Always explain the gap between what is said and what is meant.</p>
