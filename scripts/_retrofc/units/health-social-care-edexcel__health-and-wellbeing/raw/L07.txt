--- DESCRIPTION ---
How person-centred care considers needs, wishes and circumstances to benefit everyone.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Is the Person-Centred Approach?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The <dfn class="term" data-def="An approach to care that focuses on the individual's specific needs, wishes and circumstances rather than a one-size-fits-all model.">person-centred approach</dfn> is a way of planning and delivering care that puts the individual at the heart of every decision. Instead of offering the same advice to everyone, health and social care professionals tailor their recommendations so they are realistic and meaningful for each person.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">This approach links directly to the skills, attributes and values you studied in Component 2. It turns those values — such as empathy, communication and respect — into everyday practice.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Without looking, name the three pillars of person-centred care and explain what each involves.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>A person-centred approach takes into account three things: the individual’s <strong>needs</strong> (to reduce health risks), their <strong>wishes</strong> (preferences and choices) and their <strong>circumstances</strong> (age, ability, location, living conditions, support available, and physical and emotional health).</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Taking Account of Needs, Wishes & Circumstances</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Needs</strong> are the health risks that must be addressed. For example, a person with high blood pressure needs recommendations that will lower it, such as reducing salt intake or increasing physical activity. The professional identifies the clinical priorities.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Wishes</strong> are the individual’s own preferences. A person who hates running should not be told to jog every day — they might prefer swimming or cycling. Ignoring wishes makes it far less likely that someone will follow the plan.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Circumstances</strong> cover everything about the individual’s situation. A single parent working shifts has less free time than a retired person. Someone living in a rural area may not have a gym nearby. A teenager with anxiety may need a different style of support from an older adult. Circumstances include age, ability, location, living conditions, existing support networks, and current physical and emotional health.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n9">Jamil is 52 and has been diagnosed with type 2 diabetes. He also has mobility problems due to arthritis in his knees. A non-person-centred approach might simply say “exercise more and eat less sugar.” A person-centred approach would consider his <strong>needs</strong> (managing blood glucose and weight), his <strong>wishes</strong> (he enjoys cooking and wants to stay independent), and his <strong>circumstances</strong> (limited mobility, lives alone, moderate income). The recommendation might include chair-based exercises, a referral to a dietitian who can suggest low-sugar recipes he can cook himself, and a local diabetes support group for social contact.</p>

</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Importance for Individuals</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">When care is person-centred, individuals benefit in several important ways. These benefits are a common exam focus, so make sure you can explain each one with an example.</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Comfort</strong> — individuals feel more comfortable with recommendations, advice and treatment because these have been designed around them, not imposed on them.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Confidence</strong> — they have greater confidence that the plan will work because it reflects their real situation.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Unique needs met</strong> — every person is different. Person-centred care ensures personal and cultural needs are respected.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Independence</strong> — by involving the individual in decisions, the approach promotes <dfn class="term" data-def="The ability of an individual to manage their own life, make their own choices and carry out daily activities without relying heavily on others.">independence</dfn> rather than dependence on professionals.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Motivation</strong> — individuals are more motivated to follow recommendations because they helped create them. They feel ownership of the plan.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Happiness</strong> — they feel happier and more positive about their health and wellbeing overall.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n18"><strong>Support for vulnerable individuals</strong> — more vulnerable people receive increased, tailored support that addresses their specific risks.</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n19" data-revision-tip="From memory, explain two benefits of person-centred care and how each improves outcomes.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Individuals who receive person-centred care are more likely to follow recommendations and take actions to improve their health, because the plan feels achievable and relevant to their life.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n20">Benefits for Workers & Services</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n21">The person-centred approach does not only help the individual — it also benefits health and social care workers and the services they work for.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Benefits for Workers and Services in Detail</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Job satisfaction</strong> — workers feel more fulfilled when they see individuals engaging with care plans and making real progress. Knowing that their work makes a genuine difference improves morale and reduces staff turnover.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Saves time</strong> — when recommendations are realistic and the individual is motivated, they are less likely to miss appointments or need repeated consultations for the same issue. This frees up time for other service users.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Saves money</strong> — fewer missed appointments and fewer repeat visits mean lower costs for the service. Prevention and early <dfn class="term" data-def="Action taken to deal with a problem or situation before it becomes more serious, reducing the need for more intensive treatment later.">intervention</dfn> also reduce the need for expensive emergency care later.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n25"><strong>Reduces complaints</strong> — individuals who feel heard and respected are far less likely to complain. This protects the reputation of the service and reduces the time staff spend dealing with formal complaint procedures.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n26">Linking Person-Centred Care to the 6 Cs</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n27">The person-centred approach is closely connected to the 6 Cs from Component 2. <strong>Care</strong> and <strong>compassion</strong> ensure the individual is treated with empathy and dignity. <strong>Communication</strong> means listening to the individual’s wishes. <strong>Competence</strong> ensures the professional has the knowledge to make appropriate recommendations. <strong>Courage</strong> means speaking up if a plan is not working. <strong>Commitment</strong> drives continuous improvement in the care experience.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n28">Without person-centred care, individuals may feel that their views do not matter. This can lead to disengagement, missed appointments and worsening health outcomes. By contrast, when the approach is applied well, it creates a partnership between the professional and the individual that produces better results for everyone involved.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n30">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n31">The person-centred approach considers the individual’s needs, wishes and circumstances to create tailored care plans.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n32">It benefits individuals through increased comfort, confidence, independence and motivation to follow recommendations.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n33">It benefits workers and services by improving job satisfaction, saving time and money, and reducing complaints.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n29">In the Component 3 exam, you will often be given a case study and asked to explain why a person-centred approach is important for that individual. Always refer to the individual’s specific needs, wishes and circumstances — generic answers that could apply to anyone will not reach the higher mark bands. Use the case study details to show how person-centred care leads to specific benefits such as increased motivation or greater independence.</p>
