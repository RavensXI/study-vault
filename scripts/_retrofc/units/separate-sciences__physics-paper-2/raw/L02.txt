--- DESCRIPTION ---
Calculating work done, investigating springs with Hooke's law, and elastic potential energy.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Work Done</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">When a force causes an object to move, energy is transferred. The amount of energy transferred is called the <dfn class="term" data-def="The energy transferred when a force moves an object through a distance, measured in joules.">work done</dfn>. Work done is calculated using:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">$$W = F s$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n4">Where \(W\) is work done in joules (J), \(F\) is force in newtons (N), and \(s\) is the distance moved along the line of action of the force in metres (m).</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall: how many newtons and metres define one joule, plus write the equation linking work done to energy transferred."><div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>One joule of work is done when a force of one newton moves an object one metre in the direction of the force. Work done equals energy transferred: \(W = E\).</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n6">If a force acts at right angles to the direction of motion, no work is done. For example, gravity does no work on a satellite in a circular orbit because the force is perpendicular to the direction of travel.</p>

<span>Worked Example: Work Done Against Friction</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n7">A box is pushed 8 m across a floor against a friction force of 25 N. Calculate the work done against friction.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8">\(W = Fs = 25 \times 8 = 200\) J</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9">This 200 J is transferred to the thermal energy store of the box and floor — they warm up slightly.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Hooke’s Law</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><dfn class="term" data-def="A law stating that the extension of a spring is directly proportional to the force applied, provided the limit of proportionality is not exceeded.">Hooke’s law</dfn> states that the extension of an elastic object is directly proportional to the force applied, as long as the <dfn class="term" data-def="The maximum force at which a spring still obeys Hooke's law; beyond this, the relationship is no longer linear.">limit of proportionality</dfn> is not exceeded.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12">$$F = k e$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n13">Where \(F\) is force in newtons, \(k\) is the <dfn class="term" data-def="A measure of how stiff a spring is, measured in N/m. A higher value means the spring is harder to stretch.">spring constant</dfn> in N/m, and \(e\) is the extension in metres.</p>



<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall: what shape is a force-extension graph before the limit of proportionality, and what happens to the spring beyond this po..."><div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>A force-extension graph for a spring is a straight line through the origin up to the limit of proportionality. Beyond this point, the graph curves and the spring no longer returns to its original length when the force is removed — it has been permanently deformed.</p>
</div>

<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n15">Hang a spring from a clamp stand. Measure the original (natural) length. Add masses one at a time and record the new length each time. Calculate the extension by subtracting the original length from the stretched length.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n16">Plot a force-extension graph. The gradient of the straight-line section gives the spring constant \(k\). Use a ruler with a pointer (set square) to reduce parallax error when measuring length.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<div class="higher-only">
<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Elastic Potential Energy</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">When a spring is stretched or compressed within its limit of proportionality, it stores <dfn class="term" data-def="Energy stored in a stretched or compressed elastic object that has not exceeded its limit of proportionality.">elastic potential energy</dfn>. This can be calculated using:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n19">$$E_e = \frac{1}{2} k e^2$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Where \(E_e\) is elastic potential energy in joules, \(k\) is the spring constant in N/m, and \(e\) is the extension in metres.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Worked Example: Elastic Potential Energy</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n21">A spring with a spring constant of 40 N/m is stretched by 0.15 m. Calculate the elastic potential energy stored.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n22">\(E_e = \frac{1}{2} k e^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 40 \times 0.15^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 40 \times 0.0225 = 0.45\) J</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n23">An object that returns to its original shape after the force is removed is called <dfn class="term" data-def="A material that returns to its original shape and size when the deforming force is removed.">elastic</dfn>. An object that is permanently deformed is said to have undergone <dfn class="term" data-def="A permanent change in shape that occurs when a material is stretched beyond its elastic limit.">inelastic deformation</dfn>.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n25">Key Takeaways</h3><ul><li data-narration-id="n26">Work done = force \(\times\) distance (\(W = Fs\)); work done equals energy transferred</li><li data-narration-id="n27">Hooke’s law: \(F = ke\); applies up to the limit of proportionality; force-extension graph is a straight line through the origin</li><li data-narration-id="n28">Elastic potential energy: \(E_e = \frac{1}{2}ke^2\); stored only while the object obeys Hooke’s law</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n24">In Hooke’s law questions, always check units: extension must be in metres (not cm) and force in newtons. When reading a force-extension graph, the limit of proportionality is where the straight line starts to curve. The gradient of the straight section gives the spring constant \(k\).</p>
