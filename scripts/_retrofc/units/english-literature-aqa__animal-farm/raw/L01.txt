--- DESCRIPTION ---
Why Orwell wrote Animal Farm and how it mirrors the Russian Revolution and Stalin's rise.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Why Did Orwell Write Animal Farm?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">George Orwell published <em>Animal Farm</em> in 1945, at a time when the Soviet Union was Britain’s wartime ally. Despite this, Orwell was deeply critical of <dfn class="term" data-def="A political system where the state controls all property and each person contributes according to their ability.">communism</dfn> as practised under Joseph Stalin. He had witnessed authoritarian behaviour first-hand while fighting in the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939), where Soviet-backed communists betrayed and purged their own allies.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall Orwell's exact quote about fusing two specific purposes together in Animal Farm.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Orwell described <em>Animal Farm</em> as the first book in which he tried “to fuse political purpose and artistic purpose into one whole.”</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n4">Orwell was a <dfn class="term" data-def="A person who believes in social equality and democratic control of industry, but opposes authoritarian rule.">democratic socialist</dfn> — he supported equality but opposed dictatorships of any kind. He saw how Stalin had corrupted the original ideals of the 1917 Russian Revolution, turning a movement for workers’ liberation into a brutal regime. <em>Animal Farm</em> was his way of warning readers that revolutions can be hijacked by the very people they were meant to overthrow.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">What Is an Allegory?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">An <dfn class="term" data-def="A story in which characters and events represent real people and historical events to convey a deeper meaning.">allegory</dfn> is a story that works on two levels: the surface narrative (animals running a farm) and a hidden meaning (the Russian Revolution and its aftermath). Every major character and event in <em>Animal Farm</em> maps onto a real historical figure or moment.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Old Major</strong> = Karl Marx / Vladimir Lenin — the visionary who inspires revolution but dies before it is corrupted.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Napoleon</strong> = Joseph Stalin — the cunning leader who seizes power through force, propaganda, and fear.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Snowball</strong> = Leon Trotsky — the intellectual rival who is expelled and then blamed for everything.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Squealer</strong> = Soviet propaganda machine (Pravda newspaper) — manipulates language to justify the pigs’ actions.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Boxer</strong> = The loyal working class — exploited until no longer useful, then discarded.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Mr Jones</strong> = Tsar Nicholas II — the incompetent ruler overthrown by revolution.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n13">The Russian Revolution — Essential Context</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">In 1917, the Russian people overthrew Tsar Nicholas II, who had ruled as an <dfn class="term" data-def="A ruler with absolute, unchecked power over their country.">autocrat</dfn> while millions starved. The Bolsheviks, led by Lenin, promised “Peace, Land, Bread” — equality for all workers. After Lenin’s death in 1924, a power struggle erupted between Stalin and Trotsky. Stalin won, exiled Trotsky, and established a totalitarian dictatorship.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall how Stalin's methods killed millions and the complete corrupted commandment about animal equality.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Stalin’s regime killed millions through forced collectivisation, purges, and famine. Orwell wrote <em>Animal Farm</em> to expose how “All animals are equal” becomes “but some animals are more equal than others.”</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Orwell’s Purpose — What Is the Message?</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n16">Orwell had several interlinked purposes:</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n17"><strong>1. To warn against totalitarianism:</strong> Power corrupts, and absolute power corrupts absolutely. The pigs begin as liberators and end as tyrants indistinguishable from humans.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n18"><strong>2. To show how propaganda enables dictatorship:</strong> Squealer’s manipulation of language — rewriting the commandments, distorting the truth — shows that controlling language means controlling thought.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n19"><strong>3. To criticise the complacency of the masses:</strong> The animals’ failure to question authority allows the pigs to seize power unchecked. Orwell suggests ordinary people must stay vigilant.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>4. To satirise the Soviet Union specifically:</strong> The book is a direct attack on Stalinist Russia, showing how revolutionary ideals were betrayed step by step.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n21">The Genre: Fable and Satire</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22"><em>Animal Farm</em> is both a <dfn class="term" data-def="A short story, often with animal characters, that teaches a moral lesson.">fable</dfn> and a <dfn class="term" data-def="A text that uses humour, irony, or exaggeration to criticise and expose human failings, especially in politics.">satire</dfn>. As a fable, it uses animals to deliver a moral message. As a satire, it uses irony and dark humour to expose the absurdity and cruelty of totalitarian rule. The simple, fairy-tale style makes the political message accessible — Orwell called it “a fairy story” in its subtitle, which is itself ironic given the violence and betrayal within.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n24">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">Orwell wrote <em>Animal Farm</em> to expose how Stalin betrayed the ideals of the Russian Revolution and to warn against totalitarianism.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n26">The novella is an allegory — every character and event maps onto a real historical figure or moment.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n27">Understanding Orwell’s context and purpose is essential for achieving top marks in the exam.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n23">In the AQA exam, you will answer on <em>Animal Farm</em> in Section B of Paper 2 (Modern Texts). You will be given an extract and asked to write about the extract and the whole novel. Always link Orwell’s methods to his <strong>purpose</strong> — examiners reward answers that explain <em>why</em> Orwell made particular choices, not just what happens. Use the phrase “Orwell uses [technique] to [effect] in order to [purpose]” as a structural frame.</p>
