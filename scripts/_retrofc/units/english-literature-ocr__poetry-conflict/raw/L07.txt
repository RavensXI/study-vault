--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Comparison Skills for Conflict Poetry</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The OCR poetry comparison names one poem and asks you to compare it with one you choose. For the Conflict collection, you need a poem that shares a theme but differs in attitude, method, or era. This lesson teaches the practical skills of building a sustained comparison across 4–5 paragraphs, hitting AO1 (response), AO2 (methods), and AO3 (context) in each.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: What five elements must every comparison paragraph contain?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Every comparison paragraph needs: <strong>1)</strong> A topic sentence stating the comparison point, <strong>2)</strong> Analysis of Poem A with quotation and method, <strong>3)</strong> A comparative connective, <strong>4)</strong> Analysis of Poem B with quotation and method, <strong>5)</strong> An embedded context link explaining <em>why</em> the poets differ.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Choosing Your Comparison Poem</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Strong conflict comparisons involve poems that share a subject but take different positions. If the named poem is about soldiers, choose another soldier poem for close comparison or a civilian-impact poem for contrast. If it is about social conflict, choose another social poem or a war poem to show how “conflict” has multiple meanings.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Model Comparison Paragraph</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Here is a model paragraph using the five-step structure:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12"><em>“Both Tennyson and Hughes present soldiers mid-charge, but their attitudes to duty differ sharply. Tennyson’s soldiers embody collective obedience — ‘Theirs but to do and die’ reduces their role to two actions, presenting sacrifice as noble. In contrast, Hughes’s soldier abandons patriotic ideals: ‘King, honour, human dignity, etcetera / Dropped like luxuries.’ The dismissive ‘etcetera’ reduces Tennyson’s sacred values to an afterthought. This shift reflects a century of disillusionment — after two World Wars, glorious sacrifice could no longer be sustained.”</em></p>
<p data-narration-id="n13">Notice: topic sentence (both present soldiers, but differ), Poem A analysis with quotation, comparative connective (“In contrast”), Poem B analysis with quotation, and context explaining the shift.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: write a model comparison paragraph from memory using the five-step structure.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Use varied connectives: <strong>similarities</strong> (“Similarly,” “Both poets suggest”), <strong>differences</strong> (“However,” “In contrast,” “Whereas”), <strong>nuance</strong> (“While both explore…, they differ in”). Avoid using the same connective twice.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Common Comparison Mistakes</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Two separate analyses:</strong> Writing about Poem A for three paragraphs, then Poem B for three, is NOT comparison. Discuss both poems in every paragraph.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Only similarities:</strong> The best comparisons explore both similarities AND differences.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Describing not analysing:</strong> Retelling events is not comparison. Focus on <em>how</em> poets present ideas (AO2) and <em>why</em> (AO3).</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n19">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n20">Choose a comparison poem that shares a theme but differs in attitude, method, or era.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n21">Every paragraph must contain analysis of both poems, linked by comparative connectives, with embedded context.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n22">Avoid writing two separate analyses, finding only similarities, or describing without analysing.</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n18">In the conflict comparison, examiners look for <strong>sustained comparison throughout</strong>. Never let more than two sentences pass without referencing the other poem. If you find yourself writing only about one poem, stop and add a comparative link.</p>
