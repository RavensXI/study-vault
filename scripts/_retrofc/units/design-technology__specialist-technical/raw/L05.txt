--- DESCRIPTION ---
Standard forms, sizes and components for timber, metals, polymers, textiles and papers.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Why Stock Forms Matter</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Materials are supplied in standard <dfn class="term" data-def="The shapes and sizes in which raw materials are commercially available, such as sheets, rods, tubes and planks.">stock forms</dfn>. Knowing what is available helps a designer calculate how much material to order, minimise waste, and ensure the product can be manufactured efficiently. You need to know the common stock forms, standard sizes and standard components for each material category.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Papers & Boards</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">Paper and board are sold in <strong>sheets</strong> and <strong>rolls</strong>. Standard sheet sizes follow the A-series (A0, A1, A2, A3, A4, A5). Board is measured by <strong>thickness</strong> (microns or mm), <strong>weight</strong> (gsm — grams per square metre), and colour. Corrugated card uses a ply rating (single, double, triple wall) to indicate strength. Standard components include <strong>fasteners</strong> (staples, paper clips), <strong>seals</strong> (adhesive strips) and <strong>bindings</strong> (spiral, saddle stitch).</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Cover this and list the stock forms for papers/boards, timber and metals from memory.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Every material category has its own stock forms, standard sizes and standard components. You must be able to name examples from at least one category in the exam.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Timber-Based Materials</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Timber is sold as <strong>planks</strong>, <strong>boards</strong> and <strong>standard mouldings</strong> (skirting, architrave, dowel). Sizes are specified by <strong>length</strong>, <strong>width</strong>, <strong>thickness</strong> and <strong>diameter</strong> (for dowel). Manufactured boards (MDF, plywood, chipboard) come in standard sheet sizes, typically 2440 × 1220 mm. Standard components include <dfn class="term" data-def="Pre-made fittings used to join parts of a product, such as screws, hinges and knock-down fittings.">standard components</dfn> such as woodscrews, hinges and <dfn class="term" data-def="Fittings designed to be assembled and disassembled by the end user, commonly used in flat-pack furniture.">KD (knock-down) fittings</dfn> used in flat-pack furniture (e.g. cam locks, barrel nuts).</p>



<h2 data-narration-id="n8">Metal-Based Materials</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9">Metals are available as <strong>sheet</strong>, <strong>rod</strong>, <strong>bar</strong> and <strong>tube</strong>. They are sold by <strong>length</strong>, <strong>width</strong>, <strong>thickness</strong> and <strong>diameter</strong>. Standard components include <strong>rivets</strong>, <strong>machine screws</strong>, <strong>nuts</strong> and <strong>bolts</strong>. Metals are also available in standard profiles such as angle iron, channel and I-beam.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Polymers</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Polymers come in many forms: <strong>sheet</strong> (acrylic, HIPS), <strong>rod</strong>, <strong>powder</strong> and <strong>granules</strong> (for injection moulding), <strong>foam</strong> (expanded polystyrene) and <strong>film</strong> (polythene bags). They are sold by <strong>length</strong>, <strong>width</strong>, <strong>gauge</strong> (thickness of film) and <strong>diameter</strong>. Standard components mirror those for metals — plastic screws, nuts, bolts and hinges are available for non-metal assemblies.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n12">Textile-Based Materials</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">Textiles are sold as <strong>yarns</strong> (by weight and ply) and <strong>fabrics</strong> (by the roll or metre). Fabric is measured by <strong>roll size</strong>, <strong>width</strong> (typically 90 cm, 115 cm or 150 cm) and <strong>weight</strong> (gsm). Standard components include <strong>zips</strong>, <strong>press studs</strong> (poppers), <strong>Velcro</strong>, buttons and elastic.</p>

Standard components include the <strong>E12 resistor series</strong> (12 preferred values per decade), <strong>dual in-line IC packages</strong> (DIL), and <strong>microcontrollers</strong> (PIC). These are ordered from specialist suppliers in standard packages.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n15">Calculating Material Quantities</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n16">Designers must calculate how much material they need. This involves working out <dfn class="term" data-def="The area of all external surfaces of a three-dimensional object.">surface area</dfn> (for sheet materials and coatings) and <dfn class="term" data-def="The amount of three-dimensional space a material or object occupies.">volume</dfn> (for solid components). Efficient material use means considering <dfn class="term" data-def="Arranging pattern pieces as closely together as possible on a sheet of material to minimise waste.">nesting</dfn> — arranging pattern pieces closely together on a sheet to minimise offcuts. This applies to timber sheets, metal plates, fabric layouts and card nets.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: explain what nesting is and why it reduces waste.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p><strong>Nesting</strong> and efficient pattern spacing reduce material waste and save money, especially in batch and mass production where even small savings per unit add up across thousands of items.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Worked Example: Calculating Sheet Material</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n18">A product requires six identical panels, each 300 mm × 200 mm. Standard MDF sheet size is 2440 mm × 1220 mm. Fitting 300 mm panels along the 2440 mm length gives 8 columns (8 × 300 = 2400 mm). Fitting 200 mm panels along the 1220 mm width gives 6 rows (6 × 200 = 1200 mm). That is 48 panels per sheet — so one standard sheet is more than enough for six panels, with material left over for other projects. Good nesting reduces waste to just 40 mm × 1220 mm + 2440 mm × 20 mm of offcuts.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n20">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Stock forms vary by category:</strong> sheets, planks, rods, tubes, yarns, granules, films — know which forms apply to which material.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Standard components</strong> (screws, hinges, rivets, zips, KD fittings) are pre-made items used across all categories.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Efficient material use:</strong> calculate surface area or volume, use nesting to minimise waste, and buy standard sizes to reduce cost.</li></ul>
