--- DESCRIPTION ---
The environmental and social impact of designing, manufacturing and disposing of products.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Is an Ecological Footprint?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Every product leaves a mark on the planet. From the moment raw materials are extracted to the point a product is thrown away, resources are consumed and pollution is created. An <dfn class="term" data-def="The total environmental impact of a product throughout its entire life, from raw material extraction to disposal.">ecological footprint</dfn> measures this impact. In AQA Design & Technology, you must understand the ecological and social issues that designers face — and how they can reduce harm.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall the four ecological issues linked to raw material extraction.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Key ecological issues include: <strong>deforestation</strong> (timber), <strong>mining</strong> (metals and minerals), <strong>drilling</strong> (crude oil for polymers), and <strong>farming</strong> (cotton and other natural fibres). Each process damages habitats, consumes energy and produces waste.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Product Miles & Carbon Footprint</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">The distance a product travels during its life — from raw material source, through manufacture and distribution, to the consumer and finally to disposal — is sometimes called <dfn class="term" data-def="The total distance a product and its materials travel from source to disposal, contributing to carbon emissions.">product miles</dfn>. Every stage of transportation burns fuel and releases carbon dioxide. A cotton T-shirt might involve cotton grown in India, spun into yarn in Bangladesh, dyed in China, assembled in Vietnam, and sold in the UK — racking up thousands of miles before a customer puts it on.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Carbon is also produced during manufacture itself. Smelting aluminium, firing ceramics, and injection moulding polymers all require significant energy. Designers can reduce carbon output by choosing local suppliers, using renewable energy in manufacture, and selecting materials with lower <dfn class="term" data-def="The total amount of greenhouse gases produced throughout a product's life, measured in CO2 equivalent.">carbon footprint</dfn>.</p>



<h2 data-narration-id="n7">The Six Rs</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n8">The six Rs provide a framework for sustainable design. They appear frequently in the AQA exam and you should be able to explain each one with examples.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n9" data-revision-tip="Close this and write down all six Rs with a product example for each.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The six Rs are: <strong>Reduce</strong>, <strong>Refuse</strong>, <strong>Re-use</strong>, <strong>Repair</strong>, <strong>Recycle</strong>, and <strong>Rethink</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>The Six Rs in Detail</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Reduce:</strong> Use fewer materials or less energy. Lightweighting packaging (thinner plastic bottles, smaller boxes) reduces waste and transport costs.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Refuse:</strong> Reject materials or processes that cause unnecessary harm. A manufacturer might refuse to use single-use plastic and switch to compostable alternatives.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Re-use:</strong> Design products for multiple uses. Glass milk bottles, refillable water bottles and modular furniture can all be used again rather than discarded.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Repair:</strong> Design products so they can be fixed rather than replaced. Fairphone makes smartphones with replaceable modules so users can swap a broken screen instead of buying a new phone.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Recycle:</strong> Use materials that can be reprocessed into new products. Aluminium cans, PET bottles and cardboard are widely recycled. Designers should mark products with recycling codes and use mono-materials where possible to simplify recycling.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Rethink:</strong> Question whether the product is needed at all, or whether a radically different approach would work. Digital tickets replace printed card tickets; e-books replace printed textbooks.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Social Issues in Design & Manufacture</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">A product’s <dfn class="term" data-def="The impact that the production, use and disposal of a product has on people and communities.">social footprint</dfn> covers how it affects people. Designers must consider safe working conditions in factories, fair wages for workers, and the impact of pollution on local communities. The 2013 Rana Plaza factory collapse in Bangladesh killed over 1,100 garment workers and exposed dangerous conditions in the global textile supply chain.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Reducing Social & Environmental Harm</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n18"><strong>Safe working conditions:</strong> Manufacturers should comply with health and safety regulations, provide protective equipment, and avoid excessive working hours.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n19"><strong>Reducing pollution:</strong> Factories should treat wastewater before discharge, control air emissions, and manage chemical waste responsibly. Textile dyeing, for example, can pollute rivers if effluent is untreated.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Ethical sourcing:</strong> Using Fairtrade materials, FSC-certified timber, or conflict-free minerals ensures that raw material supply chains do not exploit workers or communities.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Designing for inclusivity:</strong> Products should be accessible to users of different ages, abilities and cultural backgrounds. Reducing negative social impact also means avoiding designs that promote harmful stereotypes.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n22">Choosing Appropriate Energy Sources</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n23">Switching from fossil fuels to renewable energy (solar, wind, hydroelectric) during manufacture reduces a product’s ecological footprint. Some companies offset their carbon emissions by investing in tree planting or renewable energy projects. However, the most effective strategy is to reduce energy use in the first place — through efficient machinery, shorter supply chains, and lightweight product design.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n25">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n26"><strong>Ecological footprint</strong> covers deforestation, mining, drilling and farming — plus the carbon produced during manufacture, transport and disposal.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n27"><strong>The six Rs</strong> (Reduce, Refuse, Re-use, Repair, Recycle, Rethink) are a framework for sustainable design that you must know with examples.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n28"><strong>Social footprint</strong> means considering worker safety, fair wages, pollution and ethical sourcing throughout the supply chain.</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n24">Questions on sustainability often ask you to <strong>explain</strong> or <strong>evaluate</strong> how a designer can reduce a product’s ecological footprint. Always reference the <strong>six Rs</strong> by name and give <strong>specific product examples</strong>. A common mistake is listing the six Rs without explaining how each one applies to the product in the question.</p>
