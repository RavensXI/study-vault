--- DESCRIPTION ---
How finishes protect and enhance products across timber, metals, polymers and textiles.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Why Apply a Surface Finish?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">A raw material straight from the workshop is rarely ready for the consumer. Surface treatments and finishes serve two purposes: they <strong>protect</strong> the material from damage (corrosion, moisture, UV degradation, wear) and they <strong>enhance its appearance</strong> (colour, texture, gloss). You need to know specific finishes for each material category and to explain why each one is chosen.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall the two main purposes of surface treatments and finishes.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Surface finishes have two key purposes: <strong>functional protection</strong> (preventing corrosion, rot, UV damage, moisture absorption) and <strong>aesthetic enhancement</strong> (improving colour, texture, gloss and visual appeal).</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Papers & Boards</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5"><dfn class="term" data-def="Applying ink designs to paper or card surfaces using methods such as offset lithography, screen printing or digital printing.">Printing</dfn> is the primary finish for papers and boards — it adds colour, text and graphics. <dfn class="term" data-def="Creating a raised or recessed pattern on paper or card by pressing it between shaped dies.">Embossing</dfn> creates a raised pattern by pressing the material between shaped dies, giving a tactile quality used on greetings cards and luxury packaging. <dfn class="term" data-def="Applying a glossy, protective ultraviolet-cured coating to paper or card to improve durability and appearance.">UV varnishing</dfn> applies a glossy, scratch-resistant coating cured by ultraviolet light — often used on magazine covers and product boxes to make colours more vibrant and the surface more durable.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Timber-Based Materials</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Painting</strong> covers the natural grain and provides a coloured, protective layer. Oil-based and water-based paints both seal the surface against moisture. <strong>Varnishing</strong> provides a transparent or tinted protective coat that shows the natural grain — polyurethane varnish is hard-wearing and water-resistant, making it ideal for tabletops and floors. <dfn class="term" data-def="Pressure-treating timber with chemical preservatives to protect it against rot, fungal decay and insect attack.">Tanalising</dfn> is a pressure treatment that forces preservative chemicals deep into the timber, protecting it against rot and insect attack. Tanalised timber is widely used for fencing, decking and garden furniture.</p>



<h2 data-narration-id="n8">Metal-Based Materials</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9">Metals are particularly vulnerable to <dfn class="term" data-def="The gradual degradation of a metal caused by chemical reactions with its environment, such as rusting of steel.">corrosion</dfn> (e.g. rust on steel, tarnishing on copper). Several finishes address this:</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Metal Finishing Processes</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Dip coating:</strong> The metal component is heated and dipped into a tank of polymer powder or liquid plastic, which melts and forms a smooth, coloured coating. Used on tool handles, dish racks and playground equipment.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Powder coating:</strong> Dry polymer powder is sprayed onto the metal surface using an electrostatic charge, then baked in an oven to form a tough, even finish. It is more durable than paint and available in many colours. Used on bicycle frames, car wheels and metal furniture.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Galvanising:</strong> The metal is coated with a layer of <dfn class="term" data-def="Coating iron or steel with a layer of zinc to protect it from corrosion.">zinc</dfn> by hot-dip galvanising (dipping into molten zinc) or electro-galvanising. The zinc acts as a sacrificial layer — it corrodes before the steel underneath. Used on gates, crash barriers and roofing sheets.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Anodising:</strong> An electrochemical process that thickens the natural oxide layer on aluminium, making it harder and more resistant to corrosion. The porous surface can absorb dyes, allowing a range of colours. Used on aluminium window frames, phone cases and kitchen utensils.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Polymers</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15">Polymers often need less finishing than metals or timber because they do not corrode or rot. However, finishes can still improve appearance and durability. <strong>Polishing</strong> (buffing with fine abrasive) creates a high-gloss finish on acrylic. <strong>Printing</strong> adds graphics to polymer surfaces — pad printing transfers ink from a silicone pad onto curved shapes (e.g. logos on bottle caps). <strong>Vinyl decals</strong> are adhesive graphics applied to polymer casings (phone cases, laptop covers, product branding).</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Textile-Based Materials</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">Textiles are finished to add colour, pattern or functional properties. <strong>Printing</strong> applies colour designs to fabric using screen printing, digital printing or block printing. <strong>Dyeing</strong> saturates the entire fabric with colour — it can be done at the fibre, yarn or fabric stage. <dfn class="term" data-def="A chemical treatment applied to textile fibres or fabrics to repel liquids and resist staining.">Stain protection</dfn> (e.g. Scotchgard) applies a chemical coating that causes liquids to bead on the surface rather than soak in, protecting upholstery and outdoor clothing.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n18" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: name one specific finish for each of the five material categories.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>In the exam, you should be able to name <strong>at least one specific finish per material category</strong> and explain both its protective and aesthetic benefits.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n19">Electronic & Mechanical Systems</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>PCB lacquering</strong> applies a protective conformal coating to printed circuit boards, shielding components from moisture, dust and chemical contamination. <strong>Lubrication</strong> reduces friction between moving parts in mechanical systems, preventing wear and overheating. Both are functional finishes with no aesthetic purpose.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Choosing the Right Finish</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n21">The choice of finish depends on: the material category, the product's environment (indoor vs outdoor, wet vs dry), the desired appearance, cost, and whether the finish needs to be food-safe. Outdoor products need UV and weather resistance. Food-contact products need non-toxic, food-grade coatings. High-traffic surfaces need scratch and abrasion resistance.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n23">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Two purposes:</strong> surface finishes protect materials (corrosion, moisture, UV, wear) and enhance appearance (colour, texture, gloss).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n25"><strong>Know one finish per category:</strong> printing/UV varnishing (paper), painting/varnishing/tanalising (timber), dip/powder coating/galvanising (metal), polishing/vinyl decals (polymer), dyeing/stain protection (textile).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n26"><strong>Match finish to product:</strong> consider the environment, durability needs, food safety and desired appearance when choosing a finish.</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n22">Surface finish questions often ask you to <strong>explain</strong> or <strong>justify</strong> a finish for a specific product. Always state the finish by name (e.g. “powder coating”, not just “coating”), explain how it is applied, and describe both its <strong>protective</strong> and <strong>aesthetic</strong> benefits. A common mistake is describing the finish without linking it to the product’s requirements.</p>
