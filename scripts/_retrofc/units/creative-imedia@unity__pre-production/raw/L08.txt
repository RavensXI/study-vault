<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Regulation, Certification and Classification</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The media industry is regulated by several organisations that set standards for content, protect audiences, and ensure media products are appropriate for their intended viewers. The R093 specification requires you to know the <strong>roles and responsibilities</strong> of four key regulatory bodies, the <strong>purpose of regulation</strong>, and how it <strong>impacts media production</strong>.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Regulatory Bodies</h2>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Define: there are four regulatory bodies you must know.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>There are four regulatory bodies you must know: <strong>ASA</strong> (advertising), <strong>Ofcom</strong> (broadcasting and communications), <strong>BBFC</strong> (films) and <strong>PEGI</strong> (video games). Each one covers a different type of media product.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>ASA — Advertising Standards Authority</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n5">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The UK body responsible for regulating advertising across all media, ensuring adverts are legal, decent, honest and truthful. It investigates complaints and can require misleading adverts to be withdrawn.">ASA (Advertising Standards Authority)</dfn> regulates <strong>advertising in all forms of media</strong> — including TV, radio, print, online, social media and billboards.</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n6">
<li>Ensures adverts are <strong>legal, decent, honest and truthful</strong></li>
<li>Investigates complaints from the public about misleading or offensive adverts</li>
<li>Can require adverts to be changed or withdrawn</li>
<li>Works with the Committee of Advertising Practice (CAP) to set advertising codes</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Impact on media production:</strong> Advertising producers must check that all claims are truthful and evidenced, content is not offensive to the target audience, and any disclaimers are clearly visible. A banned advert wastes budget and damages the brand’s reputation.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Ofcom — The Office of Communications</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n8"><dfn class="term" data-def="The UK regulatory body for broadcasting, telecoms and postal services. It sets standards for TV and radio content, investigates complaints, and ensures fair competition in communications markets.">Ofcom (The Office of Communications)</dfn> regulates <strong>broadcasting and communication services</strong> in the UK — including TV, radio, telecoms and the postal service.</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n9">
<li>Sets standards for what can be broadcast on TV and radio (the Broadcasting Code)</li>
<li>Investigates complaints from viewers and listeners</li>
<li>Ensures broadcasters do not breach content rules (e.g. showing violent or sexual content before the 9pm watershed)</li>
<li>Promotes fair competition in communication markets</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Impact on media production:</strong> TV and radio producers must comply with Ofcom’s Broadcasting Code. Content shown before the watershed must be suitable for children. Producers must be aware of scheduling restrictions and content guidelines.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>BBFC — British Board of Film Classification</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n11">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The independent body responsible for classifying films and cinema trailers in the UK using age ratings (U, PG, 12A, 12, 15, 18) to indicate content suitability for different audiences.">BBFC (British Board of Film Classification)</dfn> classifies <strong>films, cinema trailers, and some online content</strong>.</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n12">
<li><strong>U</strong> — suitable for all audiences</li>
<li><strong>PG</strong> — general viewing, but some scenes may be unsuitable for young children</li>
<li><strong>12A / 12</strong> — suitable for 12 years and over (12A allows younger children with an adult in cinemas)</li>
<li><strong>15</strong> — suitable only for 15 years and over</li>
<li><strong>18</strong> — adults only</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Impact on media production:</strong> Film producers must consider their target audience when creating content. If a film aimed at teenagers receives an 18 certificate, it will lose much of its audience. Producers may edit content to achieve a lower rating.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>PEGI — Pan European Game Information</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n14"><dfn class="term" data-def="A European rating system for video games that uses age recommendations (3, 7, 12, 16, 18) and content descriptors to help consumers make informed choices about which games are appropriate.">PEGI (Pan European Game Information)</dfn> is the <strong>rating system for video games</strong> across Europe.</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n15">
<li>Uses age ratings: <strong>3, 7, 12, 16, 18</strong></li>
<li>Uses content descriptors (icons) showing the type of content: violence, bad language, fear, drugs, sex, discrimination, gambling, in-game purchases</li>
<li>Helps consumers (especially parents) make informed choices when buying games</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Impact on media production:</strong> Game developers must consider the content of their game and the target audience. Including violent or mature content will result in a higher age rating, which may limit the potential market.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Health and Safety in Media Production</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Health and safety must be considered in <strong>all phases</strong> of media production — pre-production, production and post-production. The specification requires you to know common risks and hazards, how to mitigate them, what risk assessments are, and the purpose of <dfn class="term" data-def="A pre-production visit to a potential filming location to assess its suitability, identify potential hazards, check for noise issues, assess lighting conditions, and confirm access and parking arrangements.">location recces</dfn>.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n19" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. List the health and safety considerations described here.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>A <strong>risk assessment</strong> is a document that identifies potential hazards in a media production, assesses how likely each hazard is to cause harm, and records the actions taken to reduce or eliminate the risk. It must be completed <strong>before</strong> production begins.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n20">Common Risks and Hazards</h2>
<ul data-narration-id="n21">
<li><strong>Electrical hazards</strong> — cables, lighting rigs, and equipment must be <dfn class="term" data-def="Portable Appliance Testing — a process of checking electrical equipment for safety by inspecting the plug, cable, and appliance, then testing with a PAT tester to ensure it is safe to use.">PAT tested</dfn> and cables taped down to prevent tripping</li>
<li><strong>Manual handling</strong> — heavy equipment (cameras, lights, set pieces) must be lifted correctly to avoid injury</li>
<li><strong>Working at height</strong> — using scaffolding, ladders, or crane equipment requires safety precautions</li>
<li><strong>Lone working</strong> — crew members working alone in remote locations face additional safety risks</li>
<li><strong>Environmental hazards</strong> — weather conditions, uneven ground, water, traffic near filming locations</li>
<li><strong>Fire risks</strong> — hot lights, pyrotechnics, or overcrowded studio spaces</li>
</ul>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Risk Assessments and Location Recces</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n22">A <strong>risk assessment</strong> typically includes:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n23">
<li>A list of identified hazards</li>
<li>Who might be harmed and how</li>
<li>The level of risk (likelihood and severity)</li>
<li>Control measures — actions to reduce or eliminate the risk</li>
<li>The person responsible for implementing each control measure</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n24">A <strong>location recce</strong> is a pre-production visit to a potential filming location. The purpose is to:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n25">
<li>Assess the location’s suitability for the planned shots</li>
<li>Identify potential hazards (traffic, water, uneven ground)</li>
<li>Check lighting conditions (natural light, power supply for artificial lights)</li>
<li>Assess noise levels (nearby roads, airports, construction)</li>
<li>Confirm access, parking, and facilities for the crew</li>
<li>Take photographs for reference during production planning</li>
</ul>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n26">Mitigating Health and Safety Risks</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n27">Actions media producers take to reduce risk include:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n28">
<li>Completing a <strong>risk assessment</strong> before every shoot</li>
<li>Conducting a <strong>location recce</strong> to identify site-specific hazards</li>
<li>Ensuring all electrical equipment is <strong>PAT tested</strong></li>
<li>Providing <strong>first aid kits</strong> and trained first aiders on set</li>
<li>Taping down cables and using <strong>cable covers</strong></li>
<li>Briefing the crew on safety procedures at the start of each day</li>
<li>Ensuring <strong>fire extinguishers</strong> are available and exit routes are clear</li>
<li>Having a <strong>buddy system</strong> for lone workers</li>
</ul>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n30">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n31">Four regulatory bodies: <strong>ASA</strong> (adverts), <strong>Ofcom</strong> (TV/radio), <strong>BBFC</strong> (films) and <strong>PEGI</strong> (games). Each ensures media products meet content standards and are suitable for their audiences.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n32"><strong>Risk assessments</strong> identify hazards and control measures before production. <strong>Location recces</strong> assess filming locations for suitability and safety.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n33">Common hazards include electrical risks, manual handling, working at height, lone working and environmental dangers. All must be mitigated with specific actions.</li>
</ul>
