--- DESCRIPTION ---
Using mind maps and mood boards to generate and present creative ideas for media products.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Documents for Ideas Generation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Before any media product is created, the production team needs to generate and explore ideas. Two key documents are used at this stage: <dfn class="term" data-def="A diagram that starts with a central idea and branches outward with related sub-topics, used to brainstorm and organise thoughts visually.">mind maps</dfn> and <dfn class="term" data-def="A visual planning tool that collects together images, colours, fonts, textures and samples to represent the overall feel and style of a media product.">mood boards</dfn>. Both are classed as <strong>pre-production documents</strong> — they are created before the actual product is made.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">The R093 exam requires you to know the <strong>purpose</strong> of each document, its <strong>components and conventions</strong>, the <strong>hardware and software</strong> used to create it, its <strong>users</strong>, when it is <strong>appropriate</strong> to use, what makes it <strong>effective</strong>, and how it could be <strong>improved</strong>.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Mind Maps</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">A mind map is a brainstorming tool. It starts with a central idea in the middle of the page and branches outward into related sub-topics. Each branch can split further, creating a web of connected ideas.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n6" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Name the pre-production documents and what each contains.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The purpose of a mind map is to <strong>generate and organise ideas</strong>. It helps the creator explore possibilities quickly, see connections between ideas, and ensure no aspect of the brief is overlooked.</p>
</div>

<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n7">A well-constructed mind map follows these conventions:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n8">
<li><strong>Central topic</strong> — the main subject or project brief is placed in the centre</li>
<li><strong>Main branches</strong> — key categories or themes radiate outward from the centre</li>
<li><strong>Sub-branches</strong> — more detailed ideas connect to the main branches</li>
<li><strong>Keywords</strong> — short words or phrases are used, not full sentences</li>
<li><strong>Colour coding</strong> — different colours for different branches help distinguish categories</li>
<li><strong>Images and icons</strong> — small visuals can make branches more memorable</li>
</ul>
</div></div>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n9">Mind maps can be created in two ways:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n10">
<li><strong>Hand drawn</strong> — using paper and coloured pens. Quick and flexible, but harder to edit and share.</li>
<li><strong>Digital</strong> — using software such as MindMeister, XMind, or Coggle. Easy to edit, reorganise, and share with the team, but requires access to a computer and software.</li>
</ul>

<h2 data-narration-id="n11">Mood Boards</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n12">A mood board is a visual collection of images, colours, fonts, textures, and samples that represent the intended <dfn class="term" data-def="The overall look, feel and visual style of a media product, including its colour palette, typography, imagery and tone.">aesthetic</dfn> of a media product. It communicates the overall “feel” of the project to the client and the production team.</p>

<figure class="diagram"><img src="https://pub-aeb94e100e5a48f4a133be5bf206aecb.r2.dev/creative-imedia/pre-production/diagram_generated_1063c699.jpg" alt="Generated diagram for Mind Maps and Mood Boards"><figcaption>Generated diagram for Mind Maps and Mood Boards</figcaption></figure>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n13" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Name the pre-production documents and what each contains.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The purpose of a mood board is to <strong>present the visual concept</strong> and <strong>communicate the intended style</strong> of a media product to clients, designers and other stakeholders before production begins.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Components and Conventions of Mood Boards</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n14">An effective mood board typically includes:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n15">
<li><strong>Images</strong> — photographs, illustrations, or screenshots that reflect the intended visual style</li>
<li><strong>Colour palette</strong> — a selection of colours that will be used across the product</li>
<li><strong>Typography samples</strong> — examples of fonts and text styles</li>
<li><strong>Textures and patterns</strong> — materials or surface effects that contribute to the feel</li>
<li><strong>Annotations</strong> — brief notes explaining why each element has been included</li>
</ul>
</div></div>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n16">Like mind maps, mood boards can be created in two forms:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n17">
<li><strong>Physical</strong> — printed images, fabric swatches, and colour chips arranged on a board. Useful when textures and materials need to be seen in real life, but difficult to share remotely.</li>
<li><strong>Digital</strong> — created using software such as Canva, Adobe Spark, or Pinterest boards. Easy to update, share and present to remote clients, but physical textures cannot be conveyed accurately.</li>
</ul>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">When to Use Each Document</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19">Mind maps are best used at the <strong>very start</strong> of a project to brainstorm ideas before any visual direction has been decided. They are ideal for exploring content, themes and possible approaches.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Mood boards come <strong>after</strong> initial ideas have been generated. Once the team has a direction, the mood board communicates the visual style to clients and other team members. A graphic designer, web developer, or set designer would all use a mood board to ensure their work matches the agreed aesthetic.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Making Documents Effective and How to Improve Them</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n21">An <strong>effective mind map</strong> is well-organised with clear branches, uses colour coding consistently, and covers all aspects of the brief. It can be improved by adding more detail to sub-branches, including small images, or reorganising branches so the most important ideas are more prominent.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n22">An <strong>effective mood board</strong> has a clear visual theme, includes a range of relevant elements (images, colours, fonts), and is annotated so the viewer understands the choices. It can be improved by replacing low-quality images, adding annotations, or refining the colour palette to be more cohesive.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n23">Users of These Documents</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n24">Mind maps are primarily used by the <strong>creative team</strong> and <strong>project manager</strong> during brainstorming sessions. Mood boards are used by a wider group including <strong>clients</strong>, <strong>designers</strong>, <strong>directors</strong>, and <strong>marketing teams</strong> to agree on the visual direction of the product.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n26">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n27"><strong>Mind maps</strong> brainstorm and organise ideas using a central topic with branching sub-topics. They can be hand drawn or digital.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n28"><strong>Mood boards</strong> communicate the visual style of a media product using images, colours, fonts and textures. They can be physical or digital.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n29">Both documents are created in <strong>pre-production</strong>. Mind maps come first (ideas generation), mood boards follow (visual concept presentation).</li>
</ul>

Always explain <strong>why</strong> the document is appropriate for that specific situation, not just what it is.</p>
