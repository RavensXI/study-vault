--- DESCRIPTION ---
Organising assets with asset logs and planning user navigation using flowcharts.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Documents for Designing and Planning Media Products</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Once initial ideas have been generated (using mind maps and mood boards), the production team needs more detailed documents to plan the actual media product. The R093 specification covers six planning documents: asset logs, flowcharts, scripts, storyboards, visualisation diagrams and wireframe layouts. This lesson focuses on the first two: <dfn class="term" data-def="A document that records all the assets (images, audio, video, graphics) used in a media product, including details of where they came from, file formats, and any copyright permissions required.">asset logs</dfn> and <dfn class="term" data-def="A diagram that uses standard shapes and arrows to show the sequence of steps, decisions and navigation paths in a process or interactive media product.">flowcharts</dfn>.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Asset Logs</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">An asset log is a table or spreadsheet that records every asset used in a media product. An <dfn class="term" data-def="Any file or resource used in a media product, such as an image, audio clip, video file, graphic, font or piece of text.">asset</dfn> is any resource that forms part of the final product — this includes images, audio files, video clips, graphics, fonts and text content.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Recall the legislation and its key requirements.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The purpose of an asset log is to <strong>record and organise all assets</strong> used in a media product, ensuring the production team knows where every file came from, what format it is in, and whether <strong>copyright permission</strong> has been obtained.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Components of an Asset Log</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n6">A well-structured asset log typically includes columns for:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n7">
<li><strong>Asset name</strong> — a clear, descriptive file name (e.g. “hero-banner.jpg”)</li>
<li><strong>Asset type</strong> — image, audio, video, graphic, font</li>
<li><strong>File format</strong> — JPEG, PNG, MP3, WAV, MP4, etc.</li>
<li><strong>Source</strong> — where the asset came from (original creation, stock library, client-provided)</li>
<li><strong>Copyright/licence status</strong> — whether permission has been granted, what licence applies (e.g. Creative Commons), and any fees paid</li>
<li><strong>Date acquired</strong> — when the asset was sourced or created</li>
<li><strong>Used in</strong> — which part of the product the asset appears in</li>
</ul>
</div></div>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n8">Asset logs can be created using spreadsheet software such as Microsoft Excel or Google Sheets. They are used by <strong>producers</strong>, <strong>editors</strong>, <strong>designers</strong> and <strong>project managers</strong> to keep track of resources and ensure all copyright obligations are met.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9">An asset log is appropriate whenever a media product uses <strong>multiple assets from different sources</strong>. This is especially important for commercial products where using an image or music track without permission could lead to legal action.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Flowcharts</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">A flowchart shows the <strong>structure and navigation</strong> of an interactive media product. It uses standard shapes connected by arrows to represent the sequence of steps, decisions and paths a user can take.</p>

<figure class="diagram"><img src="https://pub-aeb94e100e5a48f4a133be5bf206aecb.r2.dev/creative-imedia/pre-production/diagram_asset_logs_and_flowcharts.jpg" alt="Diagram: Asset Logs and Flowcharts" loading="lazy"></figure>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n12" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Define: the purpose of a flowchart is to.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The purpose of a flowchart is to <strong>plan the navigation and structure</strong> of an interactive media product such as a website, app or game. It shows every page or screen, the decisions users make, and the paths they can follow.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Standard Flowchart Symbols</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n13">Flowcharts use internationally recognised symbols:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n14">
<li><strong>Oval / rounded rectangle</strong> — start and end points (terminal)</li>
<li><strong>Rectangle</strong> — a process or action (e.g. “Display homepage”)</li>
<li><strong>Diamond</strong> — a decision point with Yes/No or multiple outcomes (e.g. “Is the user logged in?”)</li>
<li><strong>Parallelogram</strong> — input or output of data</li>
<li><strong>Arrows</strong> — show the direction of flow between steps</li>
</ul>
</div></div>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n15">Flowcharts can be hand drawn or created using software such as Microsoft Visio, Lucidchart, or <dfn class="term" data-def="A free, open-source online diagramming tool used to create flowcharts, wireframes, network diagrams and other visual plans.">draw.io</dfn>. Digital flowcharts are easier to edit and share.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">When to Use Flowcharts</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">Flowcharts are most appropriate when planning <strong>interactive products</strong> where the user makes choices. Examples include:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n18">
<li>A website with multiple pages and navigation menus</li>
<li>A mobile app with different screens and user journeys</li>
<li>A computer game with branching storylines or menu systems</li>
<li>An interactive quiz or e-learning module</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n19">Flowcharts are used by <strong>web developers</strong>, <strong>game designers</strong>, <strong>UX designers</strong> and <strong>project managers</strong> to agree on the product structure before building begins.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Making These Documents Effective</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n20">An <strong>effective asset log</strong> is complete (every asset is recorded), accurate (correct file formats and sources), and up to date. It can be improved by adding more detailed copyright information, standardising file naming conventions, or including thumbnail previews of visual assets.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n21">An <strong>effective flowchart</strong> uses standard symbols consistently, has clear labels on every shape, and includes arrows showing all possible paths. It can be improved by adding colour coding for different sections, ensuring decision diamonds clearly show all outcomes, and testing the flowchart against user stories to check nothing is missing.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n23">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Asset logs</strong> record every resource used in a media product, tracking file names, formats, sources and copyright permissions.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n25"><strong>Flowcharts</strong> plan the navigation and structure of interactive media products using standard symbols (ovals, rectangles, diamonds, arrows).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n26">Both documents ensure organisation and clarity in pre-production — asset logs prevent copyright issues, flowcharts ensure logical user journeys.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n22">When the exam asks you to recommend a pre-production document, think about what the scenario needs. If assets need organising and copyright tracking, recommend an <strong>asset log</strong>. If the product is interactive and needs navigation planning, recommend a <strong>flowchart</strong>. Always explain the <strong>purpose</strong>, name <strong>specific components</strong>, and link your answer to the scenario.</p>
