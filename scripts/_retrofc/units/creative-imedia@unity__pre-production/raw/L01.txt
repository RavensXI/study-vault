--- DESCRIPTION ---
Creating and using work plans, Gantt charts, milestones and contingencies for media production.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Why Work Plans Matter in Media Production</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Every media product — whether it is a film, website, or advertising campaign — goes through three phases of production: <dfn class="term" data-def="The planning phase where documents such as mood boards, scripts and storyboards are created before any filming or building begins.">pre-production</dfn>, <dfn class="term" data-def="The phase where the media product is actually created, such as filming, recording audio, or building a website.">production</dfn>, and <dfn class="term" data-def="The phase after the main content is created, involving editing, sound mixing, adding titles, credits and special effects.">post-production</dfn>. A <dfn class="term" data-def="A document that sets out the tasks, timescales, milestones and resources needed to complete a media project on time and within budget.">work plan</dfn> is the document that organises all of these phases into a clear, manageable schedule.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Without a work plan, teams risk missing deadlines, running over budget, and forgetting important tasks. In the media industry, where projects often involve large teams with different specialisms, a work plan ensures everyone knows what they should be doing and when.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Name the pre-production documents and what each contains.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>A work plan is used to manage <strong>time</strong>, <strong>tasks</strong>, <strong>activities</strong> and <strong>resources</strong> for both individuals and large teams. It keeps every member of the production team on track.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Components of a Work Plan</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">The R093 exam expects you to know every component that makes up a work plan and explain the role each one plays. Here are the key components:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Phases</strong> — every media project is divided into pre-production, production and post-production. The work plan shows which tasks fall into which phase so nothing is attempted out of order.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Tasks and activities</strong> — the specific jobs that need completing. In pre-production this might include creating storyboards, writing scripts and booking locations. In production it could be filming, recording voiceovers and capturing photographs.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Workflow</strong> — the order in which tasks must be completed. Some tasks are <strong>dependent</strong> on others — for example, you cannot begin editing (post-production) until filming (production) is finished.</p>

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Timescales</strong> set start and end dates for each task, giving the team clear deadlines. Without timescales, work drifts and the final deadline is missed.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Milestones</strong> are significant checkpoints within the project — for example, “all filming completed” or “first edit reviewed by client.” They allow the project manager to check whether the project is on track at key moments.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A backup plan put in place to deal with problems that might arise during a project, such as bad weather, equipment failure or a team member being unavailable.">contingency</dfn> is a backup plan for when things go wrong. If an actor is ill, a contingency might be to film scenes that do not require that actor. If a location becomes unavailable, a backup location should already be identified.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Resources</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">A work plan also lists the <strong>resources</strong> needed for the project. The specification groups these into three categories:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n15">
<li><strong>Hardware</strong> — cameras, microphones, lighting rigs, computers, SD cards, tripods</li>
<li><strong>Software</strong> — editing suites (e.g. Adobe Premiere Pro), graphic design tools (e.g. Photoshop), audio editing software (e.g. Audacity)</li>
<li><strong>People</strong> — camera operators, directors, sound technicians, editors, actors, graphic designers</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n16">Listing resources in advance ensures that budgets are accurate, equipment is booked, and the right people are available at the right time.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Gantt Charts</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A horizontal bar chart used in project management that shows tasks along the vertical axis and time along the horizontal axis, making it easy to see task duration, overlaps and deadlines.">Gantt chart</dfn> is the most common way to present a work plan visually. Time runs along the horizontal axis (usually in days or weeks), and tasks are listed vertically. Each task is shown as a horizontal bar — the length of the bar shows how long the task will take.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n19" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Recall the resolution or dimension specifications here.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Gantt charts are useful because they show <strong>task duration</strong>, <strong>overlapping tasks</strong>, <strong>dependencies</strong> and <strong>milestones</strong> at a glance. They help teams identify where tasks can run at the same time (parallel working) and where one task must finish before another can start.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Advantages and Limitations of Work Plans</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Advantages:</strong></p>
<ul data-narration-id="n21">
<li>Everyone knows their responsibilities and deadlines</li>
<li>Progress can be tracked and problems spotted early</li>
<li>Resources are allocated efficiently, reducing waste and cost</li>
<li>Contingencies mean unexpected problems do not derail the project</li>
<li>Milestones allow quality checks at key stages</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Limitations:</strong></p>
<ul data-narration-id="n23">
<li>Work plans take time to create and need updating as the project progresses</li>
<li>They cannot predict every possible problem</li>
<li>In very small teams or solo projects, a detailed work plan may feel excessive</li>
</ul>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n24">Work Plans for Individuals vs Large Teams</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n25">An individual freelancer might use a simple to-do list or basic Gantt chart to track their own progress. A large production team — for example, a film crew — needs a much more detailed work plan showing which department handles each task, who is responsible, and how tasks interlink.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n26">In a large team, the work plan also helps with <strong>communication</strong>. If the camera crew knows that the set will not be ready until Thursday, they can schedule other work for Monday to Wednesday. Without the work plan, they might arrive on set only to find it is not finished.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n28">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n29">A work plan organises a media project into <strong>phases</strong> (pre-production, production, post-production) and lists all <strong>tasks</strong>, <strong>timescales</strong>, <strong>milestones</strong>, <strong>contingencies</strong> and <strong>resources</strong>.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n30"><strong>Gantt charts</strong> are the standard visual format for work plans, showing task duration and overlap at a glance.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n31">Work plans benefit both individuals and large teams by improving communication, tracking progress and ensuring resources are available when needed.</li>
</ul>
