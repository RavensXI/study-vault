--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Five Key Themes</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Every exam question on Never Let Me Go connects to one or more of these themes. Prepare quotations and analysis for each.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n3">1. Identity</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">What makes someone human? The clones are biologically identical to “normal” humans but are treated as less than human. They construct identities from fragments: Ruth mimics TV, Tommy creates art, Kathy narrates. Their search for “possibles” reflects a universal desire to understand where we come from. Key quotation: “We didn’t have the Gallery to look into your souls. We had the Gallery to see if you had souls at all.”</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Cover this and explain what Miss Emily’s Gallery quotation reveals about identity.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The quotation reveals that the clones’ humanity was always in question. The Gallery existed not to celebrate their souls but to test whether they had them. Ishiguro uses this to comment on how societies define certain groups as less than human to justify their exploitation — a pattern visible in slavery, colonialism and class oppression.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n6">2. Mortality</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7"><dfn class="term" data-def="The condition of being subject to death; awareness of one’s own eventual death.">Mortality</dfn> is Ishiguro’s stated central theme. The clones face death younger, but all humans die. Ishiguro uses the clones’ compressed lifespan to intensify the universal experience of knowing you will die. The novel asks: how do you live when you know the end is coming?</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n8">3. Hope vs Acceptance</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9">Tommy hopes (the deferral myth); Kathy accepts (her restrained narration); Ruth performs (denying reality through mimicry). The deferral myth is the novel’s central exploration of hope — is it better to hope falsely or to accept reality? Ishiguro does not answer definitively, but the destruction of Tommy’s hope is one of literature’s most devastating moments.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>4. Art and Creativity</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n10">Can art prove you are human? The Gallery, Tommy’s animals and Kathy’s narration all explore this question. Art is both celebrated (Hailsham prizes creativity) and instrumentalised (used as evidence in a political campaign). Ishiguro’s answer is ambiguous: art makes the clones more sympathetic to the reader, but it does not save them within the novel’s world. Creativity is not enough to overcome systemic injustice.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n11">5. Conformity and Compliance</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Why don’t the clones rebel? <dfn class="term" data-def="Acting in accordance with expectations or rules, often without questioning them.">Conformity</dfn> is presented not as cowardice but as the product of systematic conditioning. The clones obey because they have been raised to obey. Ishiguro uses their compliance to reflect on how all societies produce obedient citizens who accept unjust systems — from economic inequality to environmental destruction.</p>
<!-- DIAGRAM -->
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n13" data-revision-tip="Close this and list all five themes with one key detail for each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Identity (Gallery quotation — ‘to see if you had souls at all’), Mortality (compressed lifespan intensifies universal experience), Hope vs Acceptance (deferral myth and its destruction), Art (creativity celebrated but cannot save), Conformity (conditioning produces obedience, parallels real-world compliance). Prepare quotations for each.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Exam Technique Recap</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15">Comparison task: Compare throughout. Structure each paragraph as a comparison rather than writing about one text then the other. Use linking phrases. Analyse language (AO2) and link to context (AO3) in every paragraph. Aim for 3-4 comparative paragraphs.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n16">Extended response: draw on the whole novel, not just one section. Embed context throughout. Use the phrase “Ishiguro presents…” to signal AO2 awareness. Build 3-4 analytical paragraphs around a brief introduction and conclusion.</p>

  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n17">Build a bank of 10-12 quotations covering all five themes. Key quotations: “Your lives are set out for you” (Miss Lucy), “told and not told” (Kathy), “My name is Kathy H.” (opening), “We didn’t have the Gallery to look into your souls” (Miss Emily), “supposed to be” (Kathy’s final words), Tommy’s scream. Having these ready lets you respond to any question quickly.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Remember: this is a closed-text exam. You cannot take the novel in. Memorise your quotations thoroughly. Short quotations embedded in your analysis are more impressive than long passages you cannot fully discuss.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n19" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: which assessment objectives are tested in the modern-text comparison, and what two tasks might you face?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Modern prose or drama is typically assessed through a comparative task (your studied text alongside an unseen same-genre extract) and an extended response on your studied text. AO1, AO2 and AO3 are all assessed. Check your exam board’s specimen paper for the exact question structure and mark weightings.</p>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n21">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n22">Five key themes: Identity, Mortality, Hope vs Acceptance, Art and Creativity, Conformity. Every question connects to at least one.</li><li data-narration-id="n23">Part (a): compare throughout every paragraph. Part (b): draw on the whole novel with embedded context.</li><li data-narration-id="n24">Analysing Ishiguro’s restraint — the gap between what Kathy says and what she means — is the most effective technique for any question.</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n20">The single most effective exam technique for Never Let Me Go is analysing Ishiguro’s restraint. In every paragraph, you can note the gap between what Kathy says and what she means. This demonstrates AO2 (analysis of language) and AO3 (the clones’ conditioning) simultaneously. It works for virtually any question on the text.</p>
