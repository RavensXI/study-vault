--- DESCRIPTION ---
Germany’s invasion of Poland on 1 September 1939, Britain’s ultimatum, and the declaration of World War Two.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---

<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Final Target: Poland</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">With the Nazi-Soviet Pact securing his eastern flank, Hitler turned on Poland. He demanded the return of <dfn class="term" data-def="A port city on the Baltic Sea with a predominantly German-speaking population.">Danzig</dfn> (a “Free City” under the League of Nations with a majority German population) and the end of the <dfn class="term" data-def="A strip of territory granted to Poland by the Treaty of Versailles, giving Poland access to the Baltic Sea.">Polish Corridor</dfn> — the strip of land separating East Prussia from the rest of Germany.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Poland stood firm. Unlike Czechoslovakia, Poland had a clear <strong>guarantee from Britain and France</strong>. After Hitler broke his promise over Czechoslovakia, they had committed to defending Poland. Hitler gambled they would back down again. This time, he was wrong.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">The Gleiwitz Incident</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">On <strong>31 August 1939</strong>, German SS troops staged a <dfn class="term" data-def="A false flag operation on 31 August 1939 in which German SS soldiers disguised themselves as Polish troops and staged a fake attack on a German radio station near the Polish border.">Gleiwitz Incident</dfn> — a false flag operation. Nazis dressed as Polish soldiers attacked several <strong>radio and communications centres</strong> on the German border near Gleiwitz. They left behind the body of a concentration camp prisoner in a Polish uniform to make it look real.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n6" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and name the operation used to stage the Gleiwitz Incident, and why Hitler needed it.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The Gleiwitz Incident was a <strong>manufactured pretext</strong>. Hitler needed an excuse to claim Germany was acting in “self-defence.” Every detail was planned by the SS under orders from Berlin. The invasion had been prepared weeks in advance.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n7">The Invasion of Poland</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n8">On <strong>1 September 1939</strong>, Germany invaded Poland using <dfn class="term" data-def="Meaning 'Lightning War' in German.">Blitzkrieg</dfn> — “Lightning War.” This new form of warfare combined speed, technology, and overwhelming force. Yet again, Hitler claimed Germany was acting in self-defence.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button aria-expanded="false" class="collapsible-toggle">

<svg class="collapsible-icon" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" viewbox="0 0 24 24"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"></polyline></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content">
<div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n9">Blitzkrieg used coordinated air and ground attacks. <dfn class="term" data-def="German tanks used extensively in World War Two.">Panzers</dfn> (tanks) smashed through Polish defences in armoured columns, while <dfn class="term" data-def="The Junkers Ju 87 dive-bomber, known for its terrifying siren.">Stukas</dfn> (dive-bombers) destroyed communications and supply lines from the air.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n10">The Polish army, though brave, was overwhelmed. Much of it relied on horse-mounted cavalry and outdated equipment — no match for German armour and air power. The speed of advance meant Poland could not organise an effective defence.</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n11">On <strong>17 September</strong>, the Soviet Red Army invaded Poland from the east — as agreed in the Nazi-Soviet Pact’s secret clause. Caught between two armies, Poland was hopeless. <strong>Warsaw surrendered on 27 September.</strong> Poland was divided between Germany and the USSR.</p>

</figure>
<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Britain and France Declare War</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">On <strong>3 September 1939</strong>, Britain and France both gave <dfn class="term" data-def="A final demand or set of conditions issued by one party to another, with a threat of serious consequences (such as war) if the conditions are not met by a specified deadline.">ultimatums</dfn> to Germany: leave Poland or face war. They had promised to do this after Hitler broke his word over Czechoslovakia.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15">Germany ignored the threat. At <strong>11:15 am on 3 September 1939</strong>, Prime Minister Neville Chamberlain addressed the nation by radio:</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n16" data-revision-tip="Without looking, who gave the radio address, and what ultimatum deadline did Britain give Germany regarding Poland?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — Chamberlain’s Broadcast</div>
<p>“I am speaking to you from the Cabinet Room at 10 Downing Street. This morning the British Ambassador in Berlin handed the German Government a final note stating that, unless we heard from them by 11 o’clock that they were prepared at once to withdraw their troops from Poland, a state of war would exist between us. I have to tell you now that no such undertaking has been received, and that consequently <strong>this country is at war with Germany</strong>.”</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n17">France declared war later that day. The guarantee to Poland had been honoured. After a decade of tension, failed diplomacy, and appeasement, Europe was at war again. It took just <strong>four weeks</strong> to defeat Poland.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>The invasion of Poland was the direct cause of World War Two. Blitzkrieg crushed Poland in four weeks. Britain and France honoured their guarantee, ending a decade of tension and appeasement with a declaration of war.</p>
<h3>Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul data-narration-id="n22">
<li><strong>Gleiwitz was a calculated lie:</strong> Hitler manufactured a pretext to justify invasion. The attack on Poland was premeditated — key evidence when assessing blame for the war.</li>
<li><strong>Blitzkrieg changed warfare:</strong> Panzers, Stukas, and motorised infantry overwhelmed Poland in four weeks, shocking the world with Germany’s military power.</li>
<li><strong>Britain and France finally acted:</strong> The guarantee to Poland was honoured — but years of appeasement had let Hitler grow far stronger than he might have been.</li>
</ul>


--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p>When assessing who caused the war, focus on <strong>individuals’ roles</strong>. Hitler was the most direct cause — he planned the invasion, manufactured the pretext, and gave the order. But also show how the Nazi-Soviet Pact made it <strong>possible</strong> by removing the two-front war threat. The best answers weigh both immediate and enabling causes.</p>
