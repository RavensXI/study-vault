--- DESCRIPTION ---
Mussolini’s invasion of Abyssinia, the failure of sanctions, and why the League was fatally discredited by 1936.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---

<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Fatal Blow</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">If Manchuria wounded the League, <dfn class="term" data-def="The African nation now known as Ethiopia.">Abyssinia</dfn> killed it. This was not a distant conflict in Asia — it was a major European power invading a fellow League member, right on Europe’s doorstep. The League’s failure proved that collective security was dead.</p>
<!-- BACKGROUND -->
<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Background: Mussolini’s Ambitions</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">Italy’s fascist dictator, <strong>Mussolini</strong>, dreamed of building a new Roman Empire. Abyssinia (modern-day Ethiopia) was a prime target. It was the <strong>only independent black country in Africa</strong> and sat between two Italian colonies (Eritrea and Italian Somaliland). It was rich in resources. Mussolini also wanted <strong>revenge</strong> for Italy’s humiliating defeat by Abyssinia at the Battle of Adwa in <strong>1896</strong>.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Close this and recall: Which League did Haile Selassie address in 1935 after the Wal-Wal clash?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>In December 1934, Italian and Abyssinian troops clashed at the <strong>Wal-Wal oasis</strong> on the border with Italian Somaliland. This gave Mussolini his excuse. Emperor <dfn class="term" data-def="The Emperor of Abyssinia (Ethiopia) who appealed to the League of Nations for help when Italy invaded in 1935.">Haile Selassie</dfn> appealed to the League for help. In <strong>June 1935</strong>, he spoke at the League in Geneva, warning of what would happen if they failed to act.</p>
</div>
<!-- LEAGUE'S RESPONSE -->
<h2 data-narration-id="n6">The League’s Response — and Failure</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Britain and France faced a dilemma. They were worried about <strong>Hitler’s Germany</strong> and wanted to keep Mussolini as an ally against the Nazi threat. Protecting Abyssinia risked pushing Italy towards Germany. They chose <strong>self-interest</strong> over the League’s principles.</p>

<div class="collapsible-content">
<div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n8">In April 1935, Britain, France, and Italy formed the <dfn class="term" data-def="An agreement made in April 1935 between Britain, France, and Italy to oppose Hitler’s attempts to change the Treaty of Versailles.">Stresa Front</dfn> — agreeing to oppose Hitler. But they <strong>deliberately avoided any mention of Abyssinia</strong>.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9">Mussolini took this silence as a <strong>green light</strong>. If Britain and France would not even raise the issue at a conference, they clearly would not stop him. He was right.</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

</button>
<div class="collapsible-content">
<div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n10">In October 1935, Italy launched a full invasion of Abyssinia, using tanks, aircraft, and even <strong>poison gas</strong> against poorly equipped Abyssinian forces. The League imposed <strong>economic sanctions</strong> — but they were deliberately weak:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n11">
<li>The sanctions did <strong>NOT include oil, coal, or steel</strong> — the very resources Italy needed for war. Mussolini warned that banning oil would be an “act of war.”</li>
<li>The <dfn class="term" data-def="The man-made waterway in Egypt connecting the Mediterranean Sea to the Red Sea.">Suez Canal</dfn> — controlled by Britain and the main route for Italian ships — was left <strong>open</strong>. Closing it would have crippled Italy’s supply lines.</li>
<li>Non-League members like the <strong>USA continued trading</strong> with Italy.</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Britain also acted out of <strong>self-interest</strong>: a speech in the House of Commons warned that banning coal exports to Italy would cost <strong>30,000 British miners</strong> their jobs. The sanctions were designed to look like action without actually hurting Italy.</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>
<!-- HOARE-LAVAL PACT -->
<h2 data-narration-id="n13">The Hoare-Laval Pact (December 1935)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">The worst moment came in December 1935. The <dfn class="term" data-def="A secret plan drawn up in December 1935 by British Foreign Secretary Samuel Hoare and French Prime Minister Pierre Laval.">Hoare-Laval Pact</dfn> was drawn up in secret by British Foreign Secretary <strong>Samuel Hoare</strong> and French Prime Minister <strong>Pierre Laval</strong>.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="Without looking, can you name the TWO politicians who lost their jobs after the Hoare-Laval Pact?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — The Hoare-Laval Pact</div>
<p>The plan would give Mussolini <strong>two-thirds of Abyssinia</strong> in exchange for stopping the war. When it was <strong>leaked to the press</strong>, there was a huge public outcry. Both Hoare and Laval were forced to resign. This was the <strong>ultimate betrayal</strong> — the League’s two leading members had secretly tried to reward an aggressor by carving up a fellow member state.</p>
</div>
<!-- OUTCOME -->
<h2 data-narration-id="n16">The Outcome</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">Italy conquered Abyssinia in <strong>May 1936</strong> and then <strong>left the League</strong>. Emperor Haile Selassie went into exile and made a famous speech to the League Assembly: “It is us today. It will be you tomorrow.”</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>The Abyssinian Crisis ended the League as a serious force. Britain and France put self-interest first, sanctions were deliberately useless, and the Hoare-Laval Pact proved the League’s own leaders did not believe in it. After Abyssinia, countries rearmed and formed their own alliances instead.</p>
<h3>Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul data-narration-id="n23">
<li><strong>Self-interest over the League:</strong> Britain and France wanted Mussolini as an ally against Hitler and sacrificed Abyssinia to keep him. The Hoare-Laval Pact proved the League’s leaders did not believe in collective security.</li>
<li><strong>Useless sanctions:</strong> Oil, coal, and steel were excluded. The Suez Canal stayed open. Even when the League acted, its actions were designed to fail.</li>
<li><strong>Devastating knock-on effects:</strong> Mussolini allied with Hitler (Rome-Berlin Axis). Hitler remilitarised the Rhineland while the world was distracted. Countries lost faith, rearmed, and formed secret alliances. The path to war was wide open.</li>
</ul>
