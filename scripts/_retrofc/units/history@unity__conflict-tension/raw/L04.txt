The other Paris peace treaties — Saint-Germain, Neuilly, Trianon and Sèvres — and the new map of Europe.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---

<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Beyond Versailles</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The Treaty of Versailles dealt with Germany, but separate treaties were needed for each of Germany’s wartime allies. These settlements reshaped the map of Europe, broke up empires, and created new nations — but they also planted the seeds of future conflict.</p>
<div class="timeline" data-narration-id="n3">
<div class="timeline-event">
<div class="timeline-date">September 1919</div>
<h4>Treaty of St Germain — Austria</h4>
<p>Broke up the Austro-Hungarian Empire. Austria lost land and its army was cut.</p>
</div>
<div class="timeline-event">
<div class="timeline-date">November 1919</div>
<h4>Treaty of Neuilly — Bulgaria</h4>
<p>Bulgaria lost territory and access to the Aegean Sea. Reparations set at £100 million.</p>
</div>
<div class="timeline-event">
<div class="timeline-date">June 1920</div>
<h4>Treaty of Trianon — Hungary</h4>
<p>Very harsh — Hungary lost over two-thirds of its territory.</p>
</div>
<div class="timeline-event">
<div class="timeline-date">August 1920</div>
<h4>Treaty of Sèvres — Turkey</h4>
<p>Broke up the <dfn class="term" data-def="The vast empire centred on modern-day Turkey that controlled much of the Middle East, North Africa, and south-eastern Europe for centuries.">Ottoman Empire</dfn>. Middle Eastern lands were given to Allied powers as mandates.</p>
</div>
<div class="timeline-event">
<div class="timeline-date">July 1923</div>
<h4>Treaty of Lausanne — Turkey (revised)</h4>
<p>Replaced Sèvres after Turkey’s nationalist revolt. No reparations or military limits.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n27" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall why demobilisation after 1918 pressured the Big Three during negotiations.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — Time Pressure</div>
<p>The Big Three were under huge time pressure. After 1918, Allied armies began to <strong>demobilise</strong> (send soldiers home). The longer talks dragged on, the weaker the Allies’ bargaining position became — they had fewer troops to back up their demands.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Treaty of St Germain (Austria, 1919)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">The Treaty of St Germain broke up the Austro-Hungarian Empire and imposed tough terms on the new, much smaller Austria.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button aria-expanded="false" class="collapsible-toggle">
<span>St Germain terms in detail</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" viewbox="0 0 24 24"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"></polyline></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content">
<div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n6">Austria lost land to the new states of Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, and Poland. Its army was cut to just <strong>30,000 men</strong>.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7"><dfn class="term" data-def="The political union of Austria and Germany.">Anschluss</dfn> (union) with Germany was banned. This contradicted <dfn class="term" data-def="The right of people to decide their own form of government and which country they belong to.">self-determination</dfn>, since both countries were German-speaking.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8">Austria was left as a small, landlocked country with a broken economy, cut off from the industry and farmland it once relied on.</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Treaty of Trianon (Hungary, 1920)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">The Treaty of Trianon was the harshest of all the settlements. Hungary suffered huge territorial losses.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button aria-expanded="false" class="collapsible-toggle">
<span>Trianon terms in detail</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" viewbox="0 0 24 24"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"></polyline></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content">
<div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n11">Hungary lost <strong>over two-thirds of its territory</strong> to Czechoslovakia, Romania, and Yugoslavia. About <strong>3 million Hungarians</strong> ended up as minorities in foreign countries.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Its army was cut to <strong>35,000 men</strong>. Hungary was left bitter and resentful, and it would push to overturn the settlement for decades.</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Treaty of Neuilly (Bulgaria, 1919)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Bulgaria’s treaty was tough but less severe than Austria’s or Hungary’s.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button aria-expanded="false" class="collapsible-toggle">
<span>Neuilly terms in detail</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" viewbox="0 0 24 24"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"></polyline></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content">
<div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n15">Bulgaria lost land to Yugoslavia and Greece, and — crucially — lost its <strong>access to the Aegean Sea</strong>, which hurt trade.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n16">The army was limited to <strong>20,000 men</strong>, and Bulgaria was ordered to pay <strong>£100 million</strong> in reparations.</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Treaty of Sèvres (Turkey, 1920) &amp; Lausanne (1923)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">The Treaty of Sèvres broke up the Ottoman Empire. Middle Eastern lands were handed to Allied powers as <dfn class="term" data-def="Territories taken from defeated nations and administered by Allied powers on behalf of the League of Nations.">mandates</dfn>. The Allies also took control of the <strong>Dardanelles Straits</strong>, a vital shipping route to the Black Sea. Turkey’s response was unique among the defeated nations.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n19" data-revision-tip="Without looking, name the leader of the Turkish nationalist uprising after the Treaty of Sevres.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — Turkey’s Challenge</div>
<p>The harsh terms of Sèvres sparked a <strong>nationalist uprising</strong> led by <strong>Mustafa Kemal Atatürk</strong>. Turkish forces fought to reclaim lost territory, forcing the Allies to negotiate a replacement treaty. The <strong>Treaty of Lausanne (1923)</strong> was significantly fairer: Turkey regained most of its land, faced <strong>no military restrictions</strong>, and paid <strong>no reparations</strong>.</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Turkey is the only defeated nation that fought back and overturned its treaty by force. This showed the peace settlement was fragile — when challenged, the Allies backed down.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n21">New States &amp; New Problems</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22">The peace settlement created new nations, including Poland, Czechoslovakia, and Yugoslavia. This looked like self-determination in action — but the reality was far messier.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n23" data-revision-tip="Close this and list TWO problems new states faced related to minorities, the economy, or politics.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — Problems with New States</div>
<ul data-narration-id="n28">
<li><strong>Minority populations</strong> — millions of Germans, Hungarians, and other ethnic groups were placed under foreign rule, creating tensions</li>
<li><strong>Economic weakness</strong> — new states inherited broken infrastructure and lacked established trade networks</li>
<li><strong>Political instability</strong> — inexperienced governments struggled to manage diverse, sometimes hostile populations</li>
</ul>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>The wider peace settlement spread the flaws of Versailles across all the defeated powers. Austria, Hungary, and Bulgaria were left weakened and bitter. New states were plagued by ethnic tensions. Turkey’s military challenge showed the whole settlement could be overturned.</p>
<h3>Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul data-narration-id="n29">
<li><strong>Self-determination was applied inconsistently:</strong> Millions of Germans and Hungarians ended up as minorities in foreign states. Austria was banned from joining Germany despite both being German-speaking.</li>
<li><strong>Turkey proved the settlement could be challenged:</strong> Atatürk’s revolt forced the Allies to replace Sèvres with the much softer Lausanne treaty — showing force could overturn the peace.</li>
<li><strong>New states inherited old problems:</strong> Poland, Czechoslovakia, and Yugoslavia were born with ethnic divisions, weak economies, and inexperienced governments — creating flashpoints for future conflict.</li>
</ul>
