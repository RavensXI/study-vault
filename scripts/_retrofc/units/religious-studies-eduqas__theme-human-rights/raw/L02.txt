<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Prejudice, Discrimination, and the Law</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="An unfair opinion or feeling about a person or group, formed without knowledge or reason, often based on race, religion, gender, or other characteristics.">Prejudice</dfn> is an unfair, preconceived attitude towards a person or group. <dfn class="term" data-def="Treating a person or group differently and unfairly because of a characteristic such as race, gender, religion, or disability.">Discrimination</dfn> is when that prejudice is acted upon &mdash; treating people unequally or unjustly. The UK Equality Act 2010 makes it illegal to discriminate on the grounds of nine &ldquo;protected characteristics,&rdquo; including race and religion. Despite legal protections, racial prejudice and discrimination remain significant social problems that religious communities have addressed both theologically and practically.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Christianity: All Made in God&rsquo;s Image</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">Christian teaching grounds racial equality in the doctrine of <em>imago Dei</em> &mdash; every person is made in God&rsquo;s image (Genesis 1:26&ndash;27) &mdash; and in the teaching of Jesus. Galatians 3:27&ndash;29 declares that in Christ &ldquo;there is neither Jew nor Gentile, neither slave nor free, nor is there male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus.&rdquo; Acts 10:34&ndash;35 records Peter&rsquo;s declaration that &ldquo;God does not show favouritism but accepts from every nation the one who fears him and does what is right.&rdquo; These passages have historically been used to argue that racial discrimination is incompatible with Christian faith.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n5">The Catholic Catechism paragraphs 1934&ndash;1938 explicitly address social equality, stating that every form of social or cultural discrimination in fundamental personal rights on the grounds of race is contrary to God&rsquo;s design. The Catechism teaches that differences between persons belong to God&rsquo;s plan and that solidarity requires recognising these differences while treating all people as equal in dignity.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n6" data-revision-tip="Without looking, write the reference and key phrase from Acts 10:34-35, and name the Catechism paragraph range that condemns racial discrimination.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Acts 10:34&ndash;35 records Peter declaring that &ldquo;God does not show favouritism but accepts from every nation the one who fears him.&rdquo; Catholic Catechism 1934&ndash;1938 states that racial discrimination in fundamental personal rights is contrary to God&rsquo;s design.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n7">Martin Luther King and Christian Activism</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Martin Luther King Jr.</strong> (1929&ndash;1968) was a Baptist minister who led the American Civil Rights Movement and drew explicitly on Christian theology to oppose racial discrimination. In his &ldquo;Letter from Birmingham Jail&rdquo; (1963), written while imprisoned for civil disobedience, King argued that unjust laws &mdash; laws that degrade human personality and contradict natural and divine law &mdash; need not be obeyed. He cited Augustine and Aquinas in support of the distinction between just and unjust laws.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9">King&rsquo;s theology was rooted in <em>agape</em> &mdash; unconditional love for all people including one&rsquo;s opponents &mdash; and in the Christian vision of the &ldquo;beloved community&rdquo; where all people are treated as equally beloved children of God. His work illustrates how Christian teaching on racial equality can motivate non-violent direct action.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Judaism: Every Person Is a World</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Jewish teaching grounds racial equality in <em>b&rsquo;tselem Elohim</em> &mdash; every person is made in the image of God &mdash; and in the Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5, which states that God created the first human as a single individual so that no one could say &ldquo;my ancestor is greater than yours.&rdquo; This passage explicitly argues against racial and ethnic hierarchy.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Jewish organisations have historically worked against racial discrimination. HIAS (the Hebrew Immigrant Aid Society), founded in 1881, provides humanitarian aid and legal assistance to refugees and migrants regardless of religion or background, arguing that Jewish memory of persecution and displacement creates a moral obligation to protect the vulnerable. Jewish teaching on <dfn class="term" data-def="The Hebrew phrase meaning 'repair of the world'; the Jewish concept of working to improve and perfect society.">tikkun olam</dfn> &mdash; repairing the world &mdash; motivates social justice work including anti-racism campaigns.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Islam: No Arab Is Superior to a Non-Arab</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Islamic teaching strongly opposes racial discrimination. Qur&rsquo;an 49:13 states that God created human beings from a single pair of a male and female and made them into different nations and tribes &ldquo;so that you may know one another&rdquo; &mdash; diversity is presented as God&rsquo;s design, not a basis for hierarchy. Qur&rsquo;an 5:8 commands believers to be &ldquo;persistently standing firm in justice&rdquo; and not to allow hatred of a people to cause them to act unjustly.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15">The Prophet Muhammad&rsquo;s farewell sermon, as transmitted in Islamic tradition, is reported to have declared that no Arab is superior to a non-Arab, and no non-Arab is superior to an Arab; no white person is superior to a black person, and no black person to a white, except through piety and good deeds. The <dfn class="term" data-def="The worldwide community of Muslims, bound by shared faith; a key Islamic concept that transcends racial and national boundaries.">ummah</dfn> is explicitly a global community transcending racial boundaries. British organisations such as the Christian Muslim Forum work to build understanding between Christian and Muslim communities and to tackle prejudice and discrimination together.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n16">Hindu teaching on human equality faces a particular historical challenge: the <dfn class="term" data-def="The ancient Hindu system of social classification into four broad categories (varnas), which historically has been associated with hierarchies of purity and status.">varna</dfn> system (sometimes confused with caste) has been used to justify social stratification. The Rig Veda 10:90 (the Purusha Sukta) describes the four varnas as emerging from the body of the cosmic person, which some have read as a divinely ordained hierarchy.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n17">However, reformers have strongly challenged discrimination based on birth. <strong>Mahatma Gandhi</strong> campaigned against the treatment of &ldquo;Harijans&rdquo; (his term for Dalits) as untouchables, arguing this was contrary to the Hindu principle of universal brotherhood. <strong>Dr B.R. Ambedkar</strong> (1891&ndash;1956), himself a Dalit, was more critical of Hinduism&rsquo;s caste structures. He eventually converted to Buddhism in 1956, arguing that Buddhism&rsquo;s rejection of caste offered genuine equality. Reform movements including Vivekananda&rsquo;s Ramakrishna Mission and ISKCON (the International Society for Krishna Consciousness) have emphasised the universal presence of Brahman in all people regardless of birth, challenging caste-based discrimination.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Sikhism: The Khalsa and Equality</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19">Sikhism teaches the fundamental equality of all people before God. The Guru Granth Sahib at ang 349 contains teachings emphasising that God created all humanity from the same divine light and that there is no high or low in God&rsquo;s creation. Guru Gobind Singh&rsquo;s founding of the <dfn class="term" data-def="The community of initiated Sikhs, founded by Guru Gobind Singh in 1699 as a community of equals committed to justice and the protection of the oppressed.">Khalsa</dfn> in 1699 was explicitly about dismantling social hierarchies: initiates from all castes drank from the same bowl, symbolising equality. The Khalsa&rsquo;s stated purpose included protecting the oppressed and challenging tyranny.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n20">In contemporary Britain, Sikh organisations have worked against racism. Khalsa Aid not only provides humanitarian relief but also advocates for human rights. The gurdwara&rsquo;s langar (community kitchen), which is open to all regardless of background, is a practical daily expression of the principle that no one is superior to another in God&rsquo;s eyes.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n21">Buddhism: Right Action and the Rejection of Hierarchy</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22">The Buddhist concept of <dfn class="term" data-def="The sixth step of the Noble Eightfold Path, calling for ethical conduct including refraining from harming living beings, stealing, and sexual misconduct.">Right Action</dfn> &mdash; part of the Noble Eightfold Path &mdash; requires avoiding actions that harm others, including discrimination. Buddhist teaching holds that all sentient beings possess Buddha-nature and are capable of liberation, making no person inherently superior or inferior to another.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n23">Dr B.R. Ambedkar&rsquo;s mass conversion movement brought millions of Dalits to Buddhism in 1956 as a means of escaping caste-based discrimination. He argued that the Buddha had taught the equality of all people regardless of birth &mdash; a radical claim in the context of mid-twentieth century India. However, the spec also notes that some Buddhist monks in Myanmar have been associated with the 969 Movement, which has at times promoted anti-Muslim sentiment &mdash; a controversial example of how religious identity can also become entangled with ethnic nationalism rather than transcending it. Scholars and many Buddhist leaders have condemned this as contrary to Buddhist teaching on compassion and non-harm.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n24" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: what does Qur'an 49:13 teach about human diversity, and how does this compare to Guru Granth Sahib ang 349?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Qur&rsquo;an 49:13 teaches that God made people into different nations and tribes so that they might know one another &mdash; diversity as divine design. The Guru Granth Sahib (ang 349) teaches that God created all humanity from the same divine light and that there is no high or low in God&rsquo;s creation.</p>
</div>

Amnesty International &mdash; which includes many secular and non-religious supporters &mdash; campaigns globally against racial discrimination, arbitrary detention, and the treatment of minorities. Humanists argue that the moral case against racism does not require religious grounding: reason and empathy are sufficient to recognise that all people share the same capacity to suffer, flourish, and pursue a meaningful life.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n27">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n28">Christianity grounds racial equality in <em>imago Dei</em> (Genesis 1:26&ndash;27), Galatians 3:27&ndash;29, and Acts 10:34&ndash;35. Martin Luther King Jr. drew on agape and the &ldquo;beloved community&rdquo; to lead non-violent civil rights activism. Catholic Catechism 1934&ndash;1938 explicitly condemns racial discrimination.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n29">Islam teaches racial equality through Qur&rsquo;an 49:13 (diversity as God&rsquo;s design) and 5:8 (stand firmly for justice); Sikhism through the Khalsa&rsquo;s founding principle of equality (Guru Granth Sahib, ang 349); Judaism through <em>b&rsquo;tselem Elohim</em> and the Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5; HIAS and World Jewish Relief work against persecution of minorities.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n30">Hinduism&rsquo;s reform movements (Gandhi, Ramakrishna, ISKCON) challenge caste discrimination; Buddhism&rsquo;s Right Action and the mass conversion of Dalits under Ambedkar challenge caste hierarchy. Humanists and Amnesty International ground anti-racism in reason and empathy.</li>
</ul>
