<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Why Sound Creation Matters</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Sound creation is the process of producing audio specifically designed for use in media. Without carefully created sound, a film would feel flat, a video game would lack immersion, and an advertisement would fail to grab attention. Understanding the different forms of media and types of sound creation is the starting point for this unit.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Forms of Media Using Sound Creation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">Sound creation is used across a wide range of media. Each form has its own requirements, but all rely on carefully designed audio to enhance the audience’s experience.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Cover this and list all ten forms of media that use sound creation.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The ten forms of media you must know are: <strong>radio broadcasts</strong>, <strong>movies</strong>, <strong>TV shows</strong>, <strong>video games</strong>, <strong>advertisements</strong>, <strong>jingles</strong>, <strong>podcasts</strong>, <strong>animations</strong>, <strong>theatre</strong>, and <strong>installations</strong>.</p>
</div>

    <p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Advertisements</strong> use short, punchy sound design to make products memorable. A <dfn class="term" data-def="A short, catchy musical phrase used in advertising to make a brand instantly recognisable.">jingle</dfn> is a specific type of sound creation — a brief, catchy tune designed to stick in the listener’s mind and associate with a product or brand.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Podcasts</strong> use voice recording, music beds, and effects to create engaging audio-only content. <strong>Animations</strong> need all sounds to be created from scratch because there is no live-action audio to capture. <strong>Theatre</strong> uses live and pre-recorded sound effects and music to support performances. <strong>Installations</strong> are immersive art or exhibition spaces that use sound to create an environment or atmosphere for visitors.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Types of Sound Creation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">There are six main types of sound creation. Each serves a different purpose within a media production.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n11">Foley</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n12"><dfn class="term" data-def="The reproduction of everyday sound effects that are added to media in post-production to enhance audio quality, such as footsteps, door creaks, or rustling clothes.">Foley</dfn> is the art of creating realistic, everyday sounds in a studio. Foley artists watch the visual footage and perform the sounds in sync — for example, walking on gravel to create footstep sounds, or crumpling paper to simulate fire. These sounds are recorded and layered into the final production to make it feel real.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n13">Ambience</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n14"><dfn class="term" data-def="Background sound that establishes the environment of a scene, such as birdsong, traffic, wind, or crowd noise.">Ambience</dfn> (also called atmosphere or room tone) is the background sound that sets the scene. It tells the audience where and when the action takes place. A busy city street has traffic and voices. A forest has birdsong and rustling leaves. Without ambience, a scene feels unnaturally silent and disconnected.</p>



<h3 data-narration-id="n15">Dialogue</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Dialogue</strong> is recorded speech between characters. In film and TV, dialogue is captured on set using boom microphones and lavalier mics, then cleaned and balanced in post-production. If on-set recording is unusable, actors re-record their lines in a studio — a process called <dfn class="term" data-def="Automated Dialogue Replacement — the process of re-recording an actor's lines in a studio to replace poor-quality on-set audio.">ADR</dfn> (Automated Dialogue Replacement).</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n17">Voice-overs</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n18">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A recorded narration or commentary that plays over visuals without the speaker appearing on screen.">voice-over</dfn> is a piece of narration or commentary recorded separately and placed over the visuals. Voice-overs are common in documentaries, advertisements, trailers, and podcasts. The voice-over artist does not appear on screen — they provide information, set the tone, or guide the audience’s understanding.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n19">Underscore</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n20">An <dfn class="term" data-def="Background music that plays underneath dialogue or action to reinforce mood, tension, or emotion without drawing attention to itself.">underscore</dfn> is background music that supports the visuals without drawing attention to itself. It sits beneath the dialogue and action, reinforcing the mood of a scene. A tense underscore might use sustained low strings, while a romantic underscore might feature gentle piano. The audience may not consciously notice the underscore, but it shapes how they feel about what they are watching.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n21" data-revision-tip="Close this and name all six types of sound creation with a one-line description of each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The six types of sound creation are: <strong>Foley</strong> (recreated everyday sounds), <strong>ambience</strong> (background environment sounds), <strong>dialogue</strong> (character speech), <strong>voice-overs</strong> (off-screen narration), <strong>underscore</strong> (background music for mood), and <strong>spot effects</strong> (specific one-off sounds synced to events).</p>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n22">Spot Effects</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n23"><dfn class="term" data-def="A specific, individual sound effect timed precisely to a visual event, such as a gunshot, explosion, or doorbell.">Spot effects</dfn> are individual sound effects that are timed precisely to a specific on-screen event. A gunshot, a car horn, a doorbell, or a glass smashing are all spot effects. Unlike ambience (which is continuous), a spot effect is a one-off sound triggered at an exact moment. Spot effects can be sourced from effects libraries, created using Foley techniques, or synthesised digitally.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n24">Imagine a scene in a thriller where a detective enters a dark warehouse. The <strong>ambience</strong> includes distant traffic and a low hum from overhead lights. <strong>Foley</strong> provides the detective’s echoing footsteps on concrete. A tense <strong>underscore</strong> builds with sustained cello. The detective speaks into a radio — this is <strong>dialogue</strong>. A narrator describes the scene — this is a <strong>voice-over</strong>. A door slams shut behind them — this is a <strong>spot effect</strong>. All six types work together to create a convincing, immersive experience.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n25" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: explain the difference between Foley and spot effects with one example of each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Foley and spot effects are different. <strong>Foley</strong> recreates everyday, continuous sounds performed in sync with visuals (e.g. footsteps, clothing rustle). <strong>Spot effects</strong> are single, precisely timed events (e.g. an explosion, a phone ring). Foley is always performed live; spot effects may come from libraries, synthesis, or Foley.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n27">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n28">Sound creation is used across ten forms of media including movies, video games, radio, podcasts, theatre, and installations.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n29">The six types of sound creation are Foley, ambience, dialogue, voice-overs, underscore, and spot effects — each serves a different purpose in a production.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n30">In any media production, multiple types of sound creation are layered together to build a convincing and immersive audio experience.</li></ul>
