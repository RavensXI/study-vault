--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Place of Mission and Evangelism</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="The calling of a religious organisation or individual to go out into the world and spread their faith.">Mission</dfn> is at the heart of Christianity. Christians believe they have a duty to share the good news about Jesus Christ with non-believers. This calling comes directly from the words of Jesus himself. <dfn class="term" data-def="Spreading the Christian gospel by public preaching or personal witness.">Evangelism</dfn> is one of the main ways this mission is carried out — through public preaching, personal conversations, and organised events that invite people to learn about the Christian faith.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Define mission in your own words.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Mission means “sent out” — it refers to the duty of Christians to go into the world and spread their faith. Evangelism is the practice of spreading the Christian gospel by public preaching or personal witness.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">The Great Commission</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">The <dfn class="term" data-def="Jesus’ instruction to his followers to spread his teachings to all the nations of the world.">Great Commission</dfn> is the instruction Jesus gave to his disciples after his resurrection. It is recorded in Matthew 28:16–20. Jesus told his eleven disciples: “Go, then, to all peoples everywhere and make them my disciples: baptise them in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, and teach them to obey everything I have commanded you.”</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">This passage is significant for three reasons. First, Jesus commands his followers to make <dfn class="term" data-def="The training of new converts in the Christian faith, encouraging them to spread the gospel and supporting fellow believers.">disciples</dfn> of all nations — not just believers, but people who actively follow his teachings. Second, he instructs them to baptise new converts, showing that baptism has been central to the Church from the very beginning. Third, he tells them to teach obedience to his commandments, meaning the disciples must pass on Jesus’ moral and spiritual teachings.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Jesus also said: “Come, follow me… and I will send you out to fish for people” (Matthew 4:19). At Pentecost, the Holy Spirit gave the disciples the gifts and courage they needed to carry out this mission.</p>

Some become <dfn class="term" data-def="A person sent on a religious mission, often to a foreign country, to promote Christianity through preaching or charitable work.">missionaries</dfn>, travelling to other countries to preach and carry out humanitarian work among the poor and disadvantaged. Others use social media, television channels like God TV, and Christian radio to reach wider audiences. Many churches invite people to attend events, services, or informal gatherings as a first step towards faith.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Church Growth</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">The Church is growing rapidly in South America, Africa, and Asia, though growth has slowed in the USA, Europe, and the Middle East. Worldwide, around 80,000 people become Christians each day, and over 500 new churches begin. The Church’s mission is to make new disciples, not just new believers — this means evangelism should be followed by training new converts in the way of following Jesus and spreading the faith.</p>

It is now used as an introduction for anyone interested in learning about Christianity. Described as “an opportunity to explore the meaning of life,” Alpha courses are held in homes, workplaces, universities, prisons, and churches.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>The Church Army</strong> — helps people find faith and works with vulnerable and marginalised people in society, bringing them into the Church.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Christ for all Nations (CfaN)</strong> — an international evangelism organisation that holds large-scale events to spread the Christian gospel worldwide.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Fresh Expressions of Church</strong> — new and creative forms of church designed to engage with people who might not otherwise think of attending a traditional church service.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n15">The Church’s Response to World Poverty</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n16">Christians believe they have a responsibility to help those living in poverty because Jesus taught that this was important. The causes of world poverty include debt, natural disasters, disease, conflict, unfair trade, lack of education, inadequate healthcare, and corrupt leaders. Three major Christian charities work to address these causes.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. What does CAFOD stand for? List each part from memory.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Three key Christian charities that respond to world poverty are CAFOD, Tearfund, and Christian Aid. Each provides both emergency relief and long-term development support, motivated by Jesus’ teachings about caring for the poor.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>CAFOD, Tearfund, and Christian Aid</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n18"><strong>CAFOD</strong> (Catholic Agency for Overseas Development) works to train and support communities to work their way out of poverty. It provides short-term aid such as food, water, and shelter during disasters. CAFOD also campaigns with the government and encourages Catholic churches and schools to donate money and pray for those in need.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n19"><strong>Tearfund</strong> works with churches worldwide to lift people out of poverty. It supplies emergency aid after natural disasters and provides long-term aid to help communities become more self-reliant. Tearfund is supported by fundraising and prayer from churches.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Christian Aid</strong> supports projects to encourage sustainable development. It provides emergency relief such as food, water, shelter, and sanitation. Christian Aid also campaigns to end poverty and global warming, and works to encourage fair trade.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n21">Biblical Teachings on Poverty</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22">Jesus’ teachings provide the motivation for Christian responses to world poverty. Jesus once told a rich man to sell everything and give to the poor, saying: “It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for someone who is rich to enter the Kingdom of God.” The Parable of the Rich Man and Lazarus tells of a wealthy man who ends up in hell for ignoring a beggar at his gate.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n23">The Bible also teaches: “Faith without action is dead” (James 2:17), meaning Christians must put their beliefs into practical action. In 1 John 3:17–18, it says: “If anyone has material possessions and sees a brother or sister in need but has no pity on them, how can the love of God be in that person? Dear children, let us not love with words or speech but with actions and truth.”</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n24" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down what you recall about parable of the Sheep and Goats (Matthew 25).">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The Parable of the Sheep and Goats (Matthew 25) teaches that those who help the hungry, thirsty, and needy are serving God himself: “Whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers and sisters of mine, you did for me.”</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n26">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n27">The Great Commission (Matthew 28:16–20) commands Christians to make disciples of all nations through baptism and teaching — this is the foundation of mission and evangelism.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n28">CAFOD, Tearfund, and Christian Aid respond to world poverty through emergency relief, long-term development, and campaigning — motivated by Jesus’ teachings about caring for the poor.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n29">Biblical teachings such as the Parable of the Sheep and Goats and “faith without action is dead” (James 2:26) show that Christians must put their beliefs into practical action to help those in need.</li></ul>
