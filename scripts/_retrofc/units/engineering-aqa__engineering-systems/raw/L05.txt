<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Analogue and Digital Signals</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Electronic systems process information as signals. There are two types: <dfn class="term" data-def="A signal that can take any value within a continuous range, such as the output voltage from a temperature sensor varying smoothly with temperature.">analogue signals</dfn> vary continuously &mdash; like the voltage from a thermistor that rises and falls as temperature changes. <dfn class="term" data-def="A signal that can only have two states: HIGH (1) or LOW (0), represented by specific voltage levels such as 5 V and 0 V.">Digital signals</dfn> have only two states: HIGH (logic 1) or LOW (logic 0). A button that is either pressed or not pressed produces a digital signal.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Most sensors produce analogue outputs. To use an analogue sensor with a digital circuit (such as a microcontroller), an <dfn class="term" data-def="A circuit that converts a continuous analogue voltage into a discrete digital number that a microcontroller or processor can read.">analogue-to-digital converter (ADC)</dfn> is needed. The ADC samples the voltage at regular intervals and converts it into a binary number. Without an ADC, the microcontroller cannot read the sensor. Examiner reports show that half of students leave ADC questions blank &mdash; memorising this definition is a guaranteed mark.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this and explain what an ADC does in one sentence, mentioning analogue voltage, binary number and microcontroller.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>An ADC (analogue-to-digital converter) converts a continuous analogue voltage into a digital binary number that a microcontroller or processor can read. Sensors that produce analogue outputs (LDR, thermistor, microphone) all need an ADC to interface with digital control systems.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Sensors</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Sensors are input devices that convert a physical quantity into an electrical signal. The two most common in engineering exams are:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n7"><strong>LDR (light-dependent resistor)</strong> &mdash; its resistance decreases as light intensity increases. In bright light: low resistance &rarr; high current. In the dark: high resistance &rarr; low current. Used in automatic street lighting, security systems and photocopiers.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Thermistor</strong> &mdash; its resistance decreases as temperature increases (NTC type &mdash; negative temperature coefficient). Used in thermostats, engine management systems and fire alarms. A thermistor in a potential divider circuit produces an analogue voltage that changes with temperature.</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n9">Other sensors include pressure sensors (transducers), proximity sensors (inductive or capacitive), and sound/vibration sensors (microphones, piezo sensors). All produce an electrical signal proportional to the physical quantity they measure.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Logic Gates</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Logic gates are the fundamental building blocks of digital electronic circuits. Each gate has one or more binary inputs (1 or 0) and produces a single binary output determined by a logical rule. You must be able to name the gate, draw its symbol and complete its truth table.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n15"><strong>NAND gate</strong> = AND + NOT. Output is LOW only when ALL inputs are HIGH; otherwise HIGH. It is the inverse of AND. NAND gates are the most common gate in integrated circuits &mdash; any logic function can be built from NAND gates alone.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>NOR gate</strong> = OR + NOT. Output is HIGH only when ALL inputs are LOW; otherwise LOW. The inverse of OR. Like NAND, any logic function can be built from NOR gates alone.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n17"><strong>XOR gate (exclusive OR)</strong>: output is HIGH only when inputs are DIFFERENT (one is 0 and the other is 1). When both inputs are the same (both 0 or both 1), output is LOW. Used in comparator circuits and error-detection systems.</p>
  </div></div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Passive Components: Resistors, Capacitors and Diodes</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19">You must be able to identify these components by their circuit symbols and describe their function:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Fixed resistor</strong> &mdash; limits current in a circuit. Symbol: rectangular box (BS 3939 UK) or zigzag. Value in ohms (&Omega;). Used in series with LEDs to prevent burnout.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Variable resistor (rheostat)</strong> &mdash; a resistor whose value can be adjusted mechanically. Used in dimmer switches, volume controls and sensor potential dividers. Symbol: rectangular box with an arrow through it.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n22"><dfn class="term" data-def="An electronic component that stores charge and blocks DC but passes AC; used for smoothing, timing and coupling in circuits.">Capacitor</dfn> &mdash; stores electrical charge. Polarised capacitors (electrolytic) must be connected the correct way round; non-polarised capacitors can be connected either way. Used for smoothing rectified DC and as timing components.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n23"><dfn class="term" data-def="A semiconductor device that allows current to flow in one direction only; used in rectifier circuits and as protection components.">Diode</dfn> &mdash; allows current to flow in one direction only. The triangle symbol points in the direction of conventional current flow; the bar is the cathode (negative terminal). Signal diodes are small and fast; rectifying diodes handle larger currents in PSU circuits.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n24"><strong>LED (light-emitting diode)</strong> &mdash; a diode that emits light when current flows through it. Must be connected the correct way round (longer leg = anode = positive). Always used with a series resistor to limit current. Output devices in control panels and status indicators.</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n25" data-revision-tip="Cover this and sketch the circuit symbols for a resistor, capacitor, diode and LED from memory, adding a label for each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Common exam error: students identify a transistor symbol as a resistor. A resistor symbol is a box (or zigzag). A transistor symbol has three connections &mdash; collector, base and emitter &mdash; with an arrow on the emitter. A diode symbol is a triangle pointing to a bar. Memorise these three symbols and you eliminate a common source of lost marks.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n27">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n28">Analogue signals vary continuously (e.g. thermistor, LDR); digital signals have only two states (HIGH/LOW). An ADC converts analogue sensor outputs into digital numbers for a microcontroller to process.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n29">AND gate output is HIGH only when all inputs are HIGH; OR gate output is HIGH when any input is HIGH; NOT gate inverts the input. NAND, NOR and XOR are their combinations and inversions.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n30">Key components: fixed and variable resistors limit current; capacitors store charge and smooth DC; diodes allow current in one direction only; LEDs emit light when forward-biased and always need a series resistor.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n26">For logic-gate questions, always draw a truth table &mdash; don&rsquo;t try to work out the output in your head. Start with all combinations of inputs (00, 01, 10, 11 for two inputs) and apply the gate rule to each row. The most common error is confusing AND (both must be 1) with OR (at least one must be 1). For component identification questions, look carefully at the number of connections: a resistor has two, a transistor has three, an LED has two but one leg is longer. Name the component precisely &mdash; &ldquo;a part that controls current&rdquo; will not earn the identification mark.</p>
