<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Chorus 3 — Overview</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Chorus 3 covers bars 40–92 of the score and is 22 bars long. Like all the choruses in Africa, it is in <dfn class="term" data-def="A major key with three sharps: F#, C# and G#.">A major</dfn>. This section repeats the music from Chorus 1 but with three notable changes that make it the climactic moment of the song.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">The dynamics here are <dfn class="term" data-def="An Italian dynamic marking meaning loud.">forte</dfn>, continuing the pattern of the choruses being louder than the verses, which are mostly mezzo-forte. The texture remains <dfn class="term" data-def="A texture where one melody line is heard with accompaniment underneath.">homophonic</dfn>, with a melody-and-accompaniment feel, but the added layers create a fuller, more intense sound.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Three Key Changes in Chorus 3</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Chorus 3 is not identical to Chorus 1 or 2. There are three specific differences you must know for the exam:</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n6" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Can you recall all 3 items listed here?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The three changes in Chorus 3 are: (1) a new electric guitar riff in the last bar of each phrase, (2) Phrase 3 (“I bless the rains down in Africa”) is repeated five times instead of the usual number, making the section significantly longer, and (3) solo vocal improvisations are added at the end of the first, second and fourth repeats of Phrase 3.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">The Outro — Bars 93–96</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">The Outro begins at bar 93 and is 4 bars long in the score. It returns to B major, the key used for the majority of the song outside the choruses. The music repeats the material from the Introduction, using the chords of <dfn class="term" data-def="A short repeated phrase, typically used as an introduction or refrain in a song.">Riff a</dfn> — the chords ♮VII, vi and ii (A, G#m and C#m in B major).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12">On the recording, this 4-bar phrase repeats continually. The key feature of the Outro is a gradual reduction in texture. Instruments drop out one by one until the music is reduced to only the rhythm track heard at the very beginning of the song, accompanied by the bass line of Riff a. The song ends with a <dfn class="term" data-def="A gradual decrease in volume until silence, commonly used to end popular songs.">fade out</dfn>.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n13" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down what you recall about outro creates a circular structure.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The Outro creates a circular structure: the song begins with layered percussion building up and ends with the texture stripping back down to that same percussion. This mirrors the Introduction and gives the song a sense of completion.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Exam Technique: Answering Questions on Africa</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">In the listening exam, you may be asked questions about Africa using a printed score extract. Here are the key strategies:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n19"><strong>Use bar numbers.</strong> Always refer to specific bar numbers when identifying features. For example, do not just say “in the chorus” — say “in bar 43.” You need to locate features precisely in the score.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Use correct musical vocabulary.</strong> Terms like syncopation, ostinato, pentatonic, conjunct, homophonic, diatonic, and riff should appear naturally in your answers. Avoid vague words like “catchy” or “nice.”</p>
<p data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Cover multiple elements.</strong> The paper has one longer-response question, which expects you to discuss several musical elements — such as melody, rhythm, texture, harmony, dynamics, and instrumentation. Organise your answer by element to make sure you cover enough ground.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n22" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Can you recall all 4 items listed here?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The paper tells you how many times each extract will be played. Use each playing to focus on different elements. First listening: melody and structure. Second: harmony and tonality. Third: rhythm and texture. Fourth: check and add detail.</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Know what is on the score vs. what is on the recording.</strong> Some features — like the unpitched percussion intro, the electric guitar riff in Chorus 3, and the vocal improvisations — are heard on the recording but do not appear in the printed vocal score. The exam may ask about differences between the two.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Remember the key ambiguity.</strong> The standard analysis places the verses in B major, but the key could also be interpreted as E major or A lydian. The choruses are definitively in A major. Exam questions will accept any of these interpretations, so use whichever your teacher has taught.</p>
