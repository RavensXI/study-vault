--- DESCRIPTION ---
How the kidneys filter blood, control water balance via ADH, and how kidney failure is treated.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Role of the Kidneys</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The kidneys are a pair of organs responsible for two key homeostatic functions: <strong>excretion</strong> (removing metabolic waste, especially urea from protein breakdown) and <strong>osmoregulation</strong> (controlling the water and ion concentration of the blood) (B3.3e). They filter the blood continuously, retaining useful substances and excreting waste products as <dfn class="term" data-def="The liquid produced by the kidneys containing water, urea and dissolved ions; the final product of blood filtration and selective reabsorption.">urine</dfn>.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Blood is delivered to each kidney via the <strong>renal artery</strong> (bringing blood <em>to</em> the kidney) and leaves via the <strong>renal vein</strong> (carrying cleaned blood <em>away</em> from the kidney). Urine drains from the kidney via the <strong>ureter</strong> to the <dfn class="term" data-def="The muscular sac that stores urine until it is expelled from the body through the urethra.">bladder</dfn>, and is expelled via the <strong>urethra</strong>. A common exam error is stating that the artery takes blood away &mdash; remember: arteries <em>arrive</em> at the organ.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="The renal ARTERY brings blood TO the kidney; the renal VEIN takes blood AWAY. Write this down and remember: arteries arrive. This is a frequent one-mark question answered incorrectly.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The renal artery delivers blood to the kidney; the renal vein carries filtered blood away. Urine drains through the ureter to the bladder and out via the urethra.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n5">How the Kidney Filters Blood</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">The functional unit of the kidney is the <dfn class="term" data-def="The microscopic filtering unit of the kidney consisting of a glomerulus, Bowman's capsule and a tubule, where filtration and selective reabsorption occur.">nephron</dfn>. Blood enters the nephron at the <strong>glomerulus</strong> &mdash; a knot of capillaries inside a cup-shaped structure called the <strong>Bowman&rsquo;s capsule</strong>. The high blood pressure in the glomerulus forces small molecules out of the blood and into the Bowman&rsquo;s capsule &mdash; this is <dfn class="term" data-def="The first stage of kidney function in which high pressure forces small molecules (water, glucose, urea, mineral ions) from the blood into the Bowman's capsule, forming the filtrate.">ultrafiltration</dfn>. Molecules that pass through include water, glucose, urea and mineral ions. Large molecules (proteins and red blood cells) are too big to pass through and remain in the blood.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">The fluid now in the tubule (the filtrate) is not yet urine &mdash; it contains useful substances that must be recovered. As the filtrate travels along the tubule, <dfn class="term" data-def="The process by which useful substances (glucose, some water and mineral ions) are actively or passively moved from the tubule back into the surrounding capillaries.">selective reabsorption</dfn> returns all glucose (via active transport), most water, and the required amount of mineral ions back into the surrounding capillaries. What remains &mdash; water, urea and excess ions &mdash; passes on to become urine (B3.3e).</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n8">A student analyses the filtrate in the Bowman&rsquo;s capsule and the urine. They find glucose in the filtrate but not in the urine. Explain why.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Step 1:</strong> Glucose is a small molecule, so it passes through the glomerulus into the filtrate during ultrafiltration. <strong>Step 2:</strong> As the filtrate passes through the tubule, all glucose is selectively reabsorbed via active transport back into the capillaries. <strong>Step 3:</strong> Therefore, no glucose remains in the fluid that becomes urine. <em>Note:</em> If glucose were found in urine, this would suggest diabetes &mdash; blood glucose is so high that the kidneys cannot reabsorb all of it.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Water Balance and ADH</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">The amount of water reabsorbed in the kidney is regulated by the hormone <dfn class="term" data-def="Antidiuretic hormone — a hormone produced by the brain and released from the pituitary gland that causes the kidney tubules to reabsorb more water, producing concentrated urine.">ADH</dfn> (antidiuretic hormone) (B3.3f). ADH is produced in the hypothalamus and released from the pituitary gland. It acts on the collecting ducts and distal tubule of the nephron, making them more permeable to water so that more water is reabsorbed back into the blood.</p>
<div class="higher-only">
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Negative Feedback: How ADH Controls Water Levels</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n12">When the blood is too concentrated (e.g. after sweating or not drinking enough), <strong>osmoreceptors</strong> in the hypothalamus detect the increase. More ADH is released &rarr; kidney tubules become more permeable &rarr; more water is reabsorbed &rarr; small volumes of <strong>concentrated (dark) urine</strong> are produced. Blood water content returns to normal.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n13">When blood is too dilute (e.g. after drinking a lot of water), osmoreceptors detect the decrease in concentration. Less ADH is released &rarr; kidney tubules become less permeable &rarr; less water is reabsorbed &rarr; large volumes of <strong>dilute (pale) urine</strong> are produced. This is a classic negative feedback loop: the output (water reabsorption and urine concentration) reverses the original change (blood concentration).</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="More ADH = more water reabsorbed = small, concentrated, dark urine. Less ADH = less water reabsorbed = large, dilute, pale urine. Cover this and state what happens to urine when you are dehydrated.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>ADH controls water reabsorption in the kidney. High blood concentration &rarr; more ADH &rarr; concentrated urine. Low blood concentration &rarr; less ADH &rarr; dilute urine. This is negative feedback control of water balance (<dfn class="term" data-def="The process by which the kidneys regulate blood water potential by adjusting the volume and concentration of urine produced, controlled by ADH.">osmoregulation</dfn>).</p>
</div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n15">Kidney Failure and Treatments</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n16">If the kidneys fail, toxic waste products (especially urea) and excess water build up in the blood. Without treatment, this is fatal. Two main treatments exist for kidney failure (B3.3g): <dfn class="term" data-def="A medical procedure that uses a machine to filter waste products and excess water from the blood when the kidneys have failed.">dialysis</dfn> and kidney transplantation.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Dialysis</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n17">In <strong>haemodialysis</strong>, the patient&rsquo;s blood is passed through a machine containing a partially permeable membrane. On the other side of the membrane is <strong>dialysis fluid</strong>, which has the same concentration of useful substances (glucose, mineral ions) as normal blood but contains no urea. Urea and excess ions diffuse from the blood through the membrane into the dialysis fluid, which is discarded. The cleaned blood is returned to the patient.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n18">Dialysis must be performed regularly (typically three times per week, each session lasting several hours). It maintains life but does not cure kidney failure. Patients must follow a careful diet to manage urea build-up between sessions. It is a significant commitment and can affect quality of life.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Kidney Transplantation</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n19">A kidney transplant replaces the failed organ with a healthy one from a compatible donor (living or recently deceased). If successful, the transplanted kidney functions normally and the patient no longer needs dialysis. Donors with closely matched <strong>tissue types</strong> (determined by surface antigens on cells) are preferable because they reduce the risk of the immune system rejecting the new organ.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n20">To prevent rejection, patients must take <strong>immunosuppressant drugs</strong> for the rest of their lives. These reduce immune system activity, preventing rejection &mdash; but they also make the patient more vulnerable to infection. Despite these disadvantages, a successful transplant typically offers a better quality of life than long-term dialysis. The main limiting factor is the availability of suitable donor kidneys.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>


--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n22">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n23">The kidneys filter blood through ultrafiltration (small molecules into the filtrate) then selectively reabsorb glucose, useful ions and water. The renal artery brings blood to the kidney; the renal vein carries it away.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n24">ADH controls water reabsorption via negative feedback: high blood concentration &rarr; more ADH &rarr; concentrated urine; low blood concentration &rarr; less ADH &rarr; dilute urine.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">Kidney failure is treated by dialysis (regular blood filtration through a partially permeable membrane) or kidney transplant (better quality of life but requires immunosuppressant drugs and a compatible donor).</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n21">Questions about the kidney often test the difference between ultrafiltration (everything small goes through the glomerulus into the filtrate) and selective reabsorption (useful substances like glucose are taken back). If asked what is found in urine, your answer should be urea, water and excess mineral ions &mdash; never glucose or protein (unless the question implies a medical condition). For ADH questions, always link the level of ADH to urine concentration: more ADH &rarr; more concentrated urine. For dialysis, explain the mechanism using diffusion through a partially permeable membrane &mdash; don&rsquo;t just say &lsquo;the machine cleans the blood&rsquo;.</p>
