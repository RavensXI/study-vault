<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Happens After We Die?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">One of the biggest questions humans have ever asked is what happens after death. Different religions and worldviews give very different answers. For AQA Religious Studies, you need to understand <dfn class="term" data-def="Everlasting life after death; the belief that existence continues beyond physical death.">akhirah</dfn> (the Islamic concept of life after death), Christian beliefs about the afterlife, and how non-religious people respond to the question.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">These beliefs are not just abstract ideas — they directly shape how people value human life and make moral decisions while they are alive.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Recall the specific examples given for christians and Muslims believe in.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Christians and Muslims believe in heaven (paradise) and hell. Some Christians, such as Catholics, also believe in purgatory. Non-religious people generally do not believe in any afterlife.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Christian Beliefs About the Afterlife</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Christians believe that after death, people face judgement from God. Those who have lived according to God’s will are rewarded with eternal life in <dfn class="term" data-def="A state of eternal happiness in the presence of God.">heaven</dfn>, while those who have rejected God face eternal separation from him in <dfn class="term" data-def="Eternal suffering and separation from God.">hell</dfn>.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">There are two main Christian beliefs about how the afterlife works:</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Immortality of the Soul vs Resurrection of the Body</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Immortality of the soul</strong> — Some Christians believe the soul is eternal and continues living immediately after death. Jesus told the thief on the cross: “Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in Paradise” (Luke 23:43). This suggests the afterlife begins straight away.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Resurrection of the body</strong> — Other Christians believe that the dead will be physically raised at the end of time. Bible verses describe people “rising from their graves,” suggesting a future bodily resurrection on Judgement Day.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n10">Some Christians, including Catholics, believe in both — the soul lives on after death but will be reunited with a resurrected body at the end of time.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Purgatory — A Catholic Belief</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11"><dfn class="term" data-def="A state after death where souls are purified of sin before entering heaven; a belief held mainly by Catholics.">Purgatory</dfn> is a state that some Christians, particularly Catholics, believe people go to after death in order to atone for their sins before they can enter heaven. It is not a permanent destination but a place of purification.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n12">Many Protestant Christians do not believe in purgatory because Jesus does not mention it directly in the Bible. They believe people go straight to heaven or hell after judgement.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Muslim Beliefs About the Afterlife</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">In Islam, belief in akhirah (life after death) is one of the six articles of faith. Muslims believe that life on earth is a test from Allah. How a person lives determines what happens to them after death.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down what you recall about muslims believe that after death.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Muslims believe that after death, the soul enters a state called <dfn class="term" data-def="A barrier or state of waiting between death and the Day of Judgement in Islamic belief.">barzakh</dfn> — a waiting period between death and the Day of Judgement. During barzakh, the soul remains in the grave and is questioned by angels about its faith and actions.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n16">On the Day of Judgement, all people will be resurrected and brought before Allah. Their deeds will be weighed, and those who have lived righteously will enter <dfn class="term" data-def="Paradise in Islam; a state of eternal peace and happiness ruled over by God.">Jannah</dfn> (paradise), while those who have done evil will be sent to <dfn class="term" data-def="Hell in Islam; a place of eternal suffering for those who have rejected God.">Jahannam</dfn> (hell).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n17">The Qur’an describes Jannah as a place of gardens, rivers, and eternal peace. Jahannam is described as a place of fire and torment. Muslims believe that living a good life — following the Five Pillars, showing compassion, and obeying Allah — is the way to earn a place in paradise.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Non-Religious Views</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19">Non-religious people, such as atheists and humanists, generally do not believe in any form of afterlife. They argue that science can only prove the existence of the physical world, and there is no evidence for a soul or a spiritual realm. When the body dies, consciousness ends.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Some non-religious people find comfort in the idea that a person’s legacy — their impact on others and the world — continues after they die, even without a supernatural afterlife.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n21" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down what you recall about other religions have different views: Hindus.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Other religions have different views: Hindus, Buddhists, and Sikhs believe in reincarnation — being reborn in a new body after death. Judaism does not have a single fixed concept of the afterlife.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n22">How Beliefs About Death Affect the Value of Life</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n23">Beliefs about what happens after death have a direct impact on how people value human life:</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>The Link Between Afterlife Beliefs and How People Live</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Those who do not believe in an afterlife</strong> may value human life more, because they see it as the only life a person has. This can motivate people to make the most of their time and treat others well.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n25"><strong>Those who believe in an afterlife</strong> may value human life because it is sacred and given by God. However, some may focus more on the afterlife than on earthly life. Christians and Muslims know they will be with God in heaven if they act the way he wants them to.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n26"><strong>Those who believe in reincarnation</strong> may value human life as a means to achieve a better next life through good actions (karma).</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n27">For Christians and Muslims, belief in the afterlife reinforces the sanctity of life. Life is valuable because God has given it, and only God should decide when it ends. Muslims believe life is a test — they will be judged on their actions and rewarded or punished accordingly. This belief motivates ethical behaviour and care for others.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n29">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n30">Christians believe in heaven and hell; some also believe in purgatory and hold different views on whether the soul is immortal, the body is resurrected, or both.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n31">Muslims believe life is a test from Allah, followed by barzakh (waiting), the Day of Judgement, and then Jannah (paradise) or Jahannam (hell).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n32">Beliefs about the afterlife directly affect how people value life — whether they see it as sacred, as a test, or as the only existence they will have.</li></ul>
