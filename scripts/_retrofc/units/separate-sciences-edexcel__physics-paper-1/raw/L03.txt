<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Energy Stores</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Energy cannot be created or destroyed &mdash; it can only be transferred between <dfn class="term" data-def="The different forms in which energy can be held, such as kinetic, thermal, gravitational potential, chemical, elastic potential, electrostatic, magnetic, and nuclear.">energy stores</dfn>. The key stores to know are: kinetic, thermal (heat), chemical, elastic potential, gravitational potential (GPE), electrostatic, magnetic, and nuclear. Each store holds energy until a transfer pathway moves it elsewhere.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3"><dfn class="term" data-def="The energy store associated with a moving object, dependent on its mass and speed.">Kinetic energy</dfn> is the energy stored in a moving object. It is calculated using:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n4">$$E_k = \frac{1}{2} m v^2$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Where \(E_k\) is kinetic energy in joules (J), \(m\) is mass in kilograms (kg), and \(v\) is speed in m/s. Notice that doubling the speed <em>quadruples</em> the kinetic energy (because of \(v^2\)).</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n6" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and write the eight energy stores. Then state the equation for kinetic energy and explain why doubling speed has a bigger effect than doubling mass.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Eight energy stores: kinetic, thermal, chemical, elastic potential, gravitational potential, electrostatic, magnetic, nuclear. Key equation: \(E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2\). Doubling speed quadruples \(E_k\); doubling mass only doubles it. This explains why faster vehicles are much harder to stop.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n7">Gravitational Potential Energy</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n8"><dfn class="term" data-def="The energy stored in an object by virtue of its position in a gravitational field; increases as an object is raised higher.">Gravitational potential energy</dfn> (GPE) is calculated using:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9">$$E_p = m g h$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n10">Where \(E_p\) is GPE in joules, \(m\) is mass in kg, \(g\) is gravitational field strength (10 N/kg near Earth&rsquo;s surface), and \(h\) is height in metres. GPE increases when an object is raised and decreases when it falls.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n12">$$E_e = \frac{1}{2} k e^2$$</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n13">Where \(E_e\) is elastic potential energy in J, \(k\) is the spring constant in N/m, and \(e\) is the extension (or compression) in metres. This equation only applies below the <dfn class="term" data-def="The maximum extension at which Hooke&rsquo;s law applies; beyond this point, deformation is not fully reversible.">limit of proportionality</dfn> &mdash; beyond this, the spring deforms permanently and the equation no longer holds.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Energy Transfer Pathways</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15">Energy moves between stores via four transfer pathways: by <strong>mechanical work</strong> (a force moving an object), by <strong>electrical work</strong> (a current doing work), by <strong>heating</strong> (thermal conduction, convection, or radiation), and by <strong>radiation</strong> (electromagnetic or sound waves). The pathway is not the same as the store &mdash; heating is a pathway; thermal energy is a store.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n16">A useful example: when a ball is thrown upwards, energy transfers from the kinetic store (moving ball) to the gravitational potential store (raised ball). At the peak, the ball is momentarily still &mdash; kinetic store is empty, GPE store is at its maximum. As the ball falls, the transfer reverses.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n21">The Law of Conservation of Energy</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22">The <dfn class="term" data-def="Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be transferred between stores or dissipated to the surroundings, but the total always remains constant.">law of conservation of energy</dfn> states that the total energy in a closed system always remains constant. Energy is never lost &mdash; it is transferred usefully, or it is <dfn class="term" data-def="Energy that is transferred to the thermal store of the surroundings and is no longer useful; sometimes called wasted energy.">dissipated</dfn> (spread out to the thermal store of the surroundings). In a real system, some energy is always dissipated as thermal energy.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n23" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and explain what conservation of energy means for a real motor that converts electrical energy into kinetic energy.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Conservation of energy: total energy in a closed system is constant. In a real motor, input electrical energy = useful kinetic energy output + thermal energy dissipated. Energy is not &lsquo;lost&rsquo; &mdash; it is spread out as low-grade heat in the surroundings.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n24">Mechanical Work Done</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n25">When a force moves an object through a distance, energy is transferred as <dfn class="term" data-def="The energy transferred when a force causes movement; W = Fs, measured in joules.">mechanical work</dfn>:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n26">$$W = F s$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n27">Where \(W\) is work done in joules, \(F\) is force in newtons, and \(s\) is the distance moved along the direction of the force in metres. 1 joule = 1 newton&ndash;metre. If you push a wall and it doesn&rsquo;t move, no work is done &mdash; even if you exert a large force.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n28">Efficiency</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n29"><dfn class="term" data-def="The proportion of input energy that is transferred usefully; efficiency = useful output energy ÷ total input energy, often expressed as a percentage or decimal.">Efficiency</dfn> measures how well a device converts input energy to useful output energy:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n30">$$\text{efficiency} = \frac{\text{useful output energy}}{\text{total input energy}}$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n31">Efficiency can be expressed as a decimal (e.g. 0.75) or a percentage (75%). No real device is 100% efficient because some energy is always dissipated as thermal energy. Efficiency can also be calculated using power: efficiency = useful output power / total input power.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n35">Power</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n36"><dfn class="term" data-def="The rate at which energy is transferred or work is done; measured in watts (W), where 1 W = 1 J/s.">Power</dfn> is the rate of energy transfer or the rate of doing work:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n37">490647P = \frac{E}{t} = \frac{W}{t}490647</p>
<p data-narration-id="n38">Where \(P\) is power in watts (W), \(E\) is energy in joules, \(W\) is work done in joules, and \(t\) is time in seconds. One watt equals one joule per second. A 100 W light bulb transfers 100 J of energy every second. For a force moving an object at constant speed, power can also be written as \(P = Fv\), where \(v\) is speed in m/s &mdash; useful when calculating engine power output.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n39">The specific heat capacity practical investigates how much energy is needed to raise the temperature of a substance by 1 &deg;C per kg. A metal block is heated electrically using a known power supply; temperature is measured at regular time intervals. The energy supplied is calculated as \(E = P \times t\), and specific heat capacity is found from \(Q = mc\Delta T\) rearranged to \(c = Q / (m \Delta T)\). Insulating the block and using a thermometer in the centre of the block reduce systematic errors from heat loss and uneven temperature distribution.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n41">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n42">Eight energy stores: kinetic, thermal, chemical, elastic potential, GPE, electrostatic, magnetic, nuclear. Key equations: \(E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2\); \(E_p = mgh\); \(E_e = \tfrac{1}{2}ke^2\). Transfer pathways: mechanical work, electrical work, heating, radiation.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n43">Conservation of energy: total energy in a closed system is constant; some is always dissipated as thermal energy. Mechanical work: \(W = Fs\). Efficiency = useful output energy &divide; total input energy (always &lt; 1 for real devices).</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n40">For energy calculation questions, write the equation first, substitute numbers with units clearly, and make sure you identify what type of energy store you are calculating before you start. A common error is applying \(E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2\) but forgetting to square \(v\) &mdash; this halves your answer and loses the final mark. For GPE-to-KE questions, set \(E_p = E_k\) and solve for the unknown &mdash; always check your answer makes physical sense (a speed of 10 000 m/s for a falling ball is clearly wrong). For efficiency questions, always check your answer is less than 1 (or less than 100%); an answer greater than 1 means you have divided input by output rather than the other way round.</p>
