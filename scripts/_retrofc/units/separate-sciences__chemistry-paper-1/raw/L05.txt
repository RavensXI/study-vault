<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Are Moles?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">A <dfn class="term" data-def="The amount of substance containing 6.02 × 10²³ particles.">mole</dfn> is the unit chemists use to count atoms, molecules and ions. Just like a dozen equals 12 items, one mole equals 6.02 × 10²³ particles. This huge number is called <dfn class="term" data-def="The number of particles in one mole of any substance, equal to 6.02 × 10²³.">Avogadro's constant</dfn>.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Recall the mass in grams of one mole of carbon and explain how this relates to its relative atomic mass.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>One mole of any element has a mass in grams equal to its relative atomic mass. For example, one mole of carbon (relative atomic mass 12) has a mass of 12g.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n4">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The mass of one mole of a substance in grams.">molar mass</dfn> connects the microscopic world of atoms to the macroscopic world we can measure. For compounds, we add up the relative atomic masses of all atoms present.</p>
</div>

<div class="higher-only">
<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Calculating Moles from Mass</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n6">The fundamental equation linking moles, mass and molar mass is:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n7">\(\text{number of moles} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{molar mass}}\)</p>

<p data-narration-id="n8">This can be rearranged to find mass or molar mass depending on what information you're given.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Worked Example: Calculating Moles</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n9">Calculate the number of moles in 44g of carbon dioxide (CO₂).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n10">Step 1: Find the molar mass of CO₂</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Carbon: 1 × 12 = 12g/mol</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Oxygen: 2 × 16 = 32g/mol</p>
<p data-narration-id="n13">Total molar mass = 12 + 32 = 44g/mol</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Step 2: Apply the formula</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15">\(\text{moles} = \frac{44}{44} = 1 \text{ mole}\)</p>
</div></div>
</div>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Concentration of Solutions</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n17">When substances dissolve in water, we create solutions. The <dfn class="term" data-def="The amount of solute dissolved per unit volume of solution.">concentration</dfn> tells us how much solute is dissolved in a given volume of solution.</p>

<div class="higher-only">
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n18" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Name the two units used to measure concentration and state which measures mass and which measures moles.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Concentration can be measured in g/dm³ (mass concentration) or mol/dm³ (molar concentration). Both are essential for different types of calculations.</p>
</div>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n19">The two main concentration equations you need to know are:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n20">$$\text{concentration (g/dm³)} = \frac{\text{mass of solute (g)}}{\text{volume of solution (dm³)}}$$</p>

<div class="higher-only">
<p data-narration-id="n21">\(\text{concentration (mol/dm³)} = \frac{\text{moles of solute}}{\text{volume of solution (dm³)}}\)</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Converting Between Units</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n22">Remember these volume conversions:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n23">1 dm³ = 1000 cm³ = 1000 ml = 1 litre</li>
<li data-narration-id="n24">To convert cm³ to dm³: divide by 1000</li>
<li data-narration-id="n25">To convert dm³ to cm³: multiply by 1000</li>
</ul>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n26">Calculating Masses in Reactions</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n27">Chemical equations show the molar ratios between reactants and products. We can use these ratios along with molar masses to calculate the masses of substances involved in reactions.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n28">The process follows these steps:</p>
<ol>
<li data-narration-id="n29">Write the balanced chemical equation</li>
<li data-narration-id="n30">Identify the molar ratio from the equation</li>
<li data-narration-id="n31">Calculate moles of the known substance</li>
<li data-narration-id="n32">Use the molar ratio to find moles of unknown substance</li>
<li data-narration-id="n33">Convert moles back to mass using molar mass</li>
</ol>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Worked Example: Mass Calculations</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n34">Calculate the mass of water produced when 16g of methane burns completely.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n35">\(CH_4 + 2O_2 \rightarrow CO_2 + 2H_2O\)</p>
<p data-narration-id="n36">Step 1: Calculate moles of methane</p>
<p data-narration-id="n37">Molar mass of CH₄ = 12 + (4 × 1) = 16 g/mol</p>
<p data-narration-id="n38">\(\text{moles of CH}_4 = \frac{16}{16} = 1 \text{ mole}\)</p>
<p data-narration-id="n39">Step 2: Use molar ratio</p>
<p data-narration-id="n40">From equation: 1 mole CH₄ produces 2 moles H₂O</p>
<p data-narration-id="n41">Therefore: moles of H₂O = 1 × 2 = 2 moles</p>
<p data-narration-id="n42">Step 3: Convert to mass</p>
<p data-narration-id="n43">Molar mass of H₂O = (2 × 1) + 16 = 18 g/mol</p>
<p data-narration-id="n44">\(\text{mass of H}_2\text{O} = 2 × 18 = 36\text{g}\)</p>
</div></div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="higher-only">
<h2 data-narration-id="n45">Limiting Reactants</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n46">In many reactions, one reactant runs out before the others. This <dfn class="term" data-def="The reactant that is completely used up first, limiting the amount of product formed.">limiting reactant</dfn> determines how much product can be formed.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n47">To identify the limiting reactant, calculate how many moles of each reactant you have, then compare this to the molar ratios in the balanced equation. The reactant that would be used up first is the limiting reactant.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n48">Common Calculation Mistakes</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n49">Students often make errors when working with quantitative chemistry. Always check your balanced equations are correct before starting calculations. Make sure you convert units properly — especially converting cm³ to dm³ for concentration calculations. Remember that molar mass uses the relative atomic mass from the periodic table, and for compounds you must add up all the atoms present.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n51">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n52">One mole contains 6.02 × 10²³ particles and has a mass equal to the relative formula mass in grams</li>
<li data-narration-id="n53">Use moles = mass ÷ molar mass to convert between mass and moles</li>
<li data-narration-id="n54">Concentration links the amount of solute to the volume of solution using mass/volume or moles/volume</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n50">In moles calculations, always show your working clearly. Start by writing the equation you're using, substitute the numbers, then calculate the answer. Check your units match throughout — if concentration is in mol/dm³, make sure volume is in dm³ not cm³. For mass calculation questions, always write the balanced equation first as this shows the examiner you understand the molar ratios.</p>
