--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Acids, Bases and the pH Scale</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">An <dfn class="term" data-def="A substance that produces hydrogen ions (H+) when dissolved in water. Acids have a pH below 7.">acid</dfn> is a substance that produces hydrogen ions (\(H^+\)) when dissolved in water. A <dfn class="term" data-def="A substance that can neutralise an acid. Alkalis are soluble bases that produce hydroxide ions (OH−) in solution.">base</dfn> is a substance that can neutralise an acid. An <dfn class="term" data-def="A soluble base that produces hydroxide ions (OH−) when dissolved in water. Alkalis have a pH above 7.">alkali</dfn> is a soluble base that produces hydroxide ions (\(OH^-\)) in water.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">The <dfn class="term" data-def="A scale from 0 to 14 that measures how acidic or alkaline a solution is. Below 7 = acidic, 7 = neutral, above 7 = alkaline.">pH scale</dfn> runs from 0 to 14. Acids have a pH below 7, neutral solutions have a pH of 7, and alkalis have a pH above 7. pH can be measured with universal indicator or a pH probe.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and write the neutralisation equation showing H+ and OH- ions forming water.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Acids produce \(H^+\) ions in water; alkalis produce \(OH^-\) ions. The pH scale: 0–6 = acidic, 7 = neutral, 8–14 = alkaline. Neutralisation: \(H^+ + OH^- \rightarrow H_2O\).</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Neutralisation Reactions</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n6"><dfn class="term" data-def="A reaction between an acid and a base that produces a salt and water. The H+ ions from the acid combine with the OH− ions from the base.">Neutralisation</dfn> occurs when an acid reacts with a base. The ionic equation is:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n7">$$H^+ + OH^- \rightarrow H_2O$$</p>

<p data-narration-id="n8">The key reactions of acids are:</p>

<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Acid + metal</strong> → salt + hydrogen. E.g. \(Mg + 2HCl \rightarrow MgCl_2 + H_2\)</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Acid + metal oxide</strong> → salt + water. E.g. \(CuO + H_2SO_4 \rightarrow CuSO_4 + H_2O\)</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Acid + metal hydroxide</strong> → salt + water. E.g. \(NaOH + HCl \rightarrow NaCl + H_2O\)</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Acid + metal carbonate</strong> → salt + water + carbon dioxide. E.g. \(CaCO_3 + 2HCl \rightarrow CaCl_2 + H_2O + CO_2\)</li>
</ul>

  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>HCl → chlorides. \(H_2SO_4\) → sulfates. \(HNO_3\) → nitrates. To predict the salt name: metal name + acid type (e.g. magnesium + sulfuric acid = magnesium sulfate).</p>
</div>

    <p data-narration-id="n16"><dfn class="term" data-def="An acid that fully ionises / dissociates in water, producing the maximum number of H+ ions. Examples: HCl, H2SO4, HNO3.">Strong acids</dfn> (e.g. hydrochloric, sulfuric, nitric acid) fully ionise in water — every acid molecule releases its \(H^+\) ions. <dfn class="term" data-def="An acid that only partially ionises in water, producing fewer H+ ions. Examples: ethanoic acid, citric acid.">Weak acids</dfn> (e.g. ethanoic acid, citric acid) only partially ionise — an equilibrium is established between ionised and un-ionised molecules. At the same concentration, a strong acid has a lower pH than a weak acid.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n18">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n19">Acids produce \(H^+\) ions, alkalis produce \(OH^-\) ions. Neutralisation: \(H^+ + OH^- \rightarrow H_2O\).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n20">Acid + base &rarr; salt + water. HCl gives chlorides, \(H_2SO_4\) gives sulfates, \(HNO_3\) gives nitrates.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n21">Strong acids fully ionise; weak acids partially ionise. A strong acid at the same concentration has a lower pH than a weak acid.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n17">When predicting the products of an acid reaction, identify the acid (for the salt type) and the base (for the metal in the salt). Then write: salt + water (for bases/oxides/hydroxides) or salt + water + CO\(_2\) (for carbonates). Always balance your equations.</p>
