--- DESCRIPTION ---
The reactivity series, displacement reactions, and how metals are extracted from their ores.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Reactivity Series</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">Metals can be arranged in order of their reactivity — this is called the <dfn class="term" data-def="A list of metals arranged in order of their reactivity, from most reactive (potassium) to least reactive (gold). The position determines how the metal is extracted.">reactivity series</dfn>. The most reactive metals (potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, aluminium) react vigorously with water and acids. Less reactive metals (zinc, iron, copper, silver, gold) react more slowly or not at all.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">A more reactive metal will <dfn class="term" data-def="A reaction where a more reactive metal takes the place of a less reactive metal in a compound, e.g. zinc displacing copper from copper sulfate solution.">displace</dfn> a less reactive metal from its compound. For example, if iron is placed in copper sulfate solution, the iron displaces the copper:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n4">$$Fe + CuSO_4 \rightarrow FeSO_4 + Cu$$</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and write out the full reactivity series from potassium to gold, including carbon and hydrogen positions.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The reactivity series (most to least reactive): K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, C, Zn, Fe, H, Cu, Ag, Au. Carbon and hydrogen are included as reference points. A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its compound.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Extraction of Metals</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n7">Metals are found in the Earth’s crust, often combined with other elements in <dfn class="term" data-def="A rock from which a metal can be extracted economically. Ores contain enough metal to make extraction worthwhile.">ores</dfn>. The method of extraction depends on the metal’s position in the reactivity series:</p>

<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Metals less reactive than carbon</strong> (e.g. iron, copper, zinc) are extracted by <dfn class="term" data-def="The removal of oxygen from a metal oxide using carbon. Carbon acts as a reducing agent, taking the oxygen away from the metal.">reduction with carbon</dfn>. Carbon is cheaper and more reactive, so it removes the oxygen from the metal oxide.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Metals more reactive than carbon</strong> (e.g. aluminium, sodium, potassium) must be extracted by <strong>electrolysis</strong>, which is more expensive because it requires large amounts of electricity.</li>
</ul>

<p data-narration-id="n10">For example, iron is extracted from iron oxide using carbon in a blast furnace:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n11">$$2Fe_2O_3 + 3C \rightarrow 4Fe + 3CO_2$$</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n12"><dfn class="term" data-def="A reaction where a substance gains oxygen or loses electrons.">Oxidation</dfn> is the gain of oxygen (or loss of electrons). <dfn class="term" data-def="A reaction where a substance loses oxygen or gains electrons.">Reduction</dfn> is the loss of oxygen (or gain of electrons). Remember: <strong>OIL RIG</strong> — Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons), Reduction Is Gain. In a displacement reaction, the more reactive metal is oxidised (loses electrons) and the less reactive metal ion is reduced (gains electrons).</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n13" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and explain why zinc is extracted with carbon but aluminum needs electrolysis, plus define oxidation and reduction.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Metals below carbon in the reactivity series are extracted by reduction with carbon. Metals above carbon need electrolysis. Oxidation = gain of oxygen / loss of electrons. Reduction = loss of oxygen / gain of electrons.</p>
</div>

However, collection and sorting can be difficult, and recycled metals may be impure.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n16">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n17">The reactivity series ranks metals from most to least reactive; more reactive metals displace less reactive ones.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n18">Metals below carbon are extracted by reduction with carbon; metals above carbon need electrolysis.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n19">OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss of electrons, Reduction Is Gain. Recycling metals saves energy and resources.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n15">When writing equations for displacement reactions, always check your reactivity series first. The more reactive metal replaces the less reactive one. For extraction questions, state whether the metal is above or below carbon and therefore whether it needs electrolysis or reduction with carbon.</p>
