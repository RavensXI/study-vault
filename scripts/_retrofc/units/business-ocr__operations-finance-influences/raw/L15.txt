--- DESCRIPTION ---
How operations, finance, marketing and HR connect — the synoptic skill Paper 2 tests.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">A Business Works as One System</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Every business has four main functions — operations, finance, marketing and human resources — and none of them works alone. A decision in one area ripples straight into the others. Launch a new product and marketing needs to know who the customer is, operations needs to know how to make it, finance needs to know what it will cost and what it will earn, and HR needs to know what skills the team needs to deliver it. This is the <dfn class="term" data-def="The way the four business functions — operations, finance, marketing and human resources — depend on each other, so a decision in one area affects all the others.">interdependent nature of business</dfn>, and it is the single biggest idea the final part of the OCR course asks you to carry into the exam.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">The lesson also introduces how financial information measures and explains performance, and how risk and reward weaves through every decision. A well-prepared student will not just describe each function separately — they will show how the functions connect.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this and list the four business functions, then say one way each depends on another.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The four business functions — operations, finance, marketing and HR — are interdependent. A decision in any one function affects all the others, which is why every major business decision has to be planned across the whole firm, not in one department alone.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Worked Example — Greggs Launches the Vegan Sausage Roll</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">In January 2019 the UK bakery chain Greggs launched a vegan sausage roll nationwide. It became the fastest-selling product in the company’s history, made the front pages, and helped push annual sales up by 7.4%. Behind the headline launch sat months of co-ordinated work across every function. Follow it through and every interdependence becomes visible.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n7">Marketing — Spotting the Opportunity</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n8">Marketing started with research. Veganuary, a UK campaign encouraging people to try plant-based eating for January, had grown tenfold between 2015 and 2018. Greggs saw a clear customer demand its current menu did not serve. Marketing built the positioning (a product as close to the original sausage roll as possible) and planned the release date to catch Veganuary. It also built the famous “vegan sausage roll in an iPhone box” media teaser, which generated free press coverage worth far more than the paid budget.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n9">Operations — Making It Work</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n10">Operations had to solve a real problem: create a sausage-roll substitute that tasted and cooked the same as the meat original, using Quorn as the filling, in factories that had always handled pork. The recipe had to be shelf-stable for daily delivery to 1,900 shops. Supply chain had to be scaled so the new product launched on day one in every shop — not just London. Stock levels had to anticipate demand that marketing was deliberately stoking up.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n11">Finance — Funding and Forecasting</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Finance modelled the cost: new ingredients, new packaging, new adverts, possible losses if the product flopped. It worked out a price that covered costs and left a margin; it set a sales forecast that let operations know how much to produce; it made sure there was cash to pay Quorn and the new suppliers before customers started buying. If finance had underestimated demand and run out of cash for ingredients, the launch would have stalled halfway through January.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n13">HR — Training and Communication</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n14">HR had to train thousands of shop staff on the new product — why it was vegan, how to heat it, how to answer customer questions. It had to brief managers on the media interest, because Greggs shops would suddenly be on the news. In some stores, HR had to adjust rotas to cope with the surge in lunchtime footfall. None of this was visible to customers, but without it the launch would have been chaos on day one.</p>

If operations had not scaled factories, shelves would have been empty. Every function enables the others. A successful launch is a system, not a department.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Risk and Reward in Every Decision</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">Every cross-function decision balances <dfn class="term" data-def="The chance that a decision will not pay off, weighed against the potential benefit if it does.">risk and reward</dfn>. Greggs could have stuck with its existing menu — zero launch cost, zero risk — but also zero share of the growing vegan market. By launching, it accepted real risks: the product might flop, factories might struggle, customers might reject the change. The reward was huge if it worked. In the event, it worked. But the decision had to be made before the outcome was known — that is what every business leader faces.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Risk and reward is not just about new products. Every pay rise, every supplier change, every store closure balances an upside against a downside. A good answer recognises this in the case-study business in front of you, not as a separate theory.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n19">How Financial Information Measures Performance</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Financial information is how a business checks whether interdependent decisions are working. <dfn class="term" data-def="Data produced by the finance function — revenue, costs, profit, margins, cash flow and ratios — used to measure performance and guide decisions.">Financial information</dfn> includes revenue, costs, profit and loss, gross and net margins, average rate of return, and cash flow figures. Greggs measured the vegan launch through sales data (did the forecast hold?), cost data (did production come in on budget?), profit margin (did the pricing work?), and cash flow (did the firm stay liquid through the launch surge?).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n21">Financial information is the feedback loop. Marketing may claim the campaign worked, but only finance can confirm how the figures moved. Operations may say production ran smoothly, but only cost data confirms whether the promised margin held. Without financial information to measure outcomes, the four functions cannot tell what is working or where to adjust next.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n22" data-revision-tip="Close this and test yourself: given a case study about a UK restaurant opening a new branch, list one task for each of the four business functions.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Synoptic questions on the synoptic section of the final paper ask you to link content from the whole course. Work through all four functions in turn, connect each to risk and reward, and use any financial information in the stimulus to support your judgement.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n23">How OCR Synoptic Questions Are Framed</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n24">the synoptic section of the final paper includes at least two synoptic questions. These are usually extended-response Discuss, Evaluate or Recommend questions worth 9 or 12 marks. The stimulus typically gives you a business facing a decision — opening a new branch, launching a product, switching supplier, entering a new market — plus numbers (a cash flow extract, a break-even figure, a margin, a sales target). To score full marks you need to:</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Structure for a Synoptic Answer</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n25"><strong>Step 1 — Identify at least two functions involved.</strong> Do not answer from only one. A new branch involves operations (site, staff) AND finance (cost, cash) AND marketing (local awareness) AND HR (hiring). Name the functions explicitly.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n26"><strong>Step 2 — Link them.</strong> Do not list separately. Show how the finance decision enables the operations plan and how marketing success depends on HR training. The word ‘because’ is your friend.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n27"><strong>Step 3 — Use the numbers.</strong> If the stimulus gives a break-even figure, margin or cash balance, use it in your reasoning. Marks are left on the table when candidates ignore the data.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n28"><strong>Step 4 — Make a supported judgement.</strong> Recommend questions need a clear decision. Weigh one side against the other and justify your answer in the specific context — ‘it depends on X’ is acceptable only if you justify which value of X would change the decision.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n30">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n31">Operations, finance, marketing and HR are interdependent — a decision in one ripples through all four.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n32">Every business decision balances risk and reward, and financial information is the feedback loop that tells managers whether the decision is working.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n33">Synoptic questions on Paper 2 expect you to link content from the whole course — name multiple functions, link them, use the data, and commit to a supported judgement.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n29">Synoptic questions reward breadth plus linkage. Walk through all four functions even if the question names one — examiners credit you for showing how a finance decision depends on an HR or operations input. Always use any numbers in the stimulus; a qualitative answer on a case study with figures is capped below the top band. For ‘Recommend’ questions make a decision — not a summary. For ‘Discuss’ and ‘Evaluate’ questions, the judgement should weigh one factor over another with a clear because-clause, not just restate the points above.</p>
