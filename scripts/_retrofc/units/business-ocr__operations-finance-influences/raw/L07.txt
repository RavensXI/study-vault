--- DESCRIPTION ---
What the finance function does and how it shapes decisions across every other business function.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Why Every Business Has a Finance Function</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Imagine a fast-growing UK business like Gymshark, the Solihull-based fitness brand. Every day the business decides whether to launch a new product, hire a new designer, sign a social-media athlete, open a new store or pay a supplier. Every single one of those decisions comes down to money. Who is tracking the money? Who tells the managers what they can afford? That is the job of the <dfn class="term" data-def="The department or team in a business responsible for managing money, recording transactions, producing financial information and supporting decision-making.">finance function</dfn>.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">The finance function is not just about filing invoices. It is the information hub of the business: every other department relies on it to plan, to spend sensibly and to prove whether what they’re doing actually works.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">The Purpose of the Finance Function</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">OCR describes the purpose of the finance function in two ways: it provides <dfn class="term" data-def="Data and reports about the money moving through a business — sales, costs, profits, cash balances and forecasts.">financial information</dfn> and it supports business planning. In practice, that breaks down into several day-to-day jobs.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Six Core Jobs of the Finance Team</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Recording transactions</strong> — every sale, purchase, payment and receipt is logged. Modern software like Xero or QuickBooks captures this automatically but a human still has to check the numbers are right.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Paying bills</strong> — suppliers are paid, staff wages are processed, HMRC gets its tax on time. Pay late and suppliers stop delivering; pay HMRC late and the business faces penalties.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Producing financial statements</strong> — the income statement, cash flow forecast and statement of financial position are built from the raw transaction data. These reports show owners and investors how the business is performing.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Budgeting</strong> — finance sets a spending plan for each department. Marketing might be given a £50,000 quarterly budget; operations a £120,000 equipment budget. The function then tracks whether each team stays within it.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Managing <dfn class="term" data-def="The movement of money into and out of a business over time, showing whether it has enough cash on hand to meet its obligations.">cash flow</dfn></strong> — finance makes sure the business has enough cash in the bank to pay what it owes, when it is owed. A profitable business can still go under if cash runs out mid-month.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Supporting decisions</strong> — before any big call (a new product launch, an overseas store, a new factory), the finance team runs the numbers and tells the decision-makers what the likely return and risk look like.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n12" data-revision-tip="Cover this and list the two purposes of the finance function named in the OCR spec.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The finance function has two named purposes in the OCR spec: providing financial information and supporting business planning. Everything the team does — bookkeeping, bill-paying, budgets, cash flow, decision support — feeds one of those two purposes.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">How Finance Connects to Every Other Function</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">The finance function only works because every other part of the business talks to it. Take Innocent Drinks, the London-based smoothie company owned by Coca-Cola. Each department brings a different kind of money question.</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Marketing</strong> wants a budget for its next advertising campaign. Finance tells them what is affordable and tracks whether the campaign returns more revenue than it costs.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Operations</strong> needs to cost raw fruit, bottling, transport and labour. Finance adds these figures up to calculate the cost per unit and flag when a product line stops being profitable.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Human resources</strong> runs payroll — wages, National Insurance and pensions — through the finance function. HR also asks finance whether the business can afford new hires.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n18"><strong>Senior managers</strong> look at the financial information the team produces to decide whether to expand, invest or cut back.</li>
</ul>

<h2 data-narration-id="n19">Worked Example — Choosing Between Two Campaigns</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Suppose Gymshark’s marketing team is deciding between two campaign ideas for autumn. Campaign A costs £80,000 and is forecast to bring in £200,000 of extra sales. Campaign B costs £150,000 and is forecast to bring in £300,000. Which should they pick?</p>
<p data-narration-id="n21">The finance team is asked to run the numbers. Campaign A generates £120,000 extra profit for £80,000 spent — a return of 150%. Campaign B generates £150,000 extra profit for £150,000 spent — a return of 100%. Campaign A is the better use of money per pound invested, even though Campaign B brings in more revenue overall. Without the finance function running this analysis, marketing might simply pick the bigger-looking option. With it, the decision is evidence-based.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Influence on Business Activity & Decision-Making</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Setting objectives</strong> — finance tells the board what is realistic. A target of 30% growth sounds ambitious; finance says whether the business has the cash to fund it and the margins to survive the stretch.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Spotting warning signs</strong> — finance watches the monthly numbers. A slow slide in gross margin or a widening gap between sales and cash received can flag a problem months before it becomes a crisis.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Talking to outside partners</strong> — banks, investors, suppliers and HMRC all want to see accurate financial statements. If the finance function gets it wrong, the business loses credibility — loans get refused, investors walk away, suppliers demand cash up front.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n25" data-revision-tip="Close this and write down three specific ways finance connects to another business function.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Every other function depends on finance: marketing uses budgets, operations uses cost data, HR uses payroll, senior managers use financial statements to decide strategy. This is why OCR treats finance as one of four interdependent business functions.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n26">A Small Business vs a Large Business</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n27">In a tiny start-up, the owner is often the finance function — using a spreadsheet or an app like Xero to log sales, pay suppliers and send invoices. As the business grows, a dedicated bookkeeper is brought in. By the time a business reaches the size of Gymshark or Innocent, finance is a full department with accountants, financial analysts, a credit control team and a Chief Financial Officer (CFO) sitting on the senior leadership team. The purpose stays the same at every size — only the scale changes.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n29">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n30">The finance function has two named purposes: providing financial information and supporting business planning.</li><li data-narration-id="n31">Its core jobs include recording transactions, paying bills, producing statements, budgeting, managing cash flow and supporting decisions.</li><li data-narration-id="n32">Finance sits at the centre of the business — marketing, operations, HR and senior managers all rely on its information to make informed decisions.</li></ul>

<p data-narration-id="n28">For ‘Explain’ questions about the finance function, don’t just define it — apply it to the case study. If the stem mentions a growing business, link finance to cash flow planning and securing investment. If the stem mentions a failing business, link finance to spotting the warning signs. OCR’s synoptic questions routinely ask how finance connects to operations, marketing or HR — practise writing ‘finance → another function’ chains. Avoid vague phrases like ‘finance is important’ — always name what it does and why it matters <em>to this business</em>.</p>
