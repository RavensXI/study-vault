--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Specialist Tools & Equipment</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The specification requires you to understand how to select and use specialist tools and equipment — including hand tools, machinery, and digital manufacturing — appropriate to the material and task. Crucially, you must also demonstrate that you can use them <strong>safely</strong> to protect yourself and others.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Hand Tools</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n3">Hand tools are powered by the user rather than electricity. They give precise control and are essential for detailed work. Key examples include:</p>
    <ul>
      <li data-narration-id="n4"><strong>Marking and measuring</strong> — steel rule, try square, marking gauge, scriber, engineer’s square. Accurate marking out is the foundation of quality work.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n5"><strong>Cutting</strong> — tenon saw, coping saw, hacksaw, craft knife, scissors. The choice depends on the material: a tenon saw for timber, a hacksaw for metal, scissors for textiles.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Shaping</strong> — chisels, planes, files, rasps. Files come in different profiles (flat, half-round, round, triangular) for different shapes.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Joining</strong> — screwdrivers, hammers, clamps, soldering iron. Clamps hold work securely while adhesive dries or while drilling.</li>
    </ul>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Machinery & Power Tools</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n8">Power tools and workshop machinery increase speed, accuracy, and capability beyond what hand tools can achieve:</p>
    <ul>
      <li data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Pillar drill</strong> — drills precise, vertical holes. Always secured with a machine vice or clamp; never hold work by hand.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Band saw</strong> — cuts curves in timber and some plastics. Blade guard adjusted to just above the workpiece.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Wood lathe</strong> — turns cylindrical shapes from timber. Requires a face shield, no loose clothing, and hair tied back.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Sewing machine</strong> — joins fabric quickly and consistently using a lockstitch mechanism.</li>
    </ul>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n13" data-revision-tip="Cover this and list three safety precautions that apply to using workshop machinery.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Workshop safety essentials: always wear appropriate PPE (safety glasses, apron, hearing protection), secure workpieces with clamps or vices, never leave machines running unattended, keep guards in place, and ensure no loose clothing, jewellery, or untied hair near rotating machinery.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Digital Design & Manufacture</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15"><dfn class="term" data-def="The use of computer software to create precise 2D or 3D digital models of a product, which can be tested, modified, and sent directly to manufacturing equipment.">CAD (Computer Aided Design)</dfn> allows designers to create, modify, and test designs digitally before any material is cut. <dfn class="term" data-def="Manufacturing controlled by computer software, where digital design files are converted into instructions for machines such as CNC routers, laser cutters, and 3D printers.">CAM (Computer Aided Manufacture)</dfn> converts those digital files into physical products using CNC machines, laser cutters, and 3D printers.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>CNC, Laser Cutting & 3D Printing</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n16"><dfn class="term" data-def="A subtractive manufacturing process where a computer-controlled router or mill cuts material away from a solid block following a digital tool path.">CNC routing/milling</dfn> uses a computer-controlled cutting tool to carve shapes from solid material. It is a <dfn class="term" data-def="A manufacturing technique that removes material from a larger piece to create the desired shape, such as cutting, drilling, or milling.">wastage</dfn> process — material is removed to create the shape.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Laser cutting</strong> uses a focused laser beam to cut or engrave sheet materials (acrylic, MDF, plywood, card) with extremely high precision. It produces clean edges with minimal kerf.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n18"><dfn class="term" data-def="An additive manufacturing process that builds objects layer by layer from a digital file, typically using thermoplastic filament, resin, or powder.">3D printing</dfn> (additive manufacturing) builds objects layer by layer. Common types include FDM (Fused Deposition Modelling) using thermoplastic filament and SLA (Stereolithography) using UV-cured resin. 3D printing is ideal for complex shapes that would be difficult to create by traditional methods.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n19">Specialist Techniques & Processes</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Manufacturing processes are grouped into four categories based on how they shape material:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Wastage</strong> — material is removed (cutting, drilling, milling, turning, filing).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Addition</strong> — material is joined together (welding, soldering, brazing, adhesives, sewing, laminating).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Deforming</strong> — material is permanently reshaped without adding or removing (bending, press forming, vacuum forming, forging).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Reforming</strong> — material is melted or dissolved and reformed into a new shape (casting, injection moulding, blow moulding, extrusion).</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n25" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: name the four manufacturing process categories and give one example of each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The four manufacturing process categories are wastage (material removed), addition (material joined), deforming (material reshaped), and reforming (material melted and reshaped). The correct process depends on the material, the shape required, and the production scale.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n26">Surface Treatments & Finishes</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n27"><dfn class="term" data-def="Coatings or treatments applied to a product's surface for protection against corrosion, moisture, or wear, or to improve its visual appearance.">Surface treatments and finishes</dfn> are applied for both functional and aesthetic reasons. Functional finishes protect against corrosion, moisture, UV damage, or wear. Aesthetic finishes improve the look and feel of the product.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n28">Examples include: <strong>paint and lacquer</strong> (wood, metal), <strong>varnish and oil</strong> (wood), <strong>powder coating</strong> (metal), <strong>anodising</strong> (aluminium), <strong>electroplating</strong> (metal), and <strong>dyeing</strong> (textiles). The choice depends on the material, the intended use, and the level of protection required.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n29" data-revision-tip="Close this and name three different surface finishes and explain whether each is primarily functional, aesthetic, or both.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Surface finishes serve functional purposes (protection from corrosion, moisture, or wear), aesthetic purposes (colour, texture, sheen), or both. Selecting the right finish is part of designing a quality product.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n31">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n32">Specialist tools include hand tools, power tools, and digital manufacturing equipment (CNC, laser cutters, 3D printers) — each chosen to match the material and task.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n33">Manufacturing processes are categorised as wastage, addition, deforming, or reforming, depending on how material is shaped.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n34">Surface treatments and finishes protect products and improve their appearance; the choice depends on material, function, and aesthetics.</li>
</ul>
