<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Building Blocks of Music</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Understanding musical structure, form and melody is essential for analysing and creating music. This lesson covers the structural sections that make up a song, the standard forms used to organise them, and the scales, intervals and devices that shape a melody.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Structural Sections</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">Most songs are built from a set of recognisable sections, each with a specific function:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n5"><strong>Intro</strong> — the opening section that establishes the mood, tempo and key before the vocals enter.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Verse</strong> — tells the story. The melody usually stays the same, but the lyrics change each time.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Pre-chorus</strong> — a short section that builds tension and anticipation before the chorus.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Chorus</strong> — the main hook of the song. It has the most memorable melody and repeats with the same lyrics and music.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Bridge</strong> — a contrasting section that provides variety and prevents the song from becoming repetitive.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Outro</strong> — the ending section, often a fade-out or a final statement of the chorus or theme.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Middle 8</strong> — an eight-bar section in the middle of the song that introduces new musical material.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Refrain</strong> — a repeated line or phrase, often at the end of a verse (shorter than a full chorus).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Coda</strong> — a concluding passage that brings the music to a definitive end (from the Italian for “tail”).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Breakdown</strong> — a stripped-back section where instruments drop out, common in electronic and dance music.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Drop</strong> — the moment after a build-up when the full beat and bass return with maximum energy (common in EDM).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Riser</strong> — an ascending sound or build-up that creates tension before a drop or chorus.</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Cover this and list as many structural sections as you can from memory. Aim for at least 10.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The twelve structural sections you need to know are: intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, bridge, outro, middle 8, refrain, coda, breakdown, drop and riser. Each has a distinct role in the song.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Musical Form</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19"><dfn class="term" data-def="The overall structure or plan of a piece of music, describing how sections are arranged.">Form</dfn> describes how sections are arranged to create the overall structure of a piece. The spec requires you to know seven forms:</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Seven Musical Forms</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <ul>
    <li data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Through-composed</strong> — no repeated sections. The music is different throughout. Common in art songs and film scores.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n21"><strong>AAA (strophic)</strong> — the same music repeats for every verse. Simple and repetitive, often used in folk songs and hymns.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n22"><strong>AB (binary)</strong> — two contrasting sections, A and B. Each is usually repeated (AABB).</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n23"><strong>ABA (ternary)</strong> — three sections where the first section returns at the end. The B section provides contrast.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n24"><strong>ABCBA (arch)</strong> — a symmetrical structure that mirrors itself. Sections move away from the opening and then return.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n25"><strong>ABACA (rondo)</strong> — the A section keeps returning between contrasting episodes (B, C, etc.).</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n26"><strong>12-bar</strong> — a repeating 12-bar chord progression using three chords (I, IV, V). The foundation of blues, rock and roll and many pop songs.</li>
    </ul>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n27">Melody: Scales</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n28">Melodies are built from <dfn class="term" data-def="A set of musical notes arranged in ascending or descending order, forming the basis of a melody or harmony.">scales</dfn>. Each scale has a specific pattern of <dfn class="term" data-def="The distance in pitch between two notes, measured in tones (T) and semitones (S).">intervals</dfn> (tones and semitones) that gives it a distinctive character.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Five Scales You Need to Know</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <ul>
    <li data-narration-id="n29"><strong>Major scale</strong> — T, T, S, T, T, T, S. Sounds bright and happy.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n30"><strong>Natural minor scale</strong> — T, S, T, T, S, T, T. Sounds sad or melancholic.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n31"><strong>Harmonic minor scale</strong> — T, S, T, T, S, T+S, S. The raised 7th note creates a distinctive “exotic” sound and a strong pull back to the root.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n32"><strong>Minor pentatonic scale</strong> — T+S, T, T, T+S, T. Five notes, widely used in blues, rock and pop melodies.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n33"><strong>Major pentatonic scale</strong> — T, T, T+S, T, T+S. Five notes, bright and open-sounding. Common in folk and pop.</li>
    </ul>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n34" data-revision-tip="Write from memory: the interval patterns for the major, natural minor and minor pentatonic scales.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Major: T,T,S,T,T,T,S. Natural minor: T,S,T,T,S,T,T. Harmonic minor: T,S,T,T,S,T+S,S. Minor pentatonic: T+S,T,T,T+S,T. Major pentatonic: T,T,T+S,T,T+S.</p>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n35">Melody: Intervals & Devices</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n36">An interval is the distance between two notes. <strong>Diatonic</strong> intervals stay within the notes of the scale. <strong>Chromatic</strong> intervals use notes outside the scale (sharps and flats not in the key).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n37">Composers use melodic <dfn class="term" data-def="Compositional techniques used to develop or transform a melody.">devices</dfn> to develop melodies:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n38"><strong>Retrograde</strong> — playing the melody backwards (reversing the note order).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n39"><strong>Inversion</strong> — turning the melody upside down. If the original goes up a step, the inversion goes down a step.</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n40"><dfn class="term" data-def="The overall shape or contour of a melody across a phrase or section.">Melodic form</dfn> describes the shape of a melody:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n41"><strong>Arch</strong> — the melody rises to a peak and then falls back down.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n42"><strong>Repetition</strong> — the same phrase or motif is repeated, often with small variations.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n43"><strong>Sequences</strong> — a melodic pattern is repeated at a higher or lower pitch.</li>
</ul>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n45">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n46">Songs are built from structural sections (intro, verse, chorus, bridge, etc.) — know all twelve for the exam.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n47">Musical forms organise sections into patterns: through-composed, strophic (AAA), binary (AB), ternary (ABA), arch (ABCBA), rondo (ABACA) and 12-bar.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n48">Melodies are built from scales (major, natural minor, harmonic minor, minor pentatonic, major pentatonic) and shaped by devices (retrograde, inversion) and melodic forms (arch, repetition, sequences).</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n44">Questions on structure and form often give you a scenario (e.g. “A song has an intro, two verses with different lyrics but the same melody, a chorus that repeats, and a bridge”) and ask you to identify the form or describe the sections. Learn the letter labels: A = first section, B = contrasting section. For scales, memorise the tone/semitone patterns — they are frequently tested in multiple-choice and short-answer questions.</p>
