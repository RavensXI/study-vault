<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Harmony, Rhythm & Instrumentation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">This lesson covers the final three musical elements you need for the exam: harmony (chords and progressions), rhythm (time signatures, devices and tempo) and instrumentation (acoustic and electronic instruments, plus DJ technology).</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Harmony</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4"><dfn class="term" data-def="The combination of two or more notes played at the same time, creating chords and chord progressions.">Harmony</dfn> is created when two or more notes sound together. The building blocks of harmony are <dfn class="term" data-def="Three or more notes played together, creating a single harmonic unit.">chords</dfn>.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n5">Chord Types</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n6">You need to know seven chord types:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Major</strong> — sounds bright and happy. Built from root, major 3rd, perfect 5th.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Minor</strong> — sounds sad or dark. Built from root, minor 3rd, perfect 5th.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Diminished</strong> — sounds tense and unstable. Built from root, minor 3rd, diminished 5th.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Augmented</strong> — sounds unsettled and dreamlike. Built from root, major 3rd, augmented 5th.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Minor 7th</strong> — a minor chord with an added minor 7th. Warm, jazzy character.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Major 7th</strong> — a major chord with an added major 7th. Smooth, sophisticated sound.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Suspended (sus)</strong> — replaces the 3rd with a 2nd (sus2) or 4th (sus4), creating an unresolved, open sound.</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Cover this and name all seven chord types, describing the character of each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Seven chord types: major (bright), minor (sad), diminished (tense), augmented (dreamlike), minor 7th (jazzy), major 7th (smooth) and suspended (unresolved). Know the character of each — this helps with analysis questions.</p>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n15">Chord Progressions</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n16">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A sequence of chords played one after another, forming the harmonic backbone of a piece.">chord progression</dfn> is a sequence of chords that forms the harmonic backbone of a piece. Progressions can be:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Diatonic</strong> — using only chords built from the notes of the key. These sound natural and expected.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n18"><strong>Chromatic</strong> — using chords that include notes from outside the key. These create surprise, tension or colour.</li>
</ul>

<h2 data-narration-id="n19">Rhythm</h2>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Time Signatures</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n20">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A notation at the start of a piece that tells you how many beats are in each bar and what note value gets one beat.">time signature</dfn> tells you how many beats are in each bar and what note value represents one beat.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Simple time</strong> — each beat divides naturally into two equal parts:</p>
    <ul>
    <li data-narration-id="n22">2/4 — two crotchet beats per bar (marches, polkas)</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n23">3/4 — three crotchet beats per bar (waltzes)</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n24">4/4 — four crotchet beats per bar (most pop, rock, hip-hop)</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n25">5/4 — five crotchet beats per bar (irregular, unsettling feel)</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n26">6/4 — six crotchet beats per bar</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n27">7/4 — seven crotchet beats per bar (progressive rock)</li>
    </ul>
    <p data-narration-id="n28"><strong>Compound time</strong> — each beat divides naturally into three equal parts, giving a swinging, lilting feel:</p>
    <ul>
    <li data-narration-id="n29">6/8 — two dotted-crotchet beats per bar (jigs, some ballads)</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n30">9/8 — three dotted-crotchet beats per bar</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n31">12/8 — four dotted-crotchet beats per bar (slow blues, gospel)</li>
    </ul>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n32">Rhythmic Devices</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n33">Rhythmic devices add interest and groove to music:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n34"><dfn class="term" data-def="Placing emphasis on beats or parts of beats that are normally weak, creating a sense of rhythmic surprise.">Syncopation</dfn> — placing accents on weak beats or off-beats, creating a sense of surprise and groove. Common in funk, jazz and pop.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n35"><strong>Triplets</strong> — three notes played in the time of two, creating a flowing, rolling feel.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n36"><strong>Dotted rhythms</strong> — a dot after a note adds half its value, creating a long-short pattern that gives energy and drive.</li>
</ul>

<h3 data-narration-id="n37">Tempo</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n38"><dfn class="term" data-def="The speed of the music, measured in beats per minute (BPM).">Tempo</dfn> is the speed of the music, measured in <strong>BPM</strong> (beats per minute). A slow ballad might be 60–80 BPM, a pop song 100–130 BPM, and a fast dance track 140–180 BPM. <strong>Tempo changes</strong> (accelerando for speeding up, rallentando for slowing down) add expression and drama.</p>



<h2 data-narration-id="n39">Instrumentation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n40"><dfn class="term" data-def="The combination of instruments chosen for a piece of music.">Instrumentation</dfn> refers to which instruments are used in a piece. The spec divides instruments into acoustic, electronic and DJ technology.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Acoustic Instruments</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <ul>
    <li data-narration-id="n41"><strong>Strings</strong> — plucked (guitar, harp, banjo) or bowed (violin, viola, cello, double bass).</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n42"><strong>Percussion</strong> — tuned (timpani, xylophone, vibraphone) or untuned (snare drum, bass drum, cymbals, tambourine).</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n43"><strong>Keyboards</strong> — piano and organ.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n44"><strong>Brass</strong> — valved (trumpet, French horn, tuba) or slide (trombone).</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n45"><strong>Woodwind</strong> — flutes (flute, piccolo) or reeds (clarinet, oboe, bassoon, saxophone).</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n46"><strong>Vocals</strong> — lead vocals (main melody and lyrics) and backing vocals (harmonies and support).</li>
    </ul>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Electronic Instruments & DJ Technology</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n47"><strong>Electronic instruments:</strong></p>
    <ul>
    <li data-narration-id="n48"><strong>Electric guitar</strong> and <strong>electric bass</strong> — use pickups and amplifiers to produce sound.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n49"><strong>Synthesiser</strong> — generates sound electronically using oscillators, filters and envelopes.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n50"><strong>Sampler</strong> — plays back recorded audio samples triggered by keys or pads.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n51"><strong>Drum machine</strong> — produces electronic or sampled drum sounds, programmed into patterns.</li>
    </ul>
    <p data-narration-id="n52"><strong>DJ technology:</strong></p>
    <ul>
    <li data-narration-id="n53"><strong>Turntables</strong> — play vinyl records, allowing scratching and beat-matching.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n54"><strong>CDJs</strong> — CD or USB-based players used by DJs for digital music playback.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n55"><strong>Mixer</strong> — blends audio from multiple sources with crossfader, EQ and effects.</li>
    <li data-narration-id="n56"><strong>DJ software</strong> — applications like Traktor or Serato that provide virtual decks, effects and beat-matching tools on a laptop.</li>
    </ul>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n57" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: name two examples for each acoustic instrument family (strings, percussion, keyboards, brass, woodwind).">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Acoustic instrument families: strings (plucked and bowed), percussion (tuned and untuned), keyboards (piano, organ), brass (valved and slide), woodwind (flutes and reeds), vocals (lead and backing). Know examples from each category.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n59">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n60">Harmony is built from seven chord types (major, minor, diminished, augmented, minor 7th, major 7th, suspended). Progressions can be diatonic or chromatic.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n61">Rhythm uses simple time (beats divide into 2) and compound time (beats divide into 3). Devices include syncopation, triplets and dotted rhythms.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n62">Instrumentation covers acoustic families (strings, percussion, keyboards, brass, woodwind, vocals), electronic instruments and DJ technology.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n58">In the exam, you may be asked to identify instruments from a description or audio extract, or to explain the difference between simple and compound time. For harmony questions, remember: diatonic = within the key, chromatic = outside the key. For instrumentation questions, make sure you can classify any given instrument into its correct family and sub-category (e.g. clarinet = woodwind, reed).</p>
