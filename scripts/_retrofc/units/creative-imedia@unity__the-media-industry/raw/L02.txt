Traditional and new media sectors, how they differ, and how the industry is evolving.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Understanding Media Sectors</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The <dfn class="term" data-def="A distinct area of the media industry that produces and distributes specific types of content, such as film, radio, or digital publishing.">media sector</dfn> is a distinct area of the media industry. The OCR specification divides the media industry into two broad categories: <strong>traditional media</strong> and <strong>new media</strong>. You need to know the sectors within each category, understand how they differ, and recognise that the industry is constantly evolving as technology advances.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Recall the key facts about traditional media sectors.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Traditional media sectors: <strong>film, television, radio, print publishing</strong>. New media sectors: <strong>computer games, interactive media, internet, digital publishing</strong>.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Traditional Media Sectors</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5"><dfn class="term" data-def="Non-digital media channels that have existed for decades, including film, television, radio, and print publishing.">Traditional media</dfn> refers to non-digital media channels that have been around for decades. These are well-established channels of communication that reach large audiences.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Film</strong> — the film industry produces movies for cinema and home viewing. Films are created by large teams including directors, camera operators, editors, and actors. Films have large budgets and can take years to produce, but reach global audiences. <strong>Television</strong> — TV broadcasts programmes including dramas, documentaries, news, and entertainment shows. TV reaches mass audiences through scheduled broadcasting and, increasingly, on-demand streaming. <strong>Radio</strong> — radio broadcasts audio content including music, talk shows, news, and drama. It is one of the oldest forms of mass media and remains popular for its accessibility. <strong>Print publishing</strong> — this covers newspapers, magazines, books, comics, and leaflets. Print was historically the main way information was shared with large audiences.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Advantages:</strong> traditional media targets a wide “mass” audience, has established credibility and trust, and produces high-quality, polished content. <strong>Disadvantages:</strong> it can be hard to target a specific group of consumers, traditional media often requires a large budget, channels like film and TV can take a long time to develop, and content cannot be quickly adapted to respond to changes in the wider world.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n8">New Media Sectors</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9"><dfn class="term" data-def="Digital media channels that have emerged more recently, including computer games, interactive media, the internet, and digital publishing.">New media</dfn> refers to digital media channels that have arrived more recently and are still evolving. These channels use digital technology to create and distribute content.</p>
<figure class="diagram"><img src="https://pub-aeb94e100e5a48f4a133be5bf206aecb.r2.dev/creative-imedia/the-media-industry/diagram_sectors_of_the_media_industry.jpg" alt="Diagram: Sectors of the Media Industry" loading="lazy"></figure>
<p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Computer games</strong> — the gaming industry creates interactive entertainment for consoles, PCs, and mobile devices. It is now one of the largest entertainment sectors globally. <strong>Interactive media</strong> — this includes any digital product that responds to user input, such as interactive websites, touchscreen kiosks, and VR/AR experiences. <strong>Internet</strong> — the internet is a platform for websites, streaming services, social media, blogs, and online advertising. It allows content to reach global audiences instantly. <strong>Digital publishing</strong> — this covers eBooks, online magazines, digital newspapers, podcasts, and web graphics. Digital publishing allows content to be distributed without physical printing costs.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Advantages and Disadvantages of New Media</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Advantages:</strong> new media can target much more specific audiences of consumers, it can be much cheaper to produce and distribute, channels can be updated quickly (minute by minute), making them cheap, flexible, and adaptable, and audiences expect the most up-to-date content. <strong>Disadvantages:</strong> there is high competition for attention online, content can be easily copied or pirated, audiences may distrust unregulated content, and technology can become outdated quickly.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n12">How Media Sectors Are Evolving</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">The media industry does not stand still. Traditional and new media are increasingly <dfn class="term" data-def="The process of different media sectors merging together, such as a newspaper having both a print edition and a website.">converging</dfn> — meaning they are merging together. A newspaper might have a print edition (traditional) and a website with video content (new media). A TV show might be broadcast live (traditional) and also streamed online (new media). Radio stations now podcast their shows for on-demand listening.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14">The specification states you must know that media sectors are evolving. This evolution is driven by technology — faster internet speeds, smartphones, and social media platforms have changed how audiences consume content. Streaming services like Netflix and Disney+ have disrupted traditional TV and film distribution. Social media platforms allow anyone to become a content creator, blurring the line between producers and consumers.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Recall the exam structure, timings, and mark allocations.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The same product can be used by different sectors. For example, a video could be used in film (cinema), television (broadcast), the internet (streaming/social media), and interactive media (embedded in a website). This cross-sector use is a key concept in the R093 exam.</p>
</div>

A radio show available as a podcast uses radio and digital publishing. A new book released on Amazon Kindle uses print publishing and digital publishing. These examples show how modern media products rarely belong to just one sector.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n18">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n19">The media industry is split into traditional media (film, TV, radio, print publishing) and new media (computer games, interactive media, internet, digital publishing).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n20">Traditional media targets mass audiences but is expensive and slow to change; new media is cheaper, flexible, and can target specific groups but faces high competition.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n21">Media sectors are evolving through convergence — the same product can be used across multiple sectors, and traditional/new media are increasingly merging.</li></ul>

If asked to explain why a sector is suitable for a scenario, link your answer to the specific audience and purpose given in the question.</p>
