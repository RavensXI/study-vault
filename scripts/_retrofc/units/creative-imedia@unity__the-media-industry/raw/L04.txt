--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Job Roles in the Media Industry</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The media industry employs people in a wide range of job roles. The OCR specification organises these into three categories: <strong>creative roles</strong>, <strong>technical roles</strong>, and <strong>senior roles</strong>. You need to know the main responsibilities of each role, how each contributes to creating media products, and which <dfn class="term" data-def="The stages of creating a media product: pre-production (planning), production (creating), post-production (editing/finishing), and distribution (delivering to audiences).">production phases</dfn> each role is specific to.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. What audience factors are described? List them from memory.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>There are four production phases: <strong>pre-production</strong> (planning, research, design), <strong>production</strong> (creating/filming/recording), <strong>post-production</strong> (editing, adding effects, refining), and <strong>distribution</strong> (delivering the finished product to audiences).</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Creative Roles</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Creative roles are responsible for the artistic and design elements of media products. These professionals bring ideas to life through visual design, writing, and artistic skills.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Animator</strong> — creates animation sequences using traditional, 2D, 3D, or stop motion techniques. Works across pre-production (research, drafts, storyboarding), production (creating animation), and post-production (refining). <strong>Content creator</strong> — produces digital content for platforms such as social media, websites, and blogs. May work across all production phases. <strong>Copywriter</strong> — writes engaging text for advertisements, websites, scripts, and marketing materials. Primarily works in pre-production and production. <strong>Graphic designer</strong> — creates visual content including logos, layouts, posters, and digital graphics. Works mainly in pre-production (design concepts) and production (creating final designs).</p>
<figure class="diagram"><img src="https://pub-aeb94e100e5a48f4a133be5bf206aecb.r2.dev/creative-imedia/the-media-industry/diagram_job_roles_in_the_media_industry_part_1.jpg" alt="Diagram: Job Roles in the Media Industry (Part 1)" loading="lazy"></figure>
<p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Illustrator/graphic artist</strong> — creates original drawings and illustrations for print and digital media, including book illustrations, comic art, and concept art. <strong>Photographer</strong> — captures still images for use in advertising, publishing, journalism, and digital media. Primarily works during production. <strong>Script writer</strong> — writes scripts for films, TV programmes, radio shows, and other media. Script writers work specifically in <dfn class="term" data-def="The first phase of media production where planning, research, concept development, and design take place before any content is created.">pre-production</dfn> — their work must be completed before production can begin. <strong>Web designer</strong> — designs the visual layout and user experience of websites, considering colour, typography, navigation, and content placement.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Pre-production only:</strong> script writer. <strong>Pre-production and production:</strong> copywriter, graphic designer, illustrator, web designer. <strong>All phases:</strong> animator (researches, creates, refines), content creator (plans, creates, edits, publishes). <strong>Production only:</strong> photographer. Some creative roles span multiple phases because they contribute to planning, creating, and refining the product.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Technical Roles</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">Technical roles focus on the equipment, technology, and technical processes needed to produce and edit media content.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Camera operator</strong> — operates camera equipment during filming, understanding shot types, angles, and movement. Works primarily during production but liaises with the client and director in pre-production to plan shots. <strong>Games programmer/developer</strong> — writes the code that makes digital games function, including gameplay mechanics, graphics rendering, and user interfaces. Works mainly in production and post-production. <strong>Sound editor</strong> — edits and mixes audio tracks after recording, adding sound effects and balancing levels. Works in <dfn class="term" data-def="The phase after production where content is edited, refined, and prepared for distribution, including adding effects, titles, and sound.">post-production</dfn>. <strong>Audio technician</strong> — operates sound recording equipment during production, ensuring high-quality audio capture. Works during production.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Video editor</strong> — assembles and edits recorded video footage in post-production, cutting scenes, adding transitions, and creating the final sequence. <strong>Web developer</strong> — builds and codes websites using programming languages, turning the web designer’s visual plans into functional sites. Works mainly in production.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Pre-production tasks:</strong> mind maps, mood boards, visualisation diagrams, storyboards, script writing, designing client briefs, identifying timescales, carrying out research, producing work plans, identifying target audience, identifying equipment needs, considering health and safety, considering legislation. <strong>Production tasks:</strong> filming, sound recording, creating media graphics, recording music/soundtracks, creating websites, creating animations. <strong>Post-production tasks:</strong> editing, adding CGI, adding sound effects, adding opening/closing titles and credits, editing music. <strong>Distribution:</strong> changing formats to meet client needs.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Name the pre-production documents and what each contains.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Some job roles are specific to one production phase (e.g. script writer — pre-production only). Some span multiple phases (e.g. animator works across pre-production, production, and post-production). The size and scale of a project may mean one individual performs more than one role.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n16">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n17">Creative roles (animator, content creator, copywriter, graphic designer, illustrator, photographer, script writer, web designer) focus on artistic and design elements of media products.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n18">Technical roles (camera operator, games programmer, sound editor, audio technician, video editor, web developer) focus on equipment, technology, and technical processes.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n19">Each role contributes to specific production phases (pre-production, production, post-production, distribution), though some roles span multiple phases and individuals may perform more than one role on smaller projects.</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n15">When asked to “explain the role of” a specific job, make sure you cover three things: (1) what the person does, (2) which production phase(s) they work in, and (3) how their work contributes to the final media product. A common mistake is only describing what the role does without linking it to the production process or explaining its contribution.</p>
