--- DESCRIPTION ---
How to compare two unseen poems: methods, structure, and a practical framework.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Part (ii): The Comparison Question</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Part (ii) gives you a second poem and asks you to compare it with the first. This is worth approximately <strong>8 marks</strong> and should take about <strong>10 minutes</strong>. The question usually asks: “How does the poet of Poem 2 also present [the same theme]?” or “Compare how both poets present [theme].” You are being tested on your ability to identify similarities <em>and</em> differences in the poets’ <dfn class="term" data-def="The techniques, strategies, and approaches a poet uses to create meaning.">methods</dfn>, not just their ideas.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">The most common mistake is writing about each poem separately. A comparison means writing about both poems <em>together</em>, constantly referring back and forth. Use <dfn class="term" data-def="Words and phrases that signal comparison or contrast, such as ‘similarly,’ ‘in contrast,’ ‘whereas,’ ‘both poets.’">comparative connectives</dfn>: “similarly,” “in contrast,” “whereas,” “both poets,” “unlike Poem 1.” Every paragraph should reference both poems.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this and write a template sentence that compares two poems’ methods, then fill it with a real example.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Compare methods, not just ideas. Do not write “Both poems are about love.” Write: “Both poems explore love, but Poem 1 uses warm imagery (‘golden light’) to present love as comforting, whereas Poem 2 uses cold imagery (‘frozen hands’) to present love as painful.” The comparison of <em>how</em> (methods) is what earns marks.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">A Framework for Comparison</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Use this structure for your Part (ii) response:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Opening sentence:</strong> State the shared theme and the key difference in how the poets treat it. <strong>Paragraph 1:</strong> Compare one specific technique or image from each poem. <strong>Paragraph 2:</strong> Compare a structural choice (form, stanza pattern, ending). <strong>Closing sentence:</strong> Sum up the most significant difference or similarity.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>What to Compare</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n8">Compare: <strong>Tone</strong> — is one poem angry and the other reflective? <strong>Imagery</strong> — do they use similar or contrasting images? <strong>Structure</strong> — is one poem regular and the other free verse? <strong>Perspective</strong> — does one use first person and the other third? <strong>Ending</strong> — does one resolve and the other remain open? You do not need to compare everything — choose 2–3 strong points of comparison and develop them.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9">The strongest comparisons focus on <em>methods that create different effects</em>. Both poems might use metaphor, but one creates comfort while the other creates unease. This kind of analysis — same technique, different effect — is what the examiner rewards most highly.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Worked Example: Comparing Two Approaches to Nature</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n10">“Both poems use nature to explore the speaker’s emotions. In Poem 1, the speaker describes a ‘gentle rain’ that ‘washed the world clean,’ using personification to present nature as healing and restorative. In contrast, Poem 2 describes a ‘storm that tore the trees apart,’ using violent verbs to present nature as destructive. While both poets use pathetic fallacy, their contrasting imagery reveals fundamentally different views: Poem 1 presents nature as a source of comfort, whereas Poem 2 presents it as a mirror for the speaker’s anger.”</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n11">Notice how every sentence references both poems. The comparison is sustained, specific, and focused on methods (personification, violent verbs, pathetic fallacy) rather than just content.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n12" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: write a comparison opening sentence that names the shared theme and key difference between two imaginary poems.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The comparison should be sustained (every sentence references both poems), specific (quote from both), and focused on methods (not just what the poems say, but how they say it). Use comparative connectives in every sentence: “similarly,” “in contrast,” “whereas,” “both poets.”</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n14">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n15">Compare methods, not just themes: analyse how the poets use different techniques to present similar subjects.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n16">Every sentence should reference both poems. Use comparative connectives to keep the comparison sustained.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n17">Focus on 2–3 strong points of comparison rather than trying to cover everything in 10 minutes.</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p>For 8 marks in 10 minutes, aim for 2 developed paragraphs. Use the formula: Poem 1 does [X] using [technique], whereas Poem 2 does [Y] using [different technique], creating a contrast between [effect] and [effect].</p>
