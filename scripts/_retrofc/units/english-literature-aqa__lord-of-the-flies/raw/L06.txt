--- DESCRIPTION ---
Ralph, Jack, Piggy, Simon, and Roger — what each character represents and key quotations.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Ralph — Democracy & Civilisation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Ralph represents <dfn class="term" data-def="A system of government in which power is held by the people through elected representatives and collective decision-making.">democratic</dfn> leadership, order, and the desire for rescue. He is elected chief because of his attractive appearance and the conch, not because of any special ability. He establishes rules (the fire, shelters, the conch as a speaking token) and tries to maintain them through consensus rather than force.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Ralph’s key weakness is that he lacks the ruthlessness to enforce his rules. When boys stop tending the fire or building shelters, he can only appeal and argue — he cannot compel. Golding uses Ralph to show that democracy requires willing participation; without it, democratic leaders are powerless. Key quotation: “The rules! … You’re breaking the rules! … The rules are the only thing we’ve got!”</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: What are Ralph's strengths and weaknesses as a leader?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Ralph is not a perfect leader — he is chosen for superficial reasons, struggles to maintain authority, and participates in Simon’s murder. Golding presents him as well-intentioned but ultimately unable to prevent civilisation’s collapse without enforcement mechanisms.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Jack — Authoritarian Power & Savagery</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Jack Merridew represents <dfn class="term" data-def="A system of government where one person or group holds absolute power and demands obedience, often through fear and force.">authoritarian</dfn> leadership, violence, and the primal desire for power. He arrives marching his choir in military formation and expects to be chief without a vote. When democracy rejects him, he builds his own tribe through intimidation, spectacle, and the promise of meat.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Jack understands instinctively what Ralph does not: that people follow strength, not rules. He offers immediate rewards (food, excitement, protection from the beast) rather than long-term goals (rescue). His face paint liberates him from civilised inhibitions, and his hunting chant creates collective frenzy. Key quotation: “Bollocks to the rules! We’re strong — we hunt!”</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n8">Jack follows the pattern of real-world dictators: he exploits fear (the beast), offers simple solutions (hunting, tribal identity), punishes dissent (torturing Samneric), and uses ritual and spectacle to maintain control. Golding, writing in 1954, had witnessed the rise of Hitler and Mussolini — leaders who came to power by exploiting economic fear and offering national identity through strength. Jack’s tribe is a miniature fascist state.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9">Unlike Roger, Jack is not purely sadistic — he is strategic. He uses violence as a tool for power, not as an end in itself. This makes him more dangerous in some ways: his cruelty has purpose, and he can inspire loyalty.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Piggy — Intellect & Reason</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Piggy represents <dfn class="term" data-def="The quality of being logical and practical in thinking, relying on evidence and reason rather than emotion or instinct.">rationality</dfn>, science, and democratic process. He is the most intelligent boy, consistently offering practical solutions and logical analysis. His glasses (used to light the fire) symbolise scientific knowledge and technological capability. Yet he is marginalised because of his appearance, accent, and physical limitations.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Piggy’s fatal flaw is his inability to act independently of the rules. He believes that the system should work simply because it <em>ought</em> to: “I got the conch! … I got a right to speak.” He cannot comprehend that rules have no power without collective enforcement. His death, alongside the conch’s destruction, represents Golding’s bleakest message: reason alone cannot survive in a world ruled by violence.</p>

<!-- DIAGRAM -->

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Simon — Spiritual Truth</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Simon represents innate goodness, spiritual insight, and the capacity to see truth. He is the only character who understands that the beast is not external but internal (“maybe it’s only us”). His solitary walks in the jungle and his confrontation with the Lord of the Flies echo biblical prophets and Christ figures — he discovers the truth, tries to deliver it, and is martyred by those he tries to save.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15">Simon is frequently described as strange, faint, and epileptic. Golding deliberately makes him an outsider: visionaries are not understood by the societies they live in. His death is the novel’s moral catastrophe. Key quotation (Lord of the Flies to Simon): “You knew, didn’t you? I’m part of you.”</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n16" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: write down one key quotation for each of the five main characters and what it reveals.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Each character embodies an idea: Ralph = democracy, Jack = dictatorship, Piggy = reason, Simon = spiritual truth, Roger = sadism. The novel tests whether these forces can coexist — and the answer is that violence and fear always overpower reason and goodness without the structures of civilisation.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Roger — Pure Sadism</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Roger is the novel’s darkest character. He begins by throwing stones that deliberately miss Henry (the “taboo of the old life” still restraining him) and ends by murdering Piggy and torturing Samneric. He represents sadism — cruelty without purpose or justification. While Jack kills for power, Roger kills for pleasure.</p>

  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Symbolism: The Conch, the Glasses, the Fire & the Beast</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n19"><strong>The conch</strong> symbolises democratic order and the right to speak. Its power declines as Jack’s tribe grows, and its destruction coincides with Piggy’s death — democracy and reason die together.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Piggy’s glasses</strong> symbolise intellectual power and scientific capability. Their theft by Jack’s tribe represents the seizure of practical resources by authoritarian forces.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n21"><strong>The signal fire</strong> symbolises the boys’ connection to civilisation and hope of rescue. Its decline mirrors their descent into savagery; its abandonment marks the triumph of the present over the future.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n22"><strong>The beast</strong> symbolises innate human evil. It shifts from an imagined creature to the dead parachutist to the Lord of the Flies, revealing that the real beast is within — “I’m part of you.”</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n23">When writing about characters in the exam, always discuss what they <em>represent</em> (their allegorical function), how Golding uses methods to present them (language, imagery, structure), and what their fate says about Golding’s message (AO1 + AO2 + AO3 working together).</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n25">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n26">Each character embodies an idea: Ralph (democracy), Jack (dictatorship), Piggy (reason), Simon (spiritual truth), Roger (sadism).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n27">Key symbols — the conch, glasses, fire, and beast — track the collapse of civilisation from democratic order to savage chaos.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n28">Golding shows that violence and fear always overpower reason and goodness when the structures of civilisation are removed.</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n24">In a character-focused essay, avoid simply describing what a character does. Instead, analyse what they <strong>represent</strong> (their allegorical function) and how Golding uses them to convey his message about human nature. For example: “Golding uses Piggy’s marginalisation to suggest that rational thought is consistently undervalued in societies that reward physical strength and charisma — a critique reflecting his post-war disillusionment with the idea that reason alone can prevent atrocity.”</p>
