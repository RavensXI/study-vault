--- DESCRIPTION ---
How a trading company conquered a subcontinent — East India Company to Crown rule, 1600 to 1858.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">A Company That Became an Empire</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The British acquired India almost by accident. In 1600 Elizabeth I granted a royal charter to a group of London merchants &mdash; the <dfn class="term" data-def="A trading company chartered in 1600 with a monopoly on English trade with Asia; it became the political ruler of India before being abolished in 1858.">East India Company</dfn> &mdash; giving them a monopoly on English trade with Asia. For the first 150 years they were one trading company among many, competing with Dutch, French and Portuguese rivals. By 1857 the same company had become the political ruler of an Indian empire of 200 million people, with its own army, courts, currency and tax system. Untangling how this happened &mdash; and what it cost both Britain and India &mdash; is the spine of the AQA factor essay on empire.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: who won the Battle of Plassey, in what year, and why it mattered.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>At the Battle of Plassey in 1757, Robert Clive defeated the Nawab of Bengal Siraj ud-Daulah using bribery, betrayal and a small Company army. Plassey gave the East India Company control of Bengal&rsquo;s tax revenue &mdash; the moment a trading company became a territorial power.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Robert Clive and the Battle of Plassey, 1757</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Robert <dfn class="term" data-def="East India Company commander whose victory at Plassey in 1757 made the Company the dominant power in Bengal; later the first Governor of Bengal.">Clive</dfn> arrived in India as a Company clerk in 1744. He showed a soldier&rsquo;s talent in skirmishes with the French at Arcot (1751) and Calcutta (1757), recapturing the city after the Nawab of Bengal had captured it the previous year (the &lsquo;Black Hole&rsquo; of Calcutta). Plassey itself was less a battle than a managed coup: Clive bribed the Nawab&rsquo;s commander, Mir Jafar, who held back his forces while Clive&rsquo;s 3,000-strong army faced down 50,000. Mir Jafar was installed as a puppet ruler. The Company received tax revenue from Bengal &mdash; the <em>diwani</em> &mdash; in 1765, formalising the new arrangement. From this point on, Bengali peasant taxes paid for British armies that conquered the rest of India.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Warren Hastings and Company Rule</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Warren <dfn class="term" data-def="First Governor-General of Bengal (1772-1785) who consolidated East India Company rule; impeached in Britain but acquitted after a long trial.">Hastings</dfn> became the first Governor-General of Bengal in 1772. He centralised Company government, reformed the tax system, expanded the Company army (the <dfn class="term" data-def="Indian soldiers serving in the East India Company army; from a Persian word for soldier.">sepoy</dfn> regiments) and patronised Indian scholarship &mdash; he commissioned the first English translation of the Bhagavad Gita. Yet his methods were ruthless: he extracted huge sums from the Begums of Oudh and the Raja of Benares to pay for war. On his return to Britain in 1785 he was impeached for corruption. The trial dragged on until 1795 when he was acquitted &mdash; but the case fixed in British public opinion the idea that Indian rule needed regulation. Pitt&rsquo;s India Act 1784 had already given Parliament a controlling Board over Company decisions.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n8">Lord Dalhousie, Governor-General from 1848, used the <strong>Doctrine of Lapse</strong> to annex Indian states whose rulers died without a natural-born male heir. Adopted heirs (a long-standing Hindu practice) were no longer accepted. Satara (1848), Jhansi (1853), Nagpur (1854) and finally Oudh (1856) all &lsquo;lapsed&rsquo; to the Company. Annexing Oudh was the spark that lit the powder keg: many sepoys came from Oudh, and now their homeland had been confiscated by the Company they served.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">The 1857 Indian Rebellion</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">In May 1857 sepoys at Meerut shot their British officers and marched on Delhi, where they declared the elderly Mughal emperor Bahadur Shah II the head of a restored empire. The trigger was the <dfn class="term" data-def="Cartridges issued in 1857 for the new Enfield rifle, rumoured to be greased with cow and pig fat &mdash; offensive to both Hindu and Muslim sepoys.">greased cartridges</dfn> for the new Enfield rifle, rumoured to be greased with cow fat (offensive to Hindus, who venerate the cow) and pig fat (offensive to Muslims, who consider the pig unclean). To load the rifle, a sepoy had to bite the cartridge open. The deeper causes were: anger at the Doctrine of Lapse; resentment at evangelical Christian missions; new laws permitting widow remarriage; and the annexation of Oudh.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11">The rising spread across northern and central India &mdash; Lucknow, Cawnpore, Jhansi (where the Rani fought as a cavalry leader). It took the British 14 months to suppress it, helped by Punjabi and Sikh troops who stayed loyal. The reprisals were savage: rebel sepoys were sometimes blown from cannons; whole villages were destroyed. Bahadur Shah was put on trial and exiled to Burma. The Company was dissolved. The Government of India Act 1858 transferred direct rule to the Crown, making Queen Victoria sovereign of India and creating the position of Viceroy. In 1877 Disraeli proclaimed Victoria <em>Empress of India</em>. The British called it the &lsquo;Indian Mutiny&rsquo;; Indian nationalists later called it the &lsquo;First War of Independence&rsquo;.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n12" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: write down three causes of the 1857 Rebellion and the year direct Crown rule began.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Causes of the 1857 Rebellion included the greased cartridges, the Doctrine of Lapse (especially the annexation of Oudh in 1856), missionary activity and laws seen as attacking Hindu and Muslim custom. The Government of India Act 1858 ended Company rule and put India under direct Crown government.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Impact on Britain and India</h2>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>How empire reshaped both societies</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n14">Empire reshaped both societies. In Britain, Indian wealth helped fund country houses (Robert Clive&rsquo;s Powis Castle holds an Indian collection that is still in place today), the Bank of England and the consumer revolution &mdash; cotton, tea, sugar, indigo. Indian words entered English: bungalow, pyjamas, shampoo, jodhpurs, verandah. The Company army opened a route to wealth for younger sons of the gentry. In India, the picture is more contested. Britain built railways (53,000 km by 1947), telegraph, postal services, irrigation canals and English-language universities. But it also imposed cash-crop economies that worsened famines (the Bengal famines of 1770 and 1943 each killed millions), restricted Indian industry to protect British exporters, and held political power above all but a handful of Indians until 1947.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n15">Linking to the Factor Essay</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n16">India is the keystone case study for the AQA factor essay. <strong>Economic resources</strong> drove the Company&rsquo;s expansion: textiles, opium, indigo, tea. <strong>War</strong> turned trading posts into provinces &mdash; Plassey, the Mysore wars, the Maratha wars, the Sikh wars, the 1857 reprisals. <strong>Ideas</strong> justified British rule: from Hastings&rsquo; orientalism to Macaulay&rsquo;s 1835 Minute on Education arguing for English-language schooling. <strong>The role of individuals</strong> is unavoidable &mdash; Clive, Hastings, Dalhousie, the Rani of Jhansi. A 16-mark essay on India needs to weigh which of these factors did the heaviest work, and the strongest answers note that economic resources and war reinforced each other: tax revenue paid for armies, armies seized more revenue.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n18">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n19">Plassey 1757 turned the East India Company into a territorial power; Warren Hastings consolidated Company government from 1772 and the Doctrine of Lapse expanded it further from 1848.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n20">The 1857 Rebellion combined the immediate trigger of the greased cartridges with deep grievances over annexation, religion and cultural reform; British reprisals were brutal.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n21">The Government of India Act 1858 ended Company rule and made the Crown the direct sovereign of India; the cultural and economic impact ran in both directions and is contested by historians.</li>
</ul>
