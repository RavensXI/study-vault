<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Structure of DNA</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="Deoxyribonucleic acid; a large polymer that carries the genetic instructions for the development and functioning of living organisms.">DNA</dfn> (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a polymer found in the nucleus of cells. It is shaped as a <dfn class="term" data-def="The twisted-ladder shape of a DNA molecule, formed by two strands of nucleotides wound around each other.">double helix</dfn> — two strands twisted around each other like a spiral ladder. Each strand is made up of repeating units called <dfn class="term" data-def="The monomer of DNA, consisting of a sugar, a phosphate group and one of four bases (A, T, C or G).">nucleotide</dfn>s.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Each nucleotide consists of three parts: a sugar (deoxyribose), a phosphate group and one of four bases — adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C) or guanine (G). The two strands are linked by <dfn class="term" data-def="A pair of bases on opposite strands of DNA held together by weak hydrogen bonds. A pairs with T, and C pairs with G.">complementary base pair</dfn>s joined by weak hydrogen bonds. Adenine always pairs with thymine (A–T), and cytosine always pairs with guanine (C–G).</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this and list the three parts of a nucleotide, then write out the complementary base pairing rules.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>A nucleotide = sugar + phosphate + base. The base pairing rules are: A pairs with T, and C pairs with G. The strands are held together by weak hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Genes, Genomes and Protein Synthesis</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A short section of DNA on a chromosome that codes for a specific protein.">gene</dfn> is a section of DNA that codes for a specific protein. Different proteins control different characteristics. The <dfn class="term" data-def="The entire set of genetic material (DNA) of an organism.">genome</dfn> is the entire DNA of an organism — all of its genes and the DNA between them. Understanding the genome helps scientists identify genes linked to diseases and develop new treatments.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Genes are found on <dfn class="term" data-def="A thread-like structure of coiled DNA found in the nucleus, carrying many genes. Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes.">chromosome</dfn>s inside the nucleus. Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes (46 in total). Each gene can exist in different versions called <dfn class="term" data-def="Different forms of the same gene. For example, the gene for eye colour has alleles for brown, blue and green.">allele</dfn>s, and it is the combination of alleles that determines an organism’s characteristics.</p>

When two gametes fuse at fertilisation, the resulting <dfn class="term" data-def="The cell formed when two gametes fuse at fertilisation, containing a full set of chromosomes.">zygote</dfn> has the full set of 46 chromosomes and a unique combination of alleles from both parents.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Monohybrid Inheritance</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Inheritance describes how characteristics are passed from parents to offspring through alleles. An allele can be <dfn class="term" data-def="An allele that is expressed in the phenotype even when only one copy is present. Shown with a capital letter (e.g. B).">dominant</dfn> (shown with a capital letter, e.g. B) or <dfn class="term" data-def="An allele that is only expressed in the phenotype when two copies are present (homozygous recessive). Shown with a lowercase letter (e.g. b).">recessive</dfn> (lowercase, e.g. b). A dominant allele is always expressed when present. A recessive allele is only expressed when two copies are inherited (one from each parent).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12">An organism with two identical alleles for a gene is <dfn class="term" data-def="Having two identical alleles for a particular gene, e.g. BB or bb.">homozygous</dfn> (BB or bb). An organism with two different alleles is <dfn class="term" data-def="Having two different alleles for a particular gene, e.g. Bb.">heterozygous</dfn> (Bb). The combination of alleles is the <dfn class="term" data-def="The genetic makeup of an organism; the alleles it carries for a particular gene (e.g. BB, Bb or bb).">genotype</dfn>; the physical characteristic that is expressed is the <dfn class="term" data-def="The observable physical characteristics of an organism, determined by its genotype and environment.">phenotype</dfn>.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n13" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: define dominant, recessive, homozygous, heterozygous, genotype and phenotype from memory.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Dominant alleles are expressed when one or two copies are present. Recessive alleles are only expressed when homozygous (two copies). Genotype = alleles an organism has. Phenotype = the characteristic shown.</p>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n24">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">DNA is a double helix of nucleotides with complementary base pairing (A–T, C–G). A gene is a section of DNA that codes for a protein.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n26">Meiosis produces four genetically different haploid gametes. Monohybrid crosses use Punnett squares to predict offspring genotypes and phenotypes.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n27">Variation is caused by genetics (mutations, sexual reproduction), the environment, or both. The Human Genome Project mapped all human DNA and has applications in medicine and forensics.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n15">For example, if both parents are heterozygous (Bb × Bb), the Punnett square gives: BB, Bb, Bb, bb. The ratio of phenotypes would be 3 dominant : 1 recessive. This means there is a 75% probability of the dominant phenotype and a 25% probability of the recessive phenotype.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n16">You also need to understand how sex is determined. Females have two X chromosomes (XX), and males have one X and one Y (XY). A Punnett square for sex inheritance shows a 50:50 chance of the offspring being male or female.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Variation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Variation refers to the differences between individuals of the same species. <dfn class="term" data-def="Differences between organisms caused by differences in their DNA, resulting from mutations or the mixing of alleles during sexual reproduction.">Genetic variation</dfn> is caused by differences in DNA — these arise through mutations and the mixing of alleles during sexual reproduction (meiosis). <dfn class="term" data-def="Differences between organisms caused by environmental factors such as diet, climate or lifestyle.">Environmental variation</dfn> results from the conditions an organism lives in, such as diet, sunlight or exercise.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n19">Most characteristics are influenced by both genes and the environment. Most phenotypic features result from multiple genes rather than a single gene. Mutations occur naturally and produce new alleles. Most <dfn class="term" data-def="A random change in the base sequence of DNA, which may alter the protein produced by a gene.">mutation</dfn>s have no effect on the phenotype. Some have a small effect, and very rarely a single mutation can significantly change the phenotype.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n20" data-revision-tip="Close this and give one example of genetic variation, one of environmental variation, and one that is influenced by both.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Variation can be genetic (from DNA/alleles), environmental (from surroundings) or both. Most mutations have no effect on phenotype. Most phenotypic features are controlled by multiple genes.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n21">The Human Genome Project</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22">The <dfn class="term" data-def="An international research project that mapped and sequenced all human DNA. Completed in 2003, it has applications in medicine, forensics and understanding evolution.">Human Genome Project</dfn> was a major international effort to map and sequence every gene in the human genome. Its potential applications include identifying genes linked to inherited diseases, developing personalised medicines, understanding evolutionary relationships between species and advancing forensic science.</p><p data-narration-id="n23">When drawing a Punnett square, always show the parental genotypes, the gametes, and the offspring genotypes and phenotypes. State the ratio clearly (e.g. 3:1 or 1:1). A common error is to write the probability as a certainty — say “there is a 25% chance” rather than “one in four offspring will be”. Remember these are probabilities, not guarantees.</p>
