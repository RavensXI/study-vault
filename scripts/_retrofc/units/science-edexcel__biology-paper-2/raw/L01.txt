--- DESCRIPTION ---
How the body maintains stable internal conditions and how the nervous system detects stimuli and coordinates responses.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Is Homeostasis?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Your body works best when its internal conditions stay within narrow limits. <dfn class="term" data-def="The regulation of internal conditions to maintain a stable environment inside the body, despite changes in the external environment.">Homeostasis</dfn> is the process that keeps factors such as body temperature, blood glucose concentration and water balance at optimal levels. Without homeostasis, enzymes would denature and chemical reactions inside cells would slow down or stop entirely.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: Name three internal conditions that homeostasis regulates.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Homeostasis maintains a stable internal environment by controlling conditions such as body temperature, blood glucose concentration and water balance — all of which affect enzyme activity.</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n4">All homeostatic control systems involve three key components. A <dfn class="term" data-def="A cell or organ that detects a change (stimulus) in the internal or external environment.">receptor</dfn> detects a change in the environment (a stimulus). A <dfn class="term" data-def="The part of a control system that receives information from receptors and coordinates the response, such as the brain or spinal cord.">coordination centre</dfn> (such as the brain or spinal cord) receives and processes the information. An <dfn class="term" data-def="A muscle or gland that carries out a response to restore optimum conditions.">effector</dfn> (a muscle or gland) carries out the response to bring the condition back to its set point.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">The Nervous System</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The organ system that detects stimuli and coordinates rapid responses, consisting of the brain, spinal cord and nerves.">nervous system</dfn> enables humans to react to their surroundings and coordinate behaviour. It uses electrical impulses that travel along nerve cells to produce fast, short-lived responses. The central nervous system (CNS) consists of the brain and the <dfn class="term" data-def="A column of nervous tissue running through the spine that relays messages between the brain and the rest of the body.">spinal cord</dfn>. Information from receptors passes along sensory neurones to the CNS, which then sends signals along motor neurones to effectors.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n7">There are three types of neurone you need to know. <dfn class="term" data-def="A nerve cell that carries electrical impulses from receptors to the central nervous system.">Sensory neurones</dfn> carry impulses from receptors to the CNS. <dfn class="term" data-def="A nerve cell found within the CNS that connects sensory neurones to motor neurones.">Relay neurones</dfn> are found entirely within the CNS and connect sensory neurones to motor neurones. <dfn class="term" data-def="A nerve cell that carries electrical impulses from the CNS to effectors such as muscles or glands.">Motor neurones</dfn> carry impulses from the CNS to effectors.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n8">Each neurone has a cell body containing the nucleus, thin fibres called <dfn class="term" data-def="A long fibre that carries electrical impulses away from the cell body of a neurone.">axon</dfn>s and <dfn class="term" data-def="A shorter branching fibre that carries electrical impulses towards the cell body of a neurone.">dendron</dfn>s. The axon is often surrounded by a <dfn class="term" data-def="A fatty insulating layer around the axon of a neurone that speeds up the transmission of electrical impulses.">myelin sheath</dfn> — a fatty layer that insulates the nerve fibre and speeds up the transmission of impulses.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Synapses</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">Neurones do not touch each other directly. The tiny gap between two neurones is called a <dfn class="term" data-def="The junction between two neurones where a small gap exists. Signals cross the gap via chemical messengers called neurotransmitters.">synapse</dfn>. When an electrical impulse reaches the end of the first neurone, it triggers the release of chemical <dfn class="term" data-def="Chemical messengers released at synapses that diffuse across the gap and bind to receptors on the next neurone, triggering a new electrical impulse.">neurotransmitter</dfn>s. These chemicals diffuse across the synaptic gap and bind to receptors on the membrane of the next neurone, triggering a new electrical impulse. This means signals can only travel in one direction across a synapse.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n11" data-revision-tip="Close this and explain in your own words how a signal crosses a synapse in three steps.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>At a synapse, electrical signals are converted into chemical signals (neurotransmitters) and then back into electrical signals in the next neurone. This ensures impulses travel in one direction only.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n12">The Reflex Arc</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A rapid, automatic response to a stimulus that does not involve conscious thought, designed to protect the body from harm.">reflex action</dfn> is an automatic, rapid response to a potentially harmful stimulus. Reflexes protect the body — for example, pulling your hand away from a hot object or your pupil constricting in bright light.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n22">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n23">Homeostasis is the regulation of internal conditions (temperature, blood glucose, water) to maintain a stable environment for enzyme activity.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n24">The nervous system uses electrical impulses along sensory, relay and motor neurones to produce rapid responses. Signals cross synapses via neurotransmitters.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">Reflex arcs bypass the brain to produce fast, automatic protective responses: receptor &rarr; sensory neurone &rarr; relay neurone &rarr; motor neurone &rarr; effector.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n16">For example, if you touch a hot plate, pain receptors in your skin detect the stimulus. The impulse travels along a sensory neurone to a relay neurone in the spinal cord. The relay neurone passes the impulse to a motor neurone, which stimulates the effector (a muscle in your arm) to contract and pull your hand away. This all happens without conscious thought — your brain is not involved in the initial response.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: draw the reflex arc from memory, labelling all six components from receptor to effector.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The reflex arc follows the pathway: stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone (CNS) → motor neurone → effector → response. The brain is bypassed, making the response extremely fast.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Why Homeostasis Matters</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19">The internal environment must stay stable because cells depend on enzyme-controlled reactions. Enzymes have an optimum temperature and pH — if conditions drift too far from the optimum, enzymes change shape (denature) and can no longer catalyse reactions. This is why the body constantly monitors and adjusts conditions using the receptor → coordination centre → effector pathway described above.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Both the nervous system and the endocrine (hormonal) system play roles in homeostasis. The nervous system produces fast, short-term responses via electrical impulses, while the endocrine system produces slower, longer-lasting responses via hormones. You will explore the endocrine system in the next lesson.</p><p data-narration-id="n21">In extended-response questions on the nervous system, make sure you describe the full reflex arc pathway in the correct order. A common mistake is to say the brain controls reflex actions — it does not. State clearly that the relay neurone is in the spinal cord and that the response is automatic and involuntary. Use precise terminology: receptor, sensory neurone, relay neurone, motor neurone, effector.</p>
