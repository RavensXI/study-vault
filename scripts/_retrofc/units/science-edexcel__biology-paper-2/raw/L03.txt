--- DESCRIPTION ---
The menstrual cycle, hormones controlling reproduction, contraception methods, and assisted reproductive technology.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Menstrual Cycle</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The <dfn class="term" data-def="A recurring cycle of approximately 28 days in which the uterus lining builds up, an egg is released, and the lining is shed if fertilisation does not occur.">menstrual cycle</dfn> is a roughly 28-day process controlled by hormones. It prepares the female body for pregnancy each month. If the released egg is not fertilised, the lining of the uterus is shed during menstruation (a period), and the cycle begins again.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">The cycle has four main stages. <strong>Days 1–5:</strong> Menstruation occurs — the uterus lining breaks down and is shed. <strong>Days 6–13:</strong> The uterus lining begins to rebuild and thicken. <strong>Day 14:</strong> <dfn class="term" data-def="The release of a mature egg from an ovary, approximately halfway through the menstrual cycle.">Ovulation</dfn> occurs — a mature egg is released from one of the ovaries. <strong>Days 15–28:</strong> The uterus lining is maintained, ready for a fertilised egg to implant. If no fertilisation occurs, the cycle starts again.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Hormones Controlling the Menstrual Cycle</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Four hormones interact to control the menstrual cycle. <dfn class="term" data-def="Follicle-stimulating hormone; produced by the pituitary gland, it stimulates the maturation of an egg in an ovary and the production of oestrogen.">FSH</dfn> (follicle-stimulating hormone) is released by the pituitary gland. It causes an egg to mature inside a follicle in the ovary and stimulates the ovaries to produce <dfn class="term" data-def="A hormone produced by the ovaries that causes the uterus lining to thicken and stimulates the pituitary gland to release LH.">oestrogen</dfn>.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Oestrogen is produced by the ovaries. It causes the uterus lining to thicken and repair after menstruation. Rising oestrogen levels also stimulate the pituitary gland to release a surge of <dfn class="term" data-def="Luteinising hormone; produced by the pituitary gland, it triggers ovulation around day 14 of the menstrual cycle.">LH</dfn> (luteinising hormone). LH triggers ovulation around day 14.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n7" data-revision-tip="Cover this and list the four hormones of the menstrual cycle and what each one does.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>FSH matures the egg and stimulates oestrogen production. Oestrogen thickens the uterus lining and triggers LH release. LH triggers ovulation. <dfn class="term" data-def="A hormone produced by the ovaries after ovulation that maintains the uterus lining and inhibits the release of FSH and LH.">Progesterone</dfn> maintains the uterus lining after ovulation and inhibits FSH and LH.</p>
</div>

<!-- DIAGRAM -->

<p data-narration-id="n8">After ovulation, the empty follicle becomes the corpus luteum and produces progesterone. Progesterone maintains the thick uterus lining so that a fertilised egg can implant. It also inhibits FSH and LH, preventing another egg from maturing. If no fertilisation occurs, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and the uterus lining is shed — beginning menstruation and the next cycle.</p>

After ovulation, progesterone from the corpus luteum inhibits both FSH and LH (negative feedback), preventing a second egg from being released. When progesterone falls, FSH is no longer inhibited, so the cycle restarts.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Contraception</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><dfn class="term" data-def="Methods used to prevent pregnancy, including hormonal and barrier approaches.">Contraception</dfn> prevents pregnancy. Methods fall into two main categories: <dfn class="term" data-def="Contraception that uses synthetic hormones to prevent ovulation or thicken cervical mucus, such as the combined pill, mini-pill, injection, implant or IUD.">hormonal contraception</dfn> and <dfn class="term" data-def="Contraception that physically prevents sperm from reaching an egg, such as condoms or diaphragms.">barrier methods</dfn>.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Hormonal methods</strong> work by releasing hormones that interfere with the menstrual cycle. The combined pill contains oestrogen and progesterone — it prevents ovulation and thickens cervical mucus so sperm cannot pass through easily. The progesterone-only pill (mini-pill) mainly thickens cervical mucus. Other options include contraceptive injections, implants and intrauterine devices (IUDs) that release hormones.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Barrier methods</strong> physically block sperm from reaching the egg. Condoms (male and female) also protect against sexually transmitted infections (STIs). Diaphragms cover the cervix and are used with spermicide. Barrier methods have no hormonal side effects but must be used correctly every time.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n14">Hormonal methods are very effective at preventing pregnancy when used correctly but can have side effects such as headaches, mood changes or blood clots. They do not protect against STIs. Barrier methods are less effective at preventing pregnancy but condoms are the only method that protects against STIs. The choice of contraception depends on reliability, side effects, convenience and whether STI protection is needed.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="Close this and name two advantages and two disadvantages of hormonal contraception compared to barrier methods.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Hormonal contraception is highly effective at preventing pregnancy but does not protect against STIs and can have side effects. Barrier methods (e.g. condoms) protect against STIs but are less effective at preventing pregnancy.</p>
</div>

<div class="higher-only">
<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">Some couples have difficulty conceiving naturally. <dfn class="term" data-def="Assisted Reproductive Technology; medical procedures used to help achieve pregnancy, including IVF and clomifene therapy.">ART</dfn> includes treatments that help people become pregnant.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n18"><dfn class="term" data-def="In vitro fertilisation; a procedure where eggs are fertilised by sperm outside the body in a laboratory, and the resulting embryo is implanted into the uterus.">IVF</dfn> (in vitro fertilisation) involves giving the mother FSH and LH to stimulate multiple eggs to mature. The eggs are collected and fertilised with sperm in a laboratory. Fertilised embryos develop for a few days before one or two are transferred to the mother’s uterus. IVF is effective but expensive, physically demanding and has a relatively low success rate per cycle.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n19"><dfn class="term" data-def="A fertility drug that stimulates the pituitary gland to release FSH and LH, triggering ovulation in women who do not ovulate regularly.">Clomifene</dfn> therapy is a fertility drug. It works by stimulating the release of FSH and LH from the pituitary gland, which triggers ovulation. It is less invasive and cheaper than IVF, though it can increase the chance of multiple pregnancies.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n20" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: explain the steps of IVF in order, from hormone injection to embryo transfer.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>IVF involves stimulating egg production with FSH/LH, collecting eggs, fertilising them with sperm in a lab, and transferring embryos to the uterus. Clomifene therapy stimulates ovulation by triggering FSH and LH release.</p>
</div></div>



--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n22">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n23">The menstrual cycle is controlled by four hormones: FSH, oestrogen, LH and progesterone, which interact through feedback loops.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n24">Hormonal contraception prevents ovulation or thickens cervical mucus; barrier methods physically block sperm. Condoms are the only method that protects against STIs.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">ART includes IVF (eggs fertilised in a lab) and clomifene therapy (drug that stimulates ovulation) to help couples who struggle to conceive naturally.</li>
</ul>
