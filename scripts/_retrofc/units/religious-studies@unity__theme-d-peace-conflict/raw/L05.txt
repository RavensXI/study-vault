<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Is Peacemaking?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="The action of trying to establish peace in the world or in a certain part of it.">Peacemaking</dfn> means actively working to bring about peace and end conflict. It goes beyond simply avoiding violence — it involves negotiation, reconciliation, and practical efforts to address the causes of war. Religious believers often feel a duty to be peacemakers because their faith teaches that peace reflects God’s will for humanity.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Religious people may work for peace between different countries, between people of different religious groups, and between any other conflicting communities. This can involve speaking out about peace, helping with negotiations, giving money to charity, or campaigning for government action.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Define a peacemaker in your own words.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>A <dfn class="term" data-def="A person who works to establish peace in the world or in a certain part of it.">peacemaker</dfn> is a person who works to establish peace. Both Christianity and Islam teach that believers should actively pursue peace rather than simply wait for conflict to end.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Christian Teachings on Peacemaking</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Christianity places a strong emphasis on working for peace. Jesus taught his followers to be peacemakers and to love even their enemies. Several key Bible passages support this:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">“Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called children of God” (Matthew 5:9). This verse from the Sermon on the Mount shows that Jesus considered peacemaking a sign of being close to God. Christians who work for peace believe they are following Jesus’ direct instruction.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8">“Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you” (Matthew 5:44). Jesus taught that Christians should respond to hostility with love, not more violence. This challenges believers to seek peaceful solutions even when facing aggression.</p>

He has helped to negotiate between different conflicting parties, such as helping to reconcile the USA and Cuba. The Church also speaks out about peace and reconciliation, helps with peace negotiations, and gives money to charity to help victims of war.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n10">The Quakers are a Christian group who are committed pacifists. During World War I and World War II, they refused to fight but took peace-making roles instead — acting as mediators between warring nations, serving as medics, cooks, and mechanics, or directly supporting victims of war.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n11">Islamic Teachings on Peacemaking</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Islam is fundamentally a religion of peace. The Arabic word “Islam” comes from “salam,” meaning peace or safety. Muslims greet each other with “salaam alaikum,” which means “peace be upon you.” This shows how central the concept of peace is to Islamic life.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n13">The Qur’an instructs Muslims to pursue peace: “Making peace is better” (Surah 2). It also teaches that Muslims must not refuse peace when it is offered: “But if they incline towards peace, you must also incline towards it, and put your trust in God” (Qur’an 8:61).</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down what you recall about prophet Muhammad said.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The Prophet Muhammad said: “We have finished lesser jihad, let us now focus on greater jihad” (Hadith). Greater <dfn class="term" data-def="The personal struggle of every Muslim to live according to the teachings of Islam.">jihad</dfn> is the inner spiritual struggle to be a good Muslim, which includes working for peace and justice in the community.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n15">The concept of <dfn class="term" data-def="The worldwide community of Muslims, united by their shared faith.">ummah</dfn> (the global Muslim community) means that Muslims feel a responsibility to support one another and to work for peace across national borders. Compassion is central to Islam — the Qur’an lists “the Merciful” and “the Compassionate” among the 99 names of Allah.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Who Are the Victims of War?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17"><dfn class="term" data-def="Those who are harmed during a war, including those killed, injured, or left homeless.">Victims of war</dfn> are not only soldiers who fight. War affects families, friends, children, and anyone living in a war zone. Buildings, water sources, land, and animals are also damaged or destroyed. The suffering caused by war can last for generations — people may lose their homes, their livelihoods, and their sense of safety.</p>

  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n18">War creates many different types of victims. Those who fight may be killed or seriously injured. Families lose loved ones and may be separated. Children grow up in fear and may miss years of education. Civilians in war-torn areas lose access to clean water, food, healthcare, and shelter. Refugees are forced to flee their homes and countries, often with nothing. The land itself suffers — buildings are destroyed, the environment is polluted, and infrastructure is ruined. This suffering can continue long after the fighting stops.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n19">Christian Responses to Victims of War</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Christian teachings encourage believers to respond to victims of war with compassion, mercy, and practical help. Jesus taught: “Love your neighbour as yourself” (Mark 12:31), which means showing care and support to those in need. The Golden Rule — “treat others as you would like to be treated” — also motivates Christians to help.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n21">In the Parable of the Sheep and the Goats, Jesus said: “Truly I tell you, just as you did it to one of the least of these who are members of my family, you did it to me” (Matthew 25:40). This teaches that people will be judged at the end of time according to whether they have helped others. Those who give food, clothing, and shelter to people in need will go to heaven. Christians therefore try to help victims of war as a direct response to this teaching.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n22" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down what you recall about christians may respond to victims of war by.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Christians may respond to victims of war by: praying for victims; campaigning for their government to provide support; helping victims directly by giving clothing, food, or shelter; and giving money to charities so that they can help victims directly.</p>
</div>

It has provided support to people affected by conflicts in Syria and South Sudan, among other places. Christian Aid provides food and water, education, healthcare, clothing, and shelter to those in need. Its work is a practical example of how Christians put Jesus’ teachings into action.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n24">Islamic Responses to Victims of War</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n25">Islam teaches Muslims to show mercy and compassion to all people, especially those suffering because of war. Muslims are encouraged to help victims of war through charity and humanitarian aid. The concept of ummah means Muslims believe in supporting fellow human beings as part of the worldwide Muslim community, regardless of nationality.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n26">The Prophet Muhammad set an example by caring for prisoners of war and treating them with dignity, showing that even enemies deserve humane treatment. The Qur’an teaches: “Whoever has saved a life, it is as though he has saved the whole of mankind” (Surah 5:32) — a Qur’anic verse that records a ruling originally given to the Children of Israel, though Islamic scholars widely apply this life-sanctity principle universally. This verse motivates Muslims to protect and support those whose lives are at risk because of war.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n27"><dfn class="term" data-def="A form of Islamic charity; one of the Five Pillars requiring Muslims to give 2.5% of their savings to those in need.">Zakah</dfn> (charitable giving) is one of the Five Pillars of Islam. Muslims are required to give 2.5% of their savings to those in need, which can include victims of war. Many Islamic charities channel zakah funds to provide emergency relief, rebuild communities, and support refugees displaced by conflict.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n29">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n30">Both Christianity and Islam teach that believers have a duty to work for peace — Jesus said “Blessed are the peacemakers” and the Qur’an teaches “Making peace is better.”</li><li data-narration-id="n31">Victims of war include soldiers, civilians, children, families, and refugees — religious believers respond through prayer, charity, campaigning, and direct practical help.</li><li data-narration-id="n32">Religious organisations like Christian Aid put faith into action by providing food, water, education, healthcare, and shelter to those affected by conflict.</li></ul>
