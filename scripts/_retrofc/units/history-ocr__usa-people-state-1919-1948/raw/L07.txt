Hundred Days, Alphabet Agencies and the Wagner Act — Roosevelt remaking American government.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Roosevelt Takes Office</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Franklin D. Roosevelt was inaugurated as the 32nd President of the United States on 4 March 1933. In his famous opening address, he told the American people that &ldquo;the only thing we have to fear is fear itself&rdquo; &mdash; framing the Depression as a crisis of confidence that decisive action could break. Within the first hundred days of his presidency, he and Congress passed a remarkable series of laws that transformed the relationship between the federal government and the American economy. The scale of intervention was without peacetime precedent.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: name five agencies created during the Hundred Days and write one specific thing each one did.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The <dfn class="term" data-def="The first 100 days of Roosevelt's presidency, from March to June 1933, during which an extraordinary series of laws created the core institutions of the New Deal.">Hundred Days</dfn> (March&ndash;June 1933) produced the core of the First New Deal. Key agencies created included: the <dfn class="term" data-def="Agricultural Adjustment Agency &mdash; paid farmers to reduce production to raise food prices, funded by a tax on food processors.">AAA</dfn> (Agricultural Adjustment Agency), which paid farmers to reduce production in order to raise food prices; the <dfn class="term" data-def="Civilian Conservation Corps &mdash; employed young unemployed men in conservation work on federal land, providing wages, food, and accommodation.">CCC</dfn> (Civilian Conservation Corps), which employed young men in national parks and forests; the <dfn class="term" data-def="Federal Emergency Relief Administration &mdash; provided $500 million in grants to states for direct relief to the unemployed.">FERA</dfn> (Federal Emergency Relief Administration), which gave half a billion dollars in grants to states for direct relief; the <dfn class="term" data-def="National Recovery Administration &mdash; set industry-wide codes on wages, hours, and prices to stabilise the economy, before being declared unconstitutional by the Supreme Court in 1935.">NRA</dfn> (National Recovery Administration), which set codes of fair competition across industries; and the <dfn class="term" data-def="Tennessee Valley Authority &mdash; a federal corporation that built dams to provide flood control, electricity, and economic development across seven southern states.">TVA</dfn> (Tennessee Valley Authority), which built dams and provided electricity to seven previously impoverished Southern states.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">The Scale of Federal Expansion</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">The New Deal represented an unprecedented expansion of federal government. Before 1933, Washington spent a tiny fraction of national income and employed a small workforce. By 1936, federal spending had roughly doubled as a share of the economy. Millions of Americans experienced the federal government directly for the first time &mdash; through a CCC paycheck, a FERA relief cheque, or a TVA electricity connection in a home that had never had power. The Glass-Steagall Act (June 1933) separated commercial and investment banking and created the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, guaranteeing bank deposits and halting the bank-run panics that had destroyed savings in 1930&ndash;33.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">The Second New Deal</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">By 1935, the Supreme Court had declared the NRA unconstitutional in the <em>Schechter Poultry</em> case (May 1935) and the AAA was also struck down the following year. Roosevelt responded with a second wave of legislation in 1935 &mdash; often called the Second New Deal &mdash; that shifted emphasis from economic co-ordination to workers&rsquo; rights and social security.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The 1935 National Labor Relations Act, which guaranteed workers the right to organise trade unions and bargain collectively, establishing the National Labor Relations Board to enforce these rights.">Wagner Act</dfn> (National Labor Relations Act, 1935) guaranteed workers the right to join trade unions and bargain collectively with employers. It was the most important piece of labour legislation in American history to that point. The <dfn class="term" data-def="The Social Security Act (1935), which created a system of old-age pensions and unemployment insurance funded by payroll contributions from workers and employers.">Social Security Act</dfn> (August 1935) established old-age pensions for workers over 65 and a system of unemployment insurance &mdash; the first federal social safety net in American history. The Works Progress Administration (<dfn class="term" data-def="Works Progress Administration &mdash; established 1935 to employ millions of Americans in public works projects ranging from construction to theatre, music, art, and writing.">WPA</dfn>), under Harry Hopkins, employed over 8 million people in building projects and arts programmes.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n9">The New Deal was not a single person&rsquo;s work. Frances Perkins, Roosevelt&rsquo;s Labor Secretary and the first woman to serve in a US Cabinet, was the principal architect of the Social Security Act and the federal minimum wage. Harry Hopkins, who ran FERA and later the WPA, was Roosevelt&rsquo;s closest adviser on relief policy and spent money on direct help faster and more effectively than Hoover had ever contemplated. Senator Robert Wagner of New York championed the labour rights act that bears his name against fierce business opposition. Eleanor Roosevelt pressed her husband to do more for African Americans, women workers, and the poor.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n10" data-revision-tip="Close this and write from memory: what did the Wagner Act and Social Security Act each specifically do? Include at least two details per act.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The Wagner Act (1935) guaranteed the right to organise trade unions and established the National Labor Relations Board to enforce those rights and investigate unfair employer practices. Trade union membership surged: the CIO (Congress of Industrial Organisations), a new federation for industrial workers, grew rapidly. The Social Security Act (1935) provided old-age pensions for workers over 65, funded by payroll taxes shared between employers and employees, and created a federal-state unemployment insurance system &mdash; the first federal safety net in American history.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>The Court-Packing Plan of 1937</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11">After the Supreme Court struck down the NRA and AAA, Roosevelt proposed in February 1937 to appoint up to six additional justices &mdash; one for each sitting justice over 70. The plan was widely seen as an attempt to pack the Court with New Deal supporters and was defeated even in the Democrat-controlled Congress. The episode damaged Roosevelt&rsquo;s political standing. Nevertheless, the Court&rsquo;s composition changed naturally through retirement in 1937&ndash;39, and it became substantially more sympathetic to New Deal legislation without the packing plan ever being enacted.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n13">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n14">The Hundred Days (March&ndash;June 1933) created the First New Deal&rsquo;s core agencies: AAA, CCC, FERA, NRA, and TVA &mdash; an unprecedented expansion of federal government intervention in the economy.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n15">The Second New Deal (1935) shifted focus to workers&rsquo; rights and social security: the Wagner Act guaranteed collective bargaining, the Social Security Act created the first federal pension and unemployment system, and the WPA eventually employed over 8 million people.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n16">The New Deal was politically contested throughout &mdash; the NRA was struck down by the Supreme Court in 1935, the court-packing plan failed in 1937, and many programmes excluded African American workers, showing the limits as well as the ambition of Roosevelt&rsquo;s response.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n12">Questions on the New Deal often ask you to distinguish between the First and Second New Deals, or to explain the significance of individual agencies. Make sure you know which agencies were created in 1933 (AAA, CCC, FERA, NRA, TVA) and which came in 1935 (Wagner Act, Social Security Act, WPA). A common exam error is to describe the New Deal as a coherent, unified plan &mdash; it was improvised and contested. The NRA was declared unconstitutional in 1935, and even the Social Security Act excluded most African American workers from its coverage (domestic servants and farm labourers were left out). Including these limits raises your answer to the top band.</p>
