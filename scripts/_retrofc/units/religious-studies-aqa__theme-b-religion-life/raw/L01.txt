Religious and scientific views on how the universe and human life began.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Religious Teachings on the Origin of the Universe</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">One of the biggest questions humans ask is: how did the universe begin? Religious traditions offer explanations through their sacred texts, while science proposes theories based on evidence. The specification requires you to understand both perspectives and how they relate to each other.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3"><strong>Christianity:</strong> The Bible describes God creating the universe in six days and resting on the seventh (Genesis 1). God created light, sky, land, plants, animals and finally humans. Genesis 1:1 states: “In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.” Christians believe God created the universe <em>ex nihilo</em> — out of nothing.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n4"><strong>Islam:</strong> The Qur’an teaches that Allah created the heavens and the earth in six periods (<dfn class="term" data-def="Periods of time mentioned in the Qur’an during which Allah created the heavens and the earth; not necessarily literal 24-hour days.">yawm</dfn>). Surah 21:30 says: “The heavens and the earth were joined together, then We split them apart.” Allah is the sole creator of everything. The Qur’an (51:47) also states: “We built the universe with great might, and We are certainly expanding it.”</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="From memory, explain the literal and non-literal interpretations of Genesis creation. State one argument for each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>There are different <dfn class="term" data-def="Ways of understanding religious texts, ranging from literal (word-for-word) to metaphorical (symbolic meaning).">interpretations</dfn> of these creation accounts. <strong>Literalists</strong> (or creationists) believe the Bible/Qur’an describes exactly what happened — God created everything in six literal days. <strong>Non-literalists</strong> believe the creation stories are metaphorical — the “days” represent long periods of time, and the stories teach <em>that</em> God created, not exactly <em>how</em>.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n6">The Big Bang Theory & Religious Views</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The scientific theory that the universe began approximately 13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense point that rapidly expanded.">Big Bang theory</dfn> is the leading scientific explanation for the origin of the universe. It proposes that the universe began around 13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense point that rapidly expanded.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n8">Many religious believers see no conflict between the Big Bang and their faith. They argue that God <em>caused</em> the Big Bang. Non-literal Christians point out that the sequence in Genesis (light, sky, land, plants, animals, humans) broadly matches the scientific order of events. The Qur’an’s reference to the heavens and earth being “joined together, then split apart” (21:30) has been interpreted by some Muslims as describing the Big Bang. Science explains <em>how</em> the universe began; religion explains <em>why</em>.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Creationist Objections to the Big Bang</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n9">Creationists reject the Big Bang because it contradicts a literal reading of Genesis. They argue that the universe was created in six 24-hour days as the Bible states. Some point out that the Big Bang cannot explain what existed <em>before</em> the explosion or what caused it — suggesting there must be a creator. Young Earth Creationists believe the universe is around 6,000–10,000 years old, based on biblical genealogies.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Origins of Human Life</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Christianity:</strong> Genesis 2:7 says God “formed a man from the dust of the ground and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life.” God then created Eve from Adam’s rib. Christians believe humans are unique because they are made in the “image of God” (<dfn class="term" data-def="The belief that humans are created in God’s likeness, giving them a unique status and dignity in creation.">imago Dei</dfn> — Genesis 1:27).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Islam:</strong> The Qur’an teaches that Allah created Adam from clay (Surah 15:26): “We created man from sounding clay.” Humans are Allah’s finest creation, appointed as <dfn class="term" data-def="The role given to humans by God to look after and care for the earth and its creatures on God’s behalf.">khalifah</dfn> (stewards or caretakers) of the earth.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Evolution & Religious Views</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Charles Darwin’s theory of <dfn class="term" data-def="The scientific theory that species change over time through natural selection, with organisms best adapted to their environment being more likely to survive and reproduce.">evolution</dfn> by natural selection proposes that life developed gradually over billions of years from simple organisms. Humans share a common ancestor with other primates.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15">Many Christians accept evolution as the process God used to create life. Pope Francis stated in 2014: “Evolution in nature is not inconsistent with the notion of creation.” They see evolution as God’s method. However, creationists reject evolution because it contradicts the literal Genesis account.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n16">Many Muslims also accept that evolution may explain how species develop, but most believe that Allah directly created Adam as the first human. Human evolution from apes is generally rejected because the Qur’an describes Adam’s special creation from clay.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n17">The Value of the World</h2>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n18" data-revision-tip="Without looking, define stewardship, dominion, and khalifah. Explain how they relate to environmental responsibility.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Religious believers see the natural world as valuable because God created it. Two key concepts are <dfn class="term" data-def="The God-given right of humans to rule over and have authority over all other living creatures and the natural world.">dominion</dfn> (authority over creation — Genesis 1:28: “Rule over the fish in the sea and the birds in the sky”) and <dfn class="term" data-def="The responsibility given to humans by God to care for and protect the earth, using its resources wisely for future generations.">stewardship</dfn> (caring for creation responsibly). Most modern Christians emphasise stewardship over dominion.</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n19">In Islam, humans are khalifah — caretakers appointed by Allah to look after the earth. The concept of <dfn class="term" data-def="A deep sense of wonder and reverence at the beauty and complexity of the natural world, often seen as evidence of God’s creative power.">awe and wonder</dfn> describes the feeling of amazement at the beauty and complexity of the natural world, which many believers see as evidence of God’s creative power. Psalm 19:1 says: “The heavens declare the glory of God.”</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Responsibility for the Environment</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n20">Both Christianity and Islam teach that humans have a responsibility to protect the environment. Christians believe they will be held accountable by God for how they have treated creation. Pope Francis’s encyclical <em>Laudato Si’</em> (2015) called on all people to protect “our common home.” In Islam, wasting natural resources is considered wrong. The Prophet Muhammad said: “Do not waste water even if you are at a running stream.” On the Day of Judgement, Muslims believe they will answer to Allah for how they treated the earth.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n22">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n23">Christianity and Islam both teach that God/Allah created the universe. Literalists believe in six-day creation; non-literalists see the accounts as metaphorical and compatible with the Big Bang.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n24">Evolution is accepted by many religious believers as God’s method, but creationists reject it. Islam generally accepts biological evolution but not that humans evolved from other species.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n25">Stewardship (caring for creation) and dominion (authority over it) are key concepts. Both religions teach that humans have a responsibility to protect the natural world.</li></ul>
