--- DESCRIPTION ---
The rise of science and business parks, the environmental impact of industry (Castle Cement case study), and changes to rural areas in the UK.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---

<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Post-Industrial Economy</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">A <dfn class="term" data-def="An economy that no longer relies on heavy industry and manufacturing, instead focusing on services, IT and knowledge-based industries.">post-industrial economy</dfn> is one that no longer depends on heavy industry and manufacturing. In the UK, primary and secondary employment has been replaced by tertiary and <dfn class="term" data-def="The sector of the economy based on knowledge, research, IT and information services.">quaternary</dfn> employment. Many of these new jobs are located in science parks and business parks.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and name 3 example jobs in the quaternary sector.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Quaternary industries include jobs in universities, IT companies, biotechnology, research and development, and creative industries such as gaming. These are part of the <strong>knowledge economy</strong>.</p>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n4">Science Parks and Business Parks</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n5"><dfn class="term" data-def="Purpose-built sites on the outskirts of cities that house high-tech and research businesses, often near universities.">Science parks</dfn> and business parks are purpose-built sites where quaternary industries locate. They are typically found on the outskirts of cities near good transport links such as motorways, A-roads, train stations and airports. They are also located close to housing for workers and near universities so that businesses can access cutting-edge research.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button aria-expanded="false" class="collapsible-toggle">
<span>Cambridge Science Park — Case Study</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2" viewbox="0 0 24 24"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"></polyline></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content">
<div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n6">Cambridge Science Park was created by Trinity College, Cambridge, in 1970. It houses businesses in electronics, pharmaceuticals and high-end manufacturing (such as computer chips). The park attracts highly skilled researchers, scientists, engineers and graduates. It is located about 50 miles from London with links to the M11 motorway, rail services and nearby airports. The buildings feature modern sustainable design with car parking, green spaces and energy-efficient features. The park is more environmentally sustainable than traditional industry because it produces less pollution, uses lighter components for transport and develops new technologies that help other products become more efficient.</p>
</div>
</div></div>
<p data-narration-id="n7">The number of science and business parks has grown because there is increasing demand for high-tech products, the UK has many strong research universities, and <dfn class="term" data-def="Groups of similar businesses located close together that benefit from shared resources and knowledge.">clusters</dfn> of related businesses in one place can boost each other’s growth.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n8">Environmental Impact of Industry — Castle Cement</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9">Industry can have major environmental impacts. A key case study is <strong>Hanson Cement</strong> (locally known as Castle Cement) near Clitheroe in Lancashire. The cement works has been operational since 1936, using the local supply of limestone as a raw material. It is well connected by the A59 to the M6 motorway and has a direct railway link for transporting cement around the country.</p>
<h3 data-narration-id="n10">Advantages</h3>
<ul data-narration-id="n11">
<li>The cement works contributes around <strong>£9 million</strong> to the local economy each year, creating a positive <dfn class="term" data-def="A chain reaction where spending in one area leads to more jobs, income and further spending.">multiplier effect</dfn>.</li>
<li>It produces 750,000 tonnes of cement per year and supplied most of the cement for the 2012 Olympics.</li>
<li>Filters in chimneys trap pollutants from burning limestone.</li>
<li>Many birds, badgers and bats live in the vegetation around the works.</li>
</ul>
<h3 data-narration-id="n12">Disadvantages</h3>
<ul data-narration-id="n13">
<li>Congestion in Clitheroe from heavy lorries transporting cement.</li>
<li>Air pollution from the cement works and lorry traffic.</li>
<li>Blasting for limestone causes noise and dust, which locals complain about.</li>
<li>The plant and quarry are visually unattractive and disrupt the Ribble Valley countryside, affecting tourism.</li>
</ul>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Without looking, list 3 ways Castle Cement is becoming more sustainable.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Castle Cement is taking steps to become more sustainable: recycling 100 million tonnes of waste products, using biodegradable cement bags, investing in fuel-efficient and hybrid lorries, increasing the use of rail transport, and planning to restore the old quarry into a nature reserve once limestone extraction ends.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n15">Rural Changes in the UK</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n16">Rural areas in the UK are experiencing very different changes depending on their location. Some are growing rapidly while others are declining. <dfn class="term" data-def="The movement of people from urban areas to rural areas, often commuting back to the city for work.">Counter-urbanisation</dfn> is the process of people moving from cities to the countryside, driven by factors like better transport links, a desire for more space and the availability of remote working.</p>
<h3 data-narration-id="n17">Growth — South Cambridgeshire</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n18">South Cambridgeshire has a population of around 150,000, which is increasing due to migration and is estimated to reach 182,000 by 2031. People move here because of good motorway links to Cambridge and London, an attractive local high street and proximity to the science parks. However, this growth brings challenges: 80% car ownership causes traffic congestion, house prices are unaffordable for many young people, commuters spend money in the city rather than locally and there is pressure on services like the NHS.</p>
<h3 data-narration-id="n19">Decline — The Outer Hebrides</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n20">The Outer Hebrides have a population of just 27,400 and have experienced a 50% population decline since 1901. Most people live on the island of Lewis. Young people leave due to limited job opportunities, poor transport links and distance from cities. Farming mostly involves sheep breeding and provides only part-time work. Fishing is limited due to environmental concerns and overseas ownership. Tourism has increased by 27% since 2007, but the islands lack the infrastructure to support large-scale tourism.</p>

<div class="collapsible-content">
<div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Positive effects:</strong> Newcomers maintain the population of small towns, help keep services such as bus routes, shops and schools running, and may set up new businesses. More facilities may be built to meet rising demand.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Negative effects:</strong> Wealthy newcomers and second-home owners push up house prices, pricing out local people. Older people retiring to rural areas raise the average age. Road traffic and noise pollution increase. Commuters create no daytime economy because they spend money in the city. The culture of an area can change dramatically.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>The UK has become a post-industrial economy, with quaternary industries locating in science and business parks near universities and transport links. Traditional industry like Castle Cement still has significant environmental impacts, though sustainability measures are being introduced. Rural areas face contrasting fortunes — some grow rapidly with counter-urbanisation while others decline as young people leave.</p>
<h3>Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Science and business parks</strong> house quaternary industries near universities and transport links, and are more environmentally sustainable than traditional industry.</li>
<li><strong>Castle Cement</strong> contributes £9 million to the local economy but causes pollution, congestion and visual impact — sustainability measures include recycling, hybrid lorries and nature reserve restoration.</li>
<li><strong>Rural change</strong> varies sharply: South Cambridgeshire is growing (causing housing pressure and congestion) while the Outer Hebrides are declining (losing young people and struggling economically).</li>
</ul>
