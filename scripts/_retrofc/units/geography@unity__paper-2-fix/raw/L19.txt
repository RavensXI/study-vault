--- DESCRIPTION ---
The environmental and social impacts of energy production, the fracking debate in the UK (Blackpool case study), and the politics of energy extraction.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---

<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Impacts of Energy Insecurity</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">When countries develop, they consume more energy. If demand outstrips supply, the result is <dfn class="term" data-def="Not having a reliable or affordable supply of energy to meet the needs of the population.">energy insecurity</dfn>. This can have serious consequences for people and the environment:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n3">
<li><strong>Blackouts</strong> — when energy supply cannot meet demand, power is cut off. People lose electricity for lighting, heating and cooking, reducing quality of life. In South Africa, electricity is regularly switched off from homes and diverted to industry during peak demand.</li>
<li><strong>Industrial output</strong> — industries may close or reduce production. People lose their jobs and have less income.</li>
<li><strong>Food production</strong> — farming relies on energy for machinery, irrigation and transport. Reduced energy leads to less food, higher prices and potential food shortages.</li>
<li><strong>Environmental damage</strong> — oil and gas pipelines are difficult to maintain and leaks can pollute soil, rivers and oceans, harming ecosystems and people’s health.</li>
<li><strong>Conflict</strong> — the need for energy security can cause conflict between nations. Many recent wars in the Middle East have been partly driven by competition over oil and gas supplies.</li>
</ul>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and list 3 negative impacts of energy insecurity mentioned that affect quality of life!">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>All of these impacts are connected. Blackouts reduce industrial output, which reduces employment and income. Less food production leads to higher prices and hunger. Competition for dwindling supplies can trigger conflict. The overall effect is a <strong>reduction in quality of life</strong>, especially in LICs that lack the resources to find alternatives.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">What Is Fracking?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6"><dfn class="term" data-def="The process of extracting natural gas from shale rock deep underground by injecting high-pressure water, sand and chemicals to create fractures.">Fracking</dfn> (hydraulic fracturing) is a method of extracting natural gas from shale rock deep underground. It was developed as technology advanced enough to reach energy sources that were previously unobtainable. Fracking could be possible in many parts of Great Britain, with the main concentrations in the North East and North West of England and eastern Wales.</p>
<h3 data-narration-id="n7">How Does Fracking Work?</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n8">The process involves several stages:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n9">
<li>A well is drilled deep into the ground, reaching the shale rock layer.</li>
<li>Water, sand and chemicals are injected into the well at very high pressure (<dfn class="term" data-def="Using liquid at high pressure to fracture rock.">hydraulic</dfn> force).</li>
<li>This creates <dfn class="term" data-def="Narrow fractures or cracks in rock, usually caused by pressure.">fissures</dfn> (cracks) in the shale rock, releasing the trapped gas.</li>
<li>The gas flows out through the well and is piped away for commercial use.</li>
</ul>
<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Fracking in the UK — The Blackpool Case Study</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Cuadrilla</strong> was the company responsible for fracking operations near Blackpool in Lancashire. This became one of the UK’s most controversial energy debates.</p>
<h3 data-narration-id="n12">Arguments For Fracking</h3>
<ul data-narration-id="n13">
<li>Fracking creates a more reliable energy supply for the UK, potentially reducing energy prices by up to 2% per year.</li>
<li>Cuadrilla created 120 jobs at its fracking sites near Blackpool.</li>
<li>Fracking sites would earn Blackpool Council £1.7 million per year in business rates.</li>
<li>Gas produces less CO₂ than coal when burned, so it is a cleaner fossil fuel.</li>
<li>It reduces the UK’s reliance on importing gas from other countries, improving energy security.</li>
</ul>
<h3 data-narration-id="n14">Arguments Against Fracking</h3>
<ul data-narration-id="n15">
<li>Drilling can cause gas and oil to mix with underground water supplies. Methane has been found in water near fracking sites, making it <dfn class="term" data-def="Made impure or unfit for use by the introduction of harmful substances.">contaminated</dfn> and unusable.</li>
<li>There have been three small earthquakes near Blackpool (magnitude 3 on the Richter Scale) linked to fracking activity.</li>
<li>Gas and oil are often transported by lorries, causing noise pollution and traffic congestion on the M55 and A583 — roads that are already busy, especially in summer.</li>
<li>Local residents and environmental groups strongly opposed the operations due to health and environmental concerns.</li>
</ul>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n16" data-revision-tip="Without looking, what magnitude quake near Blackpool led to the 2019 UK fracking moratorium?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Fracking in the UK was suspended in 2019 after a magnitude 2.9 earthquake near Blackpool. The government imposed a moratorium (temporary ban) due to the inability to predict the scale of earthquakes that fracking would cause. Despite potential energy security benefits, the environmental and community risks were judged too great.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n17">In HICs like the UK, energy insecurity leads to higher bills and reduced industrial competitiveness, but the government can invest in alternatives and imports. In LICs, the effects are far more severe: frequent blackouts shut down hospitals and schools, families burn wood for fuel (causing deforestation and indoor air pollution), and food shortages can lead to malnutrition. People in LICs who cannot afford generators simply go without power, while wealthier households in countries like South Africa can buy diesel generators to cope with outages.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>Energy insecurity has wide-ranging impacts including blackouts, reduced industrial output, food shortages, environmental damage and conflict. Fracking offered one solution to the UK’s energy supply needs, but the Blackpool case study shows that it comes with significant risks — earthquakes, water contamination and community opposition — leading to a government moratorium.</p>
<h3>Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Energy insecurity</strong> causes blackouts, job losses, food shortages and conflict — its effects are far worse in LICs than HICs.</li>
<li><strong>Fracking</strong> involves injecting high-pressure water into shale rock to release gas — it could improve UK energy security but causes earthquakes and water contamination.</li>
<li><strong>Blackpool case study</strong> — Cuadrilla created 120 jobs and £1.7 million in revenue, but three earthquakes and environmental concerns led to a moratorium on fracking in the UK.</li>
</ul>
