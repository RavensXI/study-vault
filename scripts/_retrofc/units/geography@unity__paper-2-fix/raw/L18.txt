--- DESCRIPTION ---
The global challenge of resource management, why energy demand is rising, and where areas of energy surplus and deficit exist around the world.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---

<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Why Is Energy Important?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Energy is essential for everyday life. It powers our homes, fuels our transport, runs factories and keeps hospitals, schools and businesses operating. Around half of all energy consumed globally is used by industry to manufacture products. The rest is split between transport, domestic (home) use and commercial buildings like offices and shops.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Close this box and define energy security, then explain why HICs often have it.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p><dfn class="term" data-def="Having a reliable and affordable supply of energy to meet the needs of a country's population.">Energy security</dfn> means having enough energy to meet the needs of people. <dfn class="term" data-def="Not having a reliable or affordable supply of energy, often affecting poorer countries.">Energy insecurity</dfn> means not having enough. Generally, wealthier countries (HICs) have better energy security because they can afford to buy or produce the energy they need.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Areas of Energy Surplus and Deficit</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Energy is not evenly distributed across the world. Some countries have large reserves of fossil fuels and produce more energy than they need — these have an energy <dfn class="term" data-def="When a country produces more energy than it consumes and can sell the excess to other countries.">surplus</dfn>. Others consume more energy than they can produce and must buy it from elsewhere — these have an energy <dfn class="term" data-def="When a country consumes more energy than it produces and must import energy from other countries.">deficit</dfn>.</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n6">
<li><strong>Low insecurity</strong> (energy secure) — northern North America, northern Asia (Russia), South-East Asia and Australia. These regions have large fossil fuel reserves or strong economies to purchase energy.</li>
<li><strong>High insecurity</strong> (energy insecure) — western South America, much of Africa and central Asia. These regions lack both energy resources and the money to import them.</li>
<li><strong>Medium insecurity</strong> — the UK and USA produce some energy domestically but do not have enough gas and oil to meet all their needs, so they import from countries like Russia (gas) and the Middle East (oil).</li>
</ul>
<h3 data-narration-id="n7">The Energy Mix</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n8">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The combination of different energy sources used by a country to generate its power, such as oil, gas, coal, nuclear and renewables.">energy mix</dfn> describes the proportion of energy generated from different sources. Globally, fossil fuels (oil, gas and coal) account for around 87% of all energy production. The remaining 13% comes from renewable and nuclear sources. Different countries have different energy mixes depending on what resources they have available.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Why Is Energy Demand Rising?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">Three main human factors are driving increased energy demand worldwide:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n11">
<li><strong>Population growth</strong> — the global population is growing rapidly. More people means more energy is needed for homes, transport and industry.</li>
<li><strong>Economic development</strong> — as countries get wealthier, they build more infrastructure, factories and power stations. People buy more electrical items like fridges, TVs and smartphones.</li>
<li><strong>Modern technology</strong> — the more technology that is invented, the more items need plugging in. Data centres, electric vehicles and smart devices all increase energy demand.</li>
</ul>
<h2 data-narration-id="n12">Factors Affecting Energy Supply</h2>
<h3 data-narration-id="n13">Physical Factors</h3>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Several physical factors make it harder to secure energy supplies:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n15">
<li><strong>Geology</strong> — is the energy source easy to reach? What is the cost of extracting it? Is there enough of the resource to justify the expense?</li>
<li><strong>Climate</strong> — extracting fossil fuels in extreme environments (such as the Arctic) is dangerous and expensive.</li>
<li><strong>Environment</strong> — drilling in natural habitats risks damaging ecosystems. Deep-sea drilling for gas and oil endangers ocean life.</li>
</ul>

<div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n16">When energy is easy to find, <dfn class="term" data-def="The amount of energy extracted and processed for use.">production</dfn> increases and costs fall. Cheap energy encourages higher demand. When energy becomes harder to find, production falls, prices rise and people use less. This cycle means energy bills go up and down over time. As companies make more profit, they can invest in <dfn class="term" data-def="Extracting energy from sources that were previously too difficult or expensive to access.">exploitation</dfn> — reaching energy supplies from previously unobtainable sources using new technology, such as under-sea robots for deep-sea drilling. However, exploitation carries environmental risks including oil spills and habitat destruction.</p>
</div>
</div></div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Cover this: why is the proportion of renewable energy in the global mix rising?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>As fossil fuels become scarcer and more expensive to extract, the pressure grows to find alternative energy sources. This is why the proportion of renewable energy in the global energy mix is gradually increasing.</p>
</div>


--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>Energy is unevenly distributed across the world, creating areas of surplus and deficit. Demand is rising due to population growth, economic development and the spread of modern technology, while physical factors like geology, climate and environmental concerns affect supply. The global energy mix is dominated by fossil fuels, but rising scarcity and costs are pushing the world towards alternatives.</p>
<h3>Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Energy security</strong> depends on a country’s wealth and access to resources — HICs are generally more energy secure than LICs.</li>
<li><strong>Rising demand</strong> is driven by population growth, economic development and technology — global fossil fuel supplies are becoming scarcer.</li>
<li><strong>Physical factors</strong> like geology, climate and environmental sensitivity affect where and how easily energy can be extracted.</li>
</ul>
