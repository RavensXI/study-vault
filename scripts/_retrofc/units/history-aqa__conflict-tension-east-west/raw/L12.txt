--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Why Cuba Mattered</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Cuba lay just ninety miles south of Florida. From 1898 it had been an American economic dependency: US firms owned most of its sugar industry, ran much of its electricity and telephone networks, and propped up dictators in Havana. In January 1959 the young revolutionary <dfn class="term" data-def="Cuban revolutionary leader, in power from January 1959; aligned Cuba with the USSR after US pressure and led the country until 2008.">Fidel Castro</dfn> overthrew the US-backed regime of Fulgencio Batista. Castro nationalised American property, executed political opponents and accepted Soviet trade and weapons. By the time President Kennedy took office in January 1961 the United States had a Soviet ally on its doorstep for the first time.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: when did Castro come to power, when was the Bay of Pigs, and why did the Bay of Pigs matter for what happened next?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Castro overthrew Batista in January 1959. In April 1961 a CIA-backed exile invasion at the <dfn class="term" data-def="The April 1961 CIA-backed invasion of Cuba by 1,400 anti-Castro exiles, defeated within three days; a major US humiliation.">Bay of Pigs</dfn> failed disastrously. The Bay of Pigs convinced Castro he needed Soviet protection and convinced Khrushchev that Kennedy might be too inexperienced to respond to bolder Soviet moves.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">From Bay of Pigs to Missiles</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">In April 1961 the CIA landed 1,400 anti-Castro Cuban exiles at the Bay of Pigs on Cuba&rsquo;s southern coast. Kennedy, fearing direct American involvement, withheld promised US air support. Within three days Castro&rsquo;s forces had crushed the invasion. The episode pushed Castro openly into the Soviet camp, and through 1962 Khrushchev decided to take a remarkable risk: he would secretly station nuclear missiles on Cuba. His motives were several &mdash; protecting Castro from a future US invasion, evening up a strategic balance in which the United States already had Jupiter missiles in Turkey within range of Soviet cities, and forcing Kennedy into negotiations from a stronger position.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">On 14 October 1962 a US U2 reconnaissance flight over Cuba returned with photographs of medium-range ballistic missile sites under construction. The missiles, once operational, could strike most major US cities within minutes. Kennedy was briefed on 16 October. He convened a small group of advisers known as <dfn class="term" data-def="The Executive Committee of the National Security Council, the small advisory group Kennedy convened to manage the Cuban Missile Crisis.">ExComm</dfn> and ordered the discovery kept secret while options were reviewed.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n7">The Thirteen Days</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n8">Inside ExComm, hawks led by the Joint Chiefs pressed for an air strike on the missile sites or a full invasion. Doves, including Kennedy&rsquo;s brother Robert and Defence Secretary Robert McNamara, urged caution. After six days of debate Kennedy chose a middle path. On 22 October he addressed the American people on television: he announced the discovery of the missiles, demanded their removal, and declared a naval &lsquo;quarantine&rsquo; of Cuba &mdash; in effect a blockade, but called a quarantine to avoid the legal status of an act of war.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n9">Through the week of 22-28 October Soviet ships steamed toward the quarantine line. Some turned back; others were boarded peacefully. On Saturday 27 October &mdash; later called &lsquo;Black Saturday&rsquo; &mdash; a Soviet surface-to-air missile shot down an American U2 over Cuba, killing pilot Major Rudolf Anderson. Soviet submarine commanders off the coast, out of contact with Moscow, considered launching a nuclear torpedo at US destroyers harassing them. Two of three commanders authorised a launch; one &mdash; Vasily Arkhipov &mdash; refused, requiring unanimity, and the order was not given. The world came as close to nuclear war that day as it ever has.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n10">Kennedy received two contradictory letters from Khrushchev that weekend. The first, on 26 October, offered to remove the missiles if the United States promised not to invade Cuba. The second, on 27 October, added a demand that the United States remove its Jupiter missiles from Turkey. Kennedy publicly answered the first letter and ignored the second &mdash; while privately authorising his brother Robert to assure the Soviet ambassador Anatoly Dobrynin that the Jupiter missiles would in fact be withdrawn within months, but that the deal must be kept secret.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n11">Resolution and Significance</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n12">On 28 October 1962 Khrushchev announced that the missiles would be dismantled and shipped home. The crisis was over. Publicly it looked like a clean Kennedy victory. The secret part of the deal &mdash; the Jupiter missiles being withdrawn from Turkey by April 1963 &mdash; would not be widely known for decades, allowing the orthodox &lsquo;Kennedy stared Khrushchev down&rsquo; story to take hold. A common student misconception is to repeat that orthodox view without acknowledging the secret deal: top-band answers handle both halves.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n13">The Cuban Missile Crisis produced the first concrete steps toward managed coexistence. In June 1963 a direct telephone <dfn class="term" data-def="A direct teletype communications link between Washington and Moscow established in June 1963 to prevent dangerous misunderstandings.">hotline</dfn> was opened between Washington and Moscow to prevent dangerous misunderstandings. In August 1963 the Partial Test Ban Treaty banned nuclear tests in the atmosphere, in space and underwater. Khrushchev was removed by his Politburo colleagues in October 1964, partly because the crisis had exposed Soviet missile weakness. Kennedy was assassinated in November 1963. The crisis became the unspoken reference point that pushed both successor governments toward d&eacute;tente by the end of the decade.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Close this and write down the missile crisis sequence: U2 photos (date), TV speech and quarantine (date), Black Saturday (date), Khrushchev backs down (date).">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The Cuban Missile Crisis lasted from 14 October to 28 October 1962. Its public resolution was a Soviet withdrawal in return for a US no-invasion pledge. The secret half &mdash; US withdrawal of Jupiter missiles from Turkey by April 1963 &mdash; was kept quiet for decades.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n16">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n17">Castro&rsquo;s 1959 revolution and the failed Bay of Pigs invasion of April 1961 set up the conditions for Khrushchev to deploy Soviet missiles to Cuba in 1962.</li><li data-narration-id="n18">Between 14 and 28 October 1962 Kennedy used a naval &lsquo;quarantine&rsquo; rather than air strike, and the world reached its closest moment to nuclear war on Black Saturday, 27 October.</li><li data-narration-id="n19">The crisis was resolved publicly through a Soviet withdrawal and a US no-invasion pledge, and privately through a secret deal to remove US Jupiter missiles from Turkey &mdash; a settlement that produced the Washington-Moscow hotline, the 1963 Partial Test Ban Treaty and the long road to d&eacute;tente.</li></ul>
