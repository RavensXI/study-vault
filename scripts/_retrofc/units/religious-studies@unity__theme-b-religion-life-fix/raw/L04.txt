When does life begin? The right to die? Religious and ethical views on both issues.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Abortion</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="The deliberate ending of a pregnancy by removing the foetus from the womb before it can survive independently, usually before 24 weeks.">Abortion</dfn> is the deliberate termination of a pregnancy. It remains one of the most debated moral issues in modern society. Before 1967, abortion was illegal in the UK, and women resorted to dangerous “back street” abortions that led to infections, injuries, and deaths.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Can you list the four conditions for a legal abortion?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Under the Abortion Act 1967, amended in 1990, abortion is legal in England, Scotland and Wales up to 24 weeks if two doctors agree that at least one condition applies: the mother’s life is at risk, there is risk to her physical or mental health, there is a significant chance the baby would be born with severe disabilities, or an additional child would affect the wellbeing of existing children.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Christian Views on Abortion</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Christians hold a range of views on abortion, but all agree that human life has special value because it is created by God.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">The <strong>Roman Catholic Church</strong> teaches that abortion is always wrong. Catholics believe life begins at conception and that destroying a foetus is equivalent to murder. This is rooted in the <dfn class="term" data-def="The belief that life is holy and given by God, and therefore only God has the right to take it away.">sanctity of life</dfn> — the idea that life is sacred because God created it. The Bible states: “You shall not murder” (Exodus 20:13). Catholics apply this directly to abortion.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">The Bible also suggests God has a plan for every life before birth: “Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, before you were born I set you apart” (Jeremiah 1:5). Psalm 139:13 adds: “For you created my inmost being; you knit me together in my mother’s womb.”</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8">However, <strong>other Christians</strong>, including many Anglicans and Methodists, believe abortion may be acceptable in certain circumstances. Jesus taught that the most important thing is love (agape), and it may be the most loving action to allow abortion if the mother’s life is at risk or the pregnancy resulted from rape. As Colossians 3:12 says: “Clothe yourselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness and patience.”</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Pro-life</strong> supporters believe the foetus has a right to life from conception and that abortion is morally wrong. They argue adoption is always an alternative. <strong>Pro-choice</strong> supporters believe a woman should have the right to decide what happens to her own body. They argue that a foetus is not yet a person, especially in early pregnancy, and that forcing a woman to continue an unwanted pregnancy can cause serious harm.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Islamic Views on Abortion</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Muslims believe human life is precious because it is given by Allah. The Qur’an teaches: “Do not kill your children for fear of poverty — We shall provide for them and for you — killing them is a great sin” (Qur’an 17:31). This verse is widely used to argue that abortion for financial reasons is <dfn class="term" data-def="An Arabic term meaning forbidden; actions that are prohibited in Islam.">haram</dfn> (forbidden).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12">A key concept in Islam is <dfn class="term" data-def="The point at which Muslims believe the foetus receives a soul from Allah, traditionally at 120 days after conception.">ensoulment</dfn> — the moment when the foetus receives its soul from Allah. Many scholars place this at 120 days after conception. Before ensoulment, some Muslims believe abortion may be permitted if the mother’s life is in danger. After ensoulment, abortion is generally forbidden because the foetus is considered a living being with a soul.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n13">The Qur’an also warns: “If anyone kills a person — unless in retribution for murder or spreading corruption in the land — it is as if he kills all mankind” (Qur’an 5:32) — a Qur’anic verse that records a ruling originally given to the Children of Israel, though Islamic scholars widely apply this life-sanctity principle universally. Most Muslims agree that the mother’s life takes priority over the life of the foetus.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Euthanasia</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15"><dfn class="term" data-def="The painless killing of a patient suffering from an incurable and painful disease or in an irreversible coma, often called mercy killing.">Euthanasia</dfn> comes from the Greek words eu (good) and thanatos (death), meaning “a good death.” All forms of euthanasia are currently illegal in the UK and treated as murder or manslaughter.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n16" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Can you define active, passive, voluntary and non-voluntary euthanasia?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p><strong>Active euthanasia</strong> is taking deliberate action to end a life, such as giving a lethal injection. <strong>Passive euthanasia</strong> is allowing someone to die by withdrawing or withholding treatment. <strong>Voluntary euthanasia</strong> is when the patient requests death. <strong>Non-voluntary euthanasia</strong> is when the patient cannot give consent, for example because they are in a coma.</p>
</div>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Case Study: Tony Bland</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n17">Tony Bland was 18 when he attended the Hillsborough football stadium in 1989. A crush killed 94 people on the day and left Tony in a <dfn class="term" data-def="A condition where a patient can breathe independently but has no awareness of themselves or their surroundings and no hope of recovery.">persistent vegetative state</dfn> (PVS) with irreversible brain damage. Although there was no hope of recovery, he was kept alive through tubes providing food and fluids. In 1993, the hospital and his parents obtained court permission to withdraw treatment. Tony became the first patient in UK legal history to be allowed to die through the withdrawal of life-prolonging treatment. The law did not classify this as passive euthanasia.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n18">In September 2015, the British Parliament rejected plans for a “right to die” law in England and Wales. 118 MPs voted in favour and 330 against allowing terminally ill adults to end their lives with medical supervision. In countries such as Belgium and the Netherlands, euthanasia is legal under strict conditions — the patient must request it, their suffering must be unbearable, and two doctors must be consulted.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n19">Christian Views on Euthanasia</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Most Christians oppose euthanasia because of the sanctity of life. The Ten Commandments state “You shall not murder” (Exodus 20:13), and Psalm 139:16 teaches that God has ordained every day of a person’s life. The Salvation Army has stated: “All people deserve compassion and care in suffering and dying. Euthanasia and assisted suicide should not, however, be considered acceptable responses. They undermine human dignity and are morally wrong.”</p>
<p data-narration-id="n21">However, some Christians accept the <strong>principle of double effect</strong> — where medicine given to relieve pain may shorten life as a side effect. They see this as different from intentional killing because the primary intention is to reduce suffering, not to cause death. Many Christians actively support <dfn class="term" data-def="A place where terminally ill people receive specialist care focused on comfort and dignity, rather than curing the illness.">hospice</dfn> care and <dfn class="term" data-def="Medical care focused on relieving pain and symptoms without treating the underlying cause of an illness.">palliative care</dfn> as compassionate alternatives to euthanasia. Jesus taught “Blessed are the merciful” (Matthew 5:7), and Christians apply this by caring for the dying with dignity.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Arguments For and Against Euthanasia</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n22"><strong>For:</strong> People deserve to die with dignity. If someone’s <dfn class="term" data-def="The general wellbeing of a person in relation to their health and happiness; the theory that the value of life depends on how good or satisfying it is.">quality of life</dfn> is very low, it may be more compassionate to let them die. Everyone should have the right to decide how they die. <strong>Against:</strong> Someone might recover. It is a “slippery slope” that could lead to abuse. No one should carry the burden of deciding another person’s death. It devalues the sanctity of life. As Dr Gunning warned: “Once you accept killing as a solution to a single problem, you will find tomorrow hundreds of problems for which killing can be seen as a solution.”</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n23">Islamic Views on Euthanasia</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n24">Islam is firmly opposed to all forms of euthanasia. Muslims believe in <strong>predestination</strong> — that Allah has planned every person’s life, and to end life prematurely is to interfere with His plan. The Qur’an states: “No soul may die except with God’s permission at a predestined time” (Qur’an 3:145), and “Do not kill each other, for God is merciful to you” (Qur’an 4:29).</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n25" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Define euthanasia in your own words.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Euthanasia is haram in Islam. Muslims believe all life is a gift from Allah and only Allah decides when it ends. Suffering may be a test of faith, and caring for the sick is an opportunity to do good that helps a person gain entry to paradise. Unlike some Christians, most Muslim scholars reject even the principle of double effect.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n27">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n28">Abortion is legal in the UK up to 24 weeks under specific conditions. Christians hold diverse views, from the Catholic total ban to more liberal positions. Islam permits abortion before ensoulment (120 days) in limited circumstances, especially if the mother’s life is at risk.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n29">All forms of euthanasia are illegal in the UK. Both Christianity and Islam oppose euthanasia based on the sanctity of life, though some Christians accept the principle of double effect. Islam considers euthanasia haram because only Allah determines when life ends.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n30">The tension between sanctity of life and quality of life is central to both debates — religious believers must balance respect for God-given life with compassion for those who are suffering.</li>
</ul>
