--- DESCRIPTION ---
How plants trap light energy to make glucose, and the resources producers need to grow.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Producers and the Source of All Food</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Walk into any greenhouse and you&rsquo;ll find growers carefully controlling light levels, temperature and the air composition around their crops. They&rsquo;re manipulating the same process that drives every food chain on Earth: <dfn class="term" data-def="The process by which plants, algae and some bacteria use light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen. It occurs in chloroplasts.">photosynthesis</dfn>. Organisms that carry out photosynthesis are called <dfn class="term" data-def="Organisms that make their own food from inorganic raw materials using light energy. Plants, algae and cyanobacteria are the main producers.">producers</dfn> &mdash; they form the foundation of almost every food chain because they are the only organisms capable of capturing energy from sunlight and locking it into chemical bonds.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Photosynthesis takes place in the <dfn class="term" data-def="An organelle found in plant cells that contains chlorophyll and is the site of photosynthesis.">chloroplast</dfn>, an organelle containing the green pigment <dfn class="term" data-def="The green pigment found in chloroplasts that absorbs red and blue light energy for photosynthesis, reflecting green light.">chlorophyll</dfn>. Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue wavelengths of light while reflecting green light &mdash; which is why leaves appear green. The reaction is endothermic: it takes in energy from the surroundings in the form of light.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this and write out both the word equation and the balanced symbol equation for photosynthesis. Check your products and reactants.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Photosynthesis equation: $$\text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water} \xrightarrow{\text{light}} \text{glucose} + \text{oxygen}$$<br>Symbol equation: \(6CO_2 + 6H_2O \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2\). It is an <strong>endothermic</strong> reaction &mdash; it takes in light energy.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">The Two Stages of Photosynthesis</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Photosynthesis is a two-stage process. In the first stage, light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll and used to split water molecules, releasing oxygen as a by-product. In the second stage, the hydrogen released from water is combined with carbon dioxide to produce glucose. Both stages occur within the chloroplast.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">The oxygen released in the first stage diffuses out of the leaf through tiny pores called <strong>stomata</strong> into the surrounding air. This oxygen is the source of almost all the oxygen in Earth&rsquo;s atmosphere. The glucose produced in the second stage can be used immediately by the plant in respiration, or stored, or converted into other molecules.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>What Does the Plant Do with Glucose?</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n8">Glucose made by photosynthesis has several important destinations within the plant. It may be broken down in <strong>respiration</strong> to transfer energy for life processes. Excess glucose is converted to insoluble <strong>starch</strong> for storage (starch does not affect osmosis because it is insoluble). Glucose is also converted to <strong>cellulose</strong> for building and strengthening cell walls.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9">When mineral ions such as nitrates are available from the soil, the plant can combine glucose-derived carbon skeletons with nitrogen to make <strong>amino acids</strong>, which are assembled into proteins. Glucose can also be converted into <strong>lipids</strong> (fats and oils) for energy storage in seeds. This explains why seeds are energy-rich foods: they store lipids made originally from photosynthetically produced glucose.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Limiting Factors and Rates of Photosynthesis</h2>
<div class="higher-only"><p data-narration-id="n11">Three factors determine how fast photosynthesis can proceed: <strong>light intensity</strong>, <strong>carbon dioxide concentration</strong> and <strong>temperature</strong>. Each can become a <dfn class="term" data-def="A factor that prevents the rate of photosynthesis from increasing further, even if other conditions are favourable. The factor in shortest supply sets the rate.">limiting factor</dfn> &mdash; the one in shortest supply that prevents the rate from rising. On a graph of rate against one factor, the rate rises then plateaus: the plateau shows that a different factor has become limiting.</p></div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>How Each Factor Limits the Rate</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Light intensity</strong> &mdash; more light means more energy available for the light-dependent stage. As light intensity increases, the rate rises. At high intensity, it levels off because CO\(_2\) or temperature has become the new limiting factor. If a plant is moved further from a light source, the light reaching it decreases.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Carbon dioxide concentration</strong> &mdash; CO\(_2\) is a raw material for the carbon-fixing stage of photosynthesis. Increasing its concentration provides more molecules for the reaction, so the rate increases. In sealed glasshouses, growers can pump in extra CO\(_2\) from gas burners to raise yields.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Temperature</strong> &mdash; the reactions of photosynthesis are controlled by enzymes. Higher temperatures increase the kinetic energy of molecules, speeding up enzyme-substrate collisions. Above the enzyme&rsquo;s optimum temperature (often around 25&ndash;30&deg;C for many plants), the enzyme begins to denature and the rate falls sharply.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="On a rate graph, identify where the curve flattens. Say: &lsquo;the rate is now limited by [factor X] because increasing [factor on axis] no longer raises the rate.&rsquo; Practice naming the new limiting factor from graph context.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The three limiting factors interact. A graph plateau means a <em>different</em> factor is now limiting. To raise the rate further, you must increase whichever factor is currently limiting &mdash; not the one already in excess.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Resources That Producers Need: Minerals and Water</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">Producers need more than just light and CO\(_2\). They also require <strong>water</strong>, absorbed from the soil through root hair cells by osmosis, and <strong>mineral ions</strong>, absorbed by active transport. The most important mineral ions for plant growth are <strong>nitrates</strong> (to make amino acids and proteins) and <strong>phosphates</strong> (for DNA and cell membranes). A shortage of any of these resources limits growth regardless of how much light or CO\(_2\) is available.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Water absorbed by the roots travels upward through <dfn class="term" data-def="Hollow, dead tubes in plants that transport water and dissolved mineral ions upwards from roots to leaves. Reinforced with lignin.">xylem</dfn> vessels to the leaves. There it is used in photosynthesis or evaporates from cells and diffuses out through stomata &mdash; a process called <dfn class="term" data-def="The loss of water vapour from a plant, mainly through open stomata in the leaves. It creates a continuous upward flow of water through the xylem.">transpiration</dfn>. Transpiration keeps a continuous flow of water moving through the plant, carrying dissolved minerals to where they are needed.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Commercial Greenhouses: Controlling All Three Factors</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n19">Commercial growers use glasshouses to manipulate limiting factors and maximise crop yields year-round. Artificial lighting extends the &lsquo;growing day&rsquo; and can be tailored to provide the red and blue wavelengths that chlorophyll absorbs most strongly. Gas burners add CO\(_2\) to the atmosphere inside the glasshouse. Heating systems maintain the optimum temperature for the crop being grown. Automatic irrigation delivers water directly to roots.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n20">Each improvement has a cost, so farmers must weigh the extra income from increased yields against the running costs. If CO\(_2\) is already at an optimal level, spending money to increase it further will not raise the rate &mdash; light or temperature is then the limiting factor. Understanding which factor is currently limiting is therefore essential for making cost-effective decisions.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n21" data-revision-tip="Close this and list the mineral ions a producer needs and the molecule each helps to make. Then explain why water is needed in photosynthesis itself &mdash; not just for transport.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Producers need water (split in photosynthesis to release hydrogen and oxygen), CO\(_2\), light, and mineral ions including nitrates (for amino acids and proteins) and phosphates (for DNA). Transpiration drives water and mineral delivery through the xylem.</p>
</div>


--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n23">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n24">Producers carry out photosynthesis in chloroplasts, converting CO\(_2\) and water into glucose and oxygen using light energy. The reaction is endothermic and the overall equation is \(6CO_2 + 6H_2O \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2\).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">The rate of photosynthesis is controlled by three limiting factors: light intensity, CO\(_2\) concentration and temperature. Each can become the bottleneck, shown as a plateau on a rate graph.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n26">Producers also need mineral ions (nitrates, phosphates) from the soil, absorbed by active transport, and water delivered through the xylem via the transpiration stream.</li>
</ul>
