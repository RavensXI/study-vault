--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Tawhid: The Oneness of Allah</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The single most fundamental belief in Islam is <dfn class="term" data-def="The Islamic doctrine of the absolute oneness, uniqueness and indivisibility of Allah &mdash; the foundational belief that no associate, partner or equal exists alongside God.">Tawhid</dfn> (Arabic: &lsquo;oneness&rsquo;). Allah is one, indivisible, eternal and without any partner, associate or equal. <strong>Surah 112</strong> (Al-Ikhlas &mdash; &lsquo;Sincerity&rsquo;) states: &ldquo;Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor was born, nor is there any equivalent to Him.&rdquo; Surah 112 is the most-recited summary of Tawhid and is used to correct any association of partners with Allah (<em>shirk</em>), which is considered the gravest sin in Islam.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">The characteristics of Allah described in the Qur&rsquo;an include: oneness (<em>Tawhid</em>), immanence (closeness to creation), transcendence (beyond creation), omnipotence (all-powerful), beneficence and mercy (<em>Ar-Rahman, Ar-Rahim</em>), fairness and justice. Surah 16:35&ndash;36 emphasises that Allah sent messengers to every community to call them to worship him alone.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n4">In Shi&rsquo;a Islam, divine justice has a special name: <dfn class="term" data-def="The Shi&rsquo;a Islamic principle of divine justice, holding that Allah always acts justly and that human beings have real moral freedom because Allah would not hold them responsible for actions they could not freely choose.">Adalat</dfn>. It is listed as one of the Five Roots and underscores that God&rsquo;s justice is perfect &mdash; he does not oppress, and human beings genuinely have free will because a just God must leave room for real moral choice.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: name all five characteristics of Allah taught in Surah 112, then explain why shirk is considered so serious.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Surah 112 (Al-Ikhlas) is the Qur&rsquo;anic anchor for Tawhid: Allah is &ldquo;the One, the Eternal Refuge&rdquo; who &ldquo;neither begets nor was born.&rdquo; <em>Shirk</em> (associating partners with Allah) is the greatest sin in Islam because it violates Tawhid directly.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">The Six Beliefs of Sunni Islam</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Sunni Islam organises its core beliefs into <dfn class="term" data-def="The six fundamental beliefs of Sunni Islam: belief in Allah, his angels, his holy books, his prophets, the Day of Judgement, and divine decree (al-Qadr).">six beliefs</dfn>, drawn from <em>Kitab al-iman</em> 1:4 (a hadith of the Prophet). They are:</p>
<ol>
  <li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Belief in Allah</strong> (Tawhid &mdash; the oneness of God)</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Belief in his Angels</strong> (<em>Malaikah</em>) &mdash; pure, obedient spiritual beings</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Belief in his Holy Books</strong> (<em>Kutub</em>) &mdash; revelations sent to the prophets</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Belief in his Prophets</strong> (<em>Risalah</em>) &mdash; messengers from Adam to Muhammad</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Belief in the Day of Judgement</strong> (<em>Akhirah</em>) &mdash; the final reckoning and afterlife</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Belief in divine decree</strong> (<em>al-Qadr</em>) &mdash; Allah&rsquo;s knowledge of and sovereignty over all events</li>
</ol>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n14">Shi&rsquo;a Islam uses a different organising structure: the Five Roots of <dfn class="term" data-def="The five foundational principles of Shi&rsquo;a Islam: Tawhid (oneness), Adl (justice), Nubuwwah (prophethood), Imamah (leadership), and Mi&rsquo;ad (resurrection).">Usul ad-Din</dfn>:</p>
    <ul>
      <li data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Tawhid</strong> &mdash; the oneness of Allah (Surah 112)</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n16"><strong>&lsquo;Adl</strong> (divine justice/Adalat) &mdash; Allah always acts justly; human beings have real moral freedom</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Nubuwwah</strong> &mdash; prophethood; Allah communicated through a line of prophets ending with Muhammad</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n18"><strong>Imamah</strong> &mdash; the leadership of the Imams, direct successors to Muhammad from the Prophet&rsquo;s family (<em>Ahl al-Bayt</em>); this is distinctive to Shi&rsquo;a Islam</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n19"><strong>Mi&rsquo;ad</strong> &mdash; the Day of Resurrection and Judgement</li>
    </ul>
    <p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Critical distinction:</strong> The Five Roots are Shi&rsquo;a. The Six Beliefs are Sunni. Do not conflate them in exam answers.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n21">Risalah: Prophethood</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22"><dfn class="term" data-def="The Islamic belief in prophethood &mdash; that Allah communicated his will to humanity through a succession of prophets, beginning with Adam and ending with Muhammad as the &lsquo;seal of the Prophets.&rsquo;">Risalah</dfn> is the belief in prophethood: that Allah chose specific human beings to receive and convey his guidance to the world. Surah 2:136 names the key prophets believed in by Muslims: &ldquo;We believe in Allah, and in what has been revealed to us and what was revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob and the Tribes, and in what was given to Moses, Jesus and the Prophets from their Lord.&rdquo;</p>
<p data-narration-id="n23">The named prophets in this spec are: <strong>Adam</strong> (the first human being and first prophet), <strong>Ibrahim</strong> (Abraham &mdash; friend of God, father of monotheism), <strong>Musa</strong> (Moses &mdash; who received the Tawrat/Torah), <strong>Isa</strong> (Jesus &mdash; regarded as a great prophet and the Messiah, born of the virgin Maryam, but not the Son of God) and <strong>Muhammad</strong> (the final and greatest prophet, &lsquo;seal of the Prophets&rsquo;).</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n24">The Holy Books (Kutub)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n25">Muslims believe Allah revealed holy books to his prophets. The <dfn class="term" data-def="The Muslim collective term for the holy books revealed by Allah, including the Qur&rsquo;an, the Tawrat, the Zabur and the Injil.">Kutub</dfn> (holy books) include: the <strong>Qur&rsquo;an</strong> (revealed to Muhammad, in Arabic, the final and uncorrupted revelation), the <strong>Tawrat</strong> (Torah, revealed to Musa/Moses), the <strong>Zabur</strong> (Psalms, revealed to Dawud/David), and the <strong>Injil</strong> (Gospel, revealed to Isa/Jesus). Muslims believe the earlier books became corrupted over time &mdash; the Qur&rsquo;an alone remains in its original form and therefore has supreme authority.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n26a">Both Islam and Christianity affirm that God is one, all-powerful, all-knowing, and merciful. However, there are significant differences:</p>
    <ul>
      <li data-narration-id="n26b"><strong>Tawhid vs Trinity:</strong> Islam emphatically rejects the Christian Trinity as <em>shirk</em> (associating partners with God). Surah 112 directly refutes any idea that God has offspring or equals. Christians argue that the Trinity is a refined understanding of monotheism &mdash; one God in three Persons &mdash; not polytheism, but Muslims regard this distinction as insufficient.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n26c"><strong>Prophethood of Isa:</strong> Islam affirms Jesus (Isa) as a great prophet, born of the Virgin Maryam, the Messiah, and a worker of miracles &mdash; but emphatically not the Son of God or divine. The Christian claim that Jesus is God incarnate is rejected in Islamic theology as a violation of Tawhid.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n26d"><strong>Personal relationship:</strong> Both traditions affirm that God/Allah is close to his creation. The Qur&rsquo;an teaches that Allah is &ldquo;closer to him [the human] than his jugular vein&rdquo; (Surah 50:16). Christians express this through the Incarnation &mdash; God entering human experience directly in Jesus.</li>
    </ul>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n26" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: explain in two sentences why Muslims regard the Qur'an as superior to the other holy books.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Muslims believe earlier books (Tawrat, Zabur, Injil) were gradually corrupted by human error or alteration. The Qur&rsquo;an, revealed to Muhammad in Arabic and preserved in its original form, is the final, uncorrupted Word of Allah. This is why the Qur&rsquo;an has supreme authority in Islam.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n28">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n29">Tawhid (the absolute oneness of Allah) is the foundation of all Islamic belief; Surah 112 is its Qur&rsquo;anic anchor; <em>shirk</em> (associating partners) is the greatest sin.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n30">Sunni Islam has Six Beliefs; Shi&rsquo;a Islam has the Five Roots of Usul ad-Din &mdash; the unique Shi&rsquo;a addition is Imamah (the leadership of the Imams). Do not conflate them.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n31">Risalah (prophethood) is expressed in Surah 2:136; key prophets include Adam, Ibrahim, Musa, Isa and Muhammad. Muslims accept all four holy books but regard the Qur&rsquo;an as the final, uncorrupted revelation.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n27">A very common error: writing that the Six Beliefs and Five Roots are the same thing. They are not &mdash; the Six Beliefs are Sunni; the Five Roots are Shi&rsquo;a. The key difference is <em>Imamah</em> (Shi&rsquo;a) and <em>al-Qadr</em> (Sunni). The 5-mark Explain question on Tawhid or Risalah will require a named source: use Surah 112 for Tawhid, or Surah 2:136 for Risalah/prophethood &mdash; both are in your spec and are safe citations. Do NOT invent Qur&rsquo;an references. For the 12-mark Discuss: &ldquo;Tawhid is the most important belief in Islam.&rdquo; Develop for (Tawhid is the first and most fundamental belief; shirk is the greatest sin; it organises all other Islamic doctrine) and against (Risalah is equally essential &mdash; without prophets Muslims would have no Qur&rsquo;an or guidance; the Day of Judgement may be more personally motivating). Correctly spell technical terms: &lsquo;Tawhid&rsquo;, &lsquo;Risalah&rsquo;, &lsquo;shirk&rsquo;, &lsquo;Kutub&rsquo; &mdash; SPaG marks reward precision.</p>
