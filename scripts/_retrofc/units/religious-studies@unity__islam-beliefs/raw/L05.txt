<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Why Do Muslims Believe in Life After Death?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">For Muslims, death is not the end. It marks the beginning of a new stage of existence called <dfn class="term" data-def="The Muslim belief in life after death, beginning at the Day of Judgement.">Akhirah</dfn>. This belief shapes how Muslims live every day, because they understand that their actions on Earth will be judged by Allah.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Muslims believe in Akhirah for several important reasons. The Qur’an teaches it directly, and since Muslims believe the Qur’an is the word of Allah, its teachings must be true. The Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) also taught about life after death. Akhirah is one of the Six Articles of Faith in Sunni Islam and connects to Al-Ma’ad (resurrection), one of the Five Roots of Usul ad-Din in Shi’a Islam. Muslims also believe that life is a test from Allah, so there must be a time of reward and punishment to give that test meaning.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Can you recall all 5 items listed here?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Belief in Akhirah is one of the Six Articles of Faith for Sunni Muslims and connects to Al-Ma’ad (resurrection) in the Five Roots of Usul ad-Din for Shi’a Muslims. Both branches agree that all people will face judgement after death.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Barzakh — The Waiting Stage</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">When a Muslim dies, they enter a stage called <dfn class="term" data-def="A barrier or waiting stage between death and the Day of Judgement, where the soul remains conscious in the grave.">Barzakh</dfn>. This Arabic word means “barrier” — once a person crosses into Barzakh, they cannot return to earthly life. The soul remains conscious in the grave during this period.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Muslims believe that Allah sends two angels to question the dead person about their faith. Those who answer correctly will experience comfort and peace while they wait. Those who deny Allah will face punishment in the grave. The person remains in Barzakh until the Day of Judgement arrives.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n8">Throughout a Muslim’s life, two angels sit on their shoulders recording every action. Raqib sits on the right shoulder and records good deeds, while Atid sits on the left and records bad deeds. These records form the “book of deeds” that will be presented on the Day of Judgement. As the Qur’an states: “The record of their deeds will be laid open and you will see the guilty, dismayed at what they contain” (Qur’an 18:49).</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n9">The Day of Judgement</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">Muslims believe that a day will come when Allah’s purpose for the universe has been fulfilled. The angel <dfn class="term" data-def="The angel who will blow the trumpet to announce the Day of Judgement and the destruction of the present world.">Israfil</dfn> will blow a trumpet to announce the end of the world. The present world will be destroyed and then transformed into a new world. A second trumpet blast will signal the resurrection — every person who has ever lived will be raised from the dead with a new body to stand before Allah.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Allah will then judge each person based on their book of deeds. The Qur’an teaches that if a person receives their book in their right hand, they will enter paradise. If they receive it in their left hand, they will be sent to hell. This judgement is considered fair because Allah gave humans free will, making them responsible and accountable for their choices.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n12" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down what you recall about shi’a believe in Yawm al-Qiyyamah (the Day.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The Shi’a believe in Yawm al-Qiyyamah (the Day of Judgement) as part of Al-Ma’ad. They teach that every Muslim and non-Muslim will be physically resurrected, judged by Allah, and asked to account for every word and action of their lives on Earth.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Jannah — Paradise</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14"><dfn class="term" data-def="Paradise in Islam, described as an eternal garden of peace and reward for the faithful and righteous.">Jannah</dfn> is described in the Qur’an as a beautiful, eternal garden — the final home for the faithful and righteous. The Qur’an promises: “And those who believe and do good deeds, they are dwellers of Paradise, they dwell therein forever” (Qur’an 2:82).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15">According to Islamic teaching, Jannah has seven levels. Those with the greatest reward will reach the highest levels. People will be asked to recite from the Qur’an, and the more they can recite, the higher they go. In Jannah, families are reunited, no one grows old or becomes ill, and every wish is fulfilled. There are rivers, gardens, beautiful weather, and abundant food.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n16">Muslims believe they will arrive at Jannah over the As-Sirat Bridge. The Qur’an describes Allah saying: “Enter among my servants! Enter My Paradise” (Qur’an 89:29–30). There are believed to be eight gates, each representing a good action such as giving to charity or completing Hajj. A person will be called to the gate that matches their greatest good deed. Two angels welcome people, saying “Peace be upon you — enter here and live within.”</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Jahannam — Hell</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18"><dfn class="term" data-def="Hell in Islam, a place of fire and torment for those who reject Allah and do evil.">Jahannam</dfn> is described as a place of fire and terrible torment. The Qur’an warns: “They will dwell amid scorching wind and scalding water in the shadow of black smoke, neither cool nor refreshing” (Qur’an 56:42–44). It is the punishment for those who reject Allah and commit evil.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n19">There are seven levels of Jahannam, with different punishments for different sins. The most serious sin is <dfn class="term" data-def="The sin of associating partners with Allah or worshipping anything other than Allah — the only sin Allah will not forgive.">shirk</dfn> (worshipping anything other than Allah), which is the only sin Allah will not forgive. However, for sinful believers, Jahannam can serve as a place of purification. When Allah chooses, He may move these believers on to Jannah.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Reasons for Punishment in Jahannam</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n20">The Qur’an and Hadith give several reasons why a person may be sent to Jahannam. These include living a wicked life full of evil, rejecting the teachings of the Qur’an, refusing to take responsibility for one’s sins by blaming others, and committing shirk. Some Muslims believe that polytheists will go to hell permanently, but that some non-Muslim monotheists may still be able to enter Jannah.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n21">How Belief in Akhirah Influences Muslim Life</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22">Belief in the afterlife has a powerful effect on how Muslims live. Because life on Earth is seen as a test from Allah, Muslims strive to do well in order to be rewarded in Akhirah. This belief encourages them to think carefully about their actions, behaviour, and choices every single day.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n23" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Recall the specific examples given for belief in Akhirah encourages human.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Belief in Akhirah encourages human responsibility and accountability. It urges Muslims to avoid sin, seek forgiveness when they do wrong, perform sacred duties such as reading the Qur’an and following Shari’ah law, and treat every action as an act of worship (ibadah) to Allah.</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n24">Muslims are aware that angels are constantly watching and recording their deeds. This makes them more God-conscious (taqwa) in their daily lives. Belief in Akhirah also gives Muslims a sense of justice — the good will be rewarded and the wicked will be punished — and provides purpose and meaning to their lives on Earth.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n26">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n27">Akhirah is the Muslim belief in life after death, which includes Barzakh (the waiting stage), the Day of Judgement, and the eternal destinations of Jannah (paradise) and Jahannam (hell).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n28">The angel Israfil will blow a trumpet to announce the Day of Judgement, when all people will be resurrected and judged by Allah based on their recorded deeds.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n29">Belief in Akhirah shapes Muslim daily life by encouraging responsibility, accountability, and the avoidance of sin, as every action is seen as preparation for judgement.</li>
</ul>
