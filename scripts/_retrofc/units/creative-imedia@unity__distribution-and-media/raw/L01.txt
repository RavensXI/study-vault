How media products reach audiences through online, physical, and broadcast distribution platforms.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Are Distribution Platforms?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="The methods and channels used to deliver media products to a target audience.">Distribution</dfn> refers to the methods used to deliver media products to their target audience. Once a media product has been created in the production and post-production stages, it needs to reach the people it was designed for. The platform chosen for distribution affects the file format, quality, and accessibility of the final product.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">The OCR specification identifies three main categories of distribution: <strong>online platforms</strong>, <strong>physical platforms</strong>, and <strong>physical media</strong>. Each has distinct characteristics, advantages, and disadvantages that media professionals must consider when deciding how to deliver their work.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Recall the hardware components and their roles.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The three categories of distribution are: <strong>online</strong> (apps, multimedia, web), <strong>physical platforms</strong> (computers, interactive TV, kiosks, mobile devices), and <strong>physical media</strong> (CD/DVD, memory sticks, paper-based).</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Online Distribution</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6"><dfn class="term" data-def="Distribution channels that use the internet to deliver media products, including apps, websites, and streaming services.">Online distribution</dfn> delivers media products through the internet. This is the most common method of distribution in the modern media industry. Online distribution includes:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Apps</strong> — mobile applications downloaded from app stores (Google Play, Apple App Store). These can deliver games, music, video, and interactive content directly to smartphones and tablets. Apps like Spotify, Netflix, and TikTok are examples of media products distributed this way.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Websites</strong> — content accessed through web browsers. Websites can host video, audio, images, animations, and text. Examples include YouTube, BBC iPlayer, and online news sites. Websites are accessible on any device with a browser and internet connection.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Multimedia</strong> — platforms that combine multiple media types (text, images, audio, video, animation) and deliver them online. Streaming services, social media platforms, and e-learning sites are all multimedia distribution channels.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Advantages:</strong> Widespread reach — anyone with internet access can view the content. Cheap to distribute compared to producing physical copies. Content can be updated instantly without reprinting or re-manufacturing. Products are available immediately without waiting for delivery.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> Requires internet access, which not everyone has reliably. Security risks such as piracy, hacking, and unauthorised copying. Competition is fierce because the barrier to entry is low — thousands of similar products are available online. Some users may experience buffering or slow loading if their connection is poor.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n12">Physical Platforms</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13"><dfn class="term" data-def="Specific hardware devices used to play, display, or show media products to audiences.">Physical platforms</dfn> are the actual devices used to play or show media products. They are not the media itself, but the hardware that displays it. The specification identifies four types:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Computers</strong> — desktops and laptops can display websites, play video, run software applications, and show digital graphics. They support a wide range of file formats and offer high-resolution displays.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Interactive TV</strong> — digital television services that allow viewers to interact with content, such as pressing the red button for extra features, voting on shows, or accessing catch-up services. Freeview, Sky, and Virgin Media all offer interactive features.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Kiosks</strong> — standalone touchscreen terminals found in public spaces such as shopping centres, restaurants, airports, and museums. They deliver information, allow ordering, or provide entertainment. McDonald’s ordering kiosks and museum information points are common examples.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Mobile devices</strong> — smartphones and tablets that can display media products on the move. They support apps, websites, video, audio, and games. Mobile devices are the most widely used platform for consuming media — over 60% of global internet traffic now comes from mobile devices.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n18" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Recall the exam structure, timings, and mark allocations.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Physical platforms are the <strong>devices</strong> (computers, interactive TV, kiosks, mobile devices) that display media. Physical media are the <strong>storage formats</strong> (CD, DVD, memory stick, paper) that carry the content. Do not confuse the two in the exam.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n19">Physical Media</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n20"><dfn class="term" data-def="Tangible storage formats used to store and distribute media products, such as CDs, DVDs, memory sticks, and paper-based materials.">Physical media</dfn> are tangible items that store media products and can be given to the audience. Unlike online distribution, physical media must be manufactured and physically delivered.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n21"><strong>CD/DVD</strong> — optical discs that store audio, video, software, and data. CDs hold up to 700 MB, while DVDs can store 4.7 GB (single layer) or 8.5 GB (dual layer). Blu-ray discs offer even higher capacity at 25 GB or 50 GB. Although declining in popularity, DVDs and Blu-rays are still used for film distribution and software installation.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Memory sticks</strong> (USB flash drives) — portable storage devices that can hold files of various types. Useful for transferring files between devices or distributing software, presentations, and digital portfolios. Capacities typically range from 8 GB to 256 GB.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Paper-based</strong> — printed materials such as newspapers, magazines, leaflets, brochures, posters, and flyers. Despite the growth of digital media, paper-based distribution remains important for local advertising, event promotion, and some publishing.</p>

<span>Advantages and Disadvantages of Physical Media</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Advantages:</strong> The audience has a physical copy to refer back to at any time without needing internet access. Can deliver a clear, focused message (e.g. a printed leaflet at an event). Content is private — only the person with the disc or printout has access. No risk of buffering or connectivity issues.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n25"><strong>Disadvantages:</strong> Can be lost, damaged, or scratched. Higher production and distribution costs (manufacturing, printing, postage). Not as widespread — you can only reach people who physically receive the item. Cannot be updated once produced — mistakes or changes require a new print run. Environmental impact of manufacturing and disposal.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n26">How Platform Choice Affects File Formats</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n27">The distribution platform directly affects which file formats a media professional should use for the final product. A video intended for streaming on a website needs to be compressed into a format like MP4 that loads quickly, even on slower connections. A video for a Blu-ray disc can use higher quality, less compressed formats because the disc has more storage capacity and the player can handle larger files.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n28">Images for a website should be saved as JPEG or PNG at 72 DPI to keep file sizes small and loading times fast. Images for a printed leaflet need to be at least 300 DPI in a format like TIFF or high-quality PDF to ensure sharp printing. Audio for streaming uses compressed formats like MP3 or AAC, while audio for a CD uses uncompressed WAV at 44,100 Hz sample rate.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n29">A music festival wants to promote its event. For <strong>online distribution</strong>, the organisers create a website with embedded video trailers (MP4 format, compressed), social media posts with JPEG images, and a Spotify playlist. For <strong>physical platforms</strong>, they set up interactive kiosks at the venue showing maps and schedules. For <strong>physical media</strong>, they print A5 leaflets and posters for local shops and hand out USB drives with press packs at media events. Each platform requires different file formats and quality levels to work effectively.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The exam may give you a scenario and ask you to explain which distribution platform is most suitable. Always consider the <strong>target audience</strong>, the <strong>type of product</strong>, and the <strong>advantages/disadvantages</strong> of each platform when answering.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n32">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n33">Distribution has three categories: online (apps, web, multimedia), physical platforms (computers, interactive TV, kiosks, mobile devices), and physical media (CD/DVD, memory sticks, paper-based).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n34">Online distribution is cheap and widespread but requires internet access and carries security risks; physical media gives a tangible copy but is costly and can be damaged or lost.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n35">The distribution platform directly affects file format choices — web content needs small, compressed files while print needs high-resolution, uncompressed formats.</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n31">In the R093 exam, questions on distribution platforms often appear in Section B with a scenario. You might be asked to explain an advantage of using an online platform <strong>for the audience</strong> or <strong>for the distributor</strong> — read the question carefully to know whose perspective to write from. For 2-mark “Explain” questions, always give a reason <strong>and</strong> a developed consequence (e.g. “They won’t need to wait for delivery (1), meaning they can access it immediately (1)”).</p>
