--- DESCRIPTION ---
Why new business ideas emerge through technology, changing tastes, and obsolescence.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Why Do New Business Ideas Come About?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Business is <dfn class="term" data-def="The idea that business is ever-changing because external factors like technology, consumer tastes, and the economy keep shifting.">dynamic</dfn> — it never stands still. The products and services people want today are different from what they wanted five or ten years ago. The Edexcel specification identifies three main reasons why new business ideas emerge: changes in technology, changes in what consumers want, and products or services becoming obsolete.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and name the 3 reasons new business ideas emerge: tech, consumer wants, and what else?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Three reasons why new business ideas come about: <strong>changes in technology</strong>, <strong>changes in what consumers want</strong>, and <strong>products and services becoming obsolete</strong>.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Changes in Technology</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">New technology creates opportunities for entrepreneurs by making entirely new products and services possible. Smartphone app stores created the mobile-gaming market. Streaming platforms like Spotify disrupted CD and MP3 sales. GPS-enabled smartphones allowed businesses like Deliveroo to scale quickly, and contactless payments changed how customers pay in shops — roughly one in three UK adults now use their phone to pay at least once a month.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Technology also reduces costs. <dfn class="term" data-def="Using technology to complete tasks with little human input, increasing speed and reducing errors.">Automation</dfn> cuts unit costs through speed and accuracy, meaning start-ups can charge lower prices or keep higher profit margins to help them grow. Amazon’s warehouses use robots, scanners, and software to control the schedule of packages — a level of efficiency that would be impossible without advanced technology.</p>

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>E-commerce</strong> — buying and selling online via the internet. <strong>M-commerce</strong> — sales via smartphones and tablets. <strong>Automation</strong> — using technology to complete tasks with little human input. <strong>Obsolescence</strong> — when a product or service becomes out of date.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n8">However, relying on new technology carries risk. If technology fails, production can halt, leading to lost sales and damage to the business’s reputation. A business must weigh the potential benefits of adopting new technology against the costs and risks involved.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Changes in What Consumers Want</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">Consumer tastes change over time, and businesses must keep up or risk losing sales. A <dfn class="term" data-def="A person who purchases goods or services for personal use.">consumer</dfn> is the end user of a product or service. Their preferences shift because of factors like health trends, environmental concerns, social media influence, and changes in income.</p>
<figure class="diagram"><img src="https://pub-aeb94e100e5a48f4a133be5bf206aecb.r2.dev/business/theme-1/diagram_generated_7250e786.png" alt="Generated diagram for The Dynamic Nature of Business"><figcaption>Generated diagram for The Dynamic Nature of Business</figcaption></figure>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Several recent trends show how consumer tastes are reshaping markets. Plant-based meals from brands like Quorn and Beyond Meat are replacing traditional meat products, driven by health, animal welfare, and climate concerns. The UK plant-based sector is experiencing double-digit annual growth. Sustainable packaging is another shift — 87% of shoppers say they will pay more for greener packaging, moving away from single-use plastic. Apps like Vinted and Depop have transformed second-hand fashion: over half of 18 to 24-year-olds have sold clothes online, and Vinted now has more users than ASOS Marketplace.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n12" data-revision-tip="Without looking, what % of UK homes subscribed to streaming in 2015 vs. the 2024 figure (68%)?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Streaming services show the scale of change: in 2015 just 1 in 5 UK homes had a service like Netflix, but by 2024 around 2 out of 3 homes (68%) subscribed to at least one streaming platform.</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n13">If a business ignores shifts in consumer taste, sales can fall as customers move to fresher alternatives. The business loses revenue and market share. Walkers, for example, responded to changing tastes by launching limited-edition sandwich-flavoured crisps, combining two lunchtime favourites to attract new customers.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Products and Services Becoming Obsolete</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15"><dfn class="term" data-def="Out of date and no longer wanted because something better has replaced it.">Obsolete</dfn> means a product or service is no longer used because it has been replaced by something better. VHS cassettes are obsolete because consumers now stream films. Paper maps have been replaced by GPS systems and smartphone apps. Landline phones have largely disappeared as mobile phones offer greater convenience.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Examples of Obsolete Products and Their Replacements</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Typewriters</strong> replaced by computers. <strong>Film cameras</strong> replaced by digital cameras and smartphones. <strong>Floppy disks</strong> replaced by cloud storage and USB drives. <strong>Cassette tapes</strong> replaced by MP3 downloads and streaming. <strong>DVD rental shops</strong> (like Blockbuster) replaced by Netflix and other streaming services. Some <strong>services</strong> have also become obsolete — having film developed, renting videos from a shop, and chimney sweeping are all far less common today.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n17">When a product becomes obsolete, demand falls and revenue drops. Customers switch to rivals with newer products, meaning the firm sells fewer units and finds it harder to cover its fixed costs. To survive, a business must either adapt its existing products or develop entirely new ones.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n18">How New Business Ideas Come About</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19">The specification identifies two ways new ideas emerge: <strong>original ideas</strong> and <strong>adapting existing products, services, or ideas</strong>.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n20">An <dfn class="term" data-def="A business idea that has not been used before to put a product or service into production.">original idea</dfn> is one that has not been used before. Art Fry invented the Post-it Note by accident when he needed something to keep the page open in his hymn book. Rob Law was just 21 when he combined a toy with luggage to create the Trunki suitcase — his company Magmatic now employs 80 people and has sold 2.8 million products in 100 countries. Tails.com spotted that 4 out of 5 UK dog owners treat their pet as part of the family, creating tailored dog food delivered to the door — they now have over 40,000 UK customers.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n21" data-revision-tip="Close this and define how original ideas link to unmet needs or what problem related phrase?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Original ideas often come from <strong>spotting a gap in the market</strong> (an unmet customer need) or <strong>solving a problem</strong> that no existing product addresses.</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n22"><dfn class="term" data-def="The process of modifying an existing product or service to appeal to different customers or markets.">Adapting existing ideas</dfn> means modifying a product to appeal to different customers or markets. This is less risky than launching something completely new because the original product was already successful. Examples include combining two products (a spork combines a spoon and a fork), creating newer versions of classic cars like the Ford Mustang, or McDonald’s changing its menu for different countries — offering a McBaguette with Dijon mustard in France. Aldi produces cheaper versions of designer clothing, making fashion accessible to more consumers.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Why Adapting Existing Ideas Is Less Risky</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n23">The existing product was already successful for a period of time, so the business knows there is demand. Changes can often be made without major costs. The business invests less capital, so if the adapted product succeeds, more profit is made per product sold. It also allows the business to offer more choices to customers without starting from scratch.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n25">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n26">New business ideas come about because of changes in technology, changes in consumer tastes, and products or services becoming obsolete — business is always dynamic.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n27">New ideas emerge either as original concepts (spotting a gap in the market) or by adapting existing products to suit new customers or trends.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n28">Businesses that fail to respond to change risk losing sales, market share, and ultimately failing — adapting existing ideas is less risky than creating something entirely new.</li></ul>
