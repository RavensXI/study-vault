<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Purpose of Business Activity</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Every business exists for three core reasons: to produce goods or services, to meet customer needs, and to add value. Understanding these purposes is essential for your Edexcel exam, because they explain why businesses are set up and how they survive in competitive markets.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and name the 3 purposes of business activity from the Edexcel spec.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The three purposes of business activity are: <strong>to produce goods or services</strong>, <strong>to meet customer needs</strong>, and <strong>to add value</strong>. These come directly from the Edexcel specification for 1.1.3.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Producing Goods and Services</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">To produce goods and services, a business needs four <dfn class="term" data-def="The resources needed to produce goods and services: land, labour, capital, and enterprise.">factors of production</dfn>. These are <strong>land</strong> (a place to produce, such as a farm or factory), <strong>labour</strong> (people to work in the business), <strong>capital</strong> (money and equipment to get started), and <strong>enterprise</strong> (the drive and motivation from the owner to start and run the business).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">A good is a physical product you can touch, such as a pair of trainers or a sausage roll. A service is something a business does for you, such as a haircut or a train journey. Most businesses produce one or both of these to earn revenue.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n7">Meeting Customer Needs</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n8">Successful businesses focus on what their customers actually want. Common customer expectations include fast and efficient service, high-quality products at a competitive price, friendly and helpful staff, trained employees who can answer questions, and a clean shop or easy-to-navigate website.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9">If a business fails to meet these needs, customers will go to a competitor instead. For example, McDonald’s meets the need for speed and convenience through its drive-thru and mobile ordering app, while Waitrose meets the need for quality by selling premium and locally sourced food. Businesses that consistently meet customer expectations build a loyal customer base, which leads to repeat purchases and positive word-of-mouth recommendations.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n10">What Is Added Value?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><dfn class="term" data-def="The difference between the selling price of a product and the cost of the inputs used to make it.">Added value</dfn> is the difference between what a business pays its suppliers for materials and the price it charges customers for the finished product. For example, Starbucks pays around £0.60 for the coffee beans, milk, and cup for a medium latte, but sells it for £3.50 — giving an added value of £2.90. Similarly, a Greggs sausage roll costs just £0.25 in ingredients but sells for £1.20, creating £0.95 of added value through freshly baked convenience and high-street locations. Added value is important because it allows a business to cover its other costs and make a profit.</p>
<figure class="diagram"><img src="https://pub-aeb94e100e5a48f4a133be5bf206aecb.r2.dev/business/theme-1/diagram_the_role_of_business_enterprise.jpg" alt="Diagram: The Role of Business Enterprise" loading="lazy"></figure>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n12" data-revision-tip="Without looking, what's the added value formula? What's Nike's added value on Air Force 1s?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Added value = Selling price − Cost of inputs. Nike pays roughly £20 for materials to make Air Force 1 trainers but sells them for £110, creating £90 of added value through design, branding, and celebrity endorsements.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Five Ways to Add Value</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">The exam board identifies five methods businesses use to add value: convenience, branding, quality, design, and having a <dfn class="term" data-def="A feature that makes a product or service stand out from those offered by competitors.">unique selling point</dfn> (USP).</p>

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Branding</strong> — A strong brand creates customer loyalty and allows a business to charge more. Nike’s “Just Do It” slogan and swoosh logo mean customers pay a premium even when cheaper alternatives exist.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Design</strong> — An attractive or innovative product justifies a higher price. Apple MacBooks use sleek aluminium designs that make customers willing to pay more for the look and feel. Dyson’s bladeless fans use futuristic design to charge a premium for what is essentially a fan.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Quality</strong> — High-quality products reduce complaints and encourage repeat purchases. Barbour hand-crafts waxed jackets in the UK using durable materials — some are passed down through generations.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n18"><strong>USP</strong> — A unique selling point differentiates a product from competitors. Innocent Smoothies’ USP is 100% natural ingredients with no added sugar, appealing to health-conscious consumers.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n19"><strong>Convenience</strong> — Making things easier for the customer adds value. Amazon Prime offers next-day delivery, Tesco Click & Collect lets customers order online and pick up in store, and Uber allows instant ride booking via an app.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n20">The Role of Entrepreneurship</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n21">An <dfn class="term" data-def="A person who organises resources, makes business decisions, and takes risks in order to start and run a business.">entrepreneur</dfn> is someone who takes a business idea and turns it into reality. The exam board requires you to know three key roles of an entrepreneur: organising resources, making business decisions, and taking risks.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>The Three Roles of an Entrepreneur</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Organising resources</strong> — An entrepreneur brings together the four factors of production. Andrew Jennings, founder of Berrywhite organic drinks, had to organise raw materials, a factory, workers, and marketing before he could sell a single bottle.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Making business decisions</strong> — Entrepreneurs decide on the brand name, logo, core values, pricing, promotion methods, and where to get finance. These decisions shape whether the business succeeds or fails.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Taking risks</strong> — Setting up a business means paying out money in advance for materials, equipment, and wages with no guarantee the product will sell. If the business fails, the entrepreneur bears all the costs. Leaving a regular job to start a business means giving up a secure income.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n25" data-revision-tip="Close this and define enterprise. Which of the 4 factors of production is it?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p><dfn class="term" data-def="The willingness to take a chance on a new business idea, accepting that it may fail and result in financial loss.">Enterprise</dfn> is about taking risks, being creative and innovative, and using initiative. It is one of the four factors of production and the driving force behind every new business.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n27">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n28">Businesses exist to produce goods or services, meet customer needs, and add value — the difference between selling price and cost of inputs.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n29">The five ways to add value are branding, design, quality, USP, and convenience — learn a real business example for each.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n30">An entrepreneur organises resources, makes business decisions, and takes risks — enterprise is one of the four factors of production.</li></ul>
