Business plans, stakeholders, technology, legislation, and economic influences on business.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Is a Business Plan?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A written document that sets out the business idea, target market, financial forecasts, and operational details to guide the business and attract finance.">business plan</dfn> is a written document that explains a business idea and how it will work in practice. It brings together everything an entrepreneur needs to think about before launching — from the product or service they will sell, to how they will make money, to what could go wrong.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">For the Edexcel exam, you will not be asked to write a business plan yourself. However, you must understand the role, importance, and purpose of a business plan, and be able to explain how it helps a business succeed.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Close this and list the EIGHT roles of a business plan, like 'target market' or 'sources of finance'.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>A business plan identifies: the business idea, aims and objectives, target market (from market research), forecast revenue/costs/profit, a cash-flow forecast, sources of finance, location, and the marketing mix. These are the eight roles the exam board expects you to know.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n5">The Role and Importance of a Business Plan</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">A business plan serves several key purposes. First, it forces the entrepreneur to focus on their <strong>business idea</strong> — what gap in the market they are filling, what product or service they will offer, and who their target customers are. Writing this down makes the idea concrete rather than vague.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Second, it sets out <strong>aims and objectives</strong>. A well-written plan includes SMART objectives (Specific, Measurable, Agreed, Realistic, Time-limited) so the business has clear goals to work towards. For example, “sell 150 units per week by the end of Month 2” is far more useful than “get loads of customers.”</p>
<div class="collapsible">

<span>The Eight Roles of a Business Plan (Edexcel Spec)</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>1. Identify the business idea</strong> — What product or service will be sold? What gap in the market does it fill?</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>2. Business aims and objectives</strong> — What are the SMART targets? What does the business want to achieve?</p>
<p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>3. Target market</strong> — Who are the customers? What does market research show about demand?</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>4. Forecast revenue, costs and profit</strong> — How much money will the business make, spend, and keep?</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>5. Cash-flow forecast</strong> — When will cash come in and go out each month? Are there any shortfall months?</p>
<p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>6. Sources of finance</strong> — Where will the start-up capital come from? Banks, investors, family, crowdfunding?</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14"><strong>7. Location</strong> — Where will the business operate? Is it near customers, suppliers, and transport links?</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15"><strong>8. Marketing mix</strong> — How will the product be priced, promoted, distributed, and what features will it have?</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n16">The Purpose of Planning: Reducing Risk and Obtaining Finance</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">A carefully written business plan reduces the chance of <dfn class="term" data-def="When a business cannot pay its debts and is forced to close down.">business failure</dfn>. If the owner has to write down future plans and finances, they are more likely to spot potential problems early. For example, a cash-flow forecast might reveal that the business will run out of cash in Month 3 — allowing the owner to arrange an overdraft or cut costs before it becomes a crisis.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n18">A business plan also helps to <strong>obtain finance</strong>. Banks, venture capitalists, and angel investors will not lend money unless they can see detailed evidence that the business will grow and generate profit. The better the financial information in the plan, the more confident lenders will be. A plan also helps identify the <strong>resources</strong> a business needs: human resources (staff), equipment, raw materials, technology, and vehicles.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n19">Stakeholders and Their Interests</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n20">A <dfn class="term" data-def="Any individual or group that has an interest in or is affected by the activities of a business.">stakeholder</dfn> is anyone who has an interest in a business. Different stakeholders have different — and sometimes conflicting — interests. For the exam, you need to know six key stakeholder groups and what each one wants from the business.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n21" data-revision-tip="Without looking, name THREE of the six stakeholder groups and their respective wants, e.g., 'employees want...'">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The six key stakeholders are: <strong>customers</strong> (want quality products at fair prices), <strong>employees</strong> (want fair pay, job security, and good working conditions), <strong>shareholders</strong> (want profit and a return on investment), <strong>local community</strong> (want jobs, minimal pollution, and community support), <strong>government</strong> (wants tax revenue, legal compliance, and employment), and <strong>suppliers</strong> (want regular orders and prompt payment).</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n22">Stakeholder interests can conflict. For example, shareholders may want to maximise profits by cutting wages, but employees want higher pay. A business that prioritises ethics tries to meet the wider needs of all its stakeholders, not just the owners.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n23">The Impact of Technology</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n24"><dfn class="term" data-def="The application of scientific knowledge and digital tools to create new products, improve processes, or change ways of working.">Technology</dfn> is a major external influence on business. It creates opportunities for new business ideas — for example, online platforms like Amazon and apps like Uber only exist because of advances in digital technology. It can also make existing products obsolete; film cameras, video rental shops, and traditional chimney sweeps have all declined because of technological change.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n25">Technology also changes the way businesses operate. It improves <strong>efficiency</strong> through software for payroll, stock control, and document management. It enables <strong>remote working</strong>, allowing employees to collaborate from anywhere in the world. In production, technology can lower costs, improve quality, increase productivity, and add flexibility — though it often requires significant upfront investment and staff retraining.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Consumer Law and Employment Law</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n26"><strong>Consumer law</strong> protects buyers. Key rules include: goods must match any description given, goods must be of satisfactory quality, and adverts must not be misleading. Following consumer law builds customer trust — if a faulty product is replaced or refunded, customers leave positive reviews and return. However, compliance can raise costs for small businesses through staff training and processing returns.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n27"><strong>Employment law</strong> protects workers. All employees must be paid at least the National Minimum Wage for their age. It is illegal to pay two workers differently for the same job without a good reason (discrimination). Employers hold core responsibility for health and safety in the workplace, though employees must also cooperate. Phrases like “recent graduate” in job adverts risk age discrimination unless the requirement is objectively justified.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n28">Legislation increases costs in the short term (training, compliance, admin), but it also protects the business’s reputation, builds customer loyalty, and helps attract quality job candidates.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n29">The Economy and External Influences</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n30">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The system of production, distribution, and consumption of goods and services in a country, measured by indicators like GDP, unemployment, and inflation.">economy</dfn> has a powerful effect on business performance. Four key economic factors you need to know are:</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Interest Rates, Exchange Rates, Taxation, and Unemployment</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n31"><strong>Interest rates</strong> are the cost of borrowing money. When interest rates rise, businesses pay more to repay loans, increasing their fixed costs and squeezing profits. Higher rates also discourage consumers from borrowing, reducing demand for expensive items like cars and houses.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n32"><strong>Exchange rates</strong> affect businesses that import or export. If the pound weakens, UK exports become cheaper for foreign buyers (boosting sales abroad), but imported materials cost more. If the pound strengthens, the opposite happens — exports become more expensive and imports become cheaper.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n33"><strong>Taxation</strong> affects business costs and consumer spending. Higher taxes on businesses reduce profits. Higher income tax or VAT reduces consumers’ disposable income, meaning they spend less. Lower taxes can stimulate spending and growth.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n34"><strong>Unemployment</strong> affects both demand and labour supply. High unemployment means consumers have less money to spend, reducing demand. However, it can make it easier for businesses to recruit staff because more people are looking for work, and wage demands may be lower.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n35" data-revision-tip="From memory, list FOUR external influences and briefly explain how interest rates or legislation affect a business.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>External influences are factors outside the business’s control that affect its performance. The main ones for Edexcel are: <strong>stakeholders</strong>, <strong>technology</strong>, <strong>legislation</strong> (consumer and employment law), and <strong>the economy</strong> (interest rates, exchange rates, taxation, unemployment). A business cannot control these but must plan for and respond to them.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n37">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n38">A business plan reduces risk and helps obtain finance by setting out the idea, target market, financial forecasts, location, and marketing mix in a formal document.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n39">Six key stakeholders (customers, employees, shareholders, local community, government, suppliers) each have different interests that can sometimes conflict.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n40">External influences — technology, legislation, and economic factors like interest rates, exchange rates, taxation, and unemployment — are outside the business’s control but must be planned for.</li></ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n36">For “Discuss” and “Evaluate” questions on external influences, always give both sides. For example, if asked about the impact of rising interest rates, explain how costs increase (point + because + therefore) and then consider whether the business could pass costs on to customers or find other ways to cope. Use the BLT chain: Point → Because → Leading to/Therefore. Always link back to the business in the question.</p>
