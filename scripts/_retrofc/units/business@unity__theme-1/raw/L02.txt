--- DESCRIPTION ---
Why entrepreneurs risk financial loss and what rewards like profit and independence they gain.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Is Risk in Business?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Every time someone starts a business, they are taking a <dfn class="term" data-def="The chance that something will go wrong, leading to loss of time or money.">risk</dfn>. There is no guarantee that the business will succeed. The Edexcel specification groups the main risks into three categories: <strong>business failure</strong>, <strong>financial loss</strong>, and <strong>lack of security</strong>. Understanding these risks — and balancing them against potential rewards — is central to the decision every <dfn class="term" data-def="A person who is prepared to take a risk in the hope of gaining a reward by starting and running a business.">entrepreneur</dfn> must make.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Close this box and list the three MAIN risks entrepreneurs face: failure, loss, & lack of...?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The three main risks of starting a business are: <strong>business failure</strong> (the business ceases to trade), <strong>financial loss</strong> (costs exceed revenue, and money invested is lost), and <strong>lack of security</strong> (no guaranteed income or employee benefits).</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Business Failure</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5"><dfn class="term" data-def="When a business ceases to trade, often because it cannot cover its costs or pay its debts.">Business failure</dfn> is when a business can no longer operate and closes permanently. Start-up failure rates are stark: <strong>20% of small businesses fail in their first year</strong>, 30% fail by their second year, 50% fail within five years, and 70% fail by their tenth year.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Recent high-profile UK failures show that even well-known brands are not safe. <strong>Wilko</strong> (2023) ran out of cash and closed all 400+ stores. <strong>The Body Shop</strong> UK collapsed in February 2024. <strong>Ted Baker</strong> went bust in March 2024 after debts piled up. <strong>Claire’s</strong> UK went into administration in August 2025, putting 2,150 jobs at risk.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Why Do Businesses Fail?</span>

</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n7">There are several common reasons why businesses fail:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Cash flow problems</strong> — the business cannot pay rent, wages, or suppliers on time. Wilko had the wrong stock and no cash coming in.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>High costs and low sales</strong> — energy, rent, and wages rise while consumers cut back during the cost-of-living crisis. The Body Shop closed many stores for this reason.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Too much competition</strong> — cheaper high-street rivals like Primark and B&M, plus online giants such as Amazon and Shein, take customers away.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Changing consumer tastes</strong> — if a business does not keep up with trends, customers drift away. Ted Baker became less fashionable compared to newer brands.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Failure to innovate</strong> — products can become <dfn class="term" data-def="No longer in use or out of date, often because a newer alternative has replaced it.">obsolete</dfn> if the business does not adapt. Blockbuster failed because it did not move to streaming quickly enough.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Financial Loss</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14"><dfn class="term" data-def="When a business's costs are higher than its income, resulting in the owner losing money.">Financial loss</dfn> occurs when a business’s costs exceed its revenue. An entrepreneur typically invests their own savings or borrows money from a bank to start up. If the business is not profitable, that money may never be recovered.</p>
<figure class="diagram"><img src="https://pub-aeb94e100e5a48f4a133be5bf206aecb.r2.dev/business/theme-1/diagram_business_failure_rates.png" alt="Generated diagram for Risk and Reward"><figcaption>Generated diagram for Risk and Reward</figcaption></figure>
<p data-narration-id="n15">If the business is a sole trader or partnership, the owner has <strong>unlimited liability</strong>, meaning personal <dfn class="term" data-def="Things the business or individual owns that have value, such as equipment, vehicles, or cash.">assets</dfn> like a car or even a home could be used to pay business debts. This makes financial loss one of the most serious risks an entrepreneur faces.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n16" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and calculate the financial loss if costs are 800 and revenue is 600.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p><strong>Profit</strong> is the money left when total costs are subtracted from total revenue. If costs exceed revenue, the business makes a <strong>loss</strong>. For example, if an entrepreneur buys stock for £800 but only sells it for £600, they have made a £200 financial loss.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Lack of Security</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18"><dfn class="term" data-def="The risk that an entrepreneur has no guaranteed income, employment benefits, or job safety.">Lack of security</dfn> means the entrepreneur has no guaranteed wage, no paid holiday or sick pay, and no employer pension. If an entrepreneur leaves a regular job to start a business, they give up all of these protections.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n19">Income depends entirely on sales, which can be unpredictable. In a slow month, the entrepreneur may be unable to pay personal bills such as a mortgage. Long hours and stress can also lead to burnout, and there is no safety net if the business struggles.</p>

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n20">Beyond the three main risks, entrepreneurs also face:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Cyber attacks</strong> — small firms are collectively attacked seven million times per year in the UK, costing an estimated £5.26 billion. Malware, hacking, and ransomware can steal data, cause financial loss, and damage reputation.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Economic problems</strong> — in a recession, customers save rather than spend on non-essential items, reducing sales and <dfn class="term" data-def="The money left after total costs are subtracted from total revenue.">profit</dfn>.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Poor reputation</strong> — bad customer service or safety incidents can destroy trust. After the Smiler crash, Alton Towers was fined £5 million and visitor numbers took years to recover.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Supply chain failures</strong> — KFC had to close hundreds of UK branches in 2018 after switching supplier to DHL, which struggled with the delivery volume.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n25">The Rewards of Enterprise</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n26">Despite the risks, there are strong reasons why people start businesses. The three main rewards are: <strong>profit</strong>, <strong><dfn class="term" data-def="The freedom to make your own decisions about the business without answering to a boss.">independence</dfn></strong>, and <strong>business success</strong>.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>The Three Main Rewards Explained</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n27"><strong>Profit</strong> — the entrepreneur keeps the profit the business generates. Unlike an employee on a fixed salary, there is no ceiling on what they can earn. However, they may need to reinvest some profit back into the business as equipment wears out, and if they take on a partner, profits must be shared.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n28"><strong>Independence</strong> — being your own boss means making all the major decisions: what to sell, how to price it, when to work. The government lists better work autonomy, better work challenge, and family legacy (building a business to pass on) as key benefits. This control and freedom is a powerful motivator for many entrepreneurs.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n29"><strong>Business success</strong> — a growing customer base, positive reviews, and a strong reputation bring personal satisfaction and status. The case study of Jamal Acharya shows this well: he started Web Horizons Ltd in 2018, grew it through positive reviews, signed contracts with large companies, and eventually reduced his hours to four days a week to spend more time with family.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n30" data-revision-tip="Without looking, name the three MAIN rewards of entrepreneurship: profit, independence, &...?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The three main rewards are: <strong>profit</strong> (income for the owner if the business succeeds), <strong>independence</strong> (ability to make your own decisions and control your working life), and <strong>business success</strong> (personal satisfaction, reputation, and achievement).</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n31">Balancing Risk and Reward</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n32">Entrepreneurs can take steps to reduce risk before and during their business. Producing a <strong>business plan</strong> helps spot problems early. <strong>Cash-flow forecasting</strong> reveals when money might run short. <strong>Market research</strong> helps the entrepreneur understand what customers want and are willing to pay, lowering the chance of failure. Working closely with customers — as the Edexcel exam question suggests — is one of the most effective ways to reduce the risk of financial loss.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n34">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n35">The three main risks of starting a business are business failure, financial loss, and lack of security — with 20% of start-ups failing in their first year alone.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n36">The three main rewards are profit, independence, and business success — motivating entrepreneurs to take the risk despite uncertainty.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n37">Entrepreneurs can reduce risk through business planning, cash-flow forecasting, and market research.</li></ul>
