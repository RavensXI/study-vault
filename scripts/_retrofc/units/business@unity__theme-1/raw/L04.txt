--- DESCRIPTION ---
Explore the four customer needs — price, quality, choice, and convenience — and why they matter.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Are Customer Needs?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">In business, a <dfn class="term" data-def="The different elements or factors that a customer expects, wants, and would like from a good or service.">customer need</dfn> is a problem that customers could solve by purchasing a product or service. A good business will aim to satisfy these needs. The Edexcel specification identifies four key customer needs: <strong>price</strong>, <strong>quality</strong>, <strong>choice</strong>, and <strong>convenience</strong>.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Close this box and name all four customer needs: price, quality, choice and...?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The four customer needs you must know for your exam are: <strong>price</strong> (what the customer pays), <strong>quality</strong> (how good or reliable the product is), <strong>choice</strong> (the range of options available), and <strong>convenience</strong> (how easy and fast it is for the customer to buy).</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Price</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5"><dfn class="term" data-def="How much a customer pays for a product or service.">Price</dfn> is how much a customer pays for a product or service. Customers want to feel they are getting good <strong>value for money</strong> — this does not always mean the cheapest option. Value for money means the quality of the product is fair relative to the price paid. For example, a student might choose the cheapest pair of trainers available, while a professional athlete might pay more for a pair that lasts longer and performs better.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Businesses must set prices carefully. If the price is too high, customers will buy from competitors. If the price is too low, the business may not cover its costs or customers may question the quality.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n7">Quality</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n8"><dfn class="term" data-def="How good or reliable a product or service is, and how well it meets customer expectations.">Quality</dfn> means how good or reliable a product is and how well it meets customer expectations. By UK law, products must be of a “satisfactory quality” — this means the product must meet the standard that a reasonable person would regard as acceptable.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9">Quality is linked to price. A jacket costing £10 would not be expected to be the same quality as one costing £1,600. However, both must be satisfactory for their price point. Businesses like JD Sports stock trusted brands such as Nike and Adidas because customers associate these names with reliable, long-lasting products.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Real-World Example: JD Sports and a Local Barber</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>JD Sports</strong> (product-focused) targets 15–18-year-old students. It meets customer needs through: student discount periods and sale rails (<em>price</em>), trusted brands that last (<em>quality</em>), a wide range of sizes, colours, and styles (<em>choice</em>), and click-and-collect with easy returns (<em>convenience</em>).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>A local barber</strong> (service-focused) targets working adults with limited time. It meets customer needs through: clear price lists and loyalty cards (<em>price</em>), trained barbers using quality products (<em>quality</em>), different services like skin fades and beard trims (<em>choice</em>), and walk-ins plus online booking with evening and Saturday hours (<em>convenience</em>).</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n12">Choice</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">Choice refers to the range of options customers can pick from. Not every customer wants the same product or service, so businesses often produce a <strong>product range</strong> to meet different needs. For example, a car manufacturer might offer an SUV for families, a small car for new drivers, and a sports car for customers who want luxury and performance.</p>
<figure class="diagram"><img src="https://pub-aeb94e100e5a48f4a133be5bf206aecb.r2.dev/business/theme-1/diagram_customer_needs.jpg" alt="Diagram: Customer Needs" loading="lazy"></figure>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Supermarkets are a good example of choice in action. A typical supermarket might stock over 400 types of yoghurt. While this seems excessive, it allows the business to cater to customers with different dietary requirements, taste preferences, and budgets. However, offering <em>limited</em> choice can also be a strategy — fewer options can help customers choose more quickly, reducing the chance they abandon their purchase.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n15">Convenience</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n16"><dfn class="term" data-def="The ease with which a customer can purchase and receive a product or service, saving them time and effort.">Customer convenience</dfn> means saving the customer time and effort. The task of business owners is to find a way of trading that makes purchasing as easy as possible. Examples of convenience include: next-day delivery, personalisation of products, self-service checkouts, and choosing a good business location.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Without looking, what customer need does Deliveroo exemplify, and how does it do it?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Deliveroo is a strong example of convenience. It offers home delivery from restaurants that do not provide their own delivery service, allowing customers to access high-quality restaurant food from the comfort of their own home or office.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Why Identifying Customer Needs Matters</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19">Understanding customer needs is essential for two reasons the exam board requires you to know: <strong>generating sales</strong> and <strong>business survival</strong>.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Generating Sales and Business Survival</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Generating sales:</strong> A business needs to generate sales to survive. It can do this by lowering the price, promoting the product through advertising, running competitions or other promotional activities, and improving the product. Meeting customer needs directly leads to higher sales because satisfied customers are more likely to make purchases and return for repeat business.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Business survival:</strong> If a business does not identify and satisfy customer needs, it will not survive. The business must find out what customers want and need, then provide that product or service at the right price, in the right place, at the right time. If a rival serves customer needs better, market share can fall. Loyal customers help a business survive during downturns because they continue to buy even when times are difficult.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n22" data-revision-tip="Cover this box: What four negative outcomes happen if a business misses customer needs?">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>If a business fails to identify customer needs, it risks losing customers, gaining a bad reputation through negative reviews, falling sales revenue, and ultimately business failure through insolvency.</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n23">A business that understands its customers will offer a suitable product or service. This means the business will make sales and potentially boost profits. Businesses can check if they are meeting customer needs by monitoring sales figures, gathering customer feedback, and tracking repeat purchases.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n25">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n26">The four customer needs are price, quality, choice, and convenience — businesses must meet these to attract and retain customers.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n27">Identifying customer needs is essential for generating sales and ensuring business survival in a competitive market.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n28">Failing to meet customer needs leads to lost customers, falling market share, and ultimately business failure.</li></ul>
