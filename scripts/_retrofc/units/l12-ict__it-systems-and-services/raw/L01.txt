Computing devices, input and output hardware, storage devices, internal components, and the ports that connect them.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Is Hardware?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="The physical components of a computer system that you can touch, including devices inside the machine and those connected to it.">Hardware</dfn> is the collection of physical components that make up a computer system. Every piece of equipment — from a keyboard to a graphics card — is hardware. Hardware is usually divided into five categories: computing devices, input devices, output devices, storage devices, and internal components. Ports link them all together. Understanding each category is essential because IT professionals are expected to advise clients on choosing, setting up and troubleshooting hardware.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Types of Computing Device</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">A <dfn class="term" data-def="Any electronic device capable of accepting data, processing it and producing a result; includes desktops, laptops, tablets, smartphones, servers and embedded devices.">computing device</dfn> is any machine that can accept data, process it and produce a result. The most common types are desktop computers, which sit on a desk and offer powerful processing and easy upgrades; laptop computers, which are portable versions of a desktop with a built-in screen, keyboard and battery; tablets, which use a touchscreen and are designed primarily for consumption and lightweight tasks; and smartphones, which combine telephony with computing power in a pocket-sized device. Servers are specialised computers that store data, run applications and respond to requests from other machines on a network. Embedded devices — such as those inside a smart washing machine or a car's engine management system — are computers built into another product for a dedicated purpose.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n5">Choosing the right computing device depends on the task. A graphic designer producing high-resolution video will need a powerful desktop; a sales representative who travels constantly will prefer a lightweight laptop; a warehouse operative scanning barcodes may use a rugged handheld device. IT professionals must match device to need.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Input Devices</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">An <dfn class="term" data-def="A hardware component that allows a user or external source to send data into a computer system for processing.">input device</dfn> is any component that feeds data into the computer. The most widely used input devices are the keyboard, which translates key presses into character codes, and the mouse, which translates physical movement into cursor position on screen. A touchscreen combines input and output: the display is also a sensor that detects finger position. Scanners convert physical documents or images into digital files; barcode readers (used in supermarkets and libraries) scan encoded lines into product data; webcams capture live video; microphones convert sound waves into digital audio signals; and biometric readers such as fingerprint scanners or iris recognition systems convert physical characteristics into authentication data.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n8">Each input device is chosen for the type of data it captures most efficiently. A touch-sensitive graphics tablet allows a designer to draw naturally; an RFID reader allows a warehouse to track thousands of items without any manual scanning. In exam questions, always connect the input device to the type of data it captures and the reason that method is most appropriate.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n9" data-revision-tip="Cover this and try to recall at least six different input devices with the type of data each one captures. Practise explaining why each device suits its context.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Input devices feed data INTO the computer. The key examples are keyboard (text), mouse (cursor movement), touchscreen (touch position), scanner (images), microphone (audio), webcam (video), barcode reader (product codes) and biometric reader (physical characteristics).</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Output Devices</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">An output device takes processed data from the computer and presents it in a form humans or machines can use. Monitors display visual output — an LED or OLED screen converts digital signals into light and colour. Printers produce hard copy on paper; inkjet printers spray tiny droplets of ink and suit home or small-office use, while laser printers use a heat-fused toner powder and are faster and more economical for large volumes. Speakers convert digital audio signals into sound waves. Projectors display computer output on a large screen or wall — essential in classrooms and boardrooms. Actuators are output devices that cause physical movement, for example the motor in a robotic arm that receives a signal from software and moves accordingly.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n12">Some devices are both input and output. A touchscreen monitor accepts touch as input and displays images as output. A headset with a microphone handles both. These are called input/output (I/O) devices.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Storage Devices</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Storage devices hold data when it is not being actively processed. They fall into two categories: primary storage and secondary storage. Primary storage — mainly <dfn class="term" data-def="Random Access Memory: fast, temporary storage inside the computer that holds programs and data currently in use; its contents are lost when power is removed.">RAM (Random Access Memory)</dfn> — is fast but temporary; it holds programs and data while the computer is switched on, and its contents are lost when the machine is powered off. Secondary storage is permanent (non-volatile) storage used to keep files long term. A hard disk drive (HDD) stores data on spinning magnetic platters; it is inexpensive for large capacities but can be slow and fragile. A solid state drive (SSD) stores data on flash memory chips with no moving parts; it is faster, lighter and more reliable but costs more per gigabyte. USB flash drives are portable solid-state devices ideal for transferring files between machines. Optical discs such as CDs, DVDs and Blu-rays store data on a reflective surface read by a laser; they are used for distributing software, music and film. Cloud storage stores data on remote servers accessed via the internet — covered further when we look at IT services.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="Remember: RAM is primary (fast, temporary); HDD, SSD, USB and optical are secondary (permanent). Learn one advantage and one disadvantage for HDD and SSD — these come up in compare questions.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Primary storage (RAM) is fast and temporary. Secondary storage (HDD, SSD, USB flash drive, optical disc, cloud) is permanent. SSDs are faster and more reliable than HDDs but cost more per gigabyte of capacity.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Basic Internal Components</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">Inside the computer case are several components that work together to run programs and manage data. The <dfn class="term" data-def="Central Processing Unit: the main processor of a computer that carries out instructions by fetching, decoding and executing them; often called the brain of the computer.">CPU (Central Processing Unit)</dfn> is the processor that carries out instructions; it fetches instructions from RAM, decodes them and executes them millions of times per second. The motherboard is the main circuit board to which all other components connect — it carries the CPU, RAM slots, expansion slots and connectors for drives and peripherals. RAM slots hold the memory modules. The graphics processing unit (GPU) handles the calculations needed to generate images on screen; dedicated GPUs are essential for gaming, video editing and machine learning. The power supply unit (PSU) converts mains electricity into the lower voltages the computer components need. A cooling system — typically a fan and heatsink attached to the CPU — prevents overheating, which would cause the processor to slow down or fail.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n18">When a program runs, the operating system loads it from secondary storage into RAM, and the CPU reads and executes its instructions from there. The faster the CPU and the more RAM installed, the more programs can run simultaneously without the system slowing down. Upgrading RAM is often the most cost-effective way to improve performance on an older machine.</p>

    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n19">The CPU can only work with data that is in RAM. Every time you open a program, the computer copies it from the hard drive (slow) into RAM (fast). If RAM fills up, the operating system begins using part of the hard drive as temporary memory — called virtual memory or a page file. This is dramatically slower, which is why a computer with too little RAM feels sluggish even if it has a fast processor. Adding more RAM allows more programs and data to sit in fast memory at once, reducing the need to swap in and out of slow storage. This is why IT professionals often recommend checking RAM before recommending a new CPU.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n20">Ports</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n21">Ports are physical connectors on a computer that allow external devices to be attached. USB (Universal Serial Bus) is the most common port type; it supports keyboards, mice, external drives, phones and countless other devices. USB comes in several versions: USB-A is the rectangular connector most people recognise; USB-C is the smaller oval connector used on modern laptops and smartphones because it can transfer data, video and power through a single cable. HDMI (High-Definition Multimedia Interface) carries digital video and audio to monitors and televisions — one cable replaces the older separate video and audio leads. A 3.5 mm audio jack connects headphones and speakers. An SD card slot accepts flash memory cards from cameras. An Ethernet port connects the computer to a wired network using a standard network cable. Older ports such as VGA (analogue video) and PS/2 (keyboard and mouse) are still found on legacy equipment.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n22">Match the port to the type of signal and the speed required. For connecting a monitor, HDMI or DisplayPort is preferred because they carry high-quality digital video and audio without conversion. For transferring large files quickly, USB 3.0 or USB-C offers speeds far above older USB 2.0. For a reliable, high-speed network connection — especially in an office or for gaming — an Ethernet cable into the Ethernet port gives lower latency and more stable throughput than Wi-Fi. For charging a modern smartphone or laptop, USB-C has largely replaced older proprietary connectors because it supports high wattage Power Delivery. IT support staff must recognise all common port types because misidentifying a port is a common cause of frustrating installation errors.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n24">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">Computing devices include desktops, laptops, tablets, smartphones, servers and embedded devices; the right choice depends on the task and environment.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n26">Input devices (keyboard, mouse, scanner, webcam, biometric reader) feed data into the computer; output devices (monitor, printer, speaker, projector) present processed data to the user.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n27">Storage divides into primary (RAM — fast and temporary) and secondary (HDD, SSD, USB, optical, cloud — permanent); SSDs are faster and more reliable than HDDs but cost more per gigabyte.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n28">Key internal components are the CPU (processes instructions), motherboard (connects everything), RAM (working memory), GPU (graphics), and PSU (power). Ports such as USB, HDMI and Ethernet link the computer to external devices.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n23">In questions that ask you to "identify a suitable input/output/storage device", always justify your choice by linking the device to the specific data type or task in the question. For example: "A barcode reader would be suitable because the supermarket needs to read product codes quickly at the point of sale without requiring staff to type numbers manually." Unjustified answers rarely score full marks. When comparing HDD and SSD, structure your answer using the words advantage and disadvantage, and use accurate technical terms such as access time, capacity, durability and cost per gigabyte.</p>
