--- DESCRIPTION ---
How the nervous system detects stimuli, transmits impulses and coordinates rapid responses.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Structure of the Nervous System</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The human <dfn class="term" data-def="The organ system consisting of the brain, spinal cord and nerves that detects stimuli and coordinates the body’s responses.">nervous system</dfn> allows the body to detect changes in the environment (stimuli) and produce rapid, coordinated responses (B3.1a). It consists of the <strong>central nervous system (CNS)</strong> — the brain and spinal cord — and the <strong>peripheral nervous system</strong> — nerves that extend to all parts of the body, connecting the CNS to receptors and effectors.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">The pathway for a nervous response involves a clear sequence: <strong>stimulus</strong> → <dfn class="term" data-def="Specialised cells that detect stimuli such as light, sound, temperature, pressure or chemicals.">sensory receptor</dfn> → <strong>sensory neurone</strong> → <strong>CNS (relay neurone)</strong> → <strong>motor neurone</strong> → <dfn class="term" data-def="An organ that carries out a response, such as a muscle (which contracts) or a gland (which secretes a hormone or other substance).">effector</dfn> (muscle or gland) → <strong>response</strong>.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n4">The nervous system is able to coordinate responses because it has many links between different parts of the body, has different types of sensory receptors for different stimuli (light receptors in the eyes, temperature receptors in the skin, chemical receptors in the nose and tongue), and can process information in the CNS before sending out appropriate signals (B3.1b).</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Learn the nervous pathway in order: stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone (CNS) → motor neurone → effector → response. This sequence is tested in almost every B3 exam paper.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The nervous pathway: stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone (in CNS) → motor neurone → effector → response. The CNS (brain and spinal cord) processes information and coordinates the response.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Neurones and Their Structure</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">There are three types of <dfn class="term" data-def="A nerve cell that transmits electrical impulses. Types include sensory, relay and motor neurones, each with distinct structures.">neurone</dfn>: <strong>sensory neurones</strong> carry impulses from receptors to the CNS, <strong>relay neurones</strong> are found within the CNS and connect sensory neurones to motor neurones, and <strong>motor neurones</strong> carry impulses from the CNS to effectors.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8">The OCR spec requires you to know the structure of sensory and motor neurones (B3.1b). Both have a <strong>cell body</strong> containing the nucleus, <strong>dendrites</strong> (short branches that receive impulses), and a long <strong>axon</strong> that transmits the electrical impulse. Motor neurones have a long axon running from the spinal cord to the effector. Sensory neurones have a long dendron connecting the receptor to the cell body, and a shorter axon entering the CNS. The axon may be insulated by a <dfn class="term" data-def="A fatty sheath that insulates the axon of a neurone, speeding up the transmission of electrical impulses along the nerve.">myelin sheath</dfn>, which speeds up impulse transmission.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Synapses</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">Where two neurones meet there is a tiny gap called a <dfn class="term" data-def="The junction between two neurones where a chemical (neurotransmitter) is released to transmit the signal across the gap.">synapse</dfn>. The electrical impulse cannot cross this gap directly. Instead, when the impulse arrives at the end of the first neurone, chemicals called <strong>neurotransmitters</strong> are released from vesicles into the synaptic gap. These neurotransmitters diffuse across the gap and bind to specific <strong>receptor molecules</strong> on the membrane of the next neurone, triggering a new electrical impulse that continues along the pathway (B3.1b).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11">Synapses ensure impulses travel in one direction only (because neurotransmitters are only released from one side and receptors are only on the other). They also allow the nervous system to connect many different pathways — a single neurone can have synapses with multiple other neurones, enabling complex coordination.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n12">A <strong>reflex arc</strong> is the pathway that produces a rapid, automatic, protective response (a reflex) without conscious thought (B3.1c). The pathway is: receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone (in <strong>spinal cord</strong>, not the brain) → motor neurone → effector.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n13">For example, touching a hot object: pain receptors in the skin detect heat → sensory neurone carries the impulse to the spinal cord → relay neurone passes it directly to a motor neurone → motor neurone stimulates the muscle (effector) in the arm to contract → hand is pulled away. This <strong>bypasses the brain</strong>, making the response faster and protecting the body from harm.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n14">Reflexes are important because they are faster than voluntary responses (fewer synapses to cross and a shorter pathway through the spinal cord rather than the brain). They protect the body from damage before you are consciously aware of the danger. Other examples include the pupil reflex (pupil constricts in bright light to protect the retina) and the knee-jerk reflex.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="Reflexes are automatic and do NOT involve the brain — the signal goes through the spinal cord. This makes them faster and protects the body from harm. Always state that the reflex bypasses the brain in your answer.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Reflex arcs produce rapid, automatic responses that bypass the brain, going through the spinal cord instead. This makes reflexes faster and protects the body from danger. The pathway involves fewer synapses than a voluntary response.</p>
</div>

    <p data-narration-id="n16">The ruler drop test measures reaction time (B3.1c practical). One person holds a ruler vertically; the other places their thumb and finger at the 0 cm mark without touching the ruler. When the ruler is released without warning, the subject catches it as quickly as possible. The distance fallen is recorded — a shorter distance means a faster reaction time.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n17">To improve reliability: repeat the test at least five times and calculate a <strong>mean</strong>, discard anomalous results, and ensure the same person drops the ruler each time. You can convert the distance to time using the equation: \(t = \sqrt{\frac{2d}{g}}\) where \(d\) is distance in metres and \(g\) is \(9.8 \text{ m/s}^2\). Variables that could affect reaction time include caffeine, tiredness, distractions and practice.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n18">The nervous system and endocrine system both coordinate body functions, but they work differently. The nervous system transmits <strong>electrical impulses</strong> along neurones for <strong>fast, short-lived, precise</strong> responses (e.g. pulling your hand away from heat). The endocrine system uses <strong>chemical hormones</strong> carried in the <strong>blood</strong> for <strong>slower, longer-lasting, widespread</strong> responses (e.g. controlling blood glucose over hours). The next lesson covers the endocrine system in detail.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n20">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n21">The nervous system detects stimuli via sensory receptors and coordinates responses via electrical impulses along neurones. The CNS (brain and spinal cord) processes information.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n22">Synapses use chemical neurotransmitters to transmit signals between neurones. They ensure one-way transmission and allow complex coordination.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n23">Reflex arcs produce rapid, automatic responses that bypass the brain (going through the spinal cord), making them faster for protective responses.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n19">When describing a reflex arc, always name each component in order and give a specific example. Do not say “the brain processes the signal” for a reflex — reflexes bypass the brain and go through the <strong>spinal cord</strong>. This is a common error that loses marks. For 6-mark questions, include the role of synapses (neurotransmitter release, diffusion across gap, binding to receptors) to access the highest level.</p>
