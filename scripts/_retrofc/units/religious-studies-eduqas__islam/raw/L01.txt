<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Tawhid: The Oneness of Allah</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The most fundamental belief in Islam is <dfn class="term" data-def="The Islamic doctrine of the absolute oneness and uniqueness of Allah — the central belief of Islam.">tawhid</dfn> &mdash; the absolute oneness of Allah. The Qur&rsquo;an declares tawhid in Qur&rsquo;an 3:18: &ldquo;Allah witnesses that there is no deity except Him, and so do the angels and those of knowledge.&rdquo; This is not merely a statement about numbers (that there is one God rather than many) but about the unique nature of Allah: nothing in creation can be compared to or associated with Allah. Everything else that exists &mdash; humans, angels, the universe itself &mdash; is created and dependent; Allah alone is self-sufficient and eternal.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Tawhid has three dimensions in Islamic theology: <strong>tawhid al-rububiyya</strong> (the oneness of Allah&rsquo;s lordship &mdash; he alone governs all creation), <strong>tawhid al-uluhiyya</strong> (the oneness of Allah&rsquo;s right to worship &mdash; he alone deserves devotion), and <strong>tawhid al-asma wa-l-sifat</strong> (the oneness of Allah&rsquo;s names and attributes &mdash; his qualities are unique and unlike any creature&rsquo;s). Understanding tawhid shapes every aspect of Muslim life: prayer, ethics, family, and society are all oriented towards this one reality.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Without looking, write down what tawhid means and give two ways it shapes Muslim daily life.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Tawhid &mdash; the absolute oneness of Allah &mdash; is confirmed in Qur&rsquo;an 3:18. The greatest sin in Islam is <dfn class="term" data-def="The sin of associating partners with Allah, or worshipping anything alongside or instead of Allah — the most serious sin in Islamic theology.">shirk</dfn> (associating partners with Allah), which directly violates tawhid. Shirk is the one sin that the Qur&rsquo;an indicates cannot be forgiven if not repented.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">The Qualities of Allah and the 99 Names</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Islam teaches that Allah possesses qualities that are perfect and beyond human comprehension. The Qur&rsquo;an describes Allah as immanent (closer to a person than their own jugular vein, Qur&rsquo;an 50:16) and transcendent (above all creation). Qur&rsquo;an 46:33 points to Allah&rsquo;s power: the one who created the heavens and the earth without weariness is surely able to give life to the dead. Other key qualities include Allah as the All-Knowing (<em>Al-&lsquo;Alim</em>), the Most Merciful (<em>Al-Rahman</em>), the Most Compassionate (<em>Al-Rahim</em>), the All-Seeing (<em>Al-Basir</em>), and the All-Hearing (<em>Al-Sami&rsquo;</em>).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">The Islamic tradition recognises 99 beautiful names of Allah (<em>Asma&rsquo; Allah al-Husna</em>), drawn from the Qur&rsquo;an and the Hadith. These names describe Allah&rsquo;s attributes and are used in Muslim worship and prayer. Many Muslims use a set of prayer beads (<em>subha</em>) to count through the 99 names as a form of devotion. The names remind worshippers that Allah is both awe-inspiring in power (<em>Al-Qahhar</em>, the Subduer; <em>Al-Aziz</em>, the All-Mighty) and deeply personal in care (<em>Al-Wadud</em>, the Most Loving; <em>Al-Latif</em>, the Subtle and Kind).</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n8">Islam as Submission</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9">The word &ldquo;Islam&rdquo; comes from the Arabic root <em>s-l-m</em>, meaning peace, safety, and submission. A <dfn class="term" data-def="A follower of Islam; literally 'one who submits to Allah.'">Muslim</dfn> is one who submits to Allah &mdash; living every dimension of life in accordance with Allah&rsquo;s will as revealed in the Qur&rsquo;an and the example of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him). This submission is not passive resignation but an active, willing alignment of one&rsquo;s whole life &mdash; prayer, work, family, commerce, justice &mdash; with the divine will.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n10">The concept of <dfn class="term" data-def="The greater, inner struggle to submit one's self, desires, and actions to Allah's will — the primary meaning of jihad.">Greater Jihad</dfn> (<em>al-jihad al-akbar</em>) is the internal struggle to overcome one&rsquo;s own ego, desires, and moral failures in order to submit to Allah. This is distinguished from the Lesser Jihad, which refers to external forms of struggle including, under strict conditions, armed conflict. Many Muslims and Islamic scholars emphasise that the Greater Jihad &mdash; the daily interior battle to be a better person &mdash; is the more fundamental challenge facing every believer.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The Islamic declaration of faith: 'There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is the messenger of Allah.'">Shahadah</dfn> is the first of the Five Pillars of Sunni Islam and the gateway to the faith. It declares: &ldquo;There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is the messenger of Allah&rdquo; (<em>La ilaha illallah, Muhammadun rasulullah</em>). The Shahadah is whispered into the ear of a newborn child at birth, via the Adhan (the aqiqah on the seventh day is a separate naming ceremony), recited in the call to prayer, spoken by converts upon entering Islam, and ideally the last words a Muslim utters before death. It is both a statement of belief and a commitment of allegiance.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n12">Islam prohibits images of Allah or of any human figure in acts of worship. This prohibition, rooted in the concept of tawhid, prevents any material object from becoming associated with Allah and guards against idolatry (shirk). As a result, Islamic art channels creative energy into calligraphy (beautiful script of Qur&rsquo;anic verses), geometric patterns, and arabesque decoration rather than figurative painting or sculpture. Mosque interiors are adorned with intricate tilework and the Arabic script of Qur&rsquo;anic verses rather than statues or paintings of sacred figures.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">The Qur&rsquo;an: Revelation and Sacred Text</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Muslims believe the <dfn class="term" data-def="The sacred scripture of Islam, believed to be the direct word of Allah revealed to the Prophet Muhammad through the Angel Jibril.">Qur’an</dfn> is the direct, uncreated word of Allah, revealed to the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) through the Angel <dfn class="term" data-def="The archangel through whom Allah revealed the Qur'an to the Prophet Muhammad; known as Gabriel in the Judeo-Christian tradition.">Jibril</dfn> (Gabriel). Qur&rsquo;an 2:97&ndash;98 confirms this: &ldquo;Say: Whoever is an enemy to Jibril &mdash; it is he who has brought it (the revelation) down upon your heart by permission of Allah.&rdquo; Revelation came to Muhammad over approximately 23 years (from 610 CE until his death in 632 CE), beginning in the Cave of Hira near Mecca.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15">The Qur&rsquo;an is divided into 114 chapters (<em>surahs</em>) of varying length. The longest surah (Al-Baqarah) deals with law, theology, and ethics; the shortest (Al-Kawthar) is just three verses. Muslims regard the Qur&rsquo;an as the final, perfect, and unalterable word of Allah, superseding all previous scriptures. It is treated with great physical respect: many Muslims perform ritual washing (<em>wudu</em>) before touching it, it is stored on the highest shelf in the home, and it is never placed on the floor.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n16" data-revision-tip="Without looking, explain how the Qur'an was revealed and give two ways in which Muslims show physical respect for it.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The Qur&rsquo;an was revealed to the Prophet Muhammad through the Angel Jibril over approximately 23 years (confirmed in Qur&rsquo;an 2:97&ndash;98). It consists of 114 surahs. Muslims treat it with great physical reverence, performing wudu before touching it and never placing it on the floor.</p>
</div>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n17">Alongside the Qur&rsquo;an, two further sources guide Muslim life. The <dfn class="term" data-def="Recorded reports of the words, actions, and approvals of the Prophet Muhammad, used alongside the Qur'an to guide Muslim practice.">Hadith</dfn> are individual reports (narrations) recording what the Prophet Muhammad said, did, or approved of. The <dfn class="term" data-def="The example of the Prophet Muhammad's life and conduct, derived from the Hadith, which Muslims are encouraged to follow.">Sunnah</dfn> is the Prophet&rsquo;s example as a whole &mdash; the pattern of his life that provides a practical model for Muslims. Together, the Qur&rsquo;an, Hadith, and Sunnah form the foundation of Islamic law (<em>sharia</em>). The Hadith were collected and authenticated by scholars in the centuries after Muhammad&rsquo;s death, and the six major Hadith collections are widely accepted across Sunni Islam.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n19">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n20">Tawhid &mdash; the absolute oneness of Allah, confirmed in Qur&rsquo;an 3:18 &mdash; is Islam&rsquo;s central doctrine. Shirk (associating partners with Allah) is the gravest sin. The 99 names describe Allah&rsquo;s attributes; images of Allah or human figures are prohibited in worship contexts.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n21">Islam means submission. The Shahadah (&ldquo;there is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is his messenger&rdquo;) is the declaration of faith and the first Pillar. The Greater Jihad is the internal struggle to align one&rsquo;s life with Allah&rsquo;s will.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n22">The Qur&rsquo;an is the direct word of Allah, revealed to Muhammad through the Angel Jibril over 23 years (Qur&rsquo;an 2:97&ndash;98). It is the primary source of Islamic guidance, supplemented by the Hadith (recorded sayings of the Prophet) and Sunnah (the Prophet&rsquo;s example).</li>
</ul>
