--- DESCRIPTION ---
The structure of the heart, blood vessels and blood, plus coronary heart disease treatments.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Heart</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">The heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood around the body through a <dfn class="term" data-def="A circulatory system where blood flows through the heart twice for each complete circuit of the body: once to the lungs and once to the rest of the body.">double circulatory system</dfn>. The right side of the heart pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs (pulmonary circuit). The left side pumps oxygenated blood to the rest of the body (systemic circuit).</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">The heart has four chambers: two upper <dfn class="term" data-def="The upper chambers of the heart that receive blood from veins.">atria</dfn> (singular: atrium) that receive blood, and two lower <dfn class="term" data-def="The lower chambers of the heart that pump blood out through arteries. The left ventricle has a thicker wall because it pumps blood to the whole body.">ventricles</dfn> that pump blood out. The left ventricle has a thicker muscular wall than the right because it needs to generate higher pressure to pump blood around the entire body, whereas the right ventricle only pumps blood to the nearby lungs.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Name the four heart chambers and trace blood flow from vena cava through to aorta in the correct order.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The heart has four chambers. Blood flows: vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → body.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n5">Valves in the heart prevent the backflow of blood. The <dfn class="term" data-def="Blood vessels that supply the heart muscle itself with oxygenated blood and nutrients.">coronary arteries</dfn> supply the heart muscle with the oxygenated blood it needs to keep beating.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Blood Vessels</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n7">There are three types of blood vessel:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Arteries</strong> — carry blood away from the heart at high pressure. They have thick, muscular, elastic walls.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Veins</strong> — carry blood back to the heart at low pressure. They have thinner walls and contain valves to prevent backflow.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Capillaries</strong> — tiny vessels that connect arteries to veins. Their walls are one cell thick, allowing efficient exchange of substances (oxygen, glucose, carbon dioxide) with body tissues.</li>
</ul>



<h2 data-narration-id="n11">Blood Components</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n12">Blood is a tissue made up of four components:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Red blood cells</strong> — contain <dfn class="term" data-def="A red protein in red blood cells that binds to oxygen in the lungs and releases it in the tissues. It gives blood its red colour.">haemoglobin</dfn>, which binds to oxygen. They have a biconcave disc shape to increase surface area and no nucleus to carry more haemoglobin.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n14"><strong>White blood cells</strong> — part of the immune system. Phagocytes engulf pathogens; lymphocytes produce antibodies.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Platelets</strong> — cell fragments that help blood to clot at wound sites, forming a scab to prevent blood loss and infection.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Plasma</strong> — the liquid part of blood. It transports dissolved substances including carbon dioxide, urea, hormones and glucose.</li>
</ul>

    <p data-narration-id="n17"><dfn class="term" data-def="A condition where the coronary arteries become narrowed due to a build-up of fatty deposits (atheroma), reducing blood flow to the heart muscle.">Coronary heart disease</dfn> (CHD) occurs when layers of fatty material (atheroma) build up inside the coronary arteries, reducing blood flow to the heart muscle. This can cause a heart attack if the heart muscle does not receive enough oxygen.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n18">Treatments include: <strong>stents</strong> (wire mesh tubes inserted to hold the artery open), <strong>statins</strong> (drugs that reduce blood cholesterol levels and slow the build-up of fatty deposits), and in severe cases, <strong>heart transplants</strong> or <strong>artificial hearts</strong> (mechanical devices that keep blood flowing while waiting for a transplant).</p>
  </div></div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n19" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. List the four treatments for coronary heart disease and explain what stents and statins actually do.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Coronary heart disease is caused by fatty deposits narrowing the coronary arteries. Treatments include stents (hold arteries open), statins (lower cholesterol), heart transplants and artificial hearts.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n22">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n23">The heart pumps blood through a double circulatory system; the left ventricle has a thicker wall to generate higher pressure.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n24">Arteries have thick walls (high pressure), veins have valves (low pressure), capillaries are one cell thick (for exchange).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">Coronary heart disease is treated with stents, statins, or heart transplants/artificial hearts.</li>
</ul>
