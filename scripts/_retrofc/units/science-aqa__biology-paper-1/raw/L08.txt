--- DESCRIPTION ---
The equations and limiting factors for photosynthesis, plus aerobic and anaerobic respiration.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Photosynthesis</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="An endothermic reaction in which plants use light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.">Photosynthesis</dfn> is an endothermic reaction that takes place in the chloroplasts of plant cells. It uses light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen. The word equation is:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">$$\text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water} \xrightarrow{\text{light}} \text{glucose} + \text{oxygen}$$</p>

<p data-narration-id="n4">The balanced symbol equation is:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n5">$$6CO_2 + 6H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{light}} C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2$$</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n6" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write the balanced equation for photosynthesis including all 6CO₂, 6H₂O, C₆H₁₂O₆ and 6O₂ coefficients.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Photosynthesis is an endothermic reaction: \(6CO_2 + 6H_2O \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2\). It takes place in the chloroplasts and requires light energy absorbed by chlorophyll.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n7">The rate of photosynthesis is affected by three <dfn class="term" data-def="A factor that, when in short supply, restricts the rate of a reaction. For photosynthesis: light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, or temperature.">limiting factors</dfn>:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Light intensity</strong> — as light intensity increases, the rate of photosynthesis increases until another factor becomes limiting.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Carbon dioxide concentration</strong> — increasing \(CO_2\) increases the rate until another factor limits it.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Temperature</strong> — higher temperatures increase the rate of enzyme-controlled reactions up to an optimum, beyond which enzymes denature and the rate drops sharply.</li>
</ul>

<div class="higher-only">
<p data-narration-id="n8b">These limiting factors interact, and any one of them may be the factor that limits photosynthesis at a given time. Light intensity follows an inverse square law — it is inversely proportional to the square of the distance from the light source: \(\text{light intensity} \propto \frac{1}{d^2}\). You may need to explain graphs of photosynthesis rate involving two or three limiting factors simultaneously.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n12">Aerobic Respiration</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n13"><dfn class="term" data-def="A continuous chemical reaction in living cells that releases energy by breaking down glucose with oxygen. It occurs in the mitochondria.">Aerobic respiration</dfn> is an exothermic reaction that takes place continuously in all living cells. It occurs in the mitochondria and requires oxygen. The word equation is:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n14">$$\text{glucose} + \text{oxygen} \rightarrow \text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water}$$</p>

<p data-narration-id="n15">The balanced symbol equation is:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n16">$$C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 \rightarrow 6CO_2 + 6H_2O$$</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Name the organelle where aerobic respiration occurs and list 3 life processes that use the energy released.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Aerobic respiration: \(C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 \rightarrow 6CO_2 + 6H_2O\). It is exothermic, occurs in mitochondria, and releases energy for life processes including muscle contraction, maintaining body temperature and building molecules.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Anaerobic Respiration</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n19"><dfn class="term" data-def="Respiration without oxygen. In animals it produces lactic acid; in plants and yeast it produces ethanol and carbon dioxide (fermentation).">Anaerobic respiration</dfn> occurs when oxygen is not available. It releases much less energy per glucose molecule than aerobic respiration because glucose is only partially broken down.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n20">In <strong>animals</strong>: \(\text{glucose} \rightarrow \text{lactic acid}\)</p>

<p data-narration-id="n21">In <strong>plants and yeast</strong> (fermentation): \(\text{glucose} \rightarrow \text{ethanol} + \text{carbon dioxide}\)</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Oxygen Debt and Exercise</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n22">During intense exercise, the muscles cannot get enough oxygen for aerobic respiration, so anaerobic respiration occurs alongside it. This produces <dfn class="term" data-def="A toxic waste product of anaerobic respiration in animals. It builds up in the muscles during vigorous exercise and causes fatigue.">lactic acid</dfn>, which builds up in the muscles and causes fatigue and cramp. After exercise, you continue to breathe heavily to repay the <dfn class="term" data-def="The amount of extra oxygen the body needs after exercise to break down the lactic acid that has accumulated during anaerobic respiration.">oxygen debt</dfn> — the extra oxygen needed to break down the accumulated lactic acid.</p>
    <div class="higher-only">
    <p data-narration-id="n22b">Blood flowing through the muscles transports the lactic acid to the liver where it is converted back into glucose. Oxygen debt is the amount of extra oxygen the body needs after exercise to react with the accumulated lactic acid and remove it from the cells.</p>
    </div>
  </div></div>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n23">During exercise, the body responds to the increased demand for energy: heart rate increases, breathing rate and depth increase, and blood is redirected to the muscles. These changes supply more oxygen and glucose for aerobic respiration and remove more carbon dioxide.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n25">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n26">Photosynthesis is endothermic (in chloroplasts): \(6CO_2 + 6H_2O \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2\). Limited by light, \(CO_2\) and temperature.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n27">Aerobic respiration is exothermic (in mitochondria): \(C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 \rightarrow 6CO_2 + 6H_2O\).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n28">Anaerobic respiration releases less energy; in animals it produces lactic acid, in yeast it produces ethanol + \(CO_2\).</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n24">Make sure you can write both the word and symbol equations for photosynthesis and respiration. A common mistake is confusing which is endothermic and which is exothermic. Remember: photosynthesis takes in energy (endothermic), respiration releases energy (exothermic). For limiting factors questions, always explain why the graph plateaus.</p>
