--- DESCRIPTION ---
Risk factors for non-communicable diseases, cancer, and how plant tissues are organised.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Health and Disease</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">Health is a state of physical and mental wellbeing. Disease is a condition where part of the body does not function properly. Diseases can be <dfn class="term" data-def="A disease caused by a pathogen (bacteria, virus, fungus or protist) that can be spread from person to person.">communicable</dfn> (caused by pathogens and spread between people) or <dfn class="term" data-def="A disease that cannot be passed from person to person, often caused by lifestyle factors, such as cancer, heart disease and type 2 diabetes.">non-communicable</dfn> (not caused by pathogens and cannot be passed on). Sometimes diseases interact — for example, immune system conditions can make a person more susceptible to communicable diseases.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and name the four major NCDs plus four risk factors that increase your chances of developing them.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) are not caused by pathogens and cannot be spread. Major NCDs include cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, lung disease and cancer. Risk factors include smoking, obesity, alcohol and lack of exercise.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Risk Factors for Non-Communicable Diseases</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n5">A <dfn class="term" data-def="Something that increases the likelihood of developing a disease. Risk factors may be aspects of a person’s lifestyle or substances in their body or environment.">risk factor</dfn> is something that increases the probability of developing a disease. Key risk factors include:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Smoking</strong> — linked to lung cancer, cardiovascular disease and lung disease.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Obesity</strong> — a risk factor for type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Alcohol</strong> — linked to liver disease and some cancers.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Lack of exercise</strong> — increases the risk of cardiovascular disease and obesity.</li>
</ul>

<p data-narration-id="n10">It is important to understand the difference between a <strong>risk factor</strong> and a <strong>cause</strong>. Risk factors are correlated with disease — they make disease more likely — but correlation does not always prove causation.</p>

  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Cancer</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11"><dfn class="term" data-def="A disease caused by uncontrolled cell division, resulting in a mass of abnormal cells called a tumour.">Cancer</dfn> is caused by changes in cells that lead to uncontrolled growth and division. <strong>Benign tumours</strong> are growths of abnormal cells contained in one area — they do not invade other tissues. <strong>Malignant tumours</strong> invade neighbouring tissues and can spread to other parts of the body through the blood, forming secondary tumours — this is known as <dfn class="term" data-def="The spread of cancer cells from the primary tumour to other parts of the body, forming secondary tumours.">metastasis</dfn>.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n12">Risk factors for cancer include genetic factors, ionising radiation (e.g. UV light), certain viruses (e.g. HPV), and lifestyle factors such as smoking and obesity.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Plant Tissues and Organ Systems</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n14">Plants are also organised into tissues and organs. The main plant tissues include:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Epidermal tissue</strong> — covers the surface of the plant for protection.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Palisade mesophyll</strong> — packed with chloroplasts and located near the top of the leaf where it receives the most light for photosynthesis.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Spongy mesophyll</strong> — has air spaces that allow gases (\(CO_2\) and \(O_2\)) to diffuse easily.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n18"><strong>Xylem</strong> — <dfn class="term" data-def="Plant tissue made of hollow, dead cells that transports water and dissolved mineral ions upwards from the roots to the leaves.">xylem</dfn> tissue transports water and mineral ions from the roots to the leaves. It is made of hollow, dead cells with lignified walls.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n19"><strong>Phloem</strong> — <dfn class="term" data-def="Plant tissue that transports dissolved sugars (from photosynthesis) from the leaves to the rest of the plant. This process is called translocation.">phloem</dfn> tissue transports dissolved sugars from the leaves to the rest of the plant in a process called translocation.</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n20" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and explain what xylem and phloem transport, which direction xylem moves substances, and whether their cells are living or dead.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Xylem transports water and minerals upwards (transpiration stream). Phloem transports dissolved sugars to where they are needed (translocation). Xylem cells are dead and hollow; phloem cells are living.</p>
</div>

  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n21"><dfn class="term" data-def="The loss of water vapour from the surface of a plant, mainly through the stomata of leaves. This creates a continuous pull of water up through the xylem.">Transpiration</dfn> is the evaporation and diffusion of water from the surface of leaves, mainly through the stomata. This creates a continuous transpiration stream that pulls water up through the xylem from the roots. The rate of transpiration is affected by temperature, humidity, air movement and light intensity.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n23">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n24">Non-communicable diseases (cancer, CHD, type 2 diabetes) are linked to risk factors like smoking, obesity and alcohol — but risk factors don’t always prove causation.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">Malignant tumours invade other tissues and can metastasise; benign tumours stay in one place.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n26">Plants have specialised tissues: xylem (water transport), phloem (sugar transport), palisade mesophyll (photosynthesis).</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n22">When asked about risk factors, always use the word “increases the risk of” rather than “causes” — unless the question specifically asks about causation. Also be prepared to compare benign and malignant tumours in terms of invasion and metastasis.</p>
