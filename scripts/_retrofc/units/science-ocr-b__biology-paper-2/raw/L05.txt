--- DESCRIPTION ---
Detecting stimuli, reflexes, and how hormones and nerves coordinate the body.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Nervous System</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Your body constantly receives information from the world around you — light, temperature, sound, pain. The <dfn class="term" data-def="The organ system consisting of the brain, spinal cord and nerves that detects stimuli and coordinates rapid responses.">nervous system</dfn> processes this information and produces fast, coordinated responses. It has two main divisions: the <strong>central nervous system (CNS)</strong> — the brain and spinal cord — and the <strong>peripheral nervous system</strong>, the network of nerves connecting the CNS to every part of the body.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">The pathway of a nervous response follows a fixed sequence: a <strong>stimulus</strong> is detected by a <dfn class="term" data-def="Specialised cells that detect stimuli such as light, sound, temperature, pressure or chemicals.">sensory receptor</dfn>, which sends a signal along a <strong>sensory neurone</strong> to the CNS. A <strong>relay neurone</strong> within the CNS passes the signal to a <strong>motor neurone</strong>, which carries it to an <dfn class="term" data-def="An organ that carries out a response, such as a muscle or a gland.">effector</dfn> — a muscle that contracts or a gland that secretes. The result is a coordinated response.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this and write the full nervous pathway in order from stimulus to response, naming each stage. This sequence appears in almost every biology paper.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The nervous pathway: stimulus &rarr; sensory receptor &rarr; sensory neurone &rarr; relay neurone (CNS) &rarr; motor neurone &rarr; effector &rarr; response. The CNS processes information and coordinates the appropriate reaction.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Neurones and Synapses</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">There are three types of <dfn class="term" data-def="A nerve cell that transmits electrical impulses. Types include sensory, relay and motor neurones.">neurone</dfn>. <strong>Sensory neurones</strong> carry signals from receptors to the CNS; <strong>relay neurones</strong> connect sensory and motor neurones within the CNS; <strong>motor neurones</strong> carry signals from the CNS to effectors. All neurones have a <strong>cell body</strong>, <strong>dendrites</strong> (short branches that receive signals), and a long <strong>axon</strong> that transmits the electrical impulse. Many axons are wrapped in a <dfn class="term" data-def="A fatty sheath around the axon of a neurone that insulates it and speeds up the transmission of electrical impulses.">myelin sheath</dfn>, which speeds up signal transmission.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Where two neurones meet there is a tiny gap called a <dfn class="term" data-def="The junction between two neurones where a chemical neurotransmitter is released to carry the signal across the gap.">synapse</dfn>. An electrical impulse cannot cross this gap directly. Instead, when the impulse reaches the end of the first neurone, chemical <strong>neurotransmitters</strong> are released from vesicles into the synaptic cleft. They diffuse across and bind to specific receptor molecules on the next neurone, triggering a new electrical impulse. Synapses ensure signals travel in one direction only and allow complex coordination across many pathways.</p>

    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n8">A <dfn class="term" data-def="The neural pathway that produces a rapid, automatic response without conscious thought, bypassing the brain.">reflex arc</dfn> is a shorter version of the nervous pathway that produces automatic, protective responses. The signal travels through the spinal cord rather than all the way to the brain, which makes the response much faster. The pathway is: receptor &rarr; sensory neurone &rarr; relay neurone (in spinal cord) &rarr; motor neurone &rarr; effector.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9">Consider touching a sharp object. Pain receptors in the skin detect the stimulus and send a signal along a sensory neurone to the spinal cord. A relay neurone immediately connects to a motor neurone, which stimulates the arm muscle to contract and pull the hand away &mdash; all before the brain has consciously registered pain. The brain receives the information afterwards, which is why you feel the sensation a fraction of a second after the withdrawal.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n10">Reflexes are important because they have fewer synapses to cross and a shorter pathway, making them significantly faster than voluntary responses. Other reflexes include the pupil reflex (the pupil constricts in bright light to protect the retina) and the knee-jerk reflex.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n11" data-revision-tip="Explain to someone why a reflex is faster than a voluntary action. Your answer should mention the spinal cord, brain and number of synapses.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Reflex arcs bypass the brain &mdash; the signal goes through the spinal cord only. This reduces the distance the signal must travel and the number of synapses to cross, making the response faster and protecting the body from harm before conscious awareness.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n12">The Endocrine System</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The network of glands that release hormones directly into the blood to coordinate body functions.">endocrine system</dfn> uses chemical messengers called <dfn class="term" data-def="Chemical substances produced by endocrine glands, transported in the blood to target organs where they produce a specific effect.">hormones</dfn> to coordinate and control body functions. Unlike the nervous system, which produces fast, short-lived, localised responses, the endocrine system produces <strong>slower, longer-lasting and more widespread</strong> responses. Hormones travel in the blood to target organs; only cells with the correct receptor proteins will respond.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Key endocrine glands include the <strong>pituitary gland</strong> (the &ldquo;master gland&rdquo; that controls other glands), the <strong>thyroid gland</strong> (controls metabolic rate), the <strong>adrenal glands</strong> (produce adrenaline), and the <strong>pancreas</strong> (produces insulin and glucagon to control blood glucose). The <strong>ovaries</strong> and <strong>testes</strong> produce the sex hormones that control reproduction and secondary sexual characteristics.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Comparing the Nervous and Endocrine Systems</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n15">Both systems coordinate the body, but they work in fundamentally different ways. The nervous system uses <strong>electrical impulses</strong> along neurones &mdash; responses are <strong>fast</strong> (fractions of a second), <strong>short-lived</strong> and <strong>precise</strong> (a specific muscle contracts). The endocrine system uses <strong>hormones in the blood</strong> &mdash; responses are <strong>slower</strong> (seconds to hours), <strong>longer-lasting</strong> and <strong>widespread</strong> (affecting many tissues at once).</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n16">A useful way to remember this: nervous = fast, precise, short (like a phone call); endocrine = slow, widespread, sustained (like a broadcast message). Many physiological processes are coordinated by <em>both</em> systems working together &mdash; for example, the &ldquo;fight or flight&rdquo; response involves both nerve impulses and the release of adrenaline from the adrenal glands.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Reaction Time &mdash; Investigating the Nervous System (IaS1)</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n17">The ruler drop test is a classic investigation of reaction time. One person holds a ruler vertically while their partner places thumb and finger at the 0&nbsp;cm mark. When the ruler is released without warning, the partner catches it as quickly as possible. A shorter distance fallen means a faster reaction time.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n18">To design a reliable investigation, you must consider: what variable you are testing (e.g. does caffeine improve reaction time?), what you will keep constant (same ruler, same person catching, same hand position), how to reduce random error (repeat at least five times and calculate a mean, discard anomalous results). When writing a hypothesis, you should provide a <strong>scientific justification</strong> &mdash; for example, &ldquo;caffeine is predicted to decrease reaction time because it is a stimulant that increases neurotransmitter activity at synapses.&rdquo; A <strong>prediction</strong> states the expected outcome for a specific measurement, while a <strong>hypothesis</strong> explains <em>why</em> you expect it. These are not the same thing.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n20">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n21">The nervous system carries electrical signals along neurones; the pathway stimulus &rarr; receptor &rarr; sensory neurone &rarr; relay neurone &rarr; motor neurone &rarr; effector produces coordinated responses.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n22">Reflex arcs bypass the brain, routing signals through the spinal cord only &mdash; this makes reflexes faster and protects the body from danger before conscious awareness.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n23">The endocrine system uses hormones in the blood; responses are slower and longer-lasting than nervous responses but affect a wider range of tissues simultaneously.</li>
</ul>

For compare questions on the nervous and endocrine systems, give a feature for each side (e.g. speed, duration, messenger type) rather than describing just one system.</p>
