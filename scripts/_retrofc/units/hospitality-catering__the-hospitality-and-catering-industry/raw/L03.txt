<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Working in Hospitality & Catering</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The hospitality and catering industry offers a huge variety of job roles, from front of house positions that interact directly with customers to behind-the-scenes kitchen and housekeeping roles. The WJEC specification groups these into four areas: <strong>front of house</strong>, <strong>housekeeping</strong>, the <strong>kitchen brigade</strong>, and <strong>management</strong>. You also need to know the personal attributes, qualifications, and experience employers look for.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Front of House Roles</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4"><dfn class="term" data-def="The area of a hospitality business that customers see and interact with, including reception, dining room, and bar.">Front of house</dfn> refers to the parts of a hospitality business where staff interact directly with customers. Key front of house roles include:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n5"><strong>Front of house manager</strong> — oversees the entire front of house operation, ensures high standards of service, and handles customer complaints.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Head waiter</strong> — supervises waiting staff, coordinates table service, and ensures the dining room runs smoothly.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Waiting staff</strong> — take orders, serve food and drinks, and clear tables.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Maître d’hôtel</strong> — the chief host in a formal dining room, responsible for greeting guests and managing reservations.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Concierge</strong> — assists hotel guests with bookings, recommendations, and special requests.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Receptionist</strong> — handles check-in and check-out, takes bookings, and provides information to guests.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Valets</strong> — provide personal service to guests, such as parking cars or handling luggage.</li>
</ul>
<h2 data-narration-id="n12">Housekeeping Roles</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">Housekeeping staff ensure the accommodation and public areas of a hospitality establishment are clean, safe, and well maintained. Key roles include:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Chambermaid</strong> — cleans and prepares guest rooms, changes bedding, and restocks supplies.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Cleaner</strong> — maintains cleanliness in public areas, corridors, and facilities.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Maintenance staff</strong> — repairs and maintains the building, fixtures, and equipment.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Caretaker</strong> — oversees the general upkeep of the premises.</li>
</ul>
<h2 data-narration-id="n18">The Kitchen Brigade</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19">The <dfn class="term" data-def="A hierarchical system of kitchen roles developed by Auguste Escoffier, where each member has a specific responsibility.">kitchen brigade</dfn> is a structured hierarchy of chefs and kitchen workers. Each person has a specific role, ensuring the kitchen runs efficiently. From the most senior to the most junior, the roles are:</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n20" data-revision-tip="From memory, list the eight roles in the kitchen brigade from top to bottom.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The kitchen brigade hierarchy (top to bottom): <strong>Executive Chef</strong> → <strong>Sous Chef</strong> → <strong>Chef de Partie</strong> → <strong>Commis Chef</strong> → <strong>Pastry Chef</strong> → <strong>Kitchen Assistant</strong> → <strong>Apprentice</strong> → <strong>Kitchen Porter / Plongeur</strong>.</p>
</div>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Executive Chef</strong> — the most senior chef, responsible for the entire kitchen operation, menu planning, budgets, and staff management.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Sous Chef</strong> — the second-in-command, deputises for the executive chef and supervises daily cooking.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Chef de Partie</strong> — a section chef responsible for a particular area of the kitchen (e.g. sauces, fish, meat).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Commis Chef</strong> — a junior chef who works under a chef de partie, learning the skills of a specific section.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n25"><strong>Pastry Chef</strong> — specialises in desserts, breads, and pastries.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n26"><strong>Kitchen Assistant</strong> — assists with food preparation tasks such as peeling, chopping, and weighing ingredients.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n27"><strong>Apprentice</strong> — a trainee learning kitchen skills while working towards a qualification.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n28"><strong>Kitchen Porter / <dfn class="term" data-def="A kitchen worker responsible for washing dishes, pots, and pans, and keeping the kitchen clean.">Plongeur</dfn></strong> — responsible for washing up, keeping the kitchen clean, and general cleaning tasks.</li>
</ul>

<p data-narration-id="n29">Management roles coordinate different departments within a hospitality business. <strong>Food and beverage manager</strong> — oversees the restaurant, bar, and room service operations. <strong>Housekeeping manager</strong> — manages the cleaning and maintenance team. <strong>Marketing manager</strong> — promotes the business through advertising, social media, and partnerships to attract customers.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n30">Understanding the kitchen brigade is important because it shows how a professional kitchen is organised for maximum efficiency. Each role has a clear set of responsibilities, and every member of the team knows who they report to. In a busy restaurant serving hundreds of covers per evening, this structure prevents confusion and ensures dishes are prepared consistently to the same standard. Without the brigade system, communication would break down, orders would be missed, and food quality would suffer. Even smaller kitchens use a simplified version of this hierarchy, with the head chef taking on multiple senior roles.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n31">Personal Attributes Employers Look For</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n32">The hospitality industry requires staff to have specific personal qualities. Employers look for people who are:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n33"><strong>Organised</strong> and <strong>punctual</strong> — shifts must start on time, and tasks must be completed efficiently.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n34"><strong>Hardworking</strong> and <strong>flexible</strong> — the industry involves long hours, weekend work, and adapting to busy periods.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n35"><strong>Hygienic</strong> — food safety and cleanliness are essential, especially in kitchen and housekeeping roles.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n36"><strong>Friendly, pleasant, and calm</strong> — staff must remain professional even when dealing with difficult customers.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n37"><strong>Good communicator</strong> and <strong>team player</strong> — hospitality relies on teamwork and clear communication between departments.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n38"><strong>Good people skills</strong> and a <strong>willingness to learn and develop</strong> — customer-facing roles require empathy and a desire to improve.</li>
</ul>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n39" data-revision-tip="Pick three hospitality job roles and write down two personal attributes needed for each, without looking.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The WJEC spec lists 12 personal attributes: organised, hardworking, punctual, hygienic, pleasant, calm, friendly, good communicator, team player, good people skills, willingness to learn, and flexible.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n40">Employers also look for relevant qualifications and experience. <strong>Apprenticeships</strong> allow people to train on the job while earning a wage and gaining a qualification. <strong>Experience in the sector</strong> — such as a part-time job or summer employment — shows an employer that the candidate understands the industry. <strong>School, college, or university qualifications</strong> relevant to hospitality and catering (including this WJEC course) demonstrate knowledge and commitment.</p>
</div></div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n42">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n43">Job roles in hospitality and catering include front of house (managers, waiters, concierge, receptionists), housekeeping (chambermaids, cleaners, maintenance), the kitchen brigade (executive chef down to kitchen porter), and management.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n44">The kitchen brigade is a hierarchy with specific responsibilities at each level, from menu planning (executive chef) to washing up (kitchen porter / plongeur).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n45">Employers look for 12 key personal attributes, plus relevant qualifications and experience such as apprenticeships and part-time work.</li></ul>
