<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Front of House & Back of House</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Every hospitality and catering establishment is divided into two main areas: <dfn class="term" data-def="The areas of a hospitality venue that customers see and interact with, such as reception, the dining area, and the bar.">front of house</dfn> and <dfn class="term" data-def="The areas of a hospitality venue that customers do not usually see, including the kitchen, storage rooms, and staff areas.">back of house</dfn>. Front of house covers everything the customer experiences directly — reception desks, dining rooms, counter service areas, and bars. Back of house is where the food is prepared, stored, and managed behind the scenes. Both areas must work together smoothly to deliver a positive customer experience.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Sketch a simple restaurant floor plan from memory and label every front-of-house and back-of-house area.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p><strong>Front of house</strong> includes reception, seating areas, counter service, and the bar. <strong>Back of house</strong> includes the kitchen, storage, wash areas, and staff changing rooms.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Workflow of the Front of House</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">The front of house workflow ensures customers are greeted, seated, served, and billed efficiently. In a restaurant, this typically follows a clear sequence: the customer arrives at reception, is shown to their table, receives menus, places an order, is served food and drinks, and finally pays the bill. Each stage must run smoothly to avoid delays and complaints.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Different front of house areas serve different purposes. The <strong>reception</strong> is the first point of contact — it sets the tone for the whole visit. The <strong>seating area</strong> is where customers eat, and it must be clean, well-laid, and comfortable. <strong>Counter service</strong> areas are used in cafeterias and fast food outlets where customers collect food themselves. The <strong>bar</strong> serves drinks and may also be a waiting area before meals.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n7">Workflow of the Catering Kitchen</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n8">The kitchen workflow follows a logical sequence designed to maintain food safety and efficiency. The WJEC specification identifies seven key areas within a catering kitchen:</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>The Seven Kitchen Workflow Areas</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>1. Delivery area</strong> — where raw ingredients and supplies arrive. Deliveries are checked against order forms for quality and quantity. <strong>2. Staffing area</strong> — where staff change into uniforms and store personal belongings. <strong>3. Wash area</strong> — for washing hands and sanitising before entering the kitchen. <strong>4. Storage area</strong> — dry stores, fridges, and freezers, each kept at correct temperatures. <strong>5. Preparation and cooking area</strong> — where food is prepared and cooked, the largest and busiest part of the kitchen. <strong>6. Serving area (the pass)</strong> — where finished dishes are checked and handed to waiting staff. <strong>7. Washing/cleaning area</strong> — where used equipment and crockery are cleaned and sanitised.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Equipment and Materials</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">A professional catering kitchen uses a wide range of equipment. The specification divides this into three categories:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Large equipment</strong> includes conventional ovens, glass chillers, floor-standing food mixers, deep fat fryers, hot water urns, walk-in fridge-freezers, standing <dfn class="term" data-def="A heated container used to keep cooked food warm by placing dishes over hot water.">bain marie</dfn> units, steamers, pass-through dishwashers, glass washers, and hot plates. These are expensive to buy and maintain, so they must be used correctly and kept in good working order.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Small equipment</strong> covers utensils such as knives, chopping boards (colour-coded for food safety), pans, whisks, thermometers, and measuring jugs. <strong>Materials for cleaning</strong> include sanitisers, cloths, mops, and anti-bacterial sprays. A <strong>first aid kit</strong> and safety materials (fire blankets, fire extinguishers) must also be available at all times.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Name the three categories of kitchen equipment and list two specific items for each from memory.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Kitchen equipment falls into three groups: <strong>large equipment</strong> (ovens, fryers, walk-in fridges), <strong>small equipment</strong> (utensils, thermometers), and <strong>cleaning/safety materials</strong> (sanitisers, first aid kit, fire blankets).</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n15">Documentation and Administration</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n16">Running a catering kitchen requires careful paperwork. The specification identifies several key documents:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Stock controlling systems</strong> track what ingredients are in storage and when they need reordering. <strong>Ordering</strong> documents are sent to suppliers to request new stock. <strong>Delivery notes</strong> arrive with each delivery and are checked against the order to confirm the correct items and quantities have been received. <strong>Invoices</strong> are bills from suppliers that must be checked and paid. <strong>Food safety documentation</strong> includes temperature logs, cleaning schedules, and allergen records. <strong>Health and safety documentation</strong> covers risk assessments, accident forms, and COSHH records.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Why Documentation Matters</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n18">Accurate documentation protects the business in several ways. If a customer becomes ill, food safety records can show that correct procedures were followed. Stock control prevents waste and reduces costs. Delivery notes and invoices ensure the business only pays for what it actually received. During an Environmental Health Officer inspection, well-kept records demonstrate legal compliance and can help avoid prosecution or closure.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n19">Dress Code</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Dress codes differ between front and back of house. <strong>Front of house staff</strong> typically wear smart, clean uniforms that reflect the establishment’s brand — this might be a shirt and tie in a fine-dining restaurant or a branded polo shirt in a casual café. Hair should be neat and jewellery minimal. The aim is to look professional and create a positive first impression.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Back of house staff</strong> wear protective clothing for hygiene and safety. This includes chef’s whites (jacket, trousers, and apron), a chef’s hat or hairnet to prevent hair falling into food, non-slip safety shoes, and blue plasters if they have cuts (blue so they are visible if they fall into food). Jewellery and nail varnish are not allowed in the kitchen because they could contaminate food.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n23">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n24">Front of house (reception, dining, counter, bar) is what the customer sees; back of house (kitchen, storage, wash areas) is where food is prepared and managed behind the scenes.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n25">The kitchen workflow follows a logical sequence from delivery through to the washing/cleaning area, and equipment is grouped into large, small, and cleaning/safety categories.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n26">Accurate documentation (stock control, delivery notes, invoices, food safety and health &amp; safety records) is essential for efficiency, legal compliance, and customer safety.</li></ul>
