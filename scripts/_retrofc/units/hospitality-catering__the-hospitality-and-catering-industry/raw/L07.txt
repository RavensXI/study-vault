--- DESCRIPTION ---
How hospitality venues meet customer needs, rights, dietary requirements, and changing expectations.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Understanding Customer Requirements</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Hospitality and catering businesses exist to serve customers. To succeed, they must understand what their customers need and adapt their provision accordingly. The WJEC specification identifies several categories of customer requirement: <strong>customer needs</strong>, <strong>customer rights and inclusion</strong>, <strong>lifestyle and dietary needs</strong>, <strong>customer expectations</strong>, and <strong>customer demographics</strong>.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Customer Needs</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">At the most basic level, customers need three things from hospitality and catering provision: <strong>catering</strong> (food and drink of an appropriate quality), <strong>equipment</strong> (cutlery, crockery, seating, and facilities), and <strong>accommodation</strong> (a clean, comfortable room if staying overnight). A customer visiting a restaurant expects clean cutlery, a comfortable chair, and well-prepared food. A hotel guest expects a clean room with fresh bedding, functioning heating, and access to meals.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Cover this and name the three basic customer needs in hospitality. Give one example of how a business meets each.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The three basic customer needs in hospitality and catering are: <strong>catering</strong> (food and drink), <strong>equipment</strong> (cutlery, furniture, facilities), and <strong>accommodation</strong> (rooms in residential establishments).</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Customer Rights and Inclusion</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Every customer has the right to be treated fairly regardless of their background. Hospitality businesses must meet the requirements of <dfn class="term" data-def="Treating all people fairly and giving everyone equal access to services, regardless of characteristics such as age, disability, race, or gender.">equality</dfn> legislation, ensuring no one is discriminated against. This includes making reasonable adjustments for customers with <strong>disabilities</strong> — for example, providing wheelchair ramps, accessible toilets, menus in Braille or large print, and hearing loops at reception desks.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8"><dfn class="term" data-def="Ensuring that all customers can access and enjoy hospitality services, including those with disabilities, dietary needs, or other specific requirements.">Inclusion</dfn> means ensuring all customers can access and enjoy the services offered. A restaurant that only has stairs to its entrance excludes wheelchair users. A hotel that offers no allergen information on its menu puts allergic customers at risk. Businesses that fail to be inclusive not only break the law but also lose potential customers and damage their reputation.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Lifestyle and Dietary Needs</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">Modern customers have a wide range of lifestyle and dietary needs that hospitality providers must cater for. <strong>Lifestyle choices</strong> include vegetarianism, veganism, and religious dietary requirements (such as halal or kosher food). <strong>Nutritional needs</strong> vary depending on age, health conditions, and activity levels — a child needs different portion sizes and nutrients compared to an elderly adult.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Dietary needs</strong> include medical conditions that restrict what a person can eat. Customers with coeliac disease need gluten-free options. Those with lactose intolerance cannot have dairy products. Some customers are diabetic and need meals with controlled sugar content. Hospitality businesses must be prepared to adapt their menus and communicate clearly about what each dish contains.</p>

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n12">Restaurants now commonly label dishes with allergen symbols (such as “GF” for gluten-free or “V” for vegan). Many offer separate allergen menus on request. Hotels provide breakfast buffets with clearly labelled options including dairy-free milk, gluten-free bread, and fresh fruit. Airlines offer special meals (halal, kosher, vegetarian, vegan) that must be pre-ordered. School canteens display nutritional information and allergen charts. Failure to meet dietary needs can lead to serious illness — or even death in the case of severe allergies.</p>
</div></div>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Time available</strong> is another factor. A customer on a lunch break may only have 30 minutes, so they need quick service. A couple celebrating an anniversary may want a leisurely three-course meal. Businesses must offer options that suit different time constraints — from grab-and-go sandwiches to full tasting menus.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Customer Expectations</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15">Customer expectations go beyond basic needs. The specification identifies several factors that shape what customers expect:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Service</strong> — customers expect polite, efficient, and knowledgeable staff. A fine-dining restaurant is expected to have more formal, attentive service than a fast food outlet. <strong>Value for money</strong> — customers judge whether the price they pay matches the quality they receive. A budget hotel is not expected to have a spa, but it is expected to be clean. <strong>Trends</strong> — customer tastes change over time; plant-based menus, street food concepts, and sustainability are all current trends that businesses must respond to.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="List three external factors that force hospitality businesses to adapt, without looking at your notes.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Customer expectations are shaped by: <strong>service quality</strong>, <strong>value for money</strong>, <strong>current trends</strong>, <strong>awareness of competition</strong>, <strong>media influence</strong>, <strong>environmental concerns</strong>, and <strong>seasonality</strong>.</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n18"><strong>Competition</strong> from other providers keeps standards high — if one restaurant offers free Wi-Fi and another does not, customers may choose the one that does. <strong>Media influence</strong> plays a huge role: a positive review on TripAdvisor or a feature on a TV food programme can boost bookings, while a negative social media post can cause lasting damage. <strong>Environmental concerns</strong> mean customers increasingly expect businesses to reduce food waste, use sustainable packaging, and source ingredients locally. <strong>Seasonality</strong> affects both what customers want (hot food in winter, salads in summer) and how busy venues are (coastal hotels peak in summer).</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n19">Customer Demographics</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n20"><dfn class="term" data-def="The statistical characteristics of a population, such as age, location, income level, and accessibility needs.">Demographics</dfn> are the characteristics of a customer group. The specification identifies four key demographic factors:</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>The Four Demographic Factors</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Age</strong> — a children’s menu with smaller portions and simpler flavours differs from an adult tasting menu. Elderly customers may need softer food or larger print on menus. <strong>Location</strong> — a business in a tourist area will attract different customers to one in a residential suburb. <strong>Accessibility</strong> — some customers need step-free access, hearing loops, or menus in alternative formats. <strong>Money available</strong> — a customer with a limited budget will choose differently from one with a large disposable income. Businesses must understand their target demographic to offer appropriate provision.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n23">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n24">Customers have basic needs (catering, equipment, accommodation) plus rights to equality and inclusion that businesses must legally uphold.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n25">Lifestyle and dietary needs (vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, halal, kosher) require businesses to adapt their menus and communicate allergen information clearly.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n26">Customer expectations are shaped by service quality, value for money, trends, competition, media, environmental concerns, and seasonality — and demographics (age, location, accessibility, budget) determine who the customers are.</li></ul>
