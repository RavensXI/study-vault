<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Types of Food Service</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Hospitality and catering providers use different styles of food service depending on their type of establishment, target customers, and price point. The WJEC specification requires you to know three main categories of food service: <dfn class="term" data-def="A style of food service where customers are seated at a table and food is brought to them by waiting staff.">table service</dfn>, <dfn class="term" data-def="A style of food service where customers collect their food from a serving area or counter.">counter service</dfn>, and <dfn class="term" data-def="A style of food service delivered directly to an individual, such as room service, home delivery, or vending.">personal service</dfn>.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Without looking, name the five types of table service and one distinguishing feature of each.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The three main categories of food service are <strong>table service</strong> (food brought to seated customers), <strong>counter service</strong> (customers collect food from a serving area), and <strong>personal service</strong> (food delivered directly to an individual, e.g. room service or home delivery).</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Table Service</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Table service is the most formal category. Customers sit at a table and waiting staff bring food to them. There are five types of table service you need to know:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Plate service</strong> — the most common type. Food is plated in the kitchen and carried to the table by waiting staff. Used in most restaurants.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Family-style service</strong> — dishes are placed in the centre of the table and diners help themselves. Common in informal restaurants and family gatherings.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Silver service</strong> — food is presented on silver platters and served to each guest by waiting staff using a spoon and fork. A highly skilled and formal method.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Guéridon service</strong> — food is prepared, carved, or flambéed at the table from a trolley. It creates a theatrical dining experience. Often seen in high-end restaurants.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Banquet service</strong> — a pre-set menu served to a large number of guests at a formal event, such as a wedding or awards ceremony. All courses are timed carefully.</li>
</ul>

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Plate service</strong> requires less skill from waiting staff and is cost-effective, making it the most widely used. <strong>Silver service</strong> and <strong>Guéridon service</strong> require highly trained staff and are expensive to run, but they create an exclusive dining experience that justifies premium prices. <strong>Family-style</strong> is informal and suits casual dining, while <strong>banquet service</strong> is efficient for large events because the same meal is served to everyone at the same time.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n12">Counter Service</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">Counter service means customers collect their food from a serving point rather than being served at a table. It is faster and requires fewer staff than table service. The three types are:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Cafeteria service</strong> — customers move along a counter selecting food items from a display. Common in workplace canteens and schools.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Buffet service</strong> — food is laid out on tables or counters, and customers serve themselves. Used at events, hotels, and all-you-can-eat restaurants.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Fast food service</strong> — customers order at a counter (or drive-through) and food is prepared and served quickly. Examples include McDonald’s and KFC.</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n17">Counter service is popular because it reduces labour costs — fewer waiting staff are needed since customers serve themselves or collect their food. It also increases speed of service, which suits busy environments like school lunch halls and motorway service stations. However, the dining experience is less personal, and customers may feel rushed. Buffet service in particular carries food safety risks because food is left out for extended periods, so temperature controls and hygiene procedures are critical.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Personal Service</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19">Personal service delivers food directly to an individual, wherever they are. The four types are:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Tray or trolley service</strong> — food is delivered on a tray or trolley, typically in hospitals, care homes, or on aircraft.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Vending</strong> — automated machines dispense food and drinks. Found in offices, hospitals, and transport hubs.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Home delivery</strong> — food is prepared at a restaurant or kitchen and delivered to the customer’s home. Services like Deliveroo and Just Eat have made this increasingly popular.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Takeaway</strong> — customers order food and collect it themselves to eat elsewhere.</li>
</ul>
<h2 data-narration-id="n24">Residential Services</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n25">Residential hospitality providers offer accommodation alongside their catering. The WJEC spec requires you to understand the different room types and additional services that <dfn class="term" data-def="Accommodation that includes a private bathroom attached to the bedroom.">en-suite</dfn> residential providers offer.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n26" data-revision-tip="List the five room types in order from smallest to largest from memory.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>Room types include <strong>single</strong>, <strong>double</strong>, <strong>king</strong>, <strong>family</strong>, and <strong>suite</strong>. Rooms may have <strong>en-suite</strong> bath or shower facilities (private bathroom in the room) or <strong>shared facilities</strong> (bathroom shared with other guests).</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n27">Refreshments & Additional Facilities</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n28">Residential providers typically offer a range of refreshment options:</p>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n29"><strong>Breakfast</strong> — included in many hotels and B&Bs (continental or full cooked).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n30"><strong>Lunch and evening meals</strong> — available in the hotel restaurant.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n31"><strong>24-hour room service</strong> — food delivered to the guest’s room at any time, often at premium prices.</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n32">Many residential providers also offer <strong>conference and function facilities</strong> for business meetings, weddings, and events. These can be a significant source of revenue. <strong>Leisure facilities</strong> such as spas, gyms, and swimming pools attract guests and can justify higher room rates.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Why Do Hotels Offer Extra Facilities?</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n33">Additional facilities such as conference rooms, spas, and restaurants generate extra revenue beyond room bookings. A hotel with a spa can attract guests who might otherwise stay at a competitor. Conference facilities bring business customers who often book midweek when leisure demand is lower, improving occupancy rates throughout the year. Leisure facilities also support higher star ratings, which in turn allow the hotel to charge higher prices.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n35">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n36">Food service falls into three categories: table service (plate, family-style, silver, Gu&eacute;ridon, banquet), counter service (cafeteria, buffet, fast food), and personal service (tray/trolley, vending, home delivery, takeaway).</li>
<li data-narration-id="n37">Residential services include room types (single through to suite), refreshment options (breakfast to 24-hour room service), and additional facilities like conferences and leisure.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n38">The choice of food service style depends on the type of establishment, target customer, and price point.</li></ul>
