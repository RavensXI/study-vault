Russian Revolution, Orwell’s democratic socialism, propaganda and the purpose behind Animal Farm.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Why Did Orwell Write Animal Farm?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">George Orwell (born Eric Arthur Blair, 1903) was a <dfn class="term" data-def="A political philosophy advocating equality and collective ownership through democratic means, not authoritarian control.">democratic socialist</dfn> who believed passionately in equality and freedom. His experience fighting in the Spanish Civil War (1936–39) was pivotal: he witnessed Soviet-backed communists attacking their own allies, showing him that revolutionary ideals could be betrayed by those seeking power. After Spain, Orwell became fiercely opposed to <dfn class="term" data-def="A political system where the state holds total authority over society, suppressing individual freedom.">totalitarianism</dfn> in all its forms.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and explain why Orwell wrote Animal Farm and what political position he was attacking.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Orwell wrote Animal Farm (published 1945) specifically to show how Stalin had corrupted the ideals of the Russian Revolution. Crucially, Orwell was NOT attacking socialism itself — he was attacking its corruption under authoritarian rule. This distinction is essential for AO3: Orwell’s target is tyranny, not equality.</p>
</div>
<p data-narration-id="n4">Animal Farm is subtitled “A Fairy Story” — deliberately ironic, since the content is anything but light-hearted. Orwell chose the form of a <dfn class="term" data-def="A short story with animal characters that teaches a moral lesson.">fable</dfn> to make complex political ideas accessible. By giving human qualities to animals, he could satirise Stalin’s regime without naming it directly, reaching a wider audience than any political essay could.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n5">The Russian Revolution: Essential Background</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Before 1917, Russia was ruled by Tsar Nicholas II, who held absolute power over a country where most people lived in extreme poverty. Two revolutions occurred in 1917: in February, protests forced the Tsar to abdicate; in October, the <dfn class="term" data-def="Members of the revolutionary socialist party led by Lenin that seized power in Russia in October 1917.">Bolsheviks</dfn>, led by Vladimir Lenin, seized power. They were inspired by Karl Marx, who argued that the working class should overthrow the ruling class and share wealth equally.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n7">After Lenin’s death in 1924, a power struggle erupted between Leon Trotsky (an intellectual who wanted to spread revolution worldwide) and Joseph Stalin (a pragmatic operator who wanted to consolidate power in Russia). Stalin won. He exiled Trotsky (who was later assassinated in Mexico in 1940) and established a dictatorship characterised by purges, show trials, a secret police (the NKVD) and a cult of personality. This is the precise historical sequence that Animal Farm allegorises: Old Major = Marx/Lenin, Snowball = Trotsky, Napoleon = Stalin.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n8">Propaganda and Control</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9">Stalin maintained power through <dfn class="term" data-def="Information, often misleading, used to promote a political cause or point of view.">propaganda</dfn> — the state newspaper <em>Pravda</em> (meaning “truth”) printed only what the government approved. History was rewritten: Trotsky was erased from photographs. Citizens were encouraged to report “enemies of the state” — even family members. Orwell mirrors this through Squealer, who represents the propaganda machine, and Napoleon’s dogs, who represent the secret police.</p>
<!-- DIAGRAM -->
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n10" data-revision-tip="Close this and match the three main Animal Farm characters to their real-world counterparts.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Napoleon = Stalin (dictator who seizes power through fear). Snowball = Trotsky (intellectual rival, exiled). Old Major = Marx and Lenin (the revolutionary thinker whose ideas are corrupted). Squealer = Pravda/Soviet propaganda. The dogs = NKVD/secret police. Boxer = the exploited working class.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n11">Allegory, Satire and Fable</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Animal Farm combines three literary forms. As an <dfn class="term" data-def="A story where characters and events represent real-world people and situations.">allegory</dfn>, every element maps onto the Russian Revolution. As a <dfn class="term" data-def="The use of humour, irony or exaggeration to criticise and mock politics or society.">satire</dfn>, it uses dark comedy to expose the absurdity of totalitarianism — the pigs sleeping in beds then changing the commandment to add “with sheets” is both funny and chilling. As a fable, it delivers a moral lesson through animal characters. Understanding all three forms is essential for OCR.</p>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Publication Difficulties</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n13">Orwell struggled to get Animal Farm published. During World War II, the Soviet Union was Britain’s ally against Nazi Germany, and publishers feared offending Stalin. T.S. Eliot at Faber rejected it. It was eventually published in August 1945 — just as the war ended and the Cold War began. The timing was perfect: Britain was beginning to recognise the dangers of Stalinism, and the novella became an instant bestseller.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: explain what Orwell was attacking and what he was NOT attacking in Animal Farm.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Orwell was attacking the corruption of revolutionary ideals under Stalin’s totalitarian regime. He was NOT attacking socialism itself — he was a democratic socialist who believed in equality. His target was tyranny masquerading as liberation. This distinction is the most important AO3 point for Animal Farm.</p>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n16">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul><li data-narration-id="n17">Orwell was a democratic socialist attacking totalitarianism, not socialism — this distinction is essential for AO3.</li><li data-narration-id="n18">Animal Farm allegorises the Russian Revolution: Old Major = Marx/Lenin, Snowball = Trotsky, Napoleon = Stalin, Squealer = propaganda.</li><li data-narration-id="n19">The novella combines allegory, satire and fable to make complex political ideas accessible and memorable.</li></ul>
