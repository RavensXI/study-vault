--- DESCRIPTION ---
The structure, properties, and reactions of alkenes, alcohols, and carboxylic acids.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Alkenes</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="A family of hydrocarbons that contain at least one carbon-carbon double bond (C=C), making them unsaturated.">Alkenes</dfn> are a homologous series of hydrocarbons that contain a carbon–carbon <strong>double bond</strong> (\(\text{C}=\text{C}\)). This double bond makes them <dfn class="term" data-def="A molecule that contains at least one carbon-carbon double bond, meaning it has fewer hydrogen atoms than the equivalent alkane.">unsaturated</dfn> — they have fewer hydrogen atoms than the equivalent alkane with the same number of carbon atoms.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and write the general formula for alkenes plus the molecular formulas for ethene, propene, and butene.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The general formula for alkenes is \(\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}\). The first three members are: ethene (\(\text{C}_2\text{H}_4\)), propene (\(\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\)), and butene (\(\text{C}_4\text{H}_8\)). Alkenes are more reactive than alkanes because of the \(\text{C}=\text{C}\) double bond.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n4"><strong>Test for alkenes:</strong> Add bromine water (orange) to the substance. If an alkene is present, the bromine water is <strong>decolourised</strong> (turns colourless) because bromine adds across the double bond in an <dfn class="term" data-def="A reaction where atoms or groups are added across a double bond, converting an unsaturated molecule into a saturated one.">addition reaction</dfn>. Alkanes do not decolourise bromine water because they have no double bonds.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n5">Alkenes undergo addition reactions because the double bond can open up and bond with other atoms:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n6"><strong>With hydrogen</strong> (hydrogenation): Produces an alkane. \(\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2 \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_6\) (using a nickel catalyst at 150 °C).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n7"><strong>With water/steam</strong> (hydration): Produces an alcohol. \(\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}\).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>With bromine</strong>: Produces a dibromo compound. \(\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{Br}_2 \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_4\text{Br}_2\).</li>
</ul>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Alcohols</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10"><dfn class="term" data-def="A homologous series of organic compounds containing the hydroxyl functional group (-OH).">Alcohols</dfn> contain the functional group \(-\text{OH}\) (hydroxyl group). The general formula is \(\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+1}\text{OH}\). The first three members are methanol (\(\text{CH}_3\text{OH}\)), ethanol (\(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}\)), and propanol (\(\text{C}_3\text{H}_7\text{OH}\)).</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Reactions of Alcohols</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Combustion:</strong> Alcohols burn in air to produce carbon dioxide and water. \(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 3\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{CO}_2 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}\). This makes them useful as fuels (e.g. ethanol as a biofuel).</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>With sodium:</strong> Alcohols react with sodium metal to produce hydrogen gas and a sodium alkoxide. \(2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2\text{Na} \rightarrow 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{ONa} + \text{H}_2\). This reaction is less vigorous than sodium with water.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Oxidation:</strong> Alcohols can be oxidised to form carboxylic acids. This happens when ethanol is left exposed to air (bacterial oxidation turns wine into vinegar), or when an acidified <dfn class="term" data-def="An oxidising agent used in chemistry to oxidise alcohols to carboxylic acids; it changes colour from orange to green.">potassium dichromate</dfn> solution is heated with the alcohol — the solution changes from orange to green.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>



<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall both methods of ethanol production including temperatures, the fermentation equation, and catalyst used.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Ethanol is produced in two ways: (1) <strong>Fermentation</strong> of sugars using yeast at 25–30 °C in anaerobic conditions (\(\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \rightarrow 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} + 2\text{CO}_2\)), and (2) <strong>Hydration of ethene</strong> with steam using a phosphoric acid catalyst at 300 °C and 60–70 atm.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n15">Carboxylic Acids</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n16"><dfn class="term" data-def="A homologous series of organic compounds containing the -COOH functional group, which makes them weak acids.">Carboxylic acids</dfn> contain the functional group \(-\text{COOH}\) (carboxyl group). The first three members are methanoic acid (\(\text{HCOOH}\)), ethanoic acid (\(\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}\)), and propanoic acid (\(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COOH}\)).</p>

<p data-narration-id="n17">Carboxylic acids are <dfn class="term" data-def="An acid that only partially dissociates in water, producing a lower concentration of hydrogen ions than a strong acid of the same concentration.">weak acids</dfn> — they do not fully dissociate (ionise) in water. This means a solution of ethanoic acid has a higher pH (is less acidic) than a solution of hydrochloric acid at the same concentration.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Reactions of Carboxylic Acids</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n18">Carboxylic acids react like other acids:</p>
    <ul>
      <li data-narration-id="n19"><strong>With metal carbonates:</strong> Produce a salt, water, and carbon dioxide. \(2\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \rightarrow 2\text{CH}_3\text{COONa} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2\)</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n20"><strong>With alcohols:</strong> Produce an <dfn class="term" data-def="An organic compound formed when a carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol, often with a fruity smell.">ester</dfn> and water in a condensation reaction (requires an acid catalyst). \(\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{H}_2\text{O}\)</li>
    </ul>
    <p data-narration-id="n21">Esters often have fruity smells and are used in flavourings and perfumes.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n22" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and describe the conversion pathway from alkenes to alcohols to carboxylic acids.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The three homologous series are linked: alkenes can be hydrated to make alcohols, and alcohols can be oxidised to make carboxylic acids. Understanding these links helps you see how organic chemistry fits together.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n24">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">Alkenes contain a \(\text{C}=\text{C}\) double bond, are unsaturated (general formula \(\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}\)), and decolourise bromine water through addition reactions.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n26">Alcohols contain the \(-\text{OH}\) group, burn as fuels, react with sodium, and can be oxidised to carboxylic acids.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n27">Carboxylic acids contain \(-\text{COOH}\), are weak acids (partially dissociate), and react with alcohols to form esters.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n23">The bromine water test is a very common exam question — always state that the bromine water changes from <strong>orange to colourless</strong> (not “clear”, which could mean transparent). For carboxylic acids, remember they are <strong>weak</strong> acids (partially dissociate), not dilute — weak and dilute are different concepts. If asked to write the formula of an ester, remember the pattern: acid name first + alcohol name (e.g. ethanoic acid + methanol = methyl ethanoate).</p>
