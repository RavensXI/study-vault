--- DESCRIPTION ---
How burning fuels produces pollutants, and their effects on health and the environment.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Pollutants from Fuels</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">When <dfn class="term" data-def="Fuels formed from the remains of ancient organisms over millions of years. Examples: coal, oil, natural gas. They are finite (non-renewable) resources.">fossil fuels</dfn> are burned, several pollutant gases can be released into the atmosphere. The type and amount of pollutant depends on the fuel and the conditions of combustion.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">The main pollutants are:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n4"><strong>Carbon dioxide (\(CO_2\))</strong> — produced by complete combustion of any hydrocarbon. A greenhouse gas that contributes to global warming.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n5"><strong>Carbon monoxide (\(CO\))</strong> — produced by <dfn class="term" data-def="Combustion with insufficient oxygen. It produces carbon monoxide and/or carbon (soot) instead of carbon dioxide.">incomplete combustion</dfn>. A toxic, colourless, odourless gas that binds to haemoglobin in the blood, preventing it from carrying oxygen. Can cause death.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Soot / particulates (carbon particles)</strong> — also from incomplete combustion. Cause respiratory problems, make buildings dirty, and contribute to global dimming (blocking sunlight).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Sulfur dioxide (\(SO_2\))</strong> — produced when fuels containing <dfn class="term" data-def="A non-metal element present as an impurity in some fossil fuels. When burned, it forms sulfur dioxide which causes acid rain.">sulfur</dfn> impurities are burned. Causes <dfn class="term" data-def="Rain that is more acidic than normal (pH below 5.6), caused by sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides dissolving in rainwater. It damages buildings, kills trees and acidifies lakes.">acid rain</dfn>, which damages buildings (corrodes limestone/marble), kills trees and acidifies lakes.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Nitrogen oxides (\(NO_x\))</strong> — formed when nitrogen and oxygen react at the <strong>high temperatures</strong> inside car engines. Also cause acid rain and respiratory problems.</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n9" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Name the 5 main fossil fuel pollutants and state what CO causes, what soot causes, and what both SO₂ and NOₓ cause.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Fossil fuel pollutants: \(CO_2\) (greenhouse gas), CO (toxic), soot (respiratory problems), \(SO_2\) (acid rain from sulfur impurities), \(NO_x\) (acid rain from high temperature reaction of \(N_2\) and \(O_2\)).</p>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Reducing Pollution</h2>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Catalytic Converters and Flue Gas Desulfurisation</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11"><dfn class="term" data-def="A device fitted to car exhausts that converts toxic gases (CO and NOx) into less harmful ones (CO2 and N2) using a platinum/rhodium catalyst.">Catalytic converters</dfn> in car exhausts convert toxic carbon monoxide and nitrogen oxides into less harmful gases:</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n12">$$2CO + 2NO \rightarrow 2CO_2 + N_2$$</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Flue gas desulfurisation</strong> removes \(SO_2\) from power station emissions by reacting it with calcium oxide or calcium carbonate, producing calcium sulfate (which can be used to make plaster).</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Name what catalytic converters reduce, what flue gas desulfurisation removes, and list 3 other pollution solutions.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Solutions: catalytic converters reduce CO and \(NO_x\), flue gas desulfurisation removes \(SO_2\). Using low-sulfur fuels, renewable energy, and electric vehicles also reduces pollution.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>The Global Impact of Pollution</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n15">Air pollution is a global issue. Pollutants can travel long distances in the atmosphere, meaning emissions in one country can cause acid rain in another. International agreements (such as the Paris Agreement) aim to reduce greenhouse gas emissions. Developing countries face challenges balancing economic growth with pollution reduction.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n16">Alternative approaches to reducing pollution include: switching to renewable energy sources (wind, solar, hydroelectric), using electric or hydrogen-powered vehicles, improving fuel efficiency, and developing carbon capture and storage technologies.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n18">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n19">Burning fossil fuels produces pollutants: \(CO_2\) (global warming), CO (toxic), soot (respiratory), \(SO_2\) and \(NO_x\) (acid rain).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n20">CO and \(NO_x\) are reduced by catalytic converters; \(SO_2\) is removed by flue gas desulfurisation.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n21">Long-term solutions include renewable energy, electric vehicles, and carbon capture and storage.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n17">When explaining how a pollutant is formed, always state the specific conditions. For example: \(NO_x\) forms at the high temperatures inside engines where \(N_2\) and \(O_2\) react; \(SO_2\) forms when sulfur impurities in the fuel burn. Don’t confuse incomplete combustion products (CO, soot) with products of complete combustion (\(CO_2\), \(H_2O\)).</p>
