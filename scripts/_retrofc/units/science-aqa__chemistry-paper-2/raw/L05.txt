--- DESCRIPTION ---
How the atmosphere evolved over billions of years and how greenhouse gases cause climate change.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Early Atmosphere</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">For the first billion years, the Earth’s atmosphere was very different from today. Scientists believe it was mainly <dfn class="term" data-def="CO2 made up most of the early atmosphere (like Venus and Mars today). Volcanic activity released CO2, water vapour, nitrogen and other gases.">carbon dioxide</dfn>, with little or no oxygen — similar to the atmospheres of Venus and Mars today. There was also water vapour, nitrogen and small amounts of methane and ammonia. This early atmosphere was produced by <strong>volcanic activity</strong>.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">The water vapour condensed as the Earth cooled, forming the oceans. Over billions of years, the atmosphere changed dramatically:</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>How the Atmosphere Changed</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n4"><strong>Step 1:</strong> The first simple organisms (algae and photosynthetic bacteria) evolved in the oceans. They used \(CO_2\) for photosynthesis and released \(O_2\), gradually increasing the oxygen level in the atmosphere.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n5"><strong>Step 2:</strong> \(CO_2\) was also removed by dissolving in the oceans, where it formed carbonates in sedimentary rocks and the shells/skeletons of marine organisms.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Step 3:</strong> As \(O_2\) levels increased, more complex life evolved. The ozone layer (\(O_3\)) formed, shielding life from UV radiation.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n7" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Recall the percentages of nitrogen, oxygen, and carbon dioxide in today's atmosphere and how long it's been stable.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Today’s atmosphere: approximately 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, 0.04% carbon dioxide, plus small amounts of other gases (argon, water vapour). This has been roughly stable for 200 million years.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n8">The Greenhouse Effect</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n9"><dfn class="term" data-def="Gases in the atmosphere that absorb and re-emit infrared radiation, trapping heat and warming the Earth. The main ones are carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour.">Greenhouse gases</dfn> (carbon dioxide, methane, water vapour) absorb heat radiation (infrared) from the Earth’s surface and re-emit it in all directions, including back towards Earth. This natural <dfn class="term" data-def="The process by which greenhouse gases in the atmosphere trap infrared radiation, keeping the Earth warm enough to support life.">greenhouse effect</dfn> keeps the Earth warm enough to support life.</p>



<p data-narration-id="n10">However, human activities are increasing the concentrations of greenhouse gases:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Carbon dioxide</strong> — burning fossil fuels, deforestation.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Methane</strong> — agriculture (cattle farming, rice paddies), landfill sites.</li>
</ul>

<p data-narration-id="n13">This <strong>enhanced greenhouse effect</strong> is causing <dfn class="term" data-def="The gradual increase in global average temperatures, primarily caused by increased concentrations of greenhouse gases from human activities.">global warming</dfn> — an increase in global average temperatures. The consequences include: sea level rise (thermal expansion and melting ice), more extreme weather events, changes to ecosystems and species distribution, and impacts on agriculture.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. List the human activities that increase CO₂ and CH₄, then explain the chain: enhanced greenhouse effect leads to what impacts?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Human activities increase \(CO_2\) (fossil fuels, deforestation) and \(CH_4\) (farming, landfill). Enhanced greenhouse effect → global warming → rising sea levels, extreme weather, habitat loss.</p>
</div>

    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n15">A <dfn class="term" data-def="The total amount of carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases emitted over the full life cycle of a product, service, or event.">carbon footprint</dfn> is the total amount of greenhouse gases released over the life cycle of a product or activity. It can be reduced by: using renewable energy, improving energy efficiency, carbon capture and storage, reducing meat consumption, and reducing travel.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n17">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n18">Early atmosphere: mostly \(CO_2\) from volcanoes. Photosynthesis by algae added \(O_2\) and removed \(CO_2\) over billions of years.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n19">Today’s atmosphere: ~78% nitrogen, ~21% oxygen, ~0.04% \(CO_2\).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n20">Human activities increase greenhouse gases, enhancing the greenhouse effect and causing global warming.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n16">When asked about atmospheric change, use a timeline: early atmosphere (mostly \(CO_2\), volcanic) &rarr; algae evolved (photosynthesis added \(O_2\), removed \(CO_2\)) &rarr; today (78% \(N_2\), 21% \(O_2\)). For greenhouse effect questions, always describe the mechanism: greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation.</p>
