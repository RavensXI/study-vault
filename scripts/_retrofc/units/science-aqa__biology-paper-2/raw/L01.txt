--- DESCRIPTION ---
How the body maintains stable internal conditions, and the structure of the nervous system.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Homeostasis</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="The regulation of the internal conditions of a cell or organism to maintain optimum conditions for function, in response to internal and external changes.">Homeostasis</dfn> is the regulation of the internal conditions of the body to maintain a stable environment. This is essential because cells need the right conditions to function properly — particularly for enzyme action. The conditions controlled include body temperature, blood glucose concentration, water levels and ion levels.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. List the 3 components of all control systems and name the 3 coordination centres mentioned.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Homeostasis maintains optimal internal conditions for enzyme activity. All control systems involve: receptors (detect stimuli), coordination centres (brain, spinal cord, pancreas) and effectors (muscles or glands that produce a response).</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n4">Homeostatic control systems involve three components: <dfn class="term" data-def="Cells or organs that detect changes (stimuli) in the internal or external environment.">receptors</dfn> that detect changes in the environment (stimuli), <strong>coordination centres</strong> (the brain, spinal cord and pancreas) that receive and process information, and <dfn class="term" data-def="Muscles or glands that bring about a response to a stimulus. Muscles contract; glands secrete hormones or other chemicals.">effectors</dfn> (muscles and glands) that carry out a response. Many homeostatic systems use <dfn class="term" data-def="A control mechanism where the output of a system counteracts or reverses the original change, returning conditions to the set point.">negative feedback</dfn> — a mechanism where the response works to reverse the change and return conditions to the optimum.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">The Nervous System</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n6">The nervous system enables humans to react to their surroundings and coordinate behaviour. It is made up of the <strong>central nervous system</strong> (CNS) — the brain and spinal cord — and the peripheral nervous system (sensory and motor neurones that connect receptors and effectors to the CNS).</p>

<p data-narration-id="n7">The pathway of a nervous response is: <strong>stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone (in CNS) → motor neurone → effector → response</strong>.</p>

  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n8">Neurones do not directly touch each other. The tiny gap between two neurones is called a <dfn class="term" data-def="The junction between two neurones. A chemical neurotransmitter diffuses across the gap to pass the electrical impulse from one neurone to the next.">synapse</dfn>. When an electrical impulse reaches the end of a neurone, it triggers the release of chemical <dfn class="term" data-def="A chemical substance released at a synapse that diffuses across the gap and triggers an electrical impulse in the next neurone.">neurotransmitters</dfn> that diffuse across the gap and bind to receptors on the next neurone, triggering a new electrical impulse. This allows signals to pass between neurones.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Reflex Actions</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n10"><dfn class="term" data-def="An automatic, rapid, involuntary response to a stimulus that does not involve conscious thought. The nerve pathway is called a reflex arc.">Reflex actions</dfn> are rapid, automatic responses that do not involve the conscious part of the brain. They protect the body from harm — for example, pulling your hand away from a hot object. The nerve pathway for a reflex is called a <strong>reflex arc</strong>:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n11">Receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone (in spinal cord) → motor neurone → effector</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n12" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write out the complete reflex arc pathway from receptor to effector, naming all 5 stages in order.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Reflex arcs bypass the brain, making responses faster. The pathway is: receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone (spinal cord) → motor neurone → effector. This protects the body from danger.</p>
</div>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n13">Required Practical 7 involves investigating the effect of a factor on human reaction time. A common method is the ruler drop test: one person holds a ruler vertically, the other person catches it when released. The distance the ruler falls is related to reaction time — a shorter distance means a faster reaction. Variables such as caffeine, practice, or distraction can be investigated as independent variables.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n14">The brain is the main coordination centre. It receives impulses from receptors and sends impulses to effectors. Different regions of the brain have different functions: the <strong>cerebral cortex</strong> (consciousness, thought, memory), the <strong>cerebellum</strong> (balance and coordination) and the <strong>medulla</strong> (unconscious activities like breathing and heart rate).</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n16">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n17">Homeostasis maintains stable internal conditions using receptors, coordination centres and effectors, often via negative feedback.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n18">The nervous system pathway: stimulus &rarr; receptor &rarr; sensory neurone &rarr; relay neurone &rarr; motor neurone &rarr; effector &rarr; response.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n19">Reflex arcs are rapid, involuntary responses that bypass the brain (through the spinal cord) to protect the body.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n15">When describing a reflex arc, always list the components in order and include the type of neurone at each stage. A common mistake is forgetting the relay neurone or putting the brain instead of the spinal cord. Also remember: reflexes are involuntary — they bypass the conscious brain for speed.</p>
