--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Alleles and Inheritance</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">Most characteristics are controlled by several genes working together (polygenic). Some characteristics are controlled by a single gene with two forms called <dfn class="term" data-def="Different versions of the same gene. For example, the gene for eye colour has alleles for brown, blue, green, etc.">alleles</dfn>. You inherit one allele from each parent, giving you two alleles for each gene.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">If the two alleles are the same, the individual is <dfn class="term" data-def="Having two identical alleles for a particular gene (e.g. BB or bb).">homozygous</dfn> for that gene. If they are different, the individual is <dfn class="term" data-def="Having two different alleles for a particular gene (e.g. Bb).">heterozygous</dfn>. A <dfn class="term" data-def="An allele that is always expressed in the phenotype, even when only one copy is present. Represented by a capital letter (e.g. B).">dominant</dfn> allele is always expressed when present (shown by a capital letter, e.g. B). A <dfn class="term" data-def="An allele that is only expressed when two copies are present (homozygous recessive). Represented by a lower-case letter (e.g. b).">recessive</dfn> allele is only expressed when two copies are present (bb).</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Define genotype vs phenotype, explain how many copies dominant vs recessive alleles need, and give examples.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Genotype = the combination of alleles (e.g. Bb). Phenotype = the physical characteristic expressed (e.g. brown eyes). Dominant alleles only need one copy to be expressed; recessive alleles need two copies (homozygous recessive).</p>
</div>

<div class="higher-only">
<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Punnett Squares and Genetic Diagrams</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n6">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A grid used to predict the possible genotypes and phenotypes of offspring from a genetic cross. Parent alleles go along the top and side.">Punnett square</dfn> is used to show the possible combinations of alleles in offspring. For example, if both parents are heterozygous (Bb):</p>

<p data-narration-id="n7">The offspring ratios would be: \(\frac{1}{4}\) BB, \(\frac{2}{4}\) Bb, \(\frac{1}{4}\) bb. This gives a 3:1 phenotype ratio (3 dominant : 1 recessive).</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n8">A couple are both carriers of cystic fibrosis (genotype Ff). What is the probability of their child having cystic fibrosis?</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9">Parent genotypes: Ff × Ff. The Punnett square gives: FF, Ff, Ff, ff. Only ff has cystic fibrosis. So the probability is \(\frac{1}{4}\) or 25%. Note: each child has the same probability, regardless of previous children.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Inherited Disorders</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n11">Some disorders are caused by faulty alleles inherited from parents:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n12"><strong><dfn class="term" data-def="A genetic disorder caused by a recessive allele. It causes thick, sticky mucus in the lungs and digestive system. Both parents must carry the allele (Ff) for a child to be affected (ff).">Cystic fibrosis</dfn></strong> is a disorder of cell membranes caused by a <strong>recessive</strong> allele (f). A person must inherit two copies (ff) to be affected. A carrier (Ff) has one copy but does not show symptoms. It causes thick, sticky mucus to build up in the lungs and digestive system.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n13"><strong><dfn class="term" data-def="A genetic disorder caused by a dominant allele. It causes extra fingers or toes. Only one copy of the allele is needed to be affected.">Polydactyly</dfn></strong> is caused by a <strong>dominant</strong> allele. It causes extra fingers or toes. Only one copy of the dominant allele is needed to have the condition (Dd or DD).</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. State which alleles cause cystic fibrosis vs polydactyly, identify carrier genotype, and name the prediction tool.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Cystic fibrosis = recessive disorder (need ff to be affected; Ff = carrier). Polydactyly = dominant disorder (need only one D allele). Use Punnett squares to predict offspring ratios.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Sex Determination</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n17">In humans, sex is determined by the 23rd pair of chromosomes: XX = female, XY = male. A Punnett square shows there is a 50% chance of each sex. The Y chromosome carries the SRY gene, which triggers male development.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n19">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n20">Alleles are different versions of a gene; dominant alleles are always expressed, recessive only when homozygous (bb).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n21">Punnett squares predict offspring genotype and phenotype ratios (e.g. 3:1 from two heterozygous parents).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n22">Cystic fibrosis is recessive (ff); polydactyly is dominant (Dd or DD). Sex is determined by XX (female) or XY (male).</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n18">Always show your Punnett square working clearly in the exam — even if the question doesn’t explicitly ask for one. Label parent genotypes, gametes, and offspring ratios. Remember to express probabilities as fractions, percentages or ratios. A common mistake is saying “there is a 1 in 4 chance, so 1 out of 4 children will be affected” — each child independently has the same probability.</p>
