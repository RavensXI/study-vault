--- DESCRIPTION ---
The causes of variation, Darwin’s theory of natural selection, and evidence for evolution.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Variation</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="Differences between individuals of the same species. Caused by genes, the environment, or a combination of both.">Variation</dfn> is the differences between individuals of the same species. It can be caused by:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n3"><strong>Genetic variation</strong> — differences in DNA inherited from parents. Caused by sexual reproduction (meiosis shuffles genes) and <dfn class="term" data-def="A random change in the DNA sequence of a gene. Most mutations have no effect, but some can change the protein produced, leading to new characteristics.">mutations</dfn> (random changes in DNA).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n4"><strong>Environmental variation</strong> — differences caused by the conditions an organism lives in, e.g. diet, climate, lifestyle.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n5"><strong>Combination</strong> — most characteristics (e.g. height, weight) are influenced by both genes and environment.</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n6" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and list the three causes of variation, then explain what mutations are and their usual effect on phenotype.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Variation is caused by genetics (inherited from parents), the environment (conditions), or both. Mutations are random changes in DNA that can introduce new alleles. Most mutations have no effect on the phenotype.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n7">Evolution by Natural Selection</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n8"><dfn class="term" data-def="The gradual change in the inherited characteristics of a biological population over successive generations.">Evolution</dfn> is the gradual change in inherited characteristics of a population over many generations. Charles Darwin proposed the theory of <dfn class="term" data-def="The process by which organisms with characteristics better suited to their environment are more likely to survive, reproduce and pass on their genes. Over time, this leads to evolution.">natural selection</dfn> to explain how evolution occurs:</p>

<ol>
  <li data-narration-id="n9">Within a population, there is <strong>variation</strong> in characteristics due to genetic differences.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n10">Individuals with characteristics better suited to the environment are more likely to <strong>survive</strong> and <strong>reproduce</strong> (survival of the fittest).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n11">These individuals pass on the <strong>advantageous alleles</strong> to their offspring.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n12">Over many generations, the proportion of individuals with the advantageous characteristic <strong>increases</strong>.</li>
</ol>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n13">Evidence for evolution comes from: <strong>fossils</strong> (show how organisms have changed over time), <strong>antibiotic-resistant bacteria</strong> (observed natural selection in action), and <strong>comparative anatomy</strong> (similar bone structures in different species suggest common ancestors). The fossil record is incomplete because not all organisms fossilise, and many fossils are destroyed by geological processes.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and write the four-step process of natural selection, then name three types of evidence that support it.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Natural selection: variation → survival of the fittest → advantageous alleles passed on → frequency increases over generations. Evidence includes fossils, resistant bacteria and comparative anatomy.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n15">Selective Breeding and Extinction</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n16"><dfn class="term" data-def="The process by which humans choose organisms with desired characteristics to breed together, producing offspring with those characteristics. Also called artificial selection.">Selective breeding</dfn> (artificial selection) is when humans choose organisms with desirable traits to breed together. Examples include breeding cows for higher milk yield, dogs for specific characteristics, and crops for disease resistance. A disadvantage is that it reduces <dfn class="term" data-def="The range of different alleles within a population. High genetic variation helps populations survive environmental changes.">genetic variation</dfn> within the population, making it more vulnerable to disease.</p>

  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n17"><dfn class="term" data-def="When no living members of a species remain. Causes include new predators, new diseases, environmental change, and competition.">Extinction</dfn> occurs when a species dies out completely. Causes include: new predators or diseases, environmental changes, loss of habitat, competition, and catastrophic events. Carl Linnaeus developed the classification system that organises living things into groups based on shared characteristics: kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n19">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n20">Variation is caused by genetics, environment, or both; mutations introduce new alleles.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n21">Natural selection: variation &rarr; survival of the fittest &rarr; advantageous alleles passed to offspring &rarr; population changes over generations.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n22">Selective breeding chooses desirable traits but reduces genetic variation; extinction occurs when a species cannot adapt.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n18">When explaining natural selection, always follow the four-step process: variation → competition → survival of the fittest → reproduction and passing on alleles. Use a specific example if possible (e.g. antibiotic resistance in bacteria or peppered moths). Don’t say organisms “choose” to evolve — evolution is not a conscious process.</p>
