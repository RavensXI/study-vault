--- DESCRIPTION ---
Food chains, nutrient cycling, biodiversity, and how human activities affect ecosystems.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Communities and Ecosystems</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">An <dfn class="term" data-def="A community of living organisms (biotic) interacting with the non-living (abiotic) parts of their environment.">ecosystem</dfn> is the interaction of a community of living organisms (biotic factors) with the non-living (abiotic) parts of their environment. A <dfn class="term" data-def="All the populations of different species living and interacting together in a habitat.">community</dfn> includes all the organisms of different species living together. A <dfn class="term" data-def="All the organisms of one species living in a habitat at a particular time.">population</dfn> is all the organisms of one species in a habitat.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3"><dfn class="term" data-def="Non-living factors that affect a community, such as light intensity, temperature, moisture level, soil pH and wind.">Abiotic factors</dfn> include light intensity, temperature, moisture, soil pH and mineral content, wind speed and direction, and availability of carbon dioxide and oxygen. <dfn class="term" data-def="Living factors that affect a community, such as food availability, new predators, new pathogens and competition.">Biotic factors</dfn> include availability of food, new predators, new pathogens and competition between organisms.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and name the two types of factors in ecosystems, then explain what happens in a stable community.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Ecosystems consist of biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) factors. In a stable community, the populations of all species are balanced. Changes to abiotic or biotic factors can destabilise a community.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Food Chains and Interdependence</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n6">Organisms in a community depend on each other for food, shelter and other resources. This is called <dfn class="term" data-def="The reliance of organisms in a community on each other for survival, including food, shelter and pollination.">interdependence</dfn>. Food chains show the flow of energy from producers to consumers:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Producer → Primary consumer → Secondary consumer → Tertiary consumer</strong></p>

<p data-narration-id="n8"><dfn class="term" data-def="Organisms that make their own food using photosynthesis (e.g. plants and algae). They are the start of every food chain.">Producers</dfn> (plants) convert light energy into chemical energy through photosynthesis. <dfn class="term" data-def="Organisms that eat other organisms for energy. Primary consumers eat producers; secondary consumers eat primary consumers.">Consumers</dfn> obtain energy by eating other organisms. If one species in a food chain is removed, it affects all the other organisms — this is called interdependence.</p>

Animals eat plants and release \(CO_2\) through respiration. When organisms die, <dfn class="term" data-def="Microorganisms (bacteria and fungi) that break down dead organic matter, recycling nutrients back into the soil and releasing CO2 through respiration.">decomposers</dfn> (bacteria and fungi) break down the dead material, releasing \(CO_2\) through respiration. Combustion of fossil fuels also releases \(CO_2\).</p>

</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Biodiversity and Human Impact</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n11"><dfn class="term" data-def="The variety of all the different species of organisms on Earth, or within an ecosystem. High biodiversity makes ecosystems more resilient.">Biodiversity</dfn> is the variety of different species in an ecosystem. High biodiversity makes ecosystems more stable and resilient to change. However, human activities are rapidly reducing biodiversity worldwide.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n12">Human activities that reduce biodiversity include:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Deforestation</strong> — clearing forests for agriculture and building destroys habitats.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Pollution</strong> — land, water and air pollution damage habitats and kill organisms.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Global warming</strong> — rising temperatures alter habitats and cause species migration or extinction.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Overexploitation</strong> — overfishing, hunting and intensive farming reduce populations.</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and list the four main causes reducing biodiversity, plus name four conservation methods that help maintain it.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Biodiversity is being reduced by deforestation, pollution, global warming and overexploitation. Conservation programmes, breeding programmes, seed banks and habitat restoration help maintain biodiversity.</p>
</div>

  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n18">Strategies to maintain biodiversity include: <strong>breeding programmes</strong> for endangered species, <strong>protection of rare habitats</strong> (e.g. national parks, SSSIs), <strong>reintroduction</strong> of hedgerows and field margins in agricultural areas, <strong>seed banks</strong> to preserve genetic diversity, and <strong>reducing deforestation and carbon emissions</strong>. Scientists also use techniques like <strong>quadrats</strong> and <strong>transects</strong> to sample and monitor biodiversity in ecosystems.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n20">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n21">Ecosystems = biotic + abiotic factors; organisms are interdependent through food chains and nutrient cycling.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n22">Carbon is recycled through photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition and combustion.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n23">Human activities (deforestation, pollution, global warming) reduce biodiversity; conservation strategies help protect it.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n19">When explaining the impact of removing one species from a food chain, always trace the effect both up and down the chain. For example, if a primary consumer is removed, the producer population may increase (less grazing) while the secondary consumer population decreases (less food). Always link back to interdependence.</p>
