--- DESCRIPTION ---
The structure of DNA, genetic engineering, cloning techniques, and key discoveries.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">DNA Structure</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="Deoxyribonucleic acid, a polymer made of two strands forming a double helix, which carries the genetic code in all living organisms.">DNA</dfn> (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a large molecule made of two strands twisted together in a shape called a <dfn class="term" data-def="The twisted ladder shape of the DNA molecule, formed by two complementary strands wound around each other.">double helix</dfn>. Each strand is a polymer made from repeating units called <dfn class="term" data-def="The monomer units of DNA, each consisting of a sugar, a phosphate group, and one of four bases (A, T, C, G).">nucleotides</dfn>.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and name the three components of a nucleotide plus all four bases and their complementary base pairing rules.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Each nucleotide consists of a sugar (deoxyribose), a phosphate group, and one of four bases: adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C), or guanine (G). The bases pair in a specific way: A always pairs with T, and C always pairs with G. This is called <dfn class="term" data-def="The specific pairing of DNA bases where adenine always bonds with thymine and cytosine always bonds with guanine.">complementary base pairing</dfn>.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n4">The sequence of bases along a strand of DNA forms the genetic code. Each group of three bases (a <dfn class="term" data-def="A sequence of three bases on a DNA strand that codes for one amino acid.">triplet</dfn>) codes for a particular amino acid. The order of amino acids determines the shape and function of the protein produced.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Protein Synthesis</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Proteins are synthesised on ribosomes in the cytoplasm. Since DNA cannot leave the nucleus, a copy of the gene is made as <dfn class="term" data-def="Messenger RNA, a single-stranded copy of a gene that carries the genetic code from the nucleus to the ribosomes.">mRNA</dfn> (messenger RNA) in a process called transcription. The mRNA then travels to a ribosome where it is translated into a chain of amino acids that folds into a protein.</p>

  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Transcription and Translation</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Transcription</strong> (in the nucleus): The two strands of DNA unzip. One strand is used as a template to build a complementary mRNA strand. The mRNA then leaves the nucleus through a nuclear pore.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Translation</strong> (at the ribosome): The mRNA attaches to a ribosome. Transfer RNA (<dfn class="term" data-def="Transfer RNA, small RNA molecules that carry specific amino acids to the ribosome during translation.">tRNA</dfn>) molecules bring the correct amino acids to the ribosome, matching their anticodon to the mRNA codon. The amino acids are joined together in the correct order to form a polypeptide chain.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Genetic Engineering</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10"><dfn class="term" data-def="The process of modifying the genome of an organism by introducing a gene from another organism.">Genetic engineering</dfn> involves cutting a gene out of the DNA of one organism and inserting it into the DNA of another. The organism that receives the gene is called a <dfn class="term" data-def="An organism that contains a gene from a different species, inserted through genetic engineering.">genetically modified (GM) organism</dfn> or transgenic organism.</p>

<div class="higher-only">
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n11" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and list the four numbered steps of genetic engineering, including the role of restriction enzymes and vectors.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The steps of genetic engineering: (1) The desired gene is identified and cut out using <dfn class="term" data-def="Enzymes that cut DNA at specific sequences, used to isolate genes in genetic engineering.">restriction enzymes</dfn>. (2) The gene is inserted into a vector (e.g. a bacterial plasmid or virus). (3) The vector carries the gene into the target organism's cells. (4) The target organism now expresses the new gene and produces the desired protein.</p>
</div>



<p data-narration-id="n12">A key example is the production of human insulin by bacteria. The human insulin gene is inserted into bacterial plasmids, and the bacteria are grown in fermenters to produce large quantities of insulin for treating diabetes.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Cloning</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Cloning produces genetically identical copies of an organism. There are different types:</p>

<p data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Plant cloning (tissue culture):</strong> Small groups of cells are taken from a plant and grown in a sterile growth medium containing nutrients and plant hormones. Each group develops into a genetically identical plant. This is used to produce large numbers of rare or desirable plants quickly.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Animal cloning (adult cell cloning):</strong> This was used to create Dolly the sheep in 1996. The nucleus from an adult body cell is inserted into an enucleated (nucleus removed) egg cell. An electric shock stimulates the egg to divide, and the embryo is implanted into a surrogate mother. The offspring is genetically identical to the adult that donated the nucleus.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>The History of Genetics</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Gregor Mendel</strong> (1860s) discovered the basic laws of inheritance by studying pea plants. He proposed the idea of "hereditary units" (now called genes) that are passed from parents to offspring. His work was not recognised until the early 1900s because the mechanism of inheritance (chromosomes and DNA) had not yet been discovered.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n18"><strong>Watson and Crick</strong> (1953) worked out the double helix structure of DNA, building on X-ray diffraction data produced by <strong>Rosalind Franklin</strong> and <strong>Maurice Wilkins</strong>. Their model showed how DNA could carry the genetic code and replicate itself.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n19">The <strong>Human Genome Project</strong> (completed 2003) mapped the entire sequence of human DNA. This has enabled scientists to identify genes linked to diseases, develop gene therapies, and understand human evolution.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n20" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall what Dolly the sheep proved about adult cells and what year she was born.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Dolly the sheep (born 1996) was the first mammal cloned from an adult cell. This proved that a differentiated adult cell still contains all the genetic information needed to produce an entire organism.</p>
</div>


--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n22">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n23">DNA is a double helix of nucleotides with complementary base pairing (A–T, C–G). Triplets of bases code for amino acids.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n24">Genetic engineering uses restriction enzymes to cut genes and vectors (plasmids) to transfer them into new organisms, e.g. bacteria producing human insulin.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">Cloning produces genetically identical organisms — tissue culture for plants, adult cell cloning for animals (e.g. Dolly the sheep).</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n21">Questions on genetic engineering often ask you to describe the steps in order — learn the sequence (identify gene, cut with restriction enzymes, insert into vector, transfer to organism). For DNA structure questions, always state that bases pair <strong>specifically</strong>: A–T and C–G. If asked about Mendel, explain why his work was not recognised at the time (the mechanism of inheritance had not been discovered).</p>
