How the brain controls body functions, how the eye focuses light, and thermoregulation.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Human Brain</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The brain is the most complex organ in the body. It is made of billions of interconnected <dfn class="term" data-def="Nerve cells that transmit electrical impulses throughout the nervous system.">neurones</dfn> and is responsible for coordinating all of our actions, thoughts, and bodily functions. For separate biology, you need to know the functions of the main brain regions and how scientists study them.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Name the three brain regions and state what the cerebral cortex, cerebellum, and medulla oblongata each control.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The brain has three main regions: the <dfn class="term" data-def="The largest part of the brain, controls conscious thought, memory, language, and voluntary actions.">cerebral cortex</dfn> (conscious thought, memory, language), the <dfn class="term" data-def="The part of the brain at the back of the skull that coordinates balance, posture, and fine movement.">cerebellum</dfn> (balance and coordinated movement), and the <dfn class="term" data-def="The part of the brain that connects to the spinal cord and controls unconscious activities like breathing and heart rate.">medulla oblongata</dfn> (unconscious functions like breathing and heart rate).</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n4">Scientists study brain function using several methods:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n5"><strong>Studying patients with brain damage</strong> — if a specific area is damaged and a particular function is lost, this reveals what that region controls.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Electrical stimulation</strong> — stimulating different areas of the brain during surgery can trigger specific responses, mapping functions to locations.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n7"><strong>MRI scanning</strong> — magnetic resonance imaging shows which areas of the brain are active during different tasks by detecting changes in blood flow.</li>
</ul>

<p data-narration-id="n8">Brain surgery and treatment of brain disorders is difficult because the brain is extremely complex, neurones in the brain cannot easily regenerate, and damage to one area may have unpredictable knock-on effects.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">The Eye</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">The eye is a <dfn class="term" data-def="An organ that detects changes in the environment, such as the eye detecting light.">sense organ</dfn> that detects light. Light enters through the cornea, passes through the pupil, and is focused by the lens onto the <dfn class="term" data-def="The light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye containing rod and cone cells that convert light into electrical impulses.">retina</dfn> at the back of the eye.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Structure of the Eye</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Cornea:</strong> Transparent front layer that refracts (bends) light as it enters the eye.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Iris:</strong> Coloured ring of muscle that controls the size of the pupil, regulating how much light enters.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Pupil:</strong> The hole in the centre of the iris through which light passes.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Lens:</strong> A transparent, flexible structure that changes shape to focus light on the retina. This is called <dfn class="term" data-def="The process by which the ciliary muscles change the shape of the lens to focus on objects at different distances.">accommodation</dfn>.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Retina:</strong> The light-sensitive layer at the back containing receptor cells (rods for dim light, cones for colour vision).</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Optic nerve:</strong> Carries electrical impulses from the retina to the brain.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Accommodation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">The eye adjusts to focus on objects at different distances through accommodation:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n19"><strong>Distant objects:</strong> The <dfn class="term" data-def="A ring of muscle surrounding the lens that contracts or relaxes to change the shape of the lens.">ciliary muscles</dfn> relax, the suspensory ligaments pull tight, and the lens is pulled thin and flat. This reduces refraction.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n20"><strong>Near objects:</strong> The ciliary muscles contract, the suspensory ligaments loosen, and the lens becomes thick and rounded. This increases refraction to focus close objects onto the retina.</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n21" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. State which lens type corrects myopia and hyperopia, and name two surgical treatments for vision problems.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p><strong>Myopia</strong> (short-sightedness) is corrected with a concave (diverging) lens. <strong>Hyperopia</strong> (long-sightedness) is corrected with a convex (converging) lens. Both conditions can also be treated with laser eye surgery or replacement lens surgery.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Common Eye Defects</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Myopia (short-sightedness):</strong> The eyeball is too long or the lens is too curved, so light focuses in front of the retina. Distant objects appear blurry. Corrected with a concave lens.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Hyperopia (long-sightedness):</strong> The eyeball is too short or the lens cannot become curved enough, so light focuses behind the retina. Close objects appear blurry. Corrected with a convex lens.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n24">Both conditions can also be treated with contact lenses, laser eye surgery (reshaping the cornea), or replacement lens surgery.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n25">Temperature Control (Thermoregulation)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n26">The human body maintains a core temperature of approximately 37 °C. This is controlled by the <dfn class="term" data-def="A small region of the brain that acts as the body's thermostat, monitoring and regulating core body temperature.">thermoregulatory centre</dfn> in the hypothalamus, which monitors the temperature of the blood flowing through it and receives impulses from temperature receptors in the skin.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n27" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Explain why body temperature is maintained at 37°C and what happens to enzymes if core temperature deviates.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Body temperature is maintained at 37 °C because this is the optimum temperature for enzymes. If the core temperature deviates significantly, enzymes may denature and metabolic reactions slow down or stop.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n28"><strong>When the body is too hot:</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n29">Blood vessels near the skin surface <dfn class="term" data-def="The widening of blood vessels near the skin surface, allowing more blood to flow close to the surface and more heat to be lost by radiation.">vasodilate</dfn> (widen), increasing blood flow near the surface and heat loss by radiation.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n30">Sweat glands produce more sweat, which evaporates from the skin surface and transfers heat energy to the environment.</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n31"><strong>When the body is too cold:</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n32">Blood vessels near the skin surface <dfn class="term" data-def="The narrowing of blood vessels near the skin surface, reducing blood flow to the surface and reducing heat loss.">vasoconstrict</dfn> (narrow), reducing blood flow near the surface and minimising heat loss.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n33">Skeletal muscles contract rapidly (shivering), generating heat through increased respiration.</li>
</ul>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n35">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n36">The brain has three main regions: cerebral cortex (conscious thought), cerebellum (balance), and medulla oblongata (unconscious functions like breathing).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n37">The eye focuses light through accommodation — ciliary muscles change the shape of the lens. Myopia is corrected with concave lenses; hyperopia with convex lenses.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n38">Thermoregulation maintains body temperature at 37 °C through vasodilation/vasoconstriction and sweating/shivering, controlled by the hypothalamus.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n34">A very common exam error is confusing <strong>vasodilation</strong> with “blood vessels moving closer to the skin.” Blood vessels do not move — they widen or narrow. For accommodation questions, remember the sequence: ciliary muscles → suspensory ligaments → lens shape. Always state what happens to each structure, not just the end result.</p>
