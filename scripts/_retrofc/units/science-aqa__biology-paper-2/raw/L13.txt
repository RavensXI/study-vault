--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Decomposition</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="Organisms (mainly bacteria and fungi) that break down dead plant and animal matter, recycling nutrients back into the soil.">Decomposers</dfn> are organisms — mainly bacteria and fungi — that break down dead plant and animal matter, returning nutrients and minerals to the soil. This process is essential for recycling materials in ecosystems and keeping nutrient cycles running.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall the four factors affecting decomposition rate: temperature (enzyme activity), water, oxygen, and what fourth factor?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The rate of decomposition is affected by <strong>temperature</strong> (higher temperatures increase enzyme activity up to the optimum), <strong>water availability</strong> (decomposers need moisture), <strong>oxygen availability</strong> (many decomposers respire aerobically), and the <strong>number of decomposers</strong> present.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n4">Gardeners and farmers use decomposition to their advantage through <dfn class="term" data-def="A mixture of decayed organic matter produced by decomposition, used to enrich soil with nutrients.">compost</dfn>. Garden waste and food scraps are collected in compost bins where decomposers break them down over weeks or months. The resulting compost is rich in minerals and can be added to soil to improve fertility.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n5">You may be asked to describe an investigation into the effect of temperature on the rate of decomposition. A common method uses milk and <dfn class="term" data-def="An enzyme produced by microorganisms that breaks down fat in milk, causing the pH to drop (become more acidic).">lipase</dfn> enzyme with a pH indicator:</p>
    <ul>
      <li data-narration-id="n6">Milk mixed with lipase and a pH indicator (such as cresol red) is placed in water baths at different temperatures.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n7">As lipase breaks down the fat in milk, fatty acids are produced and the pH drops, causing a colour change in the indicator.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n8">The time taken for the colour change is recorded. A shorter time indicates a faster rate of decomposition.</li>
    </ul>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Food Security</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10"><dfn class="term" data-def="Having enough food to feed a population at all times, with reliable access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food.">Food security</dfn> means having enough food to feed a population at all times. The world’s population is growing rapidly, and several factors threaten our ability to produce enough food.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n11">Threats to food security include:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n12"><strong>Increasing birth rate</strong> — the global population is expected to reach 10 billion by 2050, requiring significantly more food.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Changing diets</strong> — as countries develop economically, people eat more meat, which is less energy-efficient than eating plants.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n14"><strong>New pests and pathogens</strong> — crop diseases and invasive species can destroy harvests.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Environmental change</strong> — climate change alters rainfall patterns and temperatures, reducing yields in some regions.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Cost of agricultural inputs</strong> — rising prices of fuel, fertiliser, and seeds affect farmers’ ability to produce food.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Conflicts</strong> — wars disrupt farming, supply chains, and food distribution.</li>
</ul>



<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n18" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and explain what sustainable food production means using the three key elements: yield, environmental protection, and what?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Sustainable food production means producing enough food for current needs without damaging the environment or reducing the ability of future generations to produce food. This requires balancing yield, environmental protection, and resource management.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n19">Biotechnology</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n20"><dfn class="term" data-def="The use of living organisms or biological processes in technology and industry to produce useful substances.">Biotechnology</dfn> uses living organisms or their products to make useful substances. Modern biotechnology can help address food security challenges.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Mycoprotein</strong> is a high-protein food produced from the fungus <em>Fusarium venenatum</em>. The fungus is grown in large fermenters using glucose syrup as a food source. The resulting biomass is processed into a meat substitute (sold as Quorn™). This is more efficient than rearing animals because:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n22">The fungus grows rapidly and can be harvested continuously.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n23">It requires less land and water than livestock farming.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n24">It produces fewer greenhouse gas emissions.</li>
</ul>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n25">Several approaches can help improve food security sustainably:</p>
    <ul>
      <li data-narration-id="n26"><strong>Biological control:</strong> Using natural predators to control pests instead of chemical pesticides.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n27"><strong>Genetically modified crops:</strong> Engineering crops to resist disease, tolerate drought, or produce higher yields.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n28"><strong>Hydroponics:</strong> Growing plants without soil, using mineral nutrient solutions. This can be done indoors, using less water and space.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n29"><strong>Reducing food waste:</strong> Improving storage, transportation, and distribution to prevent food being lost before it reaches consumers.</li>
    </ul>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n30" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall how Quorn™ is made: which fungus is grown in aerobic conditions with glucose syrup to produce mycoprotein?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p><dfn class="term" data-def="A protein-rich food made from the fungus Fusarium venenatum, grown in fermenters and used as a sustainable meat alternative.">Mycoprotein</dfn> (Quorn™) is produced by growing <em>Fusarium</em> fungus in aerobic conditions with glucose syrup. It is high in protein and fibre, and lower in fat than meat.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n32">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n33">Decomposers (bacteria and fungi) break down dead matter, recycling nutrients. Rate depends on temperature, moisture, oxygen, and number of decomposers.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n34">Food security is threatened by population growth, changing diets, new pests, environmental change, rising costs, and conflict.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n35">Biotechnology (e.g. mycoprotein production from <em>Fusarium</em>) offers sustainable alternatives to traditional animal farming.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n31">In questions about decomposition, always link the factors to <strong>enzyme activity</strong> — decomposers work faster because their enzymes work faster at higher temperatures (up to the optimum). For food security questions, you need to discuss multiple factors and link them to the growing global population. When writing about biotechnology, compare the efficiency and environmental impact with traditional food production.</p>
