--- DESCRIPTION ---
How the kidney filters blood, maintains water balance, and how plant hormones work.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Kidney and Excretion</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The kidneys are vital organs that filter the blood, removing waste products and excess substances while retaining useful molecules. They play a central role in <dfn class="term" data-def="The removal of waste products of metabolism from the body, including urea, carbon dioxide, and excess water.">excretion</dfn> and in maintaining the body’s internal environment through <dfn class="term" data-def="The maintenance of a constant internal environment within the body.">homeostasis</dfn>.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and name the three stages of urine production, plus what happens during filtration in the glomerulus.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The kidneys produce urine in three stages: <strong>filtration</strong> (blood is filtered at high pressure in the glomerulus), <strong>selective reabsorption</strong> (useful substances like glucose, amino acids, and some water are reabsorbed into the blood), and <strong>excretion of waste</strong> (urea, excess ions, and excess water form urine).</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">How the Kidney Works</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Each kidney contains around one million microscopic filtering units called <dfn class="term" data-def="The functional unit of the kidney, consisting of a glomerulus, Bowman's capsule, and tubules where filtration, reabsorption, and secretion occur.">nephrons</dfn>. Blood enters each nephron at the <dfn class="term" data-def="A tiny ball of capillaries inside the Bowman's capsule where blood is filtered at high pressure.">glomerulus</dfn>, a knot of capillaries surrounded by the Bowman’s capsule.</p>

<ol>
  <li data-narration-id="n6"><strong>Filtration:</strong> High blood pressure forces small molecules (water, glucose, urea, ions) out of the blood and into the Bowman’s capsule. Large molecules like proteins and blood cells are too big to pass through.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Selective reabsorption:</strong> As the filtrate passes along the tubules, all glucose is reabsorbed by active transport. Sufficient water and dissolved ions are also reabsorbed to maintain the correct blood concentration.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Formation of urine:</strong> The remaining liquid — containing urea, excess ions, and excess water — continues to the collecting duct and is stored in the bladder as urine.</li>
</ol>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Kidney Failure and Treatment</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n9">If the kidneys fail, waste products build up in the blood and become toxic. There are two main treatments:</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Dialysis:</strong> Blood is passed through a machine containing a partially permeable membrane. Urea and excess ions diffuse from the blood into the <dfn class="term" data-def="A fluid used in dialysis that has the same concentration of useful substances (glucose, ions) as normal blood, so only waste products diffuse out.">dialysis fluid</dfn>, which has the correct concentration of glucose and ions so that these are not lost. Patients typically need dialysis three times per week for several hours.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Kidney transplant:</strong> A healthy kidney from a donor replaces the failed kidney. The donor must be a tissue match to reduce the risk of rejection by the recipient’s immune system. Immunosuppressant drugs are taken for life to prevent rejection.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>



<h2 data-narration-id="n12">Water Balance and ADH</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">The concentration of urine is controlled by a hormone called <dfn class="term" data-def="Anti-diuretic hormone, released by the pituitary gland, which controls the permeability of the kidney tubules to water.">ADH</dfn> (anti-diuretic hormone), released by the pituitary gland. ADH acts on the kidney tubules to control how much water is reabsorbed back into the blood.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and explain what happens to ADH levels and urine volume when blood water concentration is too low versus too high.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>When blood water concentration is <strong>too low</strong> (e.g. after sweating), more ADH is released. This makes the kidney tubules more permeable to water, so more water is reabsorbed and a small volume of concentrated urine is produced. When blood water concentration is <strong>too high</strong> (e.g. after drinking lots), less ADH is released, less water is reabsorbed, and a large volume of dilute urine is produced.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n15">This is an example of <dfn class="term" data-def="A control mechanism where the output of a system reduces the original stimulus, bringing the system back to its set point.">negative feedback</dfn> — the response (water reabsorption) counteracts the change (too much or too little water), bringing the system back to normal.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Plant Hormones</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">Plants produce hormones that coordinate and control growth and responses to the environment. Unlike animal hormones, plant hormones are not produced in glands — they are made in growing regions and move through the plant.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n18"><dfn class="term" data-def="A plant hormone that controls cell elongation, causing shoots to grow towards light (phototropism) and roots to grow downwards (gravitropism).">Auxin</dfn> is the most important plant hormone you need to know. It is produced in the tips of shoots and roots and causes cells to elongate (lengthen).</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n19" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and describe how auxin causes phototropism in shoots and gravitropism in roots, including which side elongates more.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Auxin moves away from light in shoots. This causes the shaded side to have more auxin, so cells on the shaded side elongate more, bending the shoot <strong>towards</strong> the light (<dfn class="term" data-def="The growth response of a plant towards or away from light.">phototropism</dfn>). In roots, auxin has the opposite effect — it inhibits cell elongation, so more auxin on the lower side causes the root to bend <strong>downwards</strong> (<dfn class="term" data-def="The growth response of a plant in response to gravity.">gravitropism</dfn>).</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Commercial Uses of Plant Hormones</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n20">Plant hormones have many uses in agriculture and horticulture:</p>
    <ul>
      <li data-narration-id="n21"><strong>Auxins as weedkillers:</strong> Selective weedkillers contain synthetic auxins that cause broadleaved weeds to grow uncontrollably and die, while narrow-leaved crops (grasses) are unaffected.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n22"><strong>Auxins as rooting powder:</strong> Dipping plant cuttings in auxin stimulates root growth, allowing faster propagation.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n23"><strong>Gibberellins:</strong> Can end seed dormancy, promote flowering, and increase fruit size.</li>
      <li data-narration-id="n24"><strong>Ethene:</strong> Controls fruit ripening. Fruit can be picked unripe (for easier transport) and treated with ethene to ripen before sale.</li>
    </ul>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n26">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n27">Kidneys filter blood through filtration, selective reabsorption, and urine formation. Kidney failure is treated by dialysis or transplant.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n28">ADH controls water balance via negative feedback — more ADH means more water reabsorbed and less (concentrated) urine.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n29">Auxin controls phototropism and gravitropism in plants. It promotes cell elongation in shoots but inhibits it in roots. Plant hormones have many commercial uses.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n25">Exam questions about ADH often test whether you understand the negative feedback loop. A common mistake is stating that “more ADH means more urine” — the opposite is true. More ADH means more water reabsorption, so <strong>less</strong> urine (and it is more concentrated). For plant hormones, always specify which <em>side</em> of the shoot or root has more auxin and what effect this has on cell elongation.</p>
