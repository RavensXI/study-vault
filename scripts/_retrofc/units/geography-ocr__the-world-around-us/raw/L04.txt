--- DESCRIPTION ---
Definitions of development, indicators like GNI and HDI, and the patterns of ACs, EDCs and LIDCs.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">A Divided World</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">If you visited Switzerland and Mozambique on consecutive weeks, you would experience two different planets. Average life expectancy is 84 years in one and 60 in the other; school enrolment is 99% versus 70%; GDP per person is over 100,000 USD versus 600. Yet both are members of the United Nations. <dfn class="term" data-def="The process by which countries become wealthier, more advanced and improve quality of life for their people.">Development</dfn> is the technical name for these gaps — and explaining them is one of geography's central tasks.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Development is not just about money. It has three dimensions. <strong>Economic development</strong> measures wealth and jobs. <strong>Social development</strong> measures health, education and quality of life. <strong>Environmental development</strong> measures whether growth is sustainable for future generations. <dfn class="term" data-def="Development that meets present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.">Sustainable development</dfn> brings the three together — the goal accepted by the UN in 2015 through the 17 Sustainable Development Goals.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Measuring Development</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Different <dfn class="term" data-def="A measurable factor that helps describe a country's level of development, such as income or life expectancy.">indicators</dfn> reveal different aspects of development. The trick is to use several together rather than relying on any one statistic. Recent decades have seen indicators evolve as understanding of development has deepened.</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n6"><strong>GNI per capita</strong> — gross national income divided by population, measured in USD. Simple and globally comparable, but ignores inequality and informal economies.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Human Development Index (HDI)</strong> — combines life expectancy, years of schooling and GNI per capita into one score from 0 to 1. Recognises that development is more than money. Norway and Switzerland top the HDI; Niger and Chad sit at the bottom.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Internet users per 100 people</strong> — a 21st-century indicator that captures connectivity and digital infrastructure.</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n9">Each indicator has advantages and limits. GNI per capita is comparable but blind to inequality (a country with average income of 20,000 USD could be very equal or very unequal). HDI is more rounded but slow to update. Internet usage misses older or rural populations. Strong development analysis uses multiple indicators rather than relying on one.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n10" data-revision-tip="Cover this and write down three development indicators with one advantage and one drawback for each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>GNI per capita measures average wealth but ignores inequality. HDI combines life expectancy, schooling and income but updates slowly. Internet users per 100 captures connectivity but misses older and rural populations. Use multiple indicators for an accurate picture.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n11">Country Categories</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n12">The World Bank and UN now use a three-way classification rather than the old ‘developed/developing’ binary:</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n13"><strong>Advanced Countries (ACs)</strong> — high incomes, high HDI, mature service economies. Examples: USA, UK, Japan, Germany.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n14"><strong>Emerging and Developing Countries (EDCs)</strong> — rising incomes, expanding manufacturing, fast-growing cities. Examples: China, India, Brazil, Mexico, Indonesia.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n15"><strong>Low-Income Developing Countries (LIDCs)</strong> — low incomes, agriculture-based economies, weak infrastructure, often heavily dependent on aid. Examples: Ethiopia, Mozambique, Bangladesh, Cambodia.</li>
</ul>

<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Consequences of Uneven Development</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Uneven development matters because it shapes almost everything else in a country. People in LIDCs face higher infant mortality, lower educational attainment and shorter lives — an average baby born in Niger has a one-in-twelve chance of dying before its fifth birthday. International migration flows largely from low-development to high-development regions, putting strain on receiving countries and depriving sending countries of skilled workers. Unequal countries struggle to attract investment; without investment, they struggle to develop. The cycle is hard to break, which is why aid, trade and education policies matter so much in international politics. Climate change deepens these gaps further: LIDCs contribute least to global emissions but suffer the worst from droughts, floods and food-supply shocks — an injustice highlighted at every UN climate summit since the 2015 Paris Agreement. Understanding development gaps is therefore not just an academic exercise; it sits at the heart of the world's biggest political and economic decisions.</p>

  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Three categories: Advanced Countries (USA, Japan, UK), Emerging and Developing Countries (China, India, Brazil), and Low-Income Developing Countries (Ethiopia, Mozambique, Bangladesh). The pattern has shifted dramatically since 1990 as China and India joined the EDC group.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n21">Putting It Together</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22">Development is more than wealth: it covers economic, social and environmental progress. Indicators like GNI, HDI and internet usage capture different aspects, and the AC-EDC-LIDC classification reveals patterns that the old binary missed. The next two lessons explore why this uneven world exists and what can be done about it.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n24">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">Development has economic, social and environmental dimensions; sustainable development brings the three together.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n26">GNI per capita, HDI and internet usage are widely-used indicators, each with its own strengths and limits.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n27">Countries are now classified as Advanced (ACs), Emerging and Developing (EDCs) or Low-Income Developing (LIDCs); China and India have shifted from LIDC to EDC since 1990.</li>
</ul>
