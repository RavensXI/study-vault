--- DESCRIPTION ---
Internal vs external recruitment, selection stages, and the contract types businesses use.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n1">When a business grows, loses a worker or launches a new product, it needs to fill a job. <dfn class="term" data-def="The process of attracting and choosing new employees for a business.">Recruitment</dfn> and <dfn class="term" data-def="The process of deciding which applicant is the best fit for a job.">selection</dfn> are the linked processes of finding applicants and picking the best one. Get it right and productivity, quality and customer service all rise; get it wrong and the business spends money training someone who soon leaves.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n2">Internal vs External Recruitment</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n3"><dfn class="term" data-def="Filling a vacancy from inside the business, such as by promotion or transfer.">Internal recruitment</dfn> means filling the vacancy from existing staff — by promotion, transfer or re-deployment. John Lewis, the employee-owned department store, often promotes partners from the shop floor into management roles. Internal recruits already know the company’s culture, so induction is shorter and cheaper, and promoting from within motivates the rest of the team.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n4"><dfn class="term" data-def="Filling a vacancy by hiring someone from outside the business.">External recruitment</dfn> means hiring from outside — through job adverts, recruitment agencies, social media or universities. Fresh applicants bring new ideas and skills. A tech firm switching to AI might need engineers it cannot grow internally. The downside is cost: advertising fees, longer induction and the risk the new hire doesn’t fit.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Close this and write down two advantages of internal recruitment and two advantages of external recruitment.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Internal recruitment is faster, cheaper and motivates staff — but creates another vacancy and limits fresh ideas. External recruitment widens the talent pool but costs more and takes longer.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">The Selection Process</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Selection starts with a <dfn class="term" data-def="A study of the tasks, skills and responsibilities required for a job.">job analysis</dfn> — the business works out exactly what the role involves. This feeds into a <dfn class="term" data-def="A document setting out the duties, responsibilities and working conditions of a job.">job description</dfn> (what the post-holder will do) and a <dfn class="term" data-def="A document listing the skills, qualifications and personal qualities an ideal candidate should have.">person specification</dfn> (the skills and qualities the ideal candidate needs). The vacancy is then advertised, CVs are screened and the best candidates invited to interview.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n8">Interviews are the most common selection method, but they can be unreliable — good talkers may not be good workers. Many businesses now use extra methods: skills tests (a coding challenge at Dyson), presentations (marketing roles at Unilever), group tasks (graduate schemes at PwC) and assessment centres that combine several tests over a day. References from previous employers are usually the final step before a job offer.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n9">Why Good Recruitment Matters</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n10">An effective recruitment process raises <dfn class="term" data-def="The amount of output produced per worker in a given time.">productivity</dfn> because skilled staff complete more tasks to a higher standard. It improves quality and customer service, because employees who match the role make fewer mistakes. It also supports <dfn class="term" data-def="The ability of a business to keep its employees rather than lose them.">staff retention</dfn> — when new hires fit the role, they stay longer, cutting the cost of re-recruiting.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n11">Contracts of Employment</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Once someone is hired, their working terms are set out in a <dfn class="term" data-def="A legal agreement between employer and employee setting out working hours, pay and conditions.">contract of employment</dfn>. UK law requires one to be issued on or before an employee’s first day.</p>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n13">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A contract for employees working a full working week, typically 35-40 hours.">full-time contract</dfn> usually means 35–40 hours a week. It gives the employer a committed worker and the employee a stable income and holiday pay — used for most core roles at Premier League clubs’ academies and at retailers like M&S.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n14">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A contract for employees working fewer than full-time hours, with pro-rata pay and benefits.">part-time contract</dfn> covers fewer hours — perhaps 16 a week. It helps parents, students and carers. For the business, part-time contracts add flexibility and lower fixed wage costs.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n15">A <dfn class="term" data-def="An arrangement where two people split the duties of one full-time role between them.">job share</dfn> lets two employees split one full-time role. The business keeps a full-time post filled but gets twice the range of skills.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n16">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A contract with no guaranteed minimum hours, where workers are called in as needed.">zero-hour contract</dfn> guarantees no set hours. Workers are called in as needed — common in hospitality and retail. It gives businesses maximum flexibility and keeps costs down when demand is quiet, but workers have no guaranteed income and little security.</li>
</ul>

  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">

    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n17">Full-time contracts help a business build loyal, experienced teams and spread training costs over many years of service. Employees get job security, a clear career path and a predictable wage. Part-time contracts help businesses match staffing to demand — a café can run three staff at lunchtime and one mid-afternoon — and let businesses tap into workers who cannot commit to full hours, such as parents during school hours, widening the talent pool.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Costs of Getting It Wrong</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19">Recruitment is expensive. Estimates from the CIPD (the UK’s HR professional body) put the cost of replacing an average employee at a few thousand pounds once advert fees, manager time, lost output during the vacancy and induction training are added up. For skilled roles the figure is far higher. That is why an ‘effective’ recruitment and selection process matters — it cuts wasted spend and keeps productivity steady while the vacancy is filled. A sloppy process that picks the wrong person means repeating the whole cycle a few months later.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Effective recruitment also feeds into the wider business. A Premier League academy that recruits the right coaches develops better young players and protects the first-team pipeline. A supermarket that hires well at checkout reduces queues, raises customer service ratings and lifts repeat visits. In short, recruitment is not just a HR chore — it is a competitive advantage.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n21" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: name the four main contract types and write one sentence on when each would be appropriate.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Full-time, part-time, job share and zero-hour are the four main contract types. Each trades off flexibility for the business against security for the employee.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n23">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n24">Internal recruitment is quick and motivating; external recruitment brings fresh skills but costs more.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">Selection uses job analysis, job description, person specification, advert, shortlist, interview and references.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n26">Full-time, part-time, job share and zero-hour contracts balance business flexibility against employee security.</li>
</ul>
