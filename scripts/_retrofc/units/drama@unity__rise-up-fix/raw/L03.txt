--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Key Moment Three: Diane Nash Phone Conversation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">This scene occurs at approximately 27 minutes into the performance. It begins when Bobby Kennedy, the Attorney General, asks “Who the hell is Diane Nash?” <dfn class="term" data-def="A student who organised the second wave of Freedom Riders, who travelled from Nashville to Birmingham, Alabama, despite knowing the dangers involved.">Diane Nash</dfn> was a real-life student activist at Fisk University in Nashville who organised a second wave of Freedom Riders after the first group were attacked.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Staging and Positioning</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">The scene had two distinct staging arrangements. In the <dfn class="term" data-def="A dramatic convention where the action moves back in time to show events that happened earlier, helping the audience understand context and motivation.">flashback</dfn> to the university, Diane Nash (Dayz) was positioned centre stage with CJ, Em, and Ty sat on the floor in a circle surrounding her. They each pulled their knees to their chests and made direct eye contact with Diane Nash, straining their necks to see her. This effectively highlighted that the students were <strong>interested in the cause</strong> and listening intently.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Diane Nash’s <strong>higher level</strong> in this moment reminded the audience she was in control and taking charge of the situation. The other actors sitting at a lower level showed she held authority over both the student halls and the wider context of the Freedom Rides.</p>

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n6">During the phone call itself, Ty was positioned centre stage right (CSR), Em and CJ sat on the stools upstage centre (USC), and Dayz in role as Diane Nash was centre stage left (CSL). The stools were set at the back of the space — USR one of the larger stools, then working across to almost centre stage where the two small stools were, with the final taller stool USL.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">The three steel flats remained at the back of the space. On the flat USR was the confederate flag, the centre flat held the Ku Klux Klan hat, Uncle George’s cowboy hat, and Bull Connor’s hat. The flat USL had President Kennedy’s red tie and the LWG’s headband — creating a visual reminder of all the characters and the story so far.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n8">Physicality: Diane Nash vs the US Representative</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9">Diane Nash remained <strong>stoic and strong</strong>, rooted on the spot throughout the conversation. She maintained a straight, strong, tall back and neck with her hands placed elegantly in front of her. This physicality reinforced that she was grounded in her beliefs and would not be moved.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n10">On the line “You elected me as your leader,” Dayz used <dfn class="term" data-def="The degree of physical tension in a performer's body, ranging from completely relaxed to fully tensed, used to show emotion, power, or control.">strong muscle tension</dfn> to outstretch her right arm with the palm facing up on “you” — referencing the students. On “me,” she quickly placed her palm across her chest in a patriotic fashion, reinforcing that this issue was close to her heart.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n11" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down what you recall about US representative (played by Ty) contrasted.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — Contrasting Physicality</div>
<p>The US representative (played by Ty) <strong>contrasted</strong> sharply with Diane Nash. He used quick-paced exaggerated movements and a fast pace of speech, showing he was under pressure. Meanwhile, CJ and Em used <dfn class="term" data-def="When multiple performers move in a coordinated, synchronised way to create a visual effect or communicate a shared experience.">ensemble movement</dfn> to quickly turn their heads from side to side depending on which character was speaking — like watching a tennis match — directing the audience’s attention to whoever had the dialogue.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Voice Techniques in the Diane Nash Scene</span>

</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n12">CJ in role as Bobby Kennedy said “who the hell is Diane Nash?” using a <strong>loud volume and authoritative tone with a slow, considered pace</strong>. He elongated the word “hell,” suggesting to the audience he did not know who she was and was trying to recall the name.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n13">Dayz as Diane Nash used a <strong>loud and authoritative tone</strong> when addressing the students, signifying she was genuine and determined. On the line “I do not shrink from it” (referring to being jailed for protesting), she used a <dfn class="term" data-def="A sharp, detached rhythm of speech where words are delivered in short, separated bursts to create emphasis or tension.">staccato rhythm</dfn> with clear pauses between words, slowing the sentence down to reinforce she would not back down.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14">The US representative said “Young woman, do you not understand? You are going to get somebody killed!” using a loud volume, aggressive tone, and fast pace. This patronising delivery showed he was feeling the pressure of Diane Nash’s refusal to back down.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15">The most powerful line was: “We signed our wills, we know someone will get killed.” This was delivered in a <strong>sombre and serious tone</strong>, highlighting to the audience the extremely serious nature of the protests and the grave danger the Freedom Riders placed themselves in. This line should have a huge impact on audience members, making them question how far they would go for what they believe in.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<span>Lighting and Sound in the Diane Nash Scene</span>

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n16"><strong>Lighting:</strong> A white lighting wash was used at the start when Ty answered the phone. When the location changed to Bobby Kennedy’s office, the wash was removed and a white <dfn class="term" data-def="A type of spotlight that produces a soft-edged beam of light, often used to isolate a character or highlight a specific area of the stage.">Fresnel spotlight</dfn> was placed on Bobby Kennedy and the US representative. CJ and Em could not be seen. A white wash with a blue tinge was then used to show the change of location — the blue highlighted that the Black community felt isolated. The white wash returned for the phone call so all actors were visible.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n17"><strong>Sound:</strong> A snapshot of the American national anthem was used to open the scene, highlighting that CJ was taking on the role of Bobby Kennedy. A staccato rhythm with a deep, threatening tone signalled the change of location to Diane Nash’s flashback. Slow-paced, emotive, sombre music played at a low level when Diane Nash addressed the students, adding to the emotional atmosphere. Another snapshot of the anthem after “It is up to you” showed the shift back to Bobby Kennedy’s office.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Key Moment Four: CJ ‘Reminder’ Scene</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19">This scene occurs at approximately 9 minutes into the performance, during the “Hank scene” where the audience meets Thora, Merna, Jim, and other Freedom Riders. The actor CJ asks “like what?” out of role, and Ty breaks from his role as Jim (a White man who joined the Freedom Riders) and says “you can’t have forgotten already? Ok…go for it.”</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n20">The Diamond Formation</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n21">At the start of the moment, the actors were positioned in a <strong>diamond shape</strong>:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n22">
<li><strong>Ty</strong> (as Jim) — USL, standing on a small stool at the <strong>highest level</strong>, reflecting the power status of White people in 1960s society</li>
<li><strong>Em</strong> (as Thora) — USC</li>
<li><strong>Dayz</strong> (as Merna) — CS</li>
<li><strong>CJ</strong> (as Hank) — DSR, at the lowest point</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n23">Having Jim (White) on the highest level and Hank (Black) at the lowest, with large <dfn class="term" data-def="The physical distance between performers on stage, used to show relationships, power dynamics, or emotional connections between characters.">proxemics</dfn> between them, highlighted the large disparity in power and the inequality of the society.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n24" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. What does USC stand for? List each part from memory.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — CJ Steps Forward</div>
<p>When CJ asked the question (breaking from character), he took a step forward, putting himself on the <strong>same level</strong> as Em and Dayz. This simple movement showed that today’s society believes all people should be treated equally regardless of skin colour. Ty then stepped down from his stool to join the female actors — and all three quickly <strong>circled CJ</strong>, forcing him USC and almost pinning him against the steel flat.</p>
</div>

<figure class="diagram"><img src="https://pub-aeb94e100e5a48f4a133be5bf206aecb.r2.dev/drama/rise-up/diagram_cj_reminder_layout.jpg" alt="Diamond formation stage layout for CJ Reminder scene"></figure>
<h2 data-narration-id="n25">Physicality: The Circling Technique</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n26">CJ used <strong>closed physicality</strong> — closed body language and a hunched posture. He held his hands in front of his chest as if to create a barrier from the other actors circling him. This effectively portrayed how intimidated his character felt. When the sound collage reached full volume, CJ covered his face with both palms to “hide” from the experience, highlighting his character’s distress.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n27">Dayz said “You were told to shut up…” so aggressively that her whole physicality jerked as she shouted. Despite having her back to the audience, the venom in her voice was reflected by jarring movements in her body. She stepped forward, reducing proxemics to CJ, pushed her hands forward in an aggressive gesture, and used a <strong>peacocking motion</strong> with her head to further intimidate him. She maintained strong muscle tension throughout.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Voice Techniques in the CJ Reminder Scene</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n28">“Like what?” and “I’ve got a lot to remember! What is is?” were spoken by CJ using a <strong>cocky tone and slow pace</strong>. This showed his character did not understand the significance of his question and was nonchalant about the issues the Freedom Riders faced — highlighting his ignorance.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n29">All three Black actors used a <strong>loud volume and aggressive tone</strong> when shouting at CJ, creating tension and building an uneasy mood that helped the audience understand how the Freedom Riders would have felt. When CJ could not cope, he shouted “shhh, shh, shut up, shut up, stop, stop, stop! Harsh man” — the loud volume, panicked tone, and quick pace showed he now understood the intimidation and fear.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n30">Immediately after, Ty said “well that’s how they did it” in a <strong>calm, matter-of-fact tone</strong> at a quieter volume. This shift highlighted to the audience that Ty was satisfied CJ had learnt his lesson and understood the terror the Freedom Riders experienced.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n31" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down what you recall about CJ begged them to stop.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — The Aftermath</div>
<p>When CJ begged them to stop, all characters relaxed their posture, loosened their muscle tension, and returned to their “usual” relaxed physicality as the British actors. This clear shift showed the ordeal was over and the rehearsal would continue. CJ physically <strong>slumped over</strong> at the end — a clear sign to the audience that the experience had taken a physically and emotionally exhausting toll, reinforcing the plight of Black people in 1960s society.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n33">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n34">The Diane Nash scene uses <strong>contrasting physicality</strong> (stoic vs frantic) and <strong>authoritative voice</strong> to show courage and resistance, with lighting changes marking shifts between locations.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n35">The CJ Reminder scene uses a <strong>diamond formation</strong> with levels reflecting power status, a <strong>circling technique</strong> to build intimidation, and a <strong>sound collage</strong> to create climax.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n36">Both scenes demonstrate how actors can communicate complex themes of <strong>inequality, intimidation, and courage</strong> through carefully chosen voice and physicality techniques without any set or costume changes.</li>
</ul>
