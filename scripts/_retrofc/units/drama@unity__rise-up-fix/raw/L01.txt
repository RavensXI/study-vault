<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Introduction to Rise Up</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">For Section B of your Drama exam, you must write about a <dfn class="term" data-def="A performance watched in person by the audience, as opposed to a recorded or filmed version.">live theatre</dfn> performance you have seen. You need to analyse and evaluate the acting, design elements, and how meaning was communicated to the audience.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">The performance you watched was <strong>‘Rise Up’</strong> by Lisa Evans for the Theatre Centre, performed at Unity College’s Auditorium. Section B is an essay question worth <strong>30 marks</strong> — 20 marks for your analysis and evaluation, and 10 marks for your knowledge and understanding of drama, which you show through <dfn class="term" data-def="Specialist vocabulary related to drama, such as proxemics, levels, pace, tone, and staging terms.">drama terminology</dfn>. The whole paper lasts 1 hour 30 minutes, so plan about 30 minutes for this section.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. List the exam technique advice given here.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — Section B Requirements</div>
<p>Every Section B answer <strong>must</strong> state the name of the play, where you watched it, and when. You will be asked to either <strong>analyse</strong> or <strong>evaluate</strong> an aspect of the performance. Always include: a clear example of what you observed, why it was effective, its impact on you and the audience, and how it could be developed.</p>
</div>


<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n31" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Can you recall the three-part paragraph structure and the thumb actions?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — Paragraph Structure for Section B</div>
  <p>Every paragraph in your Section B answer must follow this structure:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Thumbs up (Positive):</strong> Give an example of something that was successful and justify why it was effective.</li>
    <li><strong>Thumb towards yourself (Impact):</strong> Explain the impact on you or the audience — what did it make you think, feel, or understand?</li>
    <li><strong>Thumbs down (Development):</strong> Suggest a development and justify why that change would be even more effective.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Remember: every paragraph needs all three — positive, impact, and development.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Synopsis: The Freedom Riders</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">‘Rise Up’ is a <dfn class="term" data-def="A play performed within the framework of another play, where characters are shown rehearsing or creating a performance.">play within a play</dfn>. A group of four British actors meet to discuss and educate the audience about the Freedom Riders of 1960s America. The play explores how a group of Black and White protesters set off on an epic journey from Washington to the Southern States of America to expose racism.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Despite laws making <dfn class="term" data-def="The enforced separation of different racial groups in a country or community, particularly in public facilities.">segregation</dfn> illegal, Black people were still not welcome in White establishments and were not allowed to sit where they wished on buses and coaches. The Freedom Riders wanted to show that this discrimination was still happening. They encountered violent racist protests in which riders were badly beaten, and they were often arrested or dumped in Ku Klux Klan territories by the police. The American government did not protect them at first — it asked them to stop instead.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n8">The play explores several important themes:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n9">
<li><strong>Equality and Racism</strong> — the central focus, demonstrated by the Freedom Riders’ journey and the violent opposition they faced</li>
<li><strong>Relationships</strong> — shown through the bonds between the Freedom Riders, and between the Little White Girl and her racist family</li>
<li><strong>Violence</strong> — demonstrated by the attack on the Freedom Riders’ bus in Anniston, Alabama</li>
<li><strong>Ambitions</strong> — shown through Diane Nash’s determination to achieve equality through non-violent protest</li>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Style: Abstract with Naturalism</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">The playwright states at the start: “Form: Fluid storytelling in which actors can move from character to character with precise and efficient movement. The piece should be played with truth but not naturalistically.”</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12">The style of the play was <dfn class="term" data-def="A non-realistic style of performance that uses symbolic staging, movement, and design to communicate meaning rather than imitating real life.">abstract</dfn>, with moments of <dfn class="term" data-def="A style of performance that aims to recreate real life on stage as accurately as possible, with realistic behaviour, speech, and settings.">naturalism</dfn>. This allowed the audience to see how the characters felt and interacted with each other. The abstract style meant performers faced out towards the audience and used symbolic staging rather than realistic settings.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n13" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Define rise Up in your own words.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — Genre</div>
<p>Rise Up is <strong>documentary drama</strong>. All the information is based on documented evidence and facts about the real Freedom Riders, though some names were changed to protect characters. It is also <strong>educational and informative</strong>, designed to teach the audience about what actually happened in 1960s America. The meaning of the play is to encourage audiences to solve problems without violence and to question their own opinions and beliefs.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n14">The Cast</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15">Rise Up was performed by just four actors who used <dfn class="term" data-def="When an actor plays more than one character in a performance, switching between roles using costume, voice, or physicality changes.">multi-rolling</dfn> to portray many different characters throughout the performance:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n16">
<li><strong>Em</strong> (Emma Dennis-Edwards) — a Black female performer who played multiple roles including a Freedom Rider in the bus explosion and an old Black lady offering refuge</li>
<li><strong>CJ</strong> (Sam Kacher) — the only White performer, who played Governor Patterson, Bobby Kennedy, and the Little White Girl</li>
<li><strong>Dayz</strong> (Kimisha Lewis) — a Black female performer who played Diane Nash and a Freedom Rider on the bus</li>
<li><strong>Ty</strong> (Edward Nkom) — a Black male performer who played Uncle George, Jim, and a young Freedom Rider</li>
</ul>

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n17">The performance used several drama conventions that you should reference in your exam:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n18">
<li><strong>Narration</strong> — actors stepped out of character to narrate events directly to the audience, helping them understand the context</li>
<li><strong>Flashback</strong> — used to show historical events such as Diane Nash addressing students at Fisk University</li>
<li><strong>Sound collage</strong> — multiple voices speaking simultaneously to build tension and create a climax</li>
<li><strong>Ensemble movement</strong> — actors moving in synchronisation to show unity or to mark key moments</li>
<li><strong>Choral work</strong> — actors speaking in unison to show shared opinions (e.g. the White shoppers)</li>
<li><strong>Facing out</strong> — all performers face the audience, reinforcing the abstract style</li>
<li><strong>Marking the moment</strong> — slow motion used during the bus explosion to emphasise its significance</li>
</ul>
</div></div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n22">The SEAS Paragraph Structure</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n23">Every paragraph in your Section B essay should follow the <strong>SEAS</strong> structure:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n24">
<li><strong>S</strong> — <strong>State</strong> what you observed (a clear example from the performance)</li>
<li><strong>E</strong> — <strong>Explain</strong> why it was effective, using drama terminology</li>
<li><strong>A</strong> — <strong>Audience</strong> — discuss the impact on you and the audience</li>
<li><strong>S</strong> — <strong>Suggest</strong> a development — how could it have been improved and why?</li>
</ul>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n25" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. List the exam technique advice given here.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — Useful Evaluation Phrases</div>
<p>Build these phrases into your writing: “This was effective because…”, “In my opinion I thought…”, “However, in reflection…”, “I particularly enjoyed…”, and “I feel that this could have been developed because…”</p>
</div>

<ul data-narration-id="n32">
<li data-narration-id="n28">‘Rise Up’ by Lisa Evans is a <strong>documentary drama</strong> performed in an <strong>abstract style with moments of naturalism</strong>, telling the story of the Freedom Riders in 1960s America.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n29">Four actors use <strong>multi-rolling</strong> to play many characters, with conventions including narration, flashback, sound collage, and ensemble movement.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n30">Your Section B essay must state where and when you watched the play, follow the <strong>SEAS paragraph structure</strong>, and use <strong>drama terminology</strong> throughout — 10 of the 30 marks are for your knowledge and understanding of drama.</li>
</ul>

A good answer shows that you <strong>analyse</strong> and <strong>evaluate</strong> specific performance and design choices. Always use the SEAS paragraph structure: State what you saw, Explain why it was effective using drama terminology, describe the Audience impact, and Suggest a development. Remember — 10 of the 30 marks are for your knowledge and understanding of drama, which you show through terminology, so make every sentence count.</p>
