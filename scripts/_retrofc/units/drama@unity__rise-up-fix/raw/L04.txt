<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Costume Design in Rise Up</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The costume design in Rise Up was deliberately <dfn class="term" data-def="Using the fewest possible elements to achieve the desired effect, allowing quick character transitions and keeping the audience focused on the actors' skills.">minimalistic</dfn>. All four actors wore <strong>base costumes only</strong> throughout the performance, with no full costume changes at any point. This simplicity was a deliberate design choice that allowed the actors to change roles quickly and kept the performance flowing at a fast pace.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Individual Costume Analysis</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n3">Each actor’s costume communicated something about their character:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n4">
<li><strong>Em</strong> — wore all black: a male-style shirt, black leggings, and black boots. This reflected the society being explored in the play where women were treated as “underdogs.” By wearing a male-style outfit, it showed she still had to take on male characteristics to be seen as successful.</li>
<li><strong>Ty</strong> — wore all black: formal shirt, trousers, and shoes. This demonstrated his <strong>respect for the Freedom Riders</strong> — he wore his best clothes to honour their story.</li>
<li><strong>Dayz</strong> — wore a black and white floral knee-length dress. This reinforced her femininity and showed that you can be successful as a woman. The <strong>black and white colours</strong> were symbolic: they showed the two races striving for equality and that attitudes to racism are simply “black and white” — right or wrong.</li>
<li><strong>CJ</strong> — wore black skinny jeans and a black polo shirt. This <strong>contrasted</strong> with Ty’s formal costume, implying CJ did not think the Freedom Riders were worth dressing up for, showing a lack of initial respect.</li>
</ul>
</div></div>
</div>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. List the exam technique advice given here.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — White Gloves as Costume Detail</div>
<p>The most significant costume addition was the <strong>white gloves</strong> worn by Em and Dayz when playing the White shopper characters. Each wore a single glove on opposite hands (Dayz left, Em right), reinforcing they were “as one” and their racist thinking was aligned. For the exam, you could suggest a development: CJ could tuck his shirt in when he realises the truth about racism, or Em could wear red lipstick to symbolise her power.</p>
</div>


<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n35" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Can you recall the three-part paragraph structure for Section B?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — Remember the Thumbs Rule</div>
  <p>When writing about design or performance in Section B, every paragraph must include: <strong>Thumbs up</strong> (what was successful and why), <strong>Thumb towards yourself</strong> (impact on you or the audience), and <strong>Thumbs down</strong> (a development and why it would be more effective). Use this structure for every point you make about costume, set, lighting, sound, or acting.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Set Design</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">The set was minimal but highly effective. No specific area of the stage was used for a particular location — this was effective because it showed that everyone was accountable for their actions regardless of where they were.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>The Steel Flats</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n8">Three <strong>steel flats</strong> were positioned at the back of the space. These were effective because:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n9">
<li>The silver surface allowed the audience to <strong>reflect on their own behaviour</strong> — the reflective material was symbolic</li>
<li>They were made to look like a <strong>Greyhound bus</strong>, with designs on the side and rivet holes for windows</li>
<li>They allowed the actors to <strong>map the journey</strong> of the Freedom Riders in red chalk, showing the dangerous route</li>
<li>At the end of the performance, they were <strong>turned around</strong> to reveal real images of real Freedom Riders — the black and white photographs symbolised that there is only right and wrong when it comes to racism</li>
<li>The images were <strong>yellowed</strong> to show the events happened a long time ago</li>
</ul>
</div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>The Stools</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n10">Four <dfn class="term" data-def="Pieces of furniture or set that can serve multiple purposes during a performance, changing their meaning depending on how they are used.">versatile</dfn> stools were used throughout — two small and two tall:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n11">
<li>The <strong>different heights</strong> clearly showed <strong>power</strong> — Uncle George stood on the tall stool when the Freedom Riders sat on small stools to show they were powerless</li>
<li>They were <strong>silver</strong> to imply they were taken from a diner where racism was prevalent, connecting to the sit-in protests</li>
<li>They were <strong>turned upside down</strong> to create a prison — the legs suggesting prison bars</li>
<li>They served as bus seats when the Freedom Riders gripped their sides during the explosion scene</li>
</ul>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n12">Props</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">A large number of <dfn class="term" data-def="Small hand-held objects used on stage to help distinguish characters, establish setting, or aid the audience in suspending disbelief.">props</dfn> were used in Rise Up. Many helped create believable and easily recognisable characters, showing that it was the <em>people who use these objects</em> who made the decision to be racist.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Key props included:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n15">
<li><strong>Red chalk</strong> — used to track the Freedom Riders’ journey on the steel flats. The red colour symbolised the danger and potential for death, as well as the passion the characters felt about equality. The markings resembled a heartbeat monitor, showing the Freedom Riders could lose their lives at any moment.</li>
<li><strong>Confederate flag and baseball cap</strong> — worn by Southern States characters, reminding the audience of the racist attitudes in the South</li>
<li><strong>Cowboy hat</strong> (Uncle George) — reminded the audience he was from the Southern States</li>
<li><strong>Cigar</strong> (Governor Patterson) — symbolised he was an offensive character who did not care for those around him</li>
<li><strong>White hat</strong> (Bull Connor) — showed he was a Southern man but more educated than a cowboy</li>
<li><strong>Ku Klux Klan hood</strong> — a powerful reminder that the Klan were prevalent during this time</li>
<li><strong>Red tie and microphone</strong> (President Kennedy) — the red showed control over danger; the microphone showed the President’s voice was heard worldwide</li>
<li><strong>Dayz’s notebook</strong> (stolen by CJ) — reminded the audience that all the information was factual</li>
<li><strong>LWG’s bow</strong> — which CJ refused to wear, demonstrating the “play within a play” convention</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n16" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Describe the process for uncle George always remained stage step by step.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — Symbolic Staging</div>
<p>Uncle George always remained <strong>stage left</strong>, implying he was not an honest or genuine character. The Little White Girl was usually positioned <strong>stage right</strong>, reinforcing her kind, questioning nature. This consistent positioning helped the audience quickly identify moral alignment.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Lighting Design</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n18">The lighting in Rise Up was relatively simplistic because it was performed by a touring theatre company. It remained mostly a white <dfn class="term" data-def="A broad spread of light covering a large area of the stage, used to create atmosphere or establish a general mood.">lighting wash</dfn> throughout, with the intention that actors should be visible at all times.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
<button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
<span>Key Lighting Moments and Possible Developments</span>
<svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n19">Different lighting states were used for different key moments:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n20">
<li><strong>Bus explosion</strong> — orange shaft of light increasing in intensity, white wash fading, creating shadows that suggested a crowd of Freedom Riders</li>
<li><strong>Bull Connor scene</strong> — lights at low intensity, lit from above with steel lighting creating shadows and atmosphere</li>
<li><strong>Final scenes</strong> — lit from all directions at high intensity, white light symbolising honesty and hope</li>
<li><strong>White Shoppers</strong> — white and blue wash, with intensity increasing when the LWG was introduced to symbolise innocence</li>
<li><strong>Prison</strong> — lit from behind at low intensity, tight spotlight reminding the audience of imprisonment</li>
</ul>
<p data-narration-id="n21">Possible developments you could suggest in an exam:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n22">
<li>A <dfn class="term" data-def="A metal template placed in front of a light source to project a pattern or shape onto the stage, such as leaves, windows, or prison bars.">gobo</dfn> with a leaf pattern when Bull Connor abandoned the Freedom Riders, to show the forest location</li>
<li>A slow red strobe when Bull Connor drops the Freedom Riders to symbolise danger and a police car</li>
<li>A straw wash changing to intense red during the bus explosion, with pulsating orange and red to create the illusion of flames and symbolise the Freedom Riders’ heartbeats</li>
<li>A cold blue wash on the White shoppers with a white spotlight on the LWG, to further isolate her innocence from their racism</li>
</ul>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n23">Sound Design</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n24">Sound was used effectively throughout Rise Up. Key examples included:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n25">
<li><strong>“Hit the Road Jack” by Ray Charles</strong> (1961) — historically accurate to the period, reinforcing the cultural context of the Freedom Rides</li>
<li><strong>Bus explosion sounds</strong> — banging on the side of the bus, crowd noise building, and a small explosion sound, all creating <dfn class="term" data-def="Sound that characters on stage can hear, as opposed to non-diegetic sound which is only heard by the audience.">diegetic</dfn> atmosphere</li>
<li><strong>American national anthem</strong> — snippets used to signal Bobby Kennedy scenes and location changes</li>
<li><strong>Sound collage</strong> — multiple voices layered to build tension and create climax (used in the CJ reminder scene and the bus explosion)</li>
<li><strong>Children’s voices</strong> — sharing opinions about racism to conclude the performance, bringing the message into the present day</li>
</ul>

<h2 data-narration-id="n26">Structuring Your Section B Essay</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n27">Use the <strong>SEAS paragraph structure</strong> for every paragraph in your response:</p>
<ul data-narration-id="n28">
<li><strong>S — State</strong> what you observed: “In the bus explosion scene, the lighting changed from a white wash to a small orange shaft of light on the middle steel flat…”</li>
<li><strong>E — Explain</strong> why it was effective: “This was effective because it clearly showed the audience the bus was alight without requiring a realistic fire effect, fitting the abstract style…”</li>
<li><strong>A — Audience</strong> impact: “This made the audience fear for the Freedom Riders’ lives and created empathy through the gradual intensity increase…”</li>
<li><strong>S — Suggest</strong> a development: “This could have been developed by adding a pulsating red and orange strobe to create the illusion of flames…”</li>
</ul>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n29" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. State the key difference described about build these into your writing.">
<div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact — Key Evaluation Phrases</div>
<p>Build these into your writing: “This was effective because…”, “In my opinion I thought…”, “However, in reflection…”, “I particularly enjoyed…”, and “I feel that this could have been developed because…” Remember — 10 of your 30 marks are for your knowledge and understanding of drama, so use terms like proxemics, levels, intensity, wash, gobo, and muscle tension in every paragraph.</p>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n31">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul data-narration-id="n36">
<li data-narration-id="n32"><strong>Costume</strong> was minimalistic (base costumes only), with the white gloves being the most significant addition — symbolising the “unified” racist thinking of the White shoppers.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n33"><strong>Set</strong> relied on three steel flats (doubling as a Greyhound bus) and four stools of different heights showing power. The flats were turned at the end to reveal real photographs of the Freedom Riders.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n34">Use the <strong>SEAS structure</strong> (State, Explain, Audience, Suggest) and always link design choices to the <strong>communication of meaning</strong> — 10 marks are for your knowledge and understanding of drama.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n30">For design-focused questions, always link the design choice back to <strong>meaning</strong>. Don’t just describe what you saw — explain what it <strong>communicated</strong> to the audience. For example, don’t write “the stools were different heights.” Instead write: “The stools were of different heights; this was effective because it clearly showed <em>power</em>. Uncle George stood on a tall stool when the Freedom Riders were sat on small stools, symbolising their powerlessness against the racism of the mob.” Always suggest a development to access the highest marks.</p>
