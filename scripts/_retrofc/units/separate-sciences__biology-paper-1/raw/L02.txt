<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Cell Cycle and Mitosis</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">Cells in your body divide to grow, repair damaged tissue and replace worn-out cells. This process follows a pattern called the <dfn class="term" data-def="The repeating sequence of cell growth, DNA replication and division that produces two identical daughter cells.">cell cycle</dfn>. Most of the cell cycle is spent in a growth phase called interphase, during which the cell’s DNA is replicated and the number of sub-cellular structures (organelles) increases.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">Once the cell has copied all of its DNA and organelles, it enters <dfn class="term" data-def="A type of cell division that produces two genetically identical daughter cells, each with the same number of chromosomes as the parent cell.">mitosis</dfn>. During mitosis, one set of chromosomes is pulled to each end of the cell. The cytoplasm and cell membrane then divide to produce two <strong>genetically identical</strong> daughter cells. Each daughter cell has the full set of 46 chromosomes (in humans).</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall: how many daughter cells does mitosis produce, what are they genetically, and name the 3 purposes of mitosis.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Mitosis produces two genetically identical daughter cells from one parent cell. It is used for growth, repair and asexual reproduction. The cell cycle consists of interphase (growth + DNA replication) followed by mitosis and cell division.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n7">Diffusion</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n8"><dfn class="term" data-def="The net movement of particles from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration, down a concentration gradient.">Diffusion</dfn> is the spreading out of particles from a region where they are at a higher concentration to a region where they are at a lower concentration. It occurs in both liquids and gases and does not require energy. Oxygen and carbon dioxide move in and out of cells by diffusion.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n9">The rate of diffusion is affected by the <strong>concentration gradient</strong> (a steeper gradient means faster diffusion), the <strong>temperature</strong> (higher temperature gives particles more kinetic energy), the <strong>surface area</strong> of the membrane and the <strong>distance</strong> the particles must travel.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n10" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall: define diffusion using 'net movement' and 'concentration', then list all 4 factors affecting diffusion rate.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Diffusion is the net movement of particles from high to low concentration. It is a passive process (no energy required). Factors affecting rate: concentration gradient, temperature, surface area and distance.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n11">Osmosis</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n12"><dfn class="term" data-def="The diffusion of water molecules from a dilute solution (high water concentration) to a concentrated solution (low water concentration) through a partially permeable membrane.">Osmosis</dfn> is the movement of water molecules across a <dfn class="term" data-def="A membrane that allows small molecules like water to pass through but blocks larger molecules.">partially permeable membrane</dfn> from a dilute solution (high water concentration) to a more concentrated solution (low water concentration). Like diffusion, osmosis is a passive process.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n13">In Required Practical 3, you investigate the effect of a range of sugar solutions on the mass of plant tissue. Potato cylinders placed in distilled water gain mass (water enters cells by osmosis) while those in concentrated sugar solution lose mass (water leaves cells).</p>

This requires energy from cellular respiration. Examples include root hair cells absorbing mineral ions from the soil and the absorption of sugar from the gut into the blood.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n15">Active transport is essential when a cell needs to accumulate substances at a higher concentration than the surrounding environment. The energy for this process comes from the mitochondria.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Comparing Transport Methods</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n17">A common exam question asks you to compare diffusion, osmosis and active transport. Diffusion and osmosis are passive (no energy needed) and move substances <strong>down</strong> the concentration gradient. Active transport requires energy and moves substances <strong>against</strong> the concentration gradient. Osmosis specifically describes the movement of water through a partially permeable membrane.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n19">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n20">Mitosis produces two genetically identical cells for growth and repair; the cell cycle includes interphase and mitosis.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n21">Diffusion moves particles from high to low concentration (passive); osmosis is the diffusion of water through a partially permeable membrane.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n22">Active transport moves substances against the concentration gradient and requires energy from respiration.</li>
</ul>
