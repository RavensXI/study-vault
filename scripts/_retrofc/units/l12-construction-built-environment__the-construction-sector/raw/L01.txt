--- DESCRIPTION ---
The construction sector: building types, infrastructure and building services engineering.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">A Large and Diverse Sector</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The industry responsible for designing, constructing, maintaining and demolishing buildings, structures and infrastructure across the UK and worldwide.">construction sector</dfn> is one of the largest industries in the United Kingdom, contributing roughly 10% of the country's economic output and employing millions of people in a huge variety of roles. Unlike many industries that focus on a single product, construction spans everything from building a family home to installing electrical grids that power entire cities. This diversity makes it one of the most dynamic and rewarding sectors to work in, with strong and consistent demand for skilled workers at every level.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Buildings and Structures</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">The construction sector works on two broad categories of built output. The first is <dfn class="term" data-def="Buildings and structures used as homes for people, including houses, flats and apartment blocks.">residential buildings</dfn> — properties designed as places where people live. These range from small terraced houses to large blocks of flats. The second broad category is non-residential buildings, which includes offices, shops, schools, hospitals, factories and warehouses — any building that serves a purpose other than housing people. Beyond these two building types, the sector also constructs bridges and roads, which are engineered structures rather than enclosed buildings.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n5">All buildings and structures share common component parts. Walls enclose and separate spaces, and can be either load-bearing (carrying the weight of floors and roofs above) or non-load-bearing (simply dividing internal spaces). Floors provide flat, safe surfaces for people to stand and move on, and transfer loads down through the structure. Openings — doors and windows — are cut into walls to allow movement and natural light; they require lintels (horizontal beams) above them to carry the load of the wall above the gap. Understanding these typical component parts is fundamental to understanding how buildings work.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n6" data-revision-tip="Learn the two main building types (residential and non-residential) plus bridges and roads. Also memorise the three typical component parts: walls, floors and openings.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The main types of buildings and structures in the construction sector are: residential buildings, non-residential buildings, bridges and roads. Every building has typical component parts: walls, floors and openings (doors and windows).</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n7">Infrastructure and Civil Engineering Products</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n8"><dfn class="term" data-def="The large-scale systems and networks — roads, railways, bridges, tunnels, energy and communications — that underpin the functioning of society.">Infrastructure</dfn> is the term used for the essential systems and networks that allow society to function. Civil engineering is the branch of construction responsible for designing, building and maintaining these large-scale projects. The main types of infrastructure that the construction sector delivers include: roads and motorways, which allow the movement of people and goods; railways, which provide mass passenger and freight transport; bridges, which carry roads or railways across rivers, valleys or other obstacles; and tunnels, which allow routes to pass under water, mountains or urban areas.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n9">Beyond transport infrastructure, the sector is also responsible for utility networks on which modern life depends. Water supply and sewerage systems collect water from sources, treat it to drinking standard and distribute it to homes and businesses, while also removing and treating wastewater. Electrical grids transmit electricity generated at power stations across the country to homes, factories and offices. Telecommunications infrastructure — including underground cable networks and masts — carries broadband, telephone and mobile data signals. The scale of these projects means they are often run by large civil engineering companies and require significant investment and planning.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">Building Services Engineering</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11"><dfn class="term" data-def="The discipline concerned with designing, installing and maintaining the mechanical, electrical and public-health systems that make buildings safe, comfortable and functional.">Building services engineering</dfn> is the branch of construction that deals with the systems installed inside buildings to make them functional. These systems are grouped into three main categories: mechanical services, electrical services, and public health services.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n12">Mechanical services include the systems that control the physical environment inside a building. Escalators and lifts move people between floors, particularly important in multi-storey buildings. Heating systems — such as central heating — keep buildings warm in winter. Ventilation systems extract stale air and bring in fresh air, while air conditioning systems (often combined with ventilation in HVAC systems) control both temperature and humidity. These systems keep buildings comfortable and safe for occupants throughout the year.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n13">Electrical services supply and distribute electrical power throughout a building. Energy supply connections bring electricity in from the national grid, and internal distribution boards divide it into circuits throughout the building. Lighting and low voltage (LV) systems provide illumination in rooms, corridors and external areas. Communication lines, telephones and IT networks keep occupants connected. Fire detection and protection systems — including smoke detectors, alarms and sprinklers — protect life and property. Security and alarm systems, including CCTV and intruder alarms, protect against theft and unauthorised access.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n14">Public health services are the plumbing systems that support hygiene and health inside buildings. These include plumbing for cold water supply (bringing fresh water to taps, toilets and appliances) and domestic hot water (heating water for showers, baths and sinks). Drainage systems remove wastewater — the used water from sinks, showers and toilets (also called sewage) — through underground pipes to the public sewer. Stormwater drainage systems collect rainwater from roofs and paved areas and direct it away from the building to prevent flooding.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="Learn the three categories of building services engineering: mechanical (lifts, HVAC), electrical (energy, lighting, fire, security) and public health (water supply, drainage). These are common exam topics.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Building services engineering has three main categories: (1) Mechanical services — escalators/lifts, heating, ventilation and air conditioning. (2) Electrical services — energy supply, lighting and LV, communications, fire detection/protection, security/alarms. (3) Public health services — cold and hot water supply, wastewater drainage and stormwater drainage.</p>
</div>

    <p data-narration-id="n16">The construction sector's large share of the economy reflects the huge range of activities it covers. New housing developments, commercial property developments, road and rail projects, maintenance of existing buildings and infrastructure replacement programmes all count as construction activity. The sector also has a strong multiplier effect: when a new school is built, it creates work for architects, engineers, groundworkers, bricklayers, electricians, plumbers and decorators, as well as supporting supply-chain businesses that manufacture bricks, steel, timber and glass. All of this activity generates significant economic output and tax revenue. In addition, there is a persistent shortage of housing in the UK and continuing need to upgrade ageing infrastructure, meaning demand for construction services remains consistently high.</p>

    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n17">In a modern building, mechanical, electrical and public health systems are deeply interconnected. For example, a hotel might use a building management system (BMS) to control its mechanical heating and ventilation according to the time of day and occupancy, while the electrical system provides the power to run those mechanical systems. The fire detection system — part of the electrical category — can automatically shut down ventilation ducts when smoke is detected, preventing fire from spreading. Meanwhile, the public health system ensures clean water is available to every room while wastewater is safely removed. Building services engineers must coordinate closely with the structural and architectural teams to route pipes, ducts and cables through the building without compromising structure or aesthetics.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n19">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n20">The construction sector covers residential buildings, non-residential buildings, bridges and roads. All buildings share typical component parts: walls, floors and openings.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n21">Infrastructure and civil engineering products include roads, railways, bridges, tunnels, water supply and sewerage systems, electrical grids and telecommunications networks.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n22">Building services engineering is divided into mechanical services (lifts, HVAC), electrical services (energy supply, lighting, fire detection, security) and public health services (water supply, drainage).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n23">The construction sector contributes around 10% of the UK economy and has strong demand for skilled workers across a very wide range of roles and disciplines.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n18">When answering questions about the construction sector, be precise with your categories. Examiners expect you to distinguish between residential and non-residential buildings, and to name specific examples of infrastructure (roads, railways, bridges, tunnels, water supply, electrical grids, telecommunications) rather than just writing "roads and things like that." For building services engineering, always remember the three groups — mechanical, electrical and public health — and be able to give at least two examples from each. If asked to "describe" a service, name it and state its function (e.g. "ventilation systems extract stale air and bring fresh air into a building").</p>
