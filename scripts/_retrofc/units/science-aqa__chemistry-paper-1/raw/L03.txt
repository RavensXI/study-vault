--- DESCRIPTION ---
How atoms bond by transferring or sharing electrons, and the properties of metallic bonds.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Ionic Bonding</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n2">When metals react with non-metals, electrons are <strong>transferred</strong> from the metal atom to the non-metal atom. The metal atom loses electrons to form a <dfn class="term" data-def="A positively charged ion formed when a metal atom loses one or more electrons.">positive ion</dfn> (cation), and the non-metal atom gains electrons to form a <dfn class="term" data-def="A negatively charged ion formed when a non-metal atom gains one or more electrons.">negative ion</dfn> (anion). Both ions then have full outer electron shells and are stable.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n3">The strong electrostatic attraction between the oppositely charged ions is called an <dfn class="term" data-def="The strong electrostatic force of attraction between oppositely charged ions, formed by the transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal.">ionic bond</dfn>. For example, in sodium chloride (NaCl), sodium loses one electron to become \(Na^+\) and chlorine gains one electron to become \(Cl^-\).</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and recall what happens to metal and non-metal atoms in ionic bonding, plus what holds the ions together.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Ionic bonding: metal atoms lose electrons (form positive ions) and non-metal atoms gain electrons (form negative ions). The ions are held together by strong electrostatic attraction in a giant ionic lattice.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Covalent Bonding</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n6">When non-metals bond with other non-metals, they <strong>share</strong> pairs of electrons. This is called a <dfn class="term" data-def="A bond formed when two non-metal atoms share one or more pairs of electrons. Each shared pair is one covalent bond.">covalent bond</dfn>. Each shared pair of electrons counts as one bond. Examples include:</p>

<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n7">\(H_2\) — a single covalent bond (one shared pair).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n8">\(O_2\) — a double covalent bond (two shared pairs: \(O=O\)).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n9">\(N_2\) — a triple covalent bond (three shared pairs: \(N \equiv N\)).</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n10">\(H_2O\) — two single covalent bonds.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n11">\(CH_4\) — four single covalent bonds.</li>
</ul>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Metallic Bonding</h2>

<p data-narration-id="n14">In metals, the outer electrons are <dfn class="term" data-def="Electrons that have left their atoms and are free to move throughout the metal structure. They form a 'sea of delocalised electrons' that holds the metal together.">delocalised</dfn> — they leave their atoms and form a “sea” of electrons that can move freely through the structure. The <dfn class="term" data-def="The strong electrostatic attraction between the positive metal ions and the sea of delocalised electrons that surrounds them.">metallic bond</dfn> is the strong electrostatic attraction between the positive metal ions and this sea of delocalised electrons.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n15" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and explain what surrounds positive metal ions in metallic bonding and why this makes metals conductive and malleable.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Metallic bonding: positive metal ions surrounded by a sea of delocalised electrons. This explains why metals conduct electricity (delocalised electrons carry charge) and are malleable (layers of ions can slide over each other).</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n18">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n19">Ionic bonding: electrons transferred from metal to non-metal, forming oppositely charged ions held in a lattice.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n20">Covalent bonding: electrons shared between non-metal atoms to form molecules.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n21">Metallic bonding: positive ions in a sea of delocalised electrons, explaining conductivity and malleability.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n17">In the exam, you may be asked to draw dot-and-cross diagrams for ionic or covalent compounds. For ionic compounds, always show the electron transfer and write the charges (e.g. \(Na^+\), \(Cl^-\)). For covalent compounds, show the shared pairs in the overlap. Remember: ionic = transfer, covalent = sharing.</p>
