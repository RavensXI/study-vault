<h2 data-narration-id="n1">The Nature of Families</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A group of people who are related by blood, marriage or adoption.">family</dfn> is a group of people who are related by blood, marriage or adoption. In the 21st century, families come in many different forms. Both Christianity and Islam teach that the family is the foundation of a stable society, but they recognise that families look very different today compared to the past.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Can you name all 6 types from memory?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>You are expected to know six types of family: nuclear, extended, single-parent, blended (step), same-sex parent, and polygamous. You must also understand the purpose of families and contemporary issues such as same-sex parenting and polygamy.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">Types of Family</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">There are several types of family that you need to know for the exam. Each has a clear definition that the exam board uses.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n6">A <dfn class="term" data-def="A couple and their children regarded as a basic social unit.">nuclear family</dfn> is a couple and their children living together as a basic social unit. An <dfn class="term" data-def="A family that extends beyond just parents and their children by including grandparents and other relatives.">extended family</dfn> goes beyond this by including grandparents, aunts, uncles and other relatives. A single-parent family is one parent raising their children alone, perhaps due to bereavement or the end of a relationship. A blended family (also called a stepfamily) is formed when a divorced or widowed person remarries and brings children from a previous relationship. Same-sex parents are two people of the same gender raising children together, often through adoption or surrogacy. <dfn class="term" data-def="The practice or custom of having more than one wife at the same time.">Polygamy</dfn> is the practice of having more than one spouse at the same time.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n7">The Purpose of Families</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n8">Both religious and non-religious people agree that the family serves important purposes in society. Families provide stability, protection and education for children. For religious believers, the family also has a spiritual purpose.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n9" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Can you recall all 4 items listed here?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The four key purposes of families on The spec are: procreation, stability and the protection of children, and educating children in a faith.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n10"><dfn class="term" data-def="Bringing babies into the world; producing offspring.">Procreation</dfn> means having children. For religious believers, this fulfils God’s command to “be fruitful and increase in number” (Genesis 1:28). The family is seen as the proper place for children to be born and raised.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n11">Families create <dfn class="term" data-def="Safety and security; a stable society is one in which people can live peaceful, productive lives.">stability</dfn> for their members and for society as a whole. They protect children by supplying their basic needs and safeguarding them from harm. The Church of England states: “The family remains the most important grouping human beings have ever developed. Children thrive, grow and develop within love and safeguarding of a family.”</p>

<p data-narration-id="n12">For religious parents, educating children in their faith is a key purpose of family life. Christian parents may baptise their children as a way of committing to raising them in the Christian faith. Muslim parents are expected to teach their children how to pray, keep halal food laws and live according to Islam.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Christian Beliefs About Families</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Christians believe the family reflects the family of God and provides the basis for a stable society. Parents have a responsibility to care for their children and raise them in the faith. The Bible teaches: “Children, obey your parents in the Lord, for this is right. ‘Honour your father and mother’” (Ephesians 6:1–2). Parents also have duties: “Fathers, do not embitter your children, or they will become discouraged” (Colossians 3:21).</p>

<p data-narration-id="n15">The Bible emphasises providing for your family: “Anyone who does not provide for their relatives, and especially for their own household, has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever” (1 Timothy 5:8). Children are described as “a heritage from the Lord, offspring a reward from him” (Psalm 127:3).</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Islamic Beliefs About Families</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">In Islam, the extended family is the basis of Islamic society and part of God’s plan for human beings. The family shapes the moral values and character of children. The Qur’an teaches respect for parents: “Your Lord has commanded that you should worship none but Him, and that you be kind to your parents” (Qur’an 17:23).</p>

<p data-narration-id="n18">The Hadith teaches that “Heaven is under the feet of the mothers,” showing the high status given to mothers in Islam. Children have a duty to care for their elderly parents with kindness and respect. Muslim parents are expected to love and care for their children, set a good example for them, and bring them up in the faith of Islam. The Hadith states: “Honour your children and perfect their manners.”</p>

Children obey their parents and may look after them when they are old. In Islam, men are traditionally expected to provide for the family while women look after the home and raise the children, though many modern British Muslim families share these duties. Children must show respect and care for their parents in old age — the Qur’an instructs: “Lower your wing in humility towards your parents in kindness” (Qur’an 17:24).</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n20">Contemporary Family Issues</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n21">Modern society raises several issues about family life that religious believers must respond to.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n22" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Define same-sex adoption in your own words.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Same-sex adoption is legal in the UK. Polygamy is not legal in the UK, but polygamous marriages performed outside the UK may be recognised.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Same-Sex Parents</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n23">If a same-sex couple wish to have children, they will generally adopt or have a child biologically related to one parent through surrogacy or sperm donation. Some traditional Christians disapprove of same-sex parents because they believe the ideal is for children to grow up with a male and female role model. Other Christians say it is more important for children to be in a secure and loving family regardless of the gender of their parents. Muslims generally do not approve of same-sex parenting because Islam considers homosexuality morally wrong and believes children should have both a male and female role model.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Polygamy</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n24"><dfn class="term" data-def="The offence of marrying someone while already married to another person.">Bigamy</dfn> (marrying someone while still married to another) is a criminal offence in the UK. Polygamy may mean that children have the support of more parent figures, but it can also cause jealousy among spouses and mean children see their parents less if a father has multiple households. Christians generally oppose polygamy, though it is not expressly forbidden in the Bible. In Islam, polygamy is permitted under Shari’ah law — a man may marry up to four wives, provided he treats them all equally. However, many Muslims in Britain do not practise polygamy.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n26">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n27">There are six types of family you need to know: nuclear, extended, single-parent, blended, same-sex parent and polygamous. The family’s purpose includes procreation, stability, protection and faith education.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n28">Christians see the family as reflecting God’s family and the foundation of society. Islam views the extended family as the basis of Islamic society and part of God’s plan.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n29">Contemporary issues include same-sex parenting (legal in the UK, with divided religious views) and polygamy (illegal in the UK but permitted in Islam under strict conditions).</li>
</ul>
