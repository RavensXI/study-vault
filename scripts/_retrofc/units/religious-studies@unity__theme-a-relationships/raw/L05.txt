<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Gender Equality in Britain Today</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2"><dfn class="term" data-def="Giving people the same rights and opportunities regardless of whether they are male or female.">Gender equality</dfn> means that men and women should be given the same rights and opportunities as each other. Many people in Britain today agree with the idea of gender equality, but there are still many examples where it does not happen. Something that prevents gender equality is <dfn class="term" data-def="Unfairly judging someone before the facts are known; holding biased opinions about an individual or group based on their gender.">gender prejudice</dfn>, which is often based on <dfn class="term" data-def="Having a fixed general idea or image of how men and women will behave.">sexual stereotyping</dfn>.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">An example of sexual stereotyping is the idea that women are more naturally caring or are the weaker sex, so they should look after the home while men go out to work. Stereotyping can lead to <dfn class="term" data-def="Acting against someone on the basis of their gender; discrimination is usually seen as wrong and may be against the law.">gender discrimination</dfn> — perhaps by not giving a man a job looking after young children or not employing a woman on a building site.</p>
<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Write down what you recall about sex Discrimination Act (1975) made gender.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The Sex Discrimination Act (1975) made gender discrimination illegal in the UK. Despite this, it still occurs — on average women get paid less than men, even when doing the same jobs. Women make up roughly half of the workforce, but men hold a higher proportion of senior positions.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Christian Views on Gender Equality</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Christians believe that all people have been created as equals in the image of God. The Bible teaches that men and women are equal in God’s sight: “There is neither Jew nor Gentile, neither slave nor free, nor is there male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus” (Galatians 3:28). God created both male and female in his image: “So God created humankind in his image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them” (Genesis 1:27).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Jesus treated women with respect, welcomed them as disciples, and showed in the story of Mary and Martha that women were capable of things other than domestic tasks. He did not conform to the status quo of his day. Christians have worked with charities to improve the treatment of women worldwide and campaigned for more equal treatment of women and men politically.</p>

  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Most Christians today see marriage as an equal partnership where the different gifts of each person, male and female, strengthen family life. Many Christians also argue that inflammatory Bible and Church teachings about gender roles could be considered out of date, reflecting the position of women at the time the Bible was written rather than God’s will for today.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n11">Women in Christian Leadership</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n12">The question of whether women can hold leadership roles in the Church is one of the most visible areas of debate about gender equality in Christianity. Some Christians, particularly Catholics, feel that women should not be <dfn class="term" data-def="The act of making someone a priest, minister, or religious leader through a formal ceremony.">ordained</dfn> as priests because of biblical teaching, because Jesus and his disciples were male, and because it is a long-standing tradition in the Catholic Church.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n13">However, other Christians see this as unfair discrimination. Many churches allow women to become vicars and ministers. The Church of England has allowed women to become bishops since 2014. These Christians argue that if God created men and women as equals, then both should be able to serve in all roles within the Church.</p>
<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Muslim Views on Gender Equality</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15">Muslims believe that God created all people equal. The Qur’an teaches that men and women were created from a single soul and have the same spiritual nature. Men and women have the same religious and moral responsibilities. Muhammad worked to unite tribes in Madinah into one community (Ummah) under God, with equality and justice for all.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n16">The Qur’an states: “People, We created you all from a single man and a single woman, and made you into races and tribes so that you could recognise one another” (Qur’an 49:13). Muhammad also taught that anyone, man or woman, who does a good deed for God’s sake will be rewarded (Qur’an 16:97). He said: “I command you to be kind to women.”</p>

Men are expected to provide for their families and make decisions about their general welfare. However, many modern British Muslim families share those duties because often both parents have jobs.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n18">Islamic law recognises the full property rights of women before and after marriage. A married woman may keep her maiden name and is entitled to receive material gifts. She does not need to spend her own money on the household and has the right to full financial support within marriage and during the waiting period of divorce.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n19" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. What percentage is given for some Muslim countries?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>In some Muslim countries, women are not given full rights under the law, but Muslims point out this is due to different cultural practices rather than the teachings of the Qur’an. Muslim women in the UK may suffer prejudice for the way they dress, for example wearing the hijab. Research shows Muslim women are 71% more likely to be unemployed than white Christian women with the same education level and language skills.</p>
</div>
<h2 data-narration-id="n20">Challenging Gender Prejudice and Discrimination</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n21">Christians who think men and women should have different roles may still see them as equal. They may think it is fair to treat them in different ways if this is God’s will. However, Christians who disagree with different gender roles may see men and women being given different roles as unequal treatment, and they may view this as prejudice and discrimination.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n22">Most Christians think it is wrong to treat people differently in any unfair way because they believe God loves everyone equally. The command to “love one’s neighbour” means that to discriminate is not loving. Many non-religious people also agree that men and women should be treated equally. Humanist principles such as fairness and equal rights support this view. Gender prejudice and discrimination is illegal in the UK.</p>

However, some non-religious people may believe men and women should have different roles due to biological differences, or may be influenced by social and cultural standards or personal prejudices.</p>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n25">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n26">Gender equality means giving people the same rights regardless of gender. The Sex Discrimination Act (1975) made gender discrimination illegal in the UK, but inequalities persist in areas like pay and senior positions.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n27">Both Christianity and Islam teach that men and women are equal before God, but there are different views within each religion about whether men and women should have the same or different roles.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n28">The debate over women’s ordination in Christianity (e.g. Catholic priests vs Church of England bishops since 2014) is a key example of how gender equality is applied differently within religions.</li>
</ul>
