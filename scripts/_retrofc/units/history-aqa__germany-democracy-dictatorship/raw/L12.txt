--- DESCRIPTION ---
From the Nuremberg Laws to the death camps — the road from prejudice to genocide.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n1">This lesson covers the most painful subject on the AQA spec: the Nazi regime's persecution and murder of millions of European Jews, alongside Sinti and Roma, disabled Germans, gay men, Soviet prisoners and political opponents. The historical task is to track the radicalisation step by step &mdash; from the boycott of April 1933 to the death camps from 1942 &mdash; and to ask the question that still divides historians: was the Holocaust a long-held plan that Hitler put into action, or a process that radicalised in stages as the regime found itself in war?</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n2">Nazi Racial Ideology</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Nazi ideology divided humanity into hierarchies of race. At the top was the imagined <dfn class="term" data-def="Pseudo-scientific Nazi term for the supposed superior &lsquo;Nordic&rsquo; race of fair-haired, blue-eyed Germans, treated by the regime as the racial elite of Europe.">Aryan</dfn> race &mdash; tall, fair-haired, blue-eyed Germans. Below them came other Europeans, then Slavs, with Jews, Sinti and Roma, and black Africans placed at the bottom as <dfn class="term" data-def="Nazi term meaning &lsquo;sub-human&rsquo;; applied especially to Jews, Sinti and Roma, Slavs and people of colour to dehumanise them and justify persecution.">Untermenschen</dfn> &mdash; sub-humans. This had no scientific basis. It was social Darwinism plus pseudo-anthropology, taught in German schools as racial hygiene from 1935. The ideology was not invented by Hitler &mdash; antisemitism had a long European history &mdash; but the Nazi state mechanised it.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n4">Mendelian skull measurement, eye colour charts and family-tree research were marshalled to classify Germans as fully Jewish, half-Jewish or quarter-Jewish (<dfn class="term" data-def="Nazi legal category for Germans of part-Jewish ancestry under the 1935 Nuremberg Laws; further subdivided into first-degree (two Jewish grandparents) and second-degree (one Jewish grandparent), with different legal restrictions.">Mischling</dfn>). What followed was not yet murder, but it built the legal infrastructure that would make organised killing possible.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n5" data-revision-tip="Cover this and write the four major persecution dates in order: 1933 boycott, 1935 Nuremberg Laws, 1938 Kristallnacht, 1942 Wannsee.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Nazi racial policy moved through clear stages: boycotts and dismissals from 1933, legal exclusion under the Nuremberg Laws (September 1935), organised violence at Kristallnacht (November 1938), mass shootings by the Einsatzgruppen from 1941, and industrial murder coordinated from the Wannsee Conference (January 1942). The radicalisation was not one decision but a sequence.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n6">Persecution 1933&ndash;38</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n7">Persecution began within weeks of Hitler's appointment. On 1 April 1933 SA squads enforced a national boycott of Jewish businesses, painting Stars of David and the word <em>Jude</em> on shop windows. The Civil Service Law of 7 April 1933 dismissed Jewish judges, teachers, doctors and academics from the public payroll. Quotas restricted Jewish students at universities and Jewish patients in hospitals.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8">In September 1935 the Reichstag passed the <dfn class="term" data-def="Two laws of 15 September 1935: the Reich Citizenship Law stripped German Jews of citizenship, and the Law for the Protection of German Blood banned marriage and sexual relations between Jews and non-Jews; introduced the Mischling categories.">Nuremberg Laws</dfn>. The Reich Citizenship Law stripped German Jews of citizenship; only those of German or Aryan blood could be Reich citizens. The Law for the Protection of German Blood banned marriage and sexual relations between Jews and non-Jews. Sinti and Roma were soon classed under the same provisions. By 1938 around half of Germany's 500,000 Jews had emigrated &mdash; the others lacked the visas, money or contacts to leave.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n9">On 7 November 1938 a young Polish Jew, Herschel Grynszpan, shot a German diplomat in Paris in protest at the deportation of his parents. Goebbels seized on the killing to authorise a coordinated wave of state-organised violence. Across Germany and Austria on the night of 9&ndash;10 November 1938, SA and SS units (some in plainclothes) attacked Jewish synagogues, businesses and homes. Around 267 synagogues were burned. Around 7,500 Jewish-owned businesses were ransacked or destroyed. At least 91 Jews were murdered. About 30,000 Jewish men were arrested and sent to concentration camps. The community was then fined one billion marks for &lsquo;causing&rsquo; the disorder. Kristallnacht was the moment Nazi antisemitism moved from social and legal persecution to organised state violence in public &mdash; with no significant German protest.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">The T4 Programme and Other Victims</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">In October 1939 (backdated to 1 September) Hitler signed an order authorising what became known as the <dfn class="term" data-def="Nazi euthanasia programme from 1939 that murdered around 70,000 mentally ill, physically disabled and chronically sick Germans by gas, lethal injection and starvation; named after the Berlin office address Tiergartenstrasse 4.">T4 programme</dfn> &mdash; the murder of disabled Germans deemed &lsquo;unworthy of life&rsquo;. Around 70,000 people were killed by gas, lethal injection or starvation in six killing centres including Hadamar and Hartheim before the official programme was halted in August 1941 after a sermon by Bishop Galen of Munster. Killings of disabled people in fact continued informally to 1945.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Other groups were targeted alongside Jews: Sinti and Roma (around 220,000 to 500,000 murdered), gay men (around 50,000 imprisoned, perhaps 15,000 sent to camps), Soviet prisoners of war (around 3.3 million dead in German captivity), Polish elites (mass murders from 1939), and political opponents. The Holocaust is centrally about the murder of European Jews; it was not only the murder of European Jews. Reducing it to a single number or a single victim group is historically inaccurate.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">From Persecution to Mass Killing, 1939&ndash;41</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">War transformed the policy. From September 1939 millions of Polish Jews came under German occupation. They were forced into ghettos &mdash; sealed urban districts in Warsaw, Lodz, Cracow and elsewhere &mdash; with starvation rations and forced labour. Around 100,000 died of disease and hunger in the Warsaw ghetto in 1941&ndash;42 alone.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15">From June 1941, with the invasion of the Soviet Union (Operation Barbarossa), <dfn class="term" data-def="Nazi mobile killing units of the SS and police that followed the invading German army into the Soviet Union from June 1941; murdered around 1.3 to 1.5 million Jews and others by mass shooting in fields, ravines and forests.">Einsatzgruppen</dfn> (SS mobile killing units) followed the German army into Soviet territory. They murdered around 1.3 to 1.5 million Jews, communist officials and others by mass shooting. At Babi Yar, near Kiev, around 33,771 Jews were shot in two days (29&ndash;30 September 1941) on the regime's own count. The killers were ordinary German police, SS, Wehrmacht support and local collaborators.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">The Wannsee Conference and the Death Camps</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">On 20 January 1942 fifteen senior officials &mdash; including Reinhard Heydrich (chair), Adolf Eichmann (secretary) and representatives of the Foreign Office, Justice Ministry and SS &mdash; met in a villa at <dfn class="term" data-def="Meeting of senior Nazi officials at a villa in Wannsee, Berlin, on 20 January 1942 chaired by Reinhard Heydrich; coordinated the Final Solution &mdash; the systematic deportation and murder of European Jews &mdash; across Nazi-controlled Europe.">Wannsee</dfn> in suburban Berlin. The minutes &mdash; one of the few surviving copies &mdash; coordinated the Final Solution: the systematic deportation and murder of all 11 million European Jews, including Jews in countries Germany did not yet occupy.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Wannsee did not start the killing. Mass shootings had been under way since June 1941. Gas vans had been used at Chelmno from December 1941. What Wannsee did was bureaucratically coordinate the murder across the Nazi state and align deportations from Western Europe with the killing centres being built in occupied Poland. From 1942 onwards Jewish communities were transported by train to six dedicated death camps: Auschwitz-Birkenau, Treblinka, Sobibor, Belzec, Chelmno and Majdanek. Roughly six million Jews were murdered by 1945 &mdash; about two thirds of pre-war European Jewry.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n19">These are not the same and the difference matters in exam answers. Concentration camps (Konzentrationslager) such as Dachau (opened 1933), Buchenwald (1937) and Sachsenhausen (1936) held political prisoners, religious minorities, Roma, gay men and Jews; deaths were from forced labour, malnutrition, disease and execution. Death camps (Vernichtungslager) such as Treblinka, Sobibor, Belzec and Chelmno were built from 1941&ndash;42 with the sole purpose of industrial murder, almost entirely of Jews. Auschwitz-Birkenau and Majdanek combined both functions. Conflating them flattens the historical record.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n20">Intentionalists and Functionalists</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n21">Historians have debated for decades how this happened. Intentionalists (Lucy Dawidowicz, Klaus Hildebrand, Daniel Goldhagen) argue Hitler held a consistent plan for racial extermination from at least 1925 and the Holocaust was the deliberate execution of that plan. Functionalists (Hans Mommsen, Christopher Browning, Raul Hilberg) argue the killing radicalised step by step as the regime confronted millions of Jews under occupation, with mid-level officials proposing increasingly murderous solutions. Most historians today take a moderate position: Hitler's antisemitism was a constant; the specific decision for industrial murder emerged in 1941&ndash;42 from the dynamics of war and occupation, not from a 1925 master plan. Naming named victims and named perpetrators &mdash; rather than abstract numbers &mdash; is the historical practice both schools agree on.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n22" data-revision-tip="Close this and explain in three sentences the difference between concentration and death camps &mdash; with one example of each and the year they were established.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Around six million Jews were murdered in the Holocaust, alongside Sinti and Roma, disabled Germans (T4), gay men, Soviet POWs and political prisoners. The radicalisation moved from boycotts (1933) to legal exclusion (1935), state violence (1938), ghettoisation (from 1939), mass shooting (from 1941) and industrial murder coordinated at Wannsee (1942). Most historians now reject a single 1925 plan in favour of stepped radicalisation under wartime conditions.</p>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n24">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
<li data-narration-id="n25">Nazi racial policy radicalised in stages: 1933 boycotts and Civil Service Law; 1935 Nuremberg Laws stripping citizenship; 1938 Kristallnacht state violence; 1939 T4 programme against the disabled; 1941 Einsatzgruppen mass shootings.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n26">The Wannsee Conference of 20 January 1942 did not start the killing but coordinated the Final Solution across the Nazi state &mdash; from 1942 European Jews were transported to dedicated death camps at Auschwitz-Birkenau, Treblinka, Sobibor, Belzec, Chelmno and Majdanek.</li>
<li data-narration-id="n27">Around six million Jews were murdered, alongside Sinti and Roma, disabled Germans, gay men and Soviet POWs &mdash; functionalist historians now generally win the debate that this emerged from stepped radicalisation under war, not a single long-held plan.</li>
</ul>

--- EXAM_TIP_HTML ---
<p data-narration-id="n23">Two precision points. First, do not write that Hitler had a clear 1925 plan to murder Europe's Jews &mdash; most current historians follow a functionalist or moderate-intentionalist view that the decision for industrial murder emerged in 1941&ndash;42 from the dynamics of war and occupation. Second, do not conflate concentration camps with death camps &mdash; concentration camps existed from 1933 with broad inmates; death camps were purpose-built from 1941&ndash;42 to murder Jews. For 8-mark Evaluate an Interpretation questions, name named victims and named perpetrators (Heydrich, Eichmann; Babi Yar; Hadamar) rather than abstract numbers, and remember non-Jewish victims (Sinti and Roma, T4, Soviet POWs, gay men).</p>
