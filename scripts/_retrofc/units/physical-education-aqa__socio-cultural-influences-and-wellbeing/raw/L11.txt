--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">How Performers Are Expected to Behave</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Sport is governed not only by written rules but by unwritten codes of conduct that define how athletes are expected to treat opponents, officials and the spirit of competition itself. These codes range from formal agreements to cultural customs, and they are often tested most severely when the pressure to win is at its greatest. Understanding the difference between behaviour that upholds sporting values and behaviour that exploits or undermines them is central to sport&rsquo;s identity as more than a simple contest of skill.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Etiquette</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4"><dfn class="term" data-def="The unwritten rules or informal codes of behaviour expected of participants in a sport, distinct from the formal written rules.">Etiquette</dfn> refers to the unwritten rules of conduct in sport &mdash; customs that participants are expected to follow even though they are not written into the official rule book. In tennis, it is considered etiquette to acknowledge a lucky net cord with a gesture to your opponent. In golf, players are expected to repair divots, rake bunkers and maintain silence when others are playing. In cricket, a fielding side traditionally applauds a departing batsman who has played well. None of these behaviours are compulsory under the laws of the game, but failing to observe them draws social disapproval and signals a disrespect for the sport&rsquo;s traditions.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Sportsmanship</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6"><dfn class="term" data-def="Behaviour that shows fair play, generosity and respect for opponents, officials and the rules of the sport.">Sportsmanship</dfn> describes behaviour that embodies fair play, respect and generosity in competition. A sportsmanlike act might include a footballer kicking the ball out of play when an opponent is injured so they can receive treatment, despite there being no rule requiring it. It includes shaking hands with opponents after a match, accepting officials&rsquo; decisions without complaint, and refusing to take advantage of an opponent&rsquo;s misfortune. Sportsmanship is widely seen as a moral standard to which all competitors should aspire, regardless of the stakes.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n7" data-revision-tip="Cover this and write a definition of etiquette and a definition of sportsmanship in your own words. Then write one sporting example for each.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Etiquette is the unwritten code of conduct in a sport (customs and traditions). Sportsmanship is the attitude and behaviour of fair play, respect and generosity towards opponents and officials. Both go beyond the written rules.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n8">Gamesmanship</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9"><dfn class="term" data-def="The use of strategies that are within the letter of the rules but violate their spirit, intended to gain a psychological or tactical advantage over an opponent.">Gamesmanship</dfn> sits at the opposite end of the conduct spectrum from sportsmanship. It involves using strategies that are technically within the rules but deliberately break their spirit to gain an unfair advantage. Examples include: a tennis player bouncing the ball an excessive number of times before serving in order to disrupt an opponent&rsquo;s concentration; a rugby player deliberately slowing down play by lying on the wrong side of a ruck for as long as the referee will allow; or a batsman calling for a new bat or changing their gloves when the bowling side is building momentum, purely to break their rhythm. Gamesmanship is legal but widely considered unsporting.</p>

Cheating &mdash; such as deliberately handling the ball in football or falsifying a time in athletics &mdash; breaks the written rules and can result in disqualification. Gamesmanship breaks the spirit but not the letter of the law. However, the boundary is contested. At what point does prolonged time-wasting become a cynical act that undermines the sport? <h2 data-narration-id="n11">The Contract to Compete</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n12">The <dfn class="term" data-def="The implicit agreement between competitors to abide by the rules and spirit of a sport, giving each other a fair chance to compete.">contract to compete</dfn> is the implicit agreement that all participants enter when they take part in a sporting contest. By agreeing to compete, athletes commit to abiding by the rules, respecting the spirit of the competition and giving their opponents a fair chance to succeed. The contract to compete underpins concepts of fair play &mdash; without it, sport would be reduced to a simple contest in which any tactic, no matter how unethical, was acceptable as long as it helped you win. A performer who deliberately injures an opponent, uses performance-enhancing drugs or engages in aggressive gamesmanship to the point of intimidation could be said to be breaking the contract to compete.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n13" data-revision-tip="Close this and write from memory: what does the contract to compete require of athletes? List three specific obligations it places on a competitor.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The contract to compete is the implicit agreement all competitors enter when they take part in sport. It requires them to abide by the rules, respect opponents and the spirit of the game, and give all participants a fair chance to succeed.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n14">Why These Concepts Matter</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n15">These concepts matter beyond individual events. Sport&rsquo;s social and cultural value rests partly on its reputation as an arena in which effort, skill and fair competition determine outcomes. When gamesmanship becomes widespread, public trust in sport erodes. When sportsmanship is absent, sport loses its role as a model of positive values for young participants. Research into youth sport suggests that the conduct of elite athletes influences the attitudes and behaviours of young people who aspire to perform at a high level. Athletes who model sportsmanship &mdash; acknowledging good play by opponents, respecting officials and competing without cynicism &mdash; provide a more powerful form of role model influence than those who win by any means necessary.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n18">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n19">Etiquette is the unwritten code of conduct in a sport (customs and traditions); sportsmanship is the broader moral attitude of fair play, respect and generosity towards opponents and officials.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n20">Gamesmanship exploits loopholes within the rules to gain a psychological or tactical advantage &mdash; it is legal but widely considered unsporting and damaging to the spirit of competition.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n21">The contract to compete is the implicit agreement all participants enter: to abide by the rules, respect the spirit of the sport and give opponents a fair chance &mdash; the foundation of fair play in all sport.</li>
</ul>
