--- DESCRIPTION ---
How energy is stored, transferred between stores, and how to calculate power and work done.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">Energy Stores</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">Energy cannot be created or destroyed — it can only be transferred between different <dfn class="term" data-def="The ways in which energy can be held, such as kinetic, thermal, or gravitational potential.">energy stores</dfn>. You need to know eight energy stores.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and write down all eight energy stores, starting with kinetic and including both types of potential energy."><div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The eight energy stores are: kinetic, thermal, chemical, elastic potential, gravitational potential, electrostatic, magnetic, and nuclear.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n4"><dfn class="term" data-def="The energy store associated with a moving object.">Kinetic energy</dfn> depends on the mass and speed of an object. The equation is:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n5">$$E_k = \frac{1}{2} m v^2$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Where \(E_k\) is kinetic energy in joules (J), \(m\) is mass in kilograms (kg), and \(v\) is speed in metres per second (m/s).</p>

<p data-narration-id="n7"><dfn class="term" data-def="The energy stored by an object raised above the ground.">Gravitational potential energy</dfn> depends on the mass, height, and gravitational field strength:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n8">$$E_p = m g h$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9">Where \(E_p\) is gravitational potential energy in joules, \(m\) is mass in kg, \(g\) is gravitational field strength (10 N/kg on Earth), and \(h\) is height in metres.</p>

</button>
<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n10"><dfn class="term" data-def="Energy stored in a stretched or compressed elastic object.">Elastic potential energy</dfn> is stored when an object is stretched or compressed:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n11">$$E_e = \frac{1}{2} k e^2$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n12">Where \(E_e\) is elastic potential energy in J, \(k\) is the spring constant in N/m, and \(e\) is the extension in metres. This only applies up to the <dfn class="term" data-def="The maximum force or extension at which Hooke's law still applies.">limit of proportionality</dfn>.</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Energy Transfers</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">Energy moves between stores by four transfer pathways: mechanically (by forces), electrically (by currents), by heating, and by radiation (light, sound, or other waves).</p>



<p data-narration-id="n15">When a ball is thrown upwards, energy transfers from the kinetic store to the gravitational potential store. At the top, the ball momentarily stops — all kinetic energy has become gravitational potential energy. As it falls, the transfer reverses.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n16" data-revision-tip="Cover this box and explain what the law of conservation of energy states about energy creation, destruction, and total energy in closed systems."><div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
<p>The law of conservation of energy states that energy can be transferred usefully, stored, or dissipated — but cannot be created or destroyed. The total energy of a closed system always stays the same.</p>
</div>

<div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
<p data-narration-id="n17">A 2 kg ball is dropped from a height of 5 m. Calculate the speed just before it hits the ground (assume no air resistance).</p>
<p data-narration-id="n18">Step 1: \(E_p = mgh = 2 \times 10 \times 5 = 100\) J</p>
<p data-narration-id="n19">Step 2: Set \(E_k = E_p\), so \(\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = 100\)</p>
<p data-narration-id="n20">Step 3: \(v^2 = \frac{2 \times 100}{2} = 100\), so \(v = 10\) m/s</p>
</div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n21">Work Done and Power</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22">When a force moves an object, <dfn class="term" data-def="The energy transferred when a force moves an object through a distance.">work done</dfn> equals the energy transferred:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n23">$$W = F s$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n24">Where \(W\) is work done in joules, \(F\) is force in newtons (N), and \(s\) is distance along the line of action of the force in metres.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n25"><dfn class="term" data-def="The rate at which energy is transferred or work is done, measured in watts.">Power</dfn> is the rate of energy transfer:</p>
<p data-narration-id="n26">$$P = \frac{E}{t} \quad \text{and} \quad P = \frac{W}{t}$$</p>
<p data-narration-id="n27">Where \(P\) is power in watts (W), \(E\) is energy transferred in joules, and \(t\) is time in seconds. One watt equals one joule per second.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n28">If an object moves at constant speed, you can combine the equations: \(P = Fv\), where \(v\) is speed in m/s. This is useful for calculating the power output of engines or motors.</p>
</div></div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n30">Key Takeaways</h3><ul><li data-narration-id="n31">There are eight energy stores; energy transfers between them by four pathways (mechanical, electrical, heating, radiation)</li><li data-narration-id="n32">Key equations: \(E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2\), \(E_p = mgh\), \(W = Fs\), \(P = E/t\)</li><li data-narration-id="n33">Energy is always conserved — it cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or dissipated</li></ul>
