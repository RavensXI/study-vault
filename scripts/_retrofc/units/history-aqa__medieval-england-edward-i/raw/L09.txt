--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">A Border Problem Edward Inherited</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">When Edward I returned from crusade in 1274 to be crowned, the most powerful Welsh ruler was Llywelyn ap Gruffudd, Prince of Wales. By the Treaty of Montgomery (1267) Henry III had recognised Llywelyn as overlord of most of native Wales, in return for an annual tribute and an act of <dfn class="term" data-def="A formal ceremony in which a vassal swore loyalty to a lord, kneeling and placing his hands between the lord&rsquo;s, in return for protection and lands.">homage</dfn> to the English king. Llywelyn refused to perform this homage to the new king five times between 1274 and 1276. He also refused to surrender his brother Dafydd, who had fled to England after a failed plot, and announced his intention to marry Eleanor de Montfort, daughter of the rebel Simon de Montfort. To Edward this was open defiance.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: name three things Llywelyn ap Gruffudd did between 1274 and 1276 that Edward treated as defiance.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Edward&rsquo;s war of 1277 grew from Llywelyn&rsquo;s repeated refusal to perform homage, his shelter of his rebel brother Dafydd and his planned marriage to the daughter of Simon de Montfort. From the king&rsquo;s view, the prince of Wales had broken every condition of the 1267 Treaty of Montgomery.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">The First Welsh War, 1277</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">In November 1276 Edward declared Llywelyn a rebel and began preparing a campaign. The army that crossed the border in summer 1277 was unlike any earlier English force in Wales. Three columns advanced, supported by a fleet from the Cinque Ports that controlled the sea around Anglesey. Edward used hundreds of foresters to clear roads through the Welsh woodlands so that his cavalry and supplies could move. Anglesey, the &lsquo;granary of Snowdonia&rsquo;, was occupied. Cut off from his harvest, Llywelyn was starved into surrender by November.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">The first war ended with the <dfn class="term" data-def="The 1277 peace treaty after the first Welsh war, in which Llywelyn lost most of his territory but kept the title Prince of Wales for his lifetime.">Treaty of Aberconwy</dfn>. Llywelyn kept the title Prince of Wales but lost most of his lands east of the Conwy River, and was reduced to a small principality of Gwynedd. He performed homage to Edward at Westminster in 1278. The Crown began building a first ring of castles &mdash; Flint, Rhuddlan, Builth and Aberystwyth &mdash; to garrison the new royal lands.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n7">The Second Welsh War, 1282&ndash;83</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n8">The peace did not last. On Palm Sunday 1282, Llywelyn&rsquo;s brother Dafydd, who had switched sides to Edward in 1277 and felt his rewards were inadequate, attacked the English-held Hawarden Castle and massacred its garrison. Other Welsh princes joined him. Llywelyn, surprised by the rising, was forced into a war he had not chosen. Edward&rsquo;s response was overwhelming &mdash; an army of more than 15,000 men, paid for in part by loans from the Riccardi of Lucca and a tax on the wool trade.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n9">The decisive moment came on 11 December 1282 at <dfn class="term" data-def="The site, near Builth Wells, where Llywelyn ap Gruffudd was killed in December 1282, ending the line of native princes of Wales.">Cilmeri</dfn> in mid-Wales. Llywelyn was caught with a small escort by an English patrol and killed; his head was struck off and sent to London, where it was paraded through the streets and displayed on the Tower. With Llywelyn dead, native Welsh resistance broke. His brother Dafydd ap Gruffudd was hunted down in summer 1283, betrayed and tried at Shrewsbury for treason. He was the first English nobleman ever sentenced to be hanged, drawn and quartered &mdash; the punishment Edward had devised for the rebellion.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n10">The Statute of Rhuddlan, 1284</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n11">In March 1284 Edward issued the <dfn class="term" data-def="The 1284 royal law that imposed English-style shire administration on conquered Gwynedd, dividing it into the new counties of Anglesey, Caernarfon, Merioneth, Cardigan and Carmarthen.">Statute of Rhuddlan</dfn>, also known as the Statute of Wales. It dismantled the principality. The lands of Llywelyn became royal property. Gwynedd was divided into the new English-style counties of Anglesey, Caernarfon and Merioneth, with Cardigan and Carmarthen further south. Each had a sheriff, an exchequer and a system of writs and royal courts based on English common law &mdash; though some Welsh customs, such as partible inheritance, were tolerated. The Marcher lordships in eastern Wales kept their semi-independent status. The conquest had transformed political geography forever.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">The Iron Ring of Castles</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">After the second war, Edward ordered the most ambitious castle programme in medieval Europe. Around the conquered north and west of Wales he built or rebuilt a chain of stone fortresses &mdash; later historians called this the &lsquo;Iron Ring&rsquo;. The four greatest were begun in 1283 and 1295: Conwy, Caernarfon, Harlech and Beaumaris. Each was placed where a fleet from England could resupply it by sea, so a Welsh rising could not starve the garrison. The total cost ran to roughly &pound;80,000 across the programme &mdash; an enormous sum, equivalent to several years of royal income.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n15">The architect of the programme was a Savoyard mason, <dfn class="term" data-def="The Savoyard master of works who designed and supervised Edward&rsquo;s Welsh castle programme between 1278 and 1309, including Conwy, Harlech and Beaumaris.">Master James of St George</dfn>. He brought ideas from the Alps: concentric walls (an outer ring inside an inner ring), polygonal towers and gatehouses that were small fortresses in their own right. Each castle was paired with a planned <dfn class="term" data-def="A new town with English settlers planted next to a royal castle, designed to bring trade, taxation and English loyalty into conquered territory.">planted borough</dfn> &mdash; a walled town settled with English merchants and craftsmen who paid rents directly to the king. Welsh inhabitants were excluded from these new towns by royal charter. The castles were instruments of military, political and economic control all at once.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n16" data-revision-tip="Close this and write down the four major castles of the Iron Ring with one identifying feature for each (e.g. concentric, polygonal towers, sea-supplied).">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The Iron Ring was a class of post-conquest fortifications &mdash; Conwy, Caernarfon, Harlech and Beaumaris &mdash; built or rebuilt after 1283 under Master James of St George. Each was sea-supplied, paired with a planted English borough and cost roughly &pound;80,000 across the whole programme.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n17">Costs and Consequences</h2>
<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>The financial price of conquest</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n18">The Welsh wars settled the political map of the British Isles for good. After 1284 Wales was no longer a separate principality. Edward&rsquo;s eldest surviving son was created Prince of Wales in 1301 &mdash; the title English heirs to the throne have used ever since. The financial cost, however, was crushing. Castles, garrisons and campaign costs forced Edward into ever heavier taxation, including the wool tax of 1294, and into permanent dependence on Italian banking houses. When the Riccardi of Lucca collapsed under the strain in 1294, the king&rsquo;s war chest collapsed with it &mdash; just as the Madog rising was starting.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h3 data-narration-id="n20">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n21">The first Welsh war (1277) ended with the Treaty of Aberconwy, reducing Llywelyn to a rump principality of Gwynedd; the second war (1282&ndash;83) ended with his death at Cilmeri and the execution of his brother Dafydd.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n22">The Statute of Rhuddlan (1284) imposed English shire administration on conquered Gwynedd, dividing it into the new counties of Anglesey, Caernarfon and Merioneth.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n23">The Iron Ring &mdash; Conwy, Caernarfon, Harlech and Beaumaris &mdash; was a class of sea-supplied fortifications, paired with planted boroughs, designed by Master James of St George at a total cost of around &pound;80,000.</li>
</ul>
