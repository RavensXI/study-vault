<h2 data-narration-id="n1">From Caribbean Foothold to Continental Push</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">By 1510 Spain had built a colony on Hispaniola but had not yet pushed onto the mainland. Within a decade that changed completely. In 1513 Vasco Nunez de Balboa walked across the isthmus of Panama and saw the Pacific Ocean. Between 1511 and 1515, Diego Velazquez conquered Cuba and made it the staging post for everything that came next. In 1519&ndash;22 Ferdinand Magellan&rsquo;s expedition sailed across the Pacific and the surviving ship circled the globe. By 1520 Spain had a Caribbean base, a Pacific shore, and a transpacific reach &mdash; the platform Cortes and Pizarro would use for the great mainland conquests of Mexico and Peru. This lesson follows the three steps that made the launch pad.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n3">Balboa Reaches the Pacific (1513)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n4">Vasco Nunez de Balboa was the unlikeliest founder of a Pacific empire. A failed pig farmer on Hispaniola, in debt, he stowed away in a barrel on a 1510 expedition to the Caribbean coast of South America. By the time the barrel was discovered at sea, the expedition had no choice but to take him on. Within a year he had become governor of Spain&rsquo;s small foothold at <dfn class="term" data-def="The early Spanish settlement on the Caribbean coast of present-day Panama, founded in 1510 and the springboard for Balboa&rsquo;s 1513 expedition across the isthmus.">Darien</dfn>, in present-day Panama, by ousting his rival in a coup. From local Indigenous informants Balboa heard that, just over the mountains to the south, lay another sea and a kingdom rich in gold &mdash; what we now call Peru.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n5">On 1 September 1513, Balboa set out with around 190 Spaniards, several hundred Indigenous porters and a pack of war dogs. They cut their way through 65 miles of mountainous tropical forest. On 25 September 1513, alone on a peak, Balboa saw a glittering ocean to the south. He came down to the shore, waded into the surf in full armour, and claimed the entire ocean &mdash; which he called the South Sea &mdash; for the Spanish Crown. He was the first European to see the Pacific from the Americas.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n6" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: when Balboa reached the Pacific, what he called it, and which European empire he claimed it for.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>On 25 September 1513, Vasco Nunez de Balboa became the first European to see the Pacific from the Americas. He waded into the surf in armour and claimed the &lsquo;South Sea&rsquo; for Spain &mdash; opening the door to a westward push that would reach Mexico, Peru and the Philippines.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n7">Balboa&rsquo;s discovery transformed Spanish strategy. The Pacific was no longer a rumour; it was a Spanish ocean. Crucially, the rumours of a southern empire of gold became official intelligence at the Spanish court &mdash; planting the seed of what Pizarro would harvest in 1532. Yet Balboa himself had a short reward: in 1519 his rival Pedrarias Davila, sent as the new royal governor, had him beheaded on a trumped-up charge of treason. The man who claimed the Pacific did not live to use it.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n8">The Conquest of Cuba (1511&ndash;15)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9">While Balboa was crossing the isthmus, another conquistador was conquering an island. In 1511 Diego Velazquez landed on Cuba with around 300 Spaniards and a force of Tainos from Hispaniola. The campaign that followed lasted three years and was brutal. Cuban Tainos resisted under a remarkable cacique called <dfn class="term" data-def="Taino cacique who fled Hispaniola for Cuba and led Indigenous resistance against the Spanish; burned at the stake by Velazquez in 1512.">Hatuey</dfn>, who had fled the slaughter on Hispaniola to warn Cuban communities of what was coming. He told them that the Spanish god was gold, and that if they did not hide it the Spanish would destroy them.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n10">In 1512 Hatuey was captured and burned at the stake. As the flames were lit, a Franciscan friar offered him conversion to Christianity, promising that he would go to heaven. The friar Bartolome de las Casas later recorded Hatuey&rsquo;s reply: he asked whether Spaniards went to heaven, and when told they did, said he would not go there &mdash; for he wanted nothing to do with such cruel people. Hatuey&rsquo;s martyrdom became one of the most powerful indictments of the conquest in Las Casas&rsquo;s writings.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n11">Velazquez completed the conquest by 1515 and founded Spanish towns including <dfn class="term" data-def="Founded by Velazquez in 1515 as the first Spanish capital of Cuba; later a key staging port for the conquest of Mexico.">Santiago de Cuba</dfn> and Havana. The encomienda system was carried over from Hispaniola, and the Cuban Taino population collapsed under it within a generation. But for Spanish purposes Cuba was now what Hispaniola had been: a base. From the harbours of Santiago and Havana, the next wave of conquistadors &mdash; including a young Hernan Cortes &mdash; would launch into the mainland. Without Cuba, there is no Mexico in 1519.</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n12">Students often write about &lsquo;the Spanish&rsquo; as one block. The reality is messier. Velazquez, having conquered Cuba, became its governor and tried to control who launched expeditions from it. He fell out with Cortes in 1519 and tried to recall him from Mexico. Pedrarias Davila, the new governor of Panama, had Balboa beheaded as a political rival in 1519. The conquistadors competed bitterly with each other for governorships, encomiendas, royal favour and the right to launch the next expedition. Showing this internal Spanish conflict is a top-band move &mdash; the empire was not a single project but a tournament between conquistadors that the Crown supervised and (eventually) controlled.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n13">Magellan&rsquo;s Voyage (1519&ndash;22)</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n14">The third leg of the conquistador platform was not a conquest but a voyage. <dfn class="term" data-def="Portuguese-born commander of the 1519&ndash;22 Spanish expedition that crossed the Pacific; killed in the Philippines, his ship completed the first circumnavigation of the globe.">Ferdinand Magellan</dfn> was a Portuguese navigator who, after falling out with the Portuguese Crown, offered his services to the young King Charles I of Spain (later Holy Roman Emperor Charles V). Charles wanted a westward route to the Spice Islands of the East Indies &mdash; the source of the cloves, nutmeg and pepper that fed Europe&rsquo;s appetite for spices &mdash; that did not infringe Portugal&rsquo;s eastward route around Africa under the Treaty of Tordesillas (1494).</p>

<p data-narration-id="n15">Magellan sailed from Seville on 10 August 1519 with five ships and around 270 men. After mutinies, ice storms in the south Atlantic and the discovery of the strait that still bears his name at the southern tip of South America, he crossed the Pacific in 99 brutal weeks &mdash; some crewmen ate leather and rats &mdash; and reached the islands he named for Saint Lazarus, today the Philippines. There, in April 1521, he was killed in a skirmish at the Battle of Mactan after intervening in a local power struggle.</p>

<p data-narration-id="n16">The expedition continued under his Basque second-in-command, <dfn class="term" data-def="Basque captain who took command after Magellan&rsquo;s death and brought the Victoria home in 1522, completing the first circumnavigation of the globe.">Juan Sebastian Elcano</dfn>. Of the five original ships and around 270 men, only one ship, the Victoria, and 18 men returned to Seville on 6 September 1522. They had become the first humans known to have circled the globe.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n17" data-revision-tip="Test yourself: name the captain who completed the first circumnavigation, the year his ship returned, and how many of the 270 original men survived.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Magellan&rsquo;s expedition (1519&ndash;22) gave Spain its claim to the Philippines and produced the first circumnavigation of the globe. Magellan was killed in the Philippines in April 1521; Juan Sebastian Elcano brought the Victoria home in September 1522 with only 18 of the original 270 men.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n18">Why It Mattered</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n19">The Spanish claim to the Philippines, founded on Magellan&rsquo;s voyage, would last until 1898 and gave Spain a foothold in Asia that pulled silk, porcelain and spices through Mexican ports across the Pacific. The Pacific was now visibly a Spanish ocean &mdash; reached by Balboa from the east, crossed by Magellan from the west. Combined with Cuba as a Caribbean staging post, Spain had the geography it needed for the next, biggest moves: Cortes&rsquo;s expedition to Mexico in 1519 (launched from Santiago de Cuba while Velazquez tried in vain to recall him), and Pizarro&rsquo;s push into Peru in 1532 (built on the rumours Balboa had collected on the Pacific shore).</p>

    <p data-narration-id="n20">Examiners flag the trap of writing three mini-paragraphs as if Balboa, Cuba and Magellan were unrelated. They are not. Balboa&rsquo;s 1513 Pacific opened the strategic question that Magellan&rsquo;s 1519&ndash;22 voyage answered: how do we cross it? The Cuban conquest of 1511&ndash;15 supplied the men, ships and food that Cortes used in 1519 to launch westward into Mexico, completing the platform. Top-band analytical narratives chain these stages with phrases like &lsquo;this in turn made possible&rsquo; or &lsquo;because of this&rsquo; rather than treating them as separate events.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n22">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n23">On 25 September 1513, Vasco Nunez de Balboa became the first European to reach the Pacific from the Americas, claiming the &lsquo;South Sea&rsquo; for Spain and gathering the rumours of a southern empire of gold that would lead to Peru.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n24">Diego Velazquez conquered Cuba (1511&ndash;15), founding Santiago and Havana &mdash; the Indigenous resistance leader Hatuey was burned at the stake in 1512 &mdash; and turning the island into the staging post from which Cortes would launch his Mexican expedition.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n25">Magellan&rsquo;s expedition (1519&ndash;22) gave Spain a Pacific crossing and a claim to the Philippines; Magellan was killed in 1521 and Elcano brought the Victoria home in 1522 to complete the first circumnavigation.</li>
</ul>
