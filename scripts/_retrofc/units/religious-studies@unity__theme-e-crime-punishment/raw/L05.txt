<h2 data-narration-id="n1">What Is the Death Penalty?</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">The <dfn class="term" data-def="Capital punishment; the execution of a criminal which is sanctioned by the state.">death penalty</dfn> (also called capital punishment) is when the state executes a person as punishment for a serious crime. It was abolished in the UK in 1965 as a temporary measure and permanently abolished in 1969. Several campaigns have tried to bring it back, but all have failed.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n3">Today, the death penalty is still legal in some countries, including parts of the USA, China, Iran, and Saudi Arabia. Since abolition in the UK, three people have been pardoned after new evidence proved they were innocent — highlighting one of the strongest arguments against capital punishment.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n4" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Can you recall all 3 items listed here?">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The death penalty was permanently abolished in the UK in 1969. Since then, three people convicted of capital crimes have been pardoned because they were later found to be innocent.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n5">Arguments For and Against the Death Penalty</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n6">The death penalty remains one of the most debated topics in ethics and religion. Both sides use strong moral and practical arguments.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Arguments in Favour of the Death Penalty</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n7"><strong>Deterrence</strong> — The threat of death may stop people from committing the most serious crimes, such as murder. In some Muslim countries, it is used as a deterrent for murder and rape.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n8"><strong>Retribution</strong> — It gives justice for the victim and their family. Some argue that the most serious crimes deserve the ultimate punishment.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n9"><strong>Protection</strong> — It is the only way to guarantee that the worst offenders can never reoffend. Life sentences do not always mean life — murderers in the UK walk free after an average of sixteen years.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n10"><strong>Cost</strong> — Some argue that housing a criminal for their entire life is a waste of resources that could be better spent elsewhere.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Arguments Against the Death Penalty</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11"><strong>Innocent people could be killed</strong> — Legal systems can fail. If someone is wrongly executed, the mistake can never be undone.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n12"><strong>No chance for reformation</strong> — The death penalty removes any possibility that the offender can change and become a better person.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n13"><strong>It is inhumane</strong> — Keeping someone on death row for years causes severe mental suffering. Many argue this is degrading and cruel.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n14"><strong>It does not deter crime</strong> — Countries that still use the death penalty still have high rates of serious crime, suggesting it does not actually prevent offending.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n15"><strong>It contradicts the law on murder</strong> — If killing is wrong, then the state is committing the same act it seeks to punish, making the executor no better than the criminal.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n16">Ethical Arguments: The Principle of Utility and the Sanctity of Life</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n17">Two key ethical arguments are used in the death penalty debate. You need to know both for the exam.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n18" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Define the principle of utility says an action in your own words.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The <dfn class="term" data-def="A philosophical idea that an action is right if it promotes the maximum happiness for the maximum number of people affected by it.">principle of utility</dfn> says an action is right if it brings the greatest happiness to the greatest number. The <dfn class="term" data-def="The belief that all life is holy and is created and loved by God.">sanctity of life</dfn> says all life is sacred because it was created by God.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n19"><strong>The Principle of Utility</strong> — If executing a dangerous criminal protects society and brings greater safety to the majority, then the death penalty could be justified. However, if it causes more pain than pleasure (for example, through wrongful executions or the suffering of death row inmates), then it would be wrong. This argument is difficult to evaluate because measuring happiness and suffering is not straightforward.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n20"><strong>The Sanctity of Life</strong> — This teaching holds that God created all life, so only God has the right to take it away. Executing a criminal is “playing God.” However, some argue that a murderer has committed such a serious sin against the sanctity of life that they have forfeited their own right to life.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n21">Christian Views on the Death Penalty</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n22">Christians hold a range of views on capital punishment. Most modern Christians oppose it, but some find support for it in the Bible.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Christian Arguments Against the Death Penalty</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n23">Most Christians believe only God has the right to take life. The Ten Commandments teach “Do not kill” (Exodus 20:13). Jesus taught forgiveness and warned against judging others harshly. In John 8, Jesus stopped a woman from being put to death for adultery, telling her to “leave your life of sin.” This shows that people should be encouraged to change rather than be executed.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n24">The prophet Ezekiel wrote: “I take no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but rather that they turn away from their ways and live” (Ezekiel 33:11). This supports reformation over retribution. Punishment should help offenders repent and seek forgiveness, which execution makes impossible.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Christian Arguments in Favour of the Death Penalty</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n25">Some Christians refer to Old Testament teachings that appear to support the death penalty. Genesis states: “Whoever sheds human blood, by humans shall their blood be shed” (Genesis 9:6). Exodus also teaches “life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth” (Exodus 21:23–24). These passages suggest that the death penalty is a just punishment for murder.</p>
    <p data-narration-id="n26">Some Christian countries and US states still practise the death penalty. Supporters argue it acts as a deterrent that protects society and allows justice to be seen to be done.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n27">Muslim Views on the Death Penalty</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n28">Islam has a complex relationship with capital punishment. <dfn class="term" data-def="Islamic law derived from the Qur’an and Hadith, covering all aspects of Muslim life.">Shari’ah law</dfn> permits the death penalty for certain serious offences, but mercy is also highly valued.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n29" data-revision-tip="Cover this box. Define under Shari’ah law, the death penalty in your own words.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>Under Shari’ah law, the death penalty is a <dfn class="term" data-def="Fixed punishments set by Allah in the Qur’an for the most serious offences.">hudud</dfn> punishment for some crimes, meaning the Qur’an says it is compulsory. However, mercy is greatly valued — the victim’s family can accept “blood money” (financial compensation) instead of execution.</p>
</div>

<p data-narration-id="n30">The Qur’an teaches: “Do not take life, which Allah has made sacred, except by right” (Surah 17:33). This means life is sacred, but there are circumstances where taking it is permitted. The Qur’an also states: “If anyone is killed unjustly, we have granted the right of retribution to his heir” (Surah 17:33). This allows the victim’s family a role in the punishment.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n31">In practice, public executions are carried out in some Muslim countries to deter others from committing the same crimes. However, many modern Muslims believe the death penalty is outdated, inhumane, and not applied fairly. Organisations like the Muslim Peace Fellowship actively oppose capital punishment.</p>

<h2 data-narration-id="n32">Case Studies: Derek Bentley and Ruth Ellis</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n33">Two famous UK cases highlight the problems with the death penalty. Derek Bentley was executed in 1953 for a murder he did not personally commit — his accomplice fired the gun. Bentley was later given a posthumous pardon in 1998. Ruth Ellis was the last woman to be executed in the UK in 1955. Both cases fuelled public demand for abolition and showed how irreversible the death penalty is when mistakes are made.</p>

<h3 data-narration-id="n35">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n36">The death penalty was permanently abolished in the UK in 1969. Arguments for it include deterrence, retribution, and protection. Arguments against include the risk of executing innocent people, no chance for reformation, and it being inhumane.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n37">The principle of utility asks whether the death penalty brings more happiness than harm. The sanctity of life says only God can take life, so executing criminals is wrong.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n38">Most Christians oppose the death penalty, citing Jesus’s teachings on forgiveness and the commandment “Do not kill.” Some use Old Testament texts like “life for life” to support it. Islam permits it under Shari’ah law but values mercy — families can accept blood money instead.</li>
</ul>
