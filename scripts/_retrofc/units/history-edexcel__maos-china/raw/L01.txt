--- DESCRIPTION ---
How war, warlordism and two rival armies set the stage for civil war in 1945.

--- CONTENT_HTML ---
<h2 data-narration-id="n1">A Country in Ruins</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n2">When Japan surrendered in August 1945, China was not at peace. Eight years of Japanese invasion had killed an estimated 14&ndash;20 million Chinese people, displaced tens of millions more and gutted the cities the <dfn class="term" data-def="The Nationalist Party of Sun Yat-sen and Chiang Kai-shek that ruled mainland China from 1928 until 1949.">Guomindang</dfn> government depended on. Inflation was rampant, the railways were broken and a generation of young men had been conscripted, killed or scattered. Behind that ruin stood a longer crisis: since the fall of the Qing dynasty in 1911, China had been fractured by warlord armies, foreign concessions and a republican government that never fully controlled its own territory.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n3" data-revision-tip="Cover this and recall: by 1945, how long had China been suffering open war and political fragmentation? Name the two periods.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>By 1945 China had endured 34 years of upheaval &mdash; warlord rule from 1916 and full Japanese invasion from 1937. The country that emerged from the Sino-Japanese War was exhausted, divided and ready for civil war.</p>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n4">The Guomindang in 1945: Strong on Paper, Weak in Reality</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n5">Chiang Kai-shek led the Guomindang (GMD) and the Republic of China government from his wartime capital of Chongqing. On the surface, the GMD looked unbeatable. It commanded a regular army of around 4.3 million soldiers, controlled the major cities, held a seat on the new United Nations Security Council, and received steady American aid &mdash; eventually about $3 billion between 1945 and 1949. American transport planes and ships moved GMD troops into former Japanese-held territory faster than the communists could reach it.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n6">Beneath this surface, however, the GMD was hollow. Wartime <dfn class="term" data-def="A rapid and continuing rise in prices that destroys the value of money and savings.">hyperinflation</dfn> was wiping out salaries and savings &mdash; by 1948 it took a wheelbarrow of banknotes to buy a sack of rice. Officials were widely seen as corrupt, hoarding US aid for personal enrichment. Conscription was brutal: peasant villages were ordered to supply soldiers, and recruits were often roped together to stop them deserting. The Guomindang relied on the cities, but the cities were where inflation hurt most.</p>

  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n7">It is tempting to treat August 1945 as a clean reset &mdash; Japan goes home, the civil war begins. The reality is messier. Japanese troops in northern China were ordered by the Allies to surrender to the GMD, but Soviet forces in Manchuria handed captured Japanese weapons to the CCP&rsquo;s Eighth Route Army instead. Refugees, conscript soldiers and stranded Japanese civilians clogged the roads. Most of the country that the GMD now claimed to rule had been governed by puppet regimes, warlords or communist guerrillas for years. The civil war did not start on a blank slate.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n8">The CCP in 1945: Yan&rsquo;an, Land Reform and the Long March Story</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n9">Mao Zedong led the <dfn class="term" data-def="The Chinese Communist Party, sole ruling party of the People's Republic of China from 1949.">Chinese Communist Party</dfn> from the dusty cave town of Yan&rsquo;an in the north-west, where the party had retreated after the Long March of 1934&ndash;35. The CCP&rsquo;s army &mdash; the Eighth Route Army and the New Fourth Army, later renamed the People&rsquo;s Liberation Army &mdash; numbered roughly one million troops. On every measurable count of equipment and air power, the GMD was stronger. But the CCP had built two assets the GMD could not match.</p>
<p data-narration-id="n10">First, the CCP had used the Sino-Japanese War to build a peasant base. In areas it controlled, the party reduced rents, redistributed land from absentee landlords and ran literacy classes. Soldiers in the Red Army were under orders not to steal from villages or harm civilians &mdash; a code known as the &lsquo;Three Rules of Discipline and Eight Points of Attention&rsquo;. The contrast with looting GMD conscripts was stark, and recruiters for the communists turned the difference into propaganda. Second, Mao had used Yan&rsquo;an to consolidate his own leadership. The Rectification Campaign of 1942&ndash;44 purged rivals such as Wang Ming, while the legend of the <dfn class="term" data-def="The 1934-35 retreat of the Chinese Red Army from Jiangxi to Yan'an, mythologised by the CCP as a heroic founding event.">Long March</dfn> was carefully shaped after the fact to present Mao as the indispensable leader.</p>

<div class="collapsible">
  <button class="collapsible-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
    <span>Misconception: "The Long March was the unambiguous origin of CCP victory"</span>
    <svg class="collapsible-icon" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><polyline points="6 9 12 15 18 9"/></svg>
  </button>
  <div class="collapsible-content"><div class="collapsible-inner">
    <p data-narration-id="n11">The Long March is the founding myth of the CCP &mdash; a year-long retreat over 5,600 miles, crossing rivers and snow-capped passes. The numbers are humbling: around 86,000 set out from Jiangxi in October 1934 and roughly 8,000 reached Yan&rsquo;an in October 1935. But the historian Sun Shuyun and the Historical Association both stress that the heroic narrative was built afterwards. During the March itself Mao manoeuvred against rival communist leaders, especially Zhang Guotao, and the propaganda value of the March only grew with retelling. Strong answers acknowledge the March mattered for survival and for Mao&rsquo;s personal authority &mdash; but treat the &lsquo;heroic origin&rsquo; story as constructed, not given.</p>
  </div></div>
</div>

<h2 data-narration-id="n12">The Marshall Mediation, 1945&ndash;46</h2>
<p data-narration-id="n13">In August 1945 Mao flew to Chongqing to negotiate with Chiang Kai-shek under American pressure. The talks produced a fragile truce. President Truman then sent General George C. Marshall to broker a coalition government and a unified army. For most of 1946, Marshall shuttled between the two sides. Zhou Enlai, Mao&rsquo;s chief negotiator, played for time. Chiang doubted Marshall&rsquo;s promises of further aid and used the lull to position GMD divisions in Manchuria. Local fighting never really stopped, and by January 1947 Marshall returned to Washington and declared his mission a failure. The full-scale civil war that followed was therefore not a sudden outbreak but a return to the path that Japan&rsquo;s surrender had only briefly interrupted.</p>

<div class="key-fact" data-narration-id="n14" data-revision-tip="Close this and list four GMD weaknesses in 1945 that mattered more than its larger army.">
  <div class="key-fact-label">Key Fact</div>
  <p>The GMD entered 1945 with 4.3 million soldiers, the cities, the UN seat and US aid &mdash; but with hyperinflation, corruption, brutal conscription and a hollowed-out countryside. The CCP had a smaller army of around 1 million but a tested rural base, disciplined troops and a unified leadership under Mao at Yan&rsquo;an.</p>
</div>

--- CONCLUSION_HTML ---
<h3 data-narration-id="n16">Key Takeaways</h3>
<ul>
  <li data-narration-id="n17">By 1945 China had been at war or fragmented for over three decades &mdash; the civil war began on a country, and a peasantry, that were already exhausted.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n18">The Guomindang held the cities, the army on paper and US backing, but was hollowed out by hyperinflation, corruption and an unpopular conscription system.</li>
  <li data-narration-id="n19">The CCP at Yan&rsquo;an had built a disciplined peasant army, a record of land reform in liberated zones and a unified leadership under Mao &mdash; the Marshall mediation only briefly delayed open war.</li>
</ul>
