# Widget brief: Timbre as an integral compositional choice

Cluster id: `timbre-integral-to-composition`  |  serves 1 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Timbre is an integral part of compositional meaning chosen alongside melody and harmony, not a decorative addition applied afterward.

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [music-aqa] Twentieth-Century Colour: Orchestration, Dynamics and Space — `/lesson/music-aqa/aos4-since-1910/1`
Triage: Students think timbre is a surface decoration applied after the melodic and harmonic ideas are complete, rather than an integral compositional choice that changes the meaning of the music itself.

> A New Sound World Before 1910, most classical music built its effect from melody, harmony and rhythm, in that order. Tone colour mattered, but it served the tune underneath it. After 1910, many composers began treating timbre itself as a subject worth composing with, not just a wrapper around a melody. The choice of instrument became part of the musical idea, not an afterthought. This shows up first in orchestration . Composers picked unusual instrument pairings, extreme registers and exposed solo colours precisely because of how they sounded, not just because of the notes they played. A phrase given to a bassoon high in its range, or to a solo flute over hushed strings, means something different from the same phrase given to a full string section &mdash; even if every pitch is identical.
