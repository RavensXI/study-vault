# Widget brief: Reactivity series as electron transfer

Cluster id: `reactivity-series-electron-transfer`  |  serves 5 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Displacement reactions occur because more reactive metals more readily lose electrons to less reactive metal ions — position in the reactivity series reflects and explains this electron-transfer tendency, not physical force or arbitrary memorisation.

> The Reactivity Series Metals can be arranged in order of their reactivity — this is called the reactivity series . The most reactive metals (potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, aluminium) react vigorously with water and acids. Less reactive metals (zinc, iron, copper, silver, gold) react more slowly or not at all. A more reactive metal will displace a less reactive metal from its compound. > The Reactivity Series Metals can be placed in order of how readily they form positive ions. The more easily a metal loses its outer electrons, the more reactive it is. Chemists have arranged metals in a reactivity series , with potassium, sodium, lithium and calcium at the top, then magnesium, aluminium, zinc and iron in the middle, and copper, silver and gold at the bottom. Carbon and hydrogen are included as non-metal reference points &mdash; their positions determine how metals can be extracted and which metals react with dilute acids. ### 3. [separate-sciences-ocr-b] Metals and Reactivity — `/lesson/separate-sciences-ocr-b/chemistry-materials-reactions/1`
Triage: Students think the reactivity series is an arbitrary list to memorize, and don't grasp that a metal 'higher' in the series will physically displace a 'lower' one because it 'wants' electrons more strongly.

> The Reactivity Series Different metals react with different levels of vigour with water, oxygen and acids. Chemists arrange metals in order of this reactivity to produce the reactivity series . From most to least reactive, the key metals to know are: Potassium (K) → Sodium (Na) → Calcium (Ca) → Magnesium (Mg) → Aluminium (Al) → Carbon (C) → Zinc (Zn) → Iron (Fe) → Hydrogen (H) → Copper (Cu) → Silver (Ag) → Gold (Au) Carbon and hydrogen are not metals, but they are included as reference points because they are important in extraction reactions. Metals above hydrogen in the series react with dilute acids to produce hydrogen gas; metals below hydrogen do not. The most reactive metals (potassium, sodium) react explosively with cold water, while moderately reactive metals like magnesium react slowly with cold water but vigorously with steam.
