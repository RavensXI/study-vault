# Widget brief: Menstrual cycle as overlapping hormone feedback, not a linear sequence

Cluster id: `menstrual-cycle-hormone-feedback`  |  serves 5 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Multiple hormones (oestrogen, LH, FSH, progesterone) rise, fall, and trigger/suppress each other simultaneously through feedback loops, rather than acting in a simple day-by-day sequence.

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [separate-sciences-ocr] Hormones, the Menstrual Cycle and Plant Hormones — `/lesson/separate-sciences-ocr/biology-paper-1/8`
Triage: Students think the menstrual cycle is a simple linear sequence (day 1→2→3...) rather than a dynamic system where four hormones rise and fall in different phases, each triggering the next through feedback—so they cannot explain why a hormone level dropping causes something to happen, or why stopping one hormone can prevent ovulation.

> The Endocrine System The endocrine system uses chemical messengers called hormones to coordinate and control body functions (B3.2a). Unlike the nervous system, which produces fast, short-lived responses via electrical impulses, the endocrine system produces slower, longer-lasting and more widespread responses. Hormones are released directly into the blood by endocrine glands and travel to their target organs . Only cells with the correct receptor molecules for a hormone will respond to it. Key endocrine glands include the pituitary gland (often called the &ldquo;master gland&rdquo; because it controls other glands), the thyroid gland , the adrenal glands , the pancreas , and the ovaries or testes . > The Menstrual Cycle The menstrual cycle prepares the female body for potential pregnancy each month. It is controlled by four interacting hormones that form a complex feedback system. Understanding the timing and role of each hormone is essential for explaining contraception and fertility treatment. The four hormones and their roles: FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone) is produced by the pituitary gland and stimulates the maturation of an egg inside a follicle in the ovary. As the follicle matures, it produces oestrogen . Oestrogen has three effects: it causes the uterus lining to thicken and repair after menstruation, it inhibits further FSH production (negative feedback), and it stimulates a surge of LH .
