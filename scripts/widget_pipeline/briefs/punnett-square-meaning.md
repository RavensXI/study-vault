# Widget brief: Punnett square cells as equally likely allele pairings

Cluster id: `punnett-square-meaning`  |  serves 4 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Each cell in a Punnett square represents a distinct, equally likely combination of alleles from the two parents meeting, not just a lookup answer.

> The Structure of DNA DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a polymer found in the nucleus of cells. It is shaped as a double helix — two strands twisted around each other like a spiral ladder. Each strand is made up of repeating units called nucleotide s. Each nucleotide consists of three parts: a sugar (deoxyribose), a phosphate group and one of four bases — adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C) or guanine (G). The two strands are linked by complementary base pair s joined by weak hydrogen bonds. Adenine always pairs with thymine (A–T), and cytosine always pairs with guanine (C–G). Triage: Students think a Punnett square is just a lookup table showing what *could* happen, not understanding that each cell represents a distinct, equally likely genetic outcome that their body had to 'choose' between during meiosis.

> Alleles and Inheritance Most characteristics are controlled by several genes working together (polygenic). Some characteristics are controlled by a single gene with two forms called alleles . You inherit one allele from each parent, giving you two alleles for each gene. If the two alleles are the same, the individual is homozygous for that gene. If they are different, the individual is heterozygous . A dominant allele is always expressed when present (shown by a capital letter, e.g. B). A recessive allele is only expressed when two copies are present (bb). Key Fact Genotype = the combination of alleles (e.g. Bb). Phenotype = the physical characteristic expressed (e.g. brown eyes). Dominant alleles only need one copy to be expressed; recessive alleles need two copies (homozygous recessive). > How Genes Are Inherited During sexual reproduction, each parent contributes one gamete &mdash; a sperm or egg cell. Each gamete carries one allele for every gene. When sperm and egg fuse at fertilisation, the resulting cell (and all body cells that develop from it) contains two alleles for each gene &mdash; one from each parent. This is how inherited characteristics are passed on. The two alleles at a gene may be identical or different. If they are identical, the individual is homozygous for that gene. If they are different, the individual is heterozygous . Whether a characteristic appears in the phenotype depends on which alleles are present and how they interact.
