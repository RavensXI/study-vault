# Widget brief: Sonata form defined by key relationships, not just repetition

Cluster id: `sonata-form-key-relationships`  |  serves 1 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Sonata form requires the second subject to modulate to a related key in the exposition, distinguishing it from simple 'theme repeated' structures

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [music-aqa] Structures You Must Recognise: Binary, Ternary, Rondo, Variations and Sonata Form — `/lesson/music-aqa/aos1-western-classical/2`
Triage: Students think sonata form is simply 'two themes played, then played again' and cannot distinguish the crucial *key relationships* that define it—especially that the second subject must modulate away in the exposition but return home in the recapitulation, and that this tonal shift is the structural anchor.

> You already know that pieces from the classical and romantic periods are built from small musical ideas, repeated and rearranged. Composers writing between 1650 and 1910 relied on a handful of standard structures to organise a whole movement. Learn to recognise five of them &mdash; binary, ternary, rondo, theme and variations, and sonata form &mdash; and you can label the structure of almost any extract in the listening exam, even one you have never heard before. Binary form Binary form has two sections, usually called A and B. Both are heard once, then normally repeated immediately, so the pattern you actually hear is AABB. Section A often ends in a different key from the one it started in, and section B leads the music back home.
