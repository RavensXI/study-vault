# Widget brief: Active listening = six specific named behaviours

Cluster id: `active-listening-behaviours`  |  serves 1 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Active listening is demonstrated through six distinct, nameable behaviours, each signalling something specific to the speaker.

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [health-social-care-ocr] Non-Verbal Communication & Active Listening — `/lesson/health-social-care-ocr/principles-of-care/8`
Triage: A student thinks active listening is just 'paying attention' or 'listening carefully', and doesn't realize it requires six specific, named behaviours that each send a distinct message to the service user.

> What We Say Without Words Research consistently shows that a significant proportion of how a message lands comes not from the words spoken but from non-verbal communication : the look on a practitioner's face, the way they position their body, whether they make eye contact or glance away. In a health or social care setting, non-verbal signals can either reinforce or completely contradict the verbal message. A care worker who says &ldquo;take as much time as you need&rdquo; while visibly checking their watch sends two opposite messages. Service users, particularly those who are anxious or in pain, read these signals carefully. Consider Blessing, a support worker at a residential home for adults with acquired brain injuries.
