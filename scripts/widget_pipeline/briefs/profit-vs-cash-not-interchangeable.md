# Widget brief: Profit is not the same as available cash

Cluster id: `profit-vs-cash-not-interchangeable`  |  serves 3 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

A recorded sale/profit does not mean cash is immediately available to pay bills; profit and cash flow are different measures.

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [business-aqa] Cash Flow — `/lesson/business-aqa/finance/2`
Triage: Students believe that if a business makes a sale and records profit, the cash is immediately available to pay bills, and that profit and cash flow are interchangeable measures of financial health.

> Cash Keeps a Business Alive A business can be profitable on paper and still collapse if it runs out of cash. Wages, rent and supplier bills all have to be paid in actual money on the day they fall due — the tax office and the landlord do not accept IOUs. Cash flow is the movement of money into and out of a business over time. If more is going out than coming in for too long, the business becomes insolvent and folds. This is why the biggest conceptual trap in Finance is assuming profit and cash are the same thing. A sale generates profit the moment it is made, but the cash may not arrive for 30, 60 or 90 days if the customer is on trade credit. Meanwhile the business still has to pay wages every Friday.
