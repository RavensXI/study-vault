# Widget brief: EM spectrum is one continuous spectrum, not distinct types

Cluster id: `em-spectrum-continuous`  |  serves 5 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

The seven regions of the EM spectrum are not fundamentally different things but a single continuous spectrum differing only in wavelength/frequency.

### 2. [science-ocr] Wave Behaviour — `/lesson/science-ocr/physics-paper-2/1`

> Types of Wave Waves transfer energy from one place to another without transferring matter . The particles of the medium vibrate about their rest positions but do not travel along with the wave. There are two main categories of wave: • Transverse waves — the vibrations are perpendicular (at right angles) to the direction of energy transfer. The wave forms crests (peaks) and troughs. Examples include light waves, water surface ripples, all electromagnetic waves, and waves on a string. • Longitudinal waves — the vibrations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer. Instead of crests and troughs, longitudinal waves have compressions (particles pushed close together) and rarefactions (particles spread apart). Sound waves are the main example at GCSE. ### 3. [separate-sciences-ocr] The Electromagnetic Spectrum — `/lesson/separate-sciences-ocr/physics-paper-2/2`
Triage: Students think frequency and wavelength are independent properties, or that increasing frequency means the wave gets 'bigger' rather than 'smaller', because prose cannot show both axes changing simultaneously while speed stays fixed.

> What Are Electromagnetic Waves? Electromagnetic (EM) waves are transverse waves that can travel through a vacuum — they do not need a medium. This makes them fundamentally different from mechanical waves like sound. In a vacuum, all EM waves travel at the same speed: $$c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$$ EM waves are produced by changes in atoms and their nuclei, or by oscillating electrical charges. They transfer energy from a source to an absorber. Because the wave equation \(v = f\lambda\) still applies and the speed is fixed in a vacuum, a higher frequency always means a shorter wavelength — and vice versa. The Electromagnetic Spectrum The electromagnetic spectrum is a continuous range of waves ordered by wavelength and frequency.
