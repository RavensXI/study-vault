# Widget brief: Separating underlying trend from seasonal/random noise

Cluster id: `time-series-trend-vs-noise`  |  serves 1 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

A time series graph's overall trend must be distinguished from short-term seasonal fluctuations or noise, so a single rise doesn't confirm a rising trend

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [statistics-aqa] Time Series, Trends and Rates of Change — `/lesson/statistics-aqa/interpreting-results-sec/3`
Triage: Students see a time series graph and treat every wiggle as equally important, unable to mentally separate the underlying trend from seasonal noise, or they confuse a rising count with a rising rate.

> What Is a Time Series? A time series is data recorded at successive time points &mdash; monthly sales figures, quarterly unemployment rates, annual birth rates. Plotting these values against time produces a time series graph (sometimes called a line chart) that lets you see patterns that would be invisible in a table of numbers. Trends The trend is the overall direction a time series is heading in the long run, ignoring short-term ups and downs. A trend might be rising (e.g. average house prices in the UK over decades), falling (e.g. infant mortality rates over the last century), or roughly constant. Identifying the trend is the first step in interpreting a time series. It is important to distinguish the trend from short-term variation.
