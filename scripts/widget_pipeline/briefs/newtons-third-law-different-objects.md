# Widget brief: Third law pairs act on different objects, don't cancel

Cluster id: `newtons-third-law-different-objects`  |  serves 4 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Newton's third law action-reaction pairs act on two different objects and never cancel each other out; they cannot explain why one object stays still

Both types are vector quantities — they have size and direction. ### 3. [science-aqa] Newton's Laws of Motion — `/lesson/science-aqa/physics-paper-2/4`

> Newton’s First Law Newton’s first law states that an object will remain at rest or continue to move at a constant velocity unless a resultant force acts on it. This means that if all forces on an object are balanced (resultant force = 0), the object will not change its motion. A stationary object stays still. A moving object keeps moving at the same speed in the same direction. Key Fact Inertia is the tendency of an object to resist a change in motion. The more mass an object has, the greater its inertia. This is why a heavy object is harder to push into motion or bring to a stop than a light one.
