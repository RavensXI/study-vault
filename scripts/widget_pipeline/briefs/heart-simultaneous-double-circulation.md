# Widget brief: Heart's two sides pump simultaneously in a double circulatory system

Cluster id: `heart-simultaneous-double-circulation`  |  serves 4 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

The left and right sides of the heart contract simultaneously (not sequentially), pumping blood through two connected loops, with the left ventricle wall thicker to generate higher pressure for the systemic circuit.

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [science-aqa] The Heart, Blood and Circulatory Disease — `/lesson/science-aqa/biology-paper-1/4`
Triage: Students think blood flows in a single loop rather than understanding that the heart simultaneously pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs AND oxygenated blood to the body in two separate, parallel circuits.

> The Heart The heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood around the body through a double circulatory system . The right side of the heart pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs (pulmonary circuit). The left side pumps oxygenated blood to the rest of the body (systemic circuit). The heart has four chambers: two upper atria (singular: atrium) that receive blood, and two lower ventricles that pump blood out. The left ventricle has a thicker muscular wall than the right because it needs to generate higher pressure to pump blood around the entire body, whereas the right ventricle only pumps blood to the nearby lungs. Key Fact The heart has four chambers. Blood flows: vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → body. Triage: Students think blood flows smoothly and continuously through the heart in one direction, and don't visualize the chambers filling and emptying in sequence, or how valves actually prevent backflow in real time.

> The Heart: Structure and Function The heart is a hollow, muscular organ roughly the size of a fist, located in the chest slightly left of centre behind the sternum. It is divided into four chambers by a central wall called the septum . The two upper chambers are called atria (singular: atrium) &mdash; the right atrium receives deoxygenated blood returning from the body, and the left atrium receives oxygenated blood returning from the lungs. The two lower chambers are called ventricles &mdash; the right ventricle pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs, and the left ventricle pumps oxygenated blood to the rest of the body. The left ventricle has a thicker, more muscular wall than the right because it must generate much higher pressure to push blood all the way around the body.
