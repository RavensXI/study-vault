# Widget brief: Half-life and exponential decay

Cluster id: `half-life-exponential-decay`  |  serves 7 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Half-life is the time for half of the remaining nuclei to decay each time (never reaching zero), producing an exponential decay curve, not a countdown to total decay.

> Atomic Structure and Isotopes Every atom has a nucleus made of protons (positive charge) and neutrons (no charge). Electrons, which carry negative charge, occupy energy levels around the nucleus. The number of protons is the atomic number and defines which element the atom is. In a neutral atom, the number of electrons equals the number of protons. Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons. They have the same atomic number but different mass numbers. Nuclear notation is written as \(^A_Z X\), where \(A\) is the mass number (protons + neutrons) and \(Z\) is the atomic number. For example, carbon-12 (\(^{12}_{6}\text{C}\)) has 6 protons and 6 neutrons; carbon-14 (\(^{14}_{6}\text{C}\)) has 6 protons and 8 neutrons.
