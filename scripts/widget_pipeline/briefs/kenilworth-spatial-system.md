> A Castle That Changed Its Purpose Kenilworth Castle served different functions across its 750-year occupied history. In its early centuries it was primarily a military fortress. Under John of Gaunt and later Robert Dudley it became a statement of aristocratic prestige. As a royal castle in the hands of the Crown it served administrative and political functions. Understanding how and why these functions changed is central to the historic environment study. Early Military and Royal Functions For much of the twelfth and thirteenth centuries, Kenilworth was primarily a defensive site and royal asset. Henry II and his successors used it as a base in the Midlands. In 1244 Henry III took formal possession of the castle, recognising its strategic importance in the centre of England.
