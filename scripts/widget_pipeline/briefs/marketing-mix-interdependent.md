# Widget brief: The four Ps are interdependent, not independent levers

Cluster id: `marketing-mix-interdependent`  |  serves 2 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Changing one of the four Ps creates knock-on pressure on the others, so they must be adjusted coherently together.

> What Is the Marketing Mix? A start-up can have a great product and still fail to sell it if the price is wrong, customers cannot find it, or nobody knows it exists. The marketing mix is the set of decisions that brings a product to market in a joined-up way. It is called &ldquo;the four Ps&rdquo; because each element starts with the letter P: Product , Price , Place and Promotion . The four Ps are not independent of each other. They must work together to give a consistent message to the customer. A business that sets a high price but sells through a budget supermarket is sending mixed signals &mdash; and customers will be confused about whether the product is premium or economy. The extended-response evaluation question in the written exam always focuses on Topic Area 4 (marketing), and high-scoring answers show how the four Ps interact and reinforce each other. ### 2. [business-edexcel] Place and the Integrated Marketing Mix — `/lesson/business-edexcel/building-a-business/7`
Triage: Students treat the four Ps as independent levers that can be pulled separately, rather than seeing them as an interconnected system where changing one forces changes in the others.

> Why &lsquo;Place&rsquo; Matters A great product at the right price with excellent promotion still fails if customers cannot find it or buy it conveniently. Distribution is the process of getting a product from the manufacturer to the end customer. The right distribution strategy reduces cost, increases reach and improves the customer experience. The two main distribution channels are retailers and e-tailers. Retailers are businesses that sell products to the public &mdash; they can be large supermarket chains, independent shops or department stores. They take on stock risk and in return earn a margin on each sale. E-tailers sell only through the internet. Large examples include ASOS, Amazon Marketplace sellers and Ocado.
