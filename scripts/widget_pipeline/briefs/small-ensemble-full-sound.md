> Music for the Stage: Pit Bands and Dramatic Sonority Musical theatre relies on a small group of live musicians &mdash; the pit band &mdash; positioned below or beside the stage to accompany singers and dancers. Unlike a symphony orchestra, a pit band is usually a reduced ensemble, often mixing acoustic and electronic instruments, chosen specifically to serve the drama rather than to showcase orchestral colour for its own sake. A typical pit band might include a handful of strings, one or two brass, a small woodwind section, keyboard (sometimes covering string or brass parts electronically), guitar, bass and drum kit. Composers and orchestrators pick sonority &mdash; the overall tone colour of a passage &mdash; to match dramatic mood: dissonant strings, a tolling organ and a shrieking factory whistle for menace, as in Sweeney Todd, or bright brass fanfares for a triumphant crowd scene.
