# Widget brief: Theatre configuration shapes performance, design and blocking

Cluster id: `theatre-configuration-impact`  |  serves 1 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Different stage configurations (thrust, in-the-round, proscenium etc.) are not interchangeable room layouts — each imposes different constraints on blocking, design and audience relationship.

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [drama-aqa] Theatre Spaces: Configuration and Audience — `/lesson/drama-aqa/theatre-roles-stagecraft/3`
Triage: Students think theatre configurations are just different room layouts with no real impact on how performance, design and blocking must change—they see 'thrust' and 'in-the-round' as interchangeable variations rather than fundamentally different performer-audience contracts.

> The Stage Configuration Question Before any set is built, any costume is made, or any performer begins rehearsing, a director and designer make a foundational choice: where will the audience sit relative to the performance? This is the performer-audience configuration . It shapes every other decision. A gobo that projects a crisp image in a proscenium theatre becomes irrelevant when the audience sits on three sides. A piece of blocking that puts a performer&rsquo;s back to the audience is a problem in end-on staging but a routine occurrence in theatre in the round. You need to understand each configuration, its advantages, its limitations, and what it demands of performers and designers. Key Fact The six staging configurations are: proscenium arch, thrust stage, theatre in the round, traverse, end-on staging, and promenade.
