> Macbeth Seeks the Witches (Act 4, Scene 1) After the banquet disaster, Macbeth actively seeks out the witches. This is a crucial shift from Act 1, where they came to him. He is no longer a passive recipient of prophecy — he demands answers: “I conjure you, by that which you profess.” The imperative verb “conjure” places Macbeth in the role of one who summons evil spirits, aligning him with the witches themselves. Shakespeare shows that Macbeth has fully embraced the supernatural forces he once feared. He tells the witches he is willing to let “the treasure / Of nature’s germens tumble all together, / Even till destruction sicken” — he would rather let nature itself be destroyed than remain ignorant of his fate.
