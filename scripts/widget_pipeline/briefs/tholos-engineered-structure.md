The tholos tomb is a large engineered structure with a long approach and corbelled stone construction built into a hillside, not a simple underground chamber

### 1. [classical-civilisation-ocr] Burial Customs & the Grave Circles — `/lesson/classical-civilisation-ocr/the-mycenaean-world/5`
Triage: Students will picture the tholos tomb as a simple underground chamber, not grasping that it is a massive engineered structure built into a hillside with a 36-metre approach, corbelled stone dome, and architectural decoration—or will fail to understand why corbelling (each stone projecting inward) creates a stable dome rather than collapsing.

> Burial as a Window onto Mycenaean Society No source tells us more about Mycenaean values and social structure than the burials. The tombs of Mycenae &mdash; their scale, their contents, their architectural sophistication &mdash; reveal a society that believed in commemorating the dead with elaborate ceremony and extraordinary wealth. The type of tomb a person received in Mycenaean culture was a direct statement of their status in life. Studying the evolution of burial types from the early shaft graves to the great tholos tombs shows us how Mycenaean society developed over roughly four centuries. We can distinguish three main types of Mycenaean burial: shaft graves , cist graves , and the later chamber tombs and tholos tombs. The richer the individual, the more elaborate the tomb type &mdash; the great tholos tombs of the 14th and 13th centuries BC belong to the height of Mycenaean power.
