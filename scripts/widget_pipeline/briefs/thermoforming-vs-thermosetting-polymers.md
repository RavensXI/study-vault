# Widget brief: Thermoforming softens reversibly; thermosetting cross-links permanently

Cluster id: `thermoforming-vs-thermosetting-polymers`  |  serves 2 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Thermoforming polymers soften reversibly when heated and can be remoulded, while thermosetting polymers undergo permanent cross-linking and cannot be reshaped once set — they behave fundamentally differently when heated.

> Polymers in the Modern World Polymers are ubiquitous in modern product design&mdash;from food packaging to aircraft interiors. All polymers share the same fundamental structure: long molecular chains of repeated units called monomers. The critical distinction for designers and engineers is whether the polymer can be re-melted and reformed (thermoforming) or whether it permanently sets on first cure (thermosetting). This determines which manufacturing processes can be used and how the product performs under heat in service. Thermoforming Polymers (Thermoplastics) Thermoforming polymers (also called thermoplastics) soften when heated and become rigid again on cooling. This cycle can be repeated many times without degradation of the molecular chains. This makes them recyclable and processable by injection moulding, vacuum forming, blow moulding and extrusion. > Three More Material Families After ferrous and non-ferrous metals, the specification introduces three further material families: plastics (divided into two distinct types), smart materials, and composites. Each has a different internal structure that determines how it behaves and what it can be used for. Getting the families right matters in the exam &mdash; a common mistake is to label any modern or technical-sounding material as &lsquo;smart&rsquo;. The definition of a smart material is precise, and you need to apply it correctly. Key Fact A smart material is a material whose property changes reversibly in response to an external stimulus. The stimulus triggers the change; remove the stimulus and the material returns to its original state. This reversibility is the key discriminator. Examples: thermochromic pigments (change colour with temperature) and shape-memory alloys such as Nitinol (return to a remembered shape when heated).
