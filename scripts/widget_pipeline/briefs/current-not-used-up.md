# Widget brief: Current is not 'used up' in a series circuit

Cluster id: `current-not-used-up`  |  serves 12 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Current is the same everywhere in a series circuit; it is energy/voltage that is shared out across components according to their resistance, not current disappearing.

Key Fact Current is measured in amperes (A) and represents the amount of charge flowing past a point per second. The equation is \(I = \frac{Q}{t}\) where I is current, Q is charge in coulombs, and t is time in seconds. Current is measured using an ammeter , which must be connected in series with the component you want to measure. This ensures all the current flows through the ammeter. > Static Electricity Charge is a fundamental property of all matter. In most objects, the number of positive charges equals the number of negative charges, so the object is electrically neutral. When two insulating materials are rubbed together, friction causes electrons to transfer from one surface to the other, creating static electricity . The material that gains electrons becomes negatively charged. The material that loses electrons becomes positively charged. It is always the electrons that move &mdash; protons are locked inside the nucleus and cannot transfer between objects. For example, when a polythene rod is rubbed with a cloth, electrons move from the cloth to the rod: the rod becomes negative and the cloth becomes positive.
