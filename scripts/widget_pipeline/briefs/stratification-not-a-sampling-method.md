# Widget brief: Stratification is a preparatory step, not a sampling method

Cluster id: `stratification-not-a-sampling-method`  |  serves 1 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Stratification divides a population into groups first; a separate method (random/systematic) is then used to actually sample within those groups

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [statistics-aqa] Sampling Methods and Stratification — `/lesson/statistics-aqa/planning-designing-enquiry/4`
Triage: Stratification is a sampling method (like random or systematic), when it is actually a preparatory step that must be combined with a sampling method.

> Why Sampling? A population is everyone or everything the investigation concerns. But collecting data from every member of a population is often impractical &mdash; too expensive, too time-consuming, or simply impossible. Instead, a researcher selects a sample : a manageable subset from which conclusions about the whole population can be drawn. The quality of those conclusions depends critically on how the sample is selected. A badly chosen sample &mdash; one that over-represents some groups and under-represents others &mdash; produces biased results. No amount of clever analysis can fix a biased sample. This is why choosing the right sampling method is one of the most important decisions in the planning stage of any investigation. Before sampling can begin, you need a sample frame : a complete list of everyone in the population. For a school survey, the sample frame might be the roll of all registered students.
