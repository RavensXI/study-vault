# Widget brief: Field lines represent strength/direction, not actual paths

Cluster id: `field-lines-as-maps-not-paths`  |  serves 4 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Electric field lines are a visual map of field strength and force direction, not physical objects or the literal path a charge follows

> Static Charge and Electron Transfer All matter is made of atoms, which contain positively charged protons , negatively charged electrons and neutral neutrons . In a neutral atom, the number of protons equals the number of electrons, so the overall charge is zero. When two insulating materials are rubbed together, electrons are transferred from one surface to the other. The material that gains electrons becomes negatively charged, while the material that loses electrons becomes positively charged. This is called static electricity . Key Fact Only electrons move when objects are charged by friction — protons are fixed in the nucleus and cannot transfer between materials. For example, when a polythene rod is rubbed with a cloth, electrons transfer from the cloth to the rod. The rod becomes negatively charged and the cloth becomes positively charged. > Magnetic Poles and Materials Every magnet has two ends called magnetic poles . One is a north-seeking pole (north pole) and the other is a south-seeking pole (south pole). The fundamental rule of magnetism is simple: unlike poles attract and like poles repel . Two north poles push each other away, while a north and a south pole pull towards each other. Only certain materials are magnetic. The four you need to know are iron , steel , cobalt , and nickel . These are called magnetic materials . > Electric Charge and Its Origins Matter is made of atoms, and atoms contain negatively charged electrons and a positively charged nucleus. In most materials, positive and negative charges balance &mdash; the object is electrically neutral. Static electricity arises when electrons are transferred from one object to another, leaving one with a surplus of negative charge and the other with a deficit (making it positively charged). Rubbing two materials together is a common way to transfer electrons. When a plastic rod is rubbed with a cloth, electrons move from the cloth to the rod. The rod gains a negative charge; the cloth gains an equal positive charge.
