# Widget brief: Structural systems bear load differently

Cluster id: `structural-systems-differ-in-load-bearing`  |  serves 2 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Different structural systems (e.g. load-bearing walls vs frames) carry weight differently, so removing a wall carries different risk depending on the system.

> How Buildings Stand Up Every building needs a structure that carries its own weight plus the loads placed on it — people, furniture, wind, snow and soil pressure. Over centuries, builders developed distinct structural forms to suit different uses and materials. Today, three systems dominate modern construction: cellular construction , rectangular frame construction, and portal frame construction . Each has particular strengths and a natural home in the built environment. Cellular Construction In cellular construction, the walls themselves are the structure. Load-bearing walls provide the main vertical support and lateral stability for floors — the building would not stand without them. Floors, roofs and beams are all bridging components supported directly by these walls, and removing or weakening a load-bearing wall can cause catastrophic failure. > The Structure of a Low-Rise Building Every low-rise building — a house, school or small commercial unit — is made up of a series of interconnected elements, each carrying specific loads and performing distinct functions. Understanding how these elements relate to one another is fundamental to working in construction. We can divide a building into two main zones: the substructure and the superstructure . Foundations Foundations sit at the very base of a building and transfer the combined weight of the structure — including people, furniture and snow loads — safely into the ground. The type of foundation chosen depends on the load the building imposes and the bearing capacity of the soil. Strip foundations run as continuous concrete strips beneath load-bearing walls.
