> Athens and the Sea The battle that truly saved Greece from Persian conquest was not fought on land but on the water. In 480 BC, the same year as Thermopylae, the Greek and Persian fleets clashed in the narrow strait between the island of Salamis and the Attic coastline. The result &mdash; a decisive Greek victory &mdash; shattered Persian naval power and made it impossible for Xerxes to supply or reinforce his army effectively. Without that victory at sea, the land battles that followed could never have ended Greek resistance. Athens was central to the Greek naval effort. In the years before 480 BC, the Athenian statesman Themistocles had persuaded the Athenian assembly to invest a windfall of silver from the mines at Laurion in building a fleet of two hundred triremes .
