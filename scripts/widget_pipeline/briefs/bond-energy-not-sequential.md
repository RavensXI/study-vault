# Widget brief: Bond breaking and making are not sequential in time

Cluster id: `bond-energy-not-sequential`  |  serves 4 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Bond breaking and bond making are simultaneous/overlapping parts of one process, and it's the relative energy stored in bonds (not timing) that determines exothermic vs endothermic.

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [science-aqa] Energy Changes in Chemical Reactions — `/lesson/science-aqa/chemistry-paper-1/9`
Triage: Students think bond breaking and bond making happen sequentially in time, rather than understanding that the energy *stored* in bonds (relative to products) determines whether a reaction is exothermic or endothermic overall.

> Introduction to Energy Changes All chemical reactions involve energy changes. When chemical bonds are broken and new bonds are formed, energy is either released to the surroundings or absorbed from the surroundings. Understanding these energy changes is crucial for predicting how reactions behave and their practical applications. Key Fact Energy is required to break chemical bonds and energy is released when new bonds are formed. Exothermic Reactions An exothermic reaction is one where energy is released to the surroundings. In these reactions, more energy is released when new bonds form than is needed to break the original bonds. This results in a net release of energy, usually observed as an increase in temperature of the surroundings. > Energy Changes in Reactions Every chemical reaction involves an energy change. Energy is either transferred to the surroundings or absorbed from them. The key principle is that energy is transferred , never created or destroyed. Two categories describe the direction of this transfer: exothermic and endothermic. Understanding which type a reaction belongs to &mdash; and the bond-level explanation for why &mdash; is central to chemistry at this level. Key Fact Exothermic reactions transfer energy to the surroundings &mdash; the temperature of the surroundings increases. Endothermic reactions take in energy from the surroundings &mdash; the temperature of the surroundings decreases. Exothermic reactions include combustion (burning fuels), neutralisation (acid + alkali), and many oxidation reactions. Everyday examples include hand warmers and self-heating cans.
