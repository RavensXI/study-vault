# Widget brief: Organelles are spatially organised, not floating randomly

Cluster id: `organelle-3d-spatial-architecture`  |  serves 4 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Organelles occupy a coordinated, densely packed 3D arrangement in the cytoplasm where position/proximity matters, rather than floating randomly

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [separate-sciences-edexcel] Cell Structure, Microscopy and Specialised Cells — `/lesson/separate-sciences-edexcel/biology-paper-1/1`
Triage: Students think organelles are separate compartments floating randomly in cytoplasm, rather than a coordinated, densely-packed 3D architecture where position and proximity matter to function.

> Eukaryotic and Prokaryotic Cells All living things are made of cells. Cells fall into two fundamental types: eukaryotic cells and prokaryotic cells. Knowing the differences between them &mdash; and being able to name sub-cellular structures &mdash; is tested across both papers of your Triple Biology exam. Eukaryotic cells include animal and plant cells. They contain a nucleus that encloses their DNA, along with membrane-bound organelles such as mitochondria and ribosomes. Prokaryotic cells &mdash; bacteria &mdash; are much smaller and simpler. They have no true nucleus; instead, their genetic material is a single loop of DNA floating freely in the cytoplasm. Prokaryotes may also carry small rings of extra DNA called plasmids . Bacteria also have a cell wall, but it is made from a different material to plant cell walls. > How We Came to Know the Cell In 1665, Robert Hooke placed a thin slice of cork under a simple microscope and saw a pattern of tiny compartments. He called them &ldquo;cells&rdquo; because they reminded him of the small rooms in a monastery. Hooke could not see inside these compartments &mdash; his instrument was far too weak. Yet that single observation launched two centuries of investigation, ultimately revealing that cells are the fundamental unit of all life. The history of our understanding of cell structure is a story of technology driving science: each new generation of microscopes unlocked a new layer of biological reality. This lesson explores two fundamental cell types &mdash; eukaryotic and prokaryotic &mdash; and traces how improvements in microscopy revealed the internal structures we now know so well.
