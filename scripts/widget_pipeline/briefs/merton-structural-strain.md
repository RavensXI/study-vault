# Widget brief: Merton's responses are structural adaptations, not personality types

Cluster id: `merton-structural-strain`  |  serves 2 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Merton's five responses to strain are structural adaptations to blocked goals available to anyone in that position, not fixed personal choices or character traits.

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [sociology-aqa] Functionalist and Subcultural Theories: Merton — `/lesson/sociology-aqa/crime-deviance/2`
Triage: Students picture Merton's five responses as fixed personality types or choices made consciously, rather than as structural adaptations—they think innovation is something a 'bad person' does, not something the gap between goals and means forces into being.

> Durkheim: Crime Is Normal and Functional Functionalist sociology begins from the premise that society works like a living organism: its institutions perform necessary functions and, when working well, produce social order and value consensus . Emile Durkheim argued that crime is not simply a social problem to be eliminated &mdash; it is a normal feature of every known society. If crime were to disappear entirely, Durkheim reasoned, society would have become so rigid and repressive that even the most minor non-conformity would be treated as a serious offence. A healthy society requires a certain level of deviance to function. Durkheim identified two functions of crime. First, crime performs a boundary maintenance function: when an offender is publicly punished, the community gathers around the shared moral boundary that has been violated, reaffirming collective values.
