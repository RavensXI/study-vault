# Widget brief: Hitler's rise was contingent, not inevitable

Cluster id: `nazi-rise-contingent-not-inevitable`  |  serves 3 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

The Nazis' rise depended on specific contingent events (Depression, political miscalculation) rather than being an inevitable result of ideology or charisma

## The lessons it will sit in

The Great Depression handed Hitler the votes that the Munich Putsch had failed to seize by force. ### 2. [history-edexcel] The Growth of Nazi Support 1929–32 — `/lesson/history-edexcel/weimar-nazi-germany/6`
Triage: Students think Nazi support grew steadily and inevitably from 1929 onwards, missing that it was contingent on multiple simultaneous pressures (economic collapse, Communist fear, elite funding, propaganda saturation, SA violence) that had to align.

> The Wall Street Crash and Its Impact on Germany In October 1929, the Wall Street Crash sent shockwaves across the world. Share prices on the New York stock exchange collapsed, and American banks demanded the repayment of their loans to Germany. Under the Dawes Plan, Germany had borrowed heavily from the USA to fund its economic recovery during the 1920s. When this money was recalled, German businesses closed, banks failed, and unemployment soared. Key Fact By January 1932, unemployment in Germany had reached over 6 million — roughly one in three of the workforce. Millions more were on short-time working or reduced wages. ### 3. [history-aqa] The failure of Weimar democracy and Hitler's appointment — `/lesson/history-aqa/germany-democracy-dictatorship/8`
Triage: Students picture Hitler's rise as inevitable popular support building steadily from 1930 onwards, when in fact Nazi support peaked in July 1932 and was actively declining by January 1933.

> By the end of 1932 the Nazi vote was falling and the party was almost bankrupt. Yet on 30 January 1933 Adolf Hitler walked into the Reich Chancellery as the new Chancellor of Germany. The puzzle of this lesson is how a party that had just lost 34 Reichstag seats in November came to power eight weeks later. The answer is not that Hitler was &lsquo;voted in&rsquo; &mdash; the Nazis never won an outright majority in a free election. He was appointed by an 85-year-old President after a sequence of backstairs deals between elite conservatives who believed they could control him.
