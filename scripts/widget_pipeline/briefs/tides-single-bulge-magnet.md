# Widget brief: Two tidal bulges, not one magnetic pull

Cluster id: `tides-single-bulge-magnet`  |  serves 1 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

The Moon does not pull ocean water towards it like a magnet making ONE bulge. Tidal force is DIFFERENTIAL gravity: the near side is pulled more than Earth’s centre, and Earth’s centre more than the far side — so water piles up on BOTH sides, and a coastline rotating through both bulges gets two high tides a day, roughly 12½ hours apart. The far-side bulge is the part the misconception cannot explain and the widget must make inevitable.

> Putting the Solar System to Scale Before we can understand eclipses and tides we need to appreciate the vast differences in size and distance between Earth, Moon and Sun. The Sun has a mean diameter of approximately 1.4 million km &#8212; about 109 times wider than Earth. The Moon , with its mean diameter of ~3&#8239;500 km, is only about 1/400th of the Sun&#8217;s diameter. Yet by a remarkable cosmic coincidence the Moon is also roughly 1/400th of the distance from Earth to the Sun. This near-perfect size-to-distance ratio means the Moon&#8217;s angular diameter as seen from Earth is almost identical to the Sun&#8217;s angular diameter (both roughly 0.5°).
