# Widget brief: Cross-cutting is a deliberate structural device, not just a pacing trick

Cluster id: `cross-cutting-structural-device`  |  serves 2 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Cross-cutting forces an audience to hold two simultaneous narrative realities in tension, serving a structural/thematic purpose beyond preventing boredom.

### 1. [drama-aqa] Dramatic Methods: Cross-Cutting and Stage Directions — `/lesson/drama-aqa/the-great-wave/5`
Triage: Cross-cutting is just a technical editing trick to prevent boredom or move the plot along, rather than a deliberate structural choice that forces the audience to hold two simultaneous realities in painful tension.

> Two Countries, One Stage Francis Turnly&rsquo;s The Great Wave takes place across two countries and 24 years. To hold those two worlds simultaneously, Turnly relies on a single structural engine: cross-cutting . Scenes in Japan and North Korea alternate in quick succession, so the audience is never allowed to settle entirely into one world before the other pulls them back. This is not accidental restlessness &mdash; it is Turnly&rsquo;s way of insisting that both lives are happening at the same time, that Hanako&rsquo;s captivity and her family&rsquo;s grief are not separate stories but a single wound. Key Fact Cross-cutting in The Great Wave creates parallel time : the audience watches Hanako adapting to life in North Korea at exactly the same moment that Reiko and Etsuko in Japan are refusing to accept she is dead. The play opens in 1887, the year of Queen Victoria&rsquo;s Golden Jubilee, and follows its threads across roughly a decade and a half of the late Victorian era. Its two-act structure holds two parallel storylines in tension throughout, intercutting between them so that the audience builds understanding of both simultaneously.
