# Widget brief: The Moon rotates at the same rate it orbits (synchronous rotation)

Cluster id: `moon-synchronous-rotation`  |  serves 2 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

The Moon does spin on its axis, but its rotation period exactly matches its orbital period around Earth, which is why we always see the same face — it is not stationary/non-rotating.

> A World That Never Turns Away The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite and our closest neighbour in space. One of its most striking features is that it always shows us the same face. Synchronous rotation means the Moon spins on its axis in exactly the same time it takes to complete one orbit of Earth — approximately 27.3 days. The result is that the near side is permanently visible from Earth, while the far side remained completely unknown until the Soviet Luna 3 spacecraft photographed it in 1959.
