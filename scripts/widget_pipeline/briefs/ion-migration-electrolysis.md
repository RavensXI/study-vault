# Widget brief: Ions migrate toward oppositely charged electrodes

Cluster id: `ion-migration-electrolysis`  |  serves 6 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

In electrolysis, ions move because opposite charges attract at a distance, not because electrodes 'pull' ions or ions move randomly.

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [science-edexcel] Electrolysis & Extraction of Metals — `/lesson/science-edexcel/chemistry-paper-1/8`
Triage: Students think cations and anions move randomly or that the electrode itself somehow 'pulls' ions, rather than understanding that opposite charges are attracted to opposite charges at a distance.

> What Is Electrolysis? Electrolysis is the process of using electrical energy from a direct current (d.c.) supply to decompose (break down) an ionic compound. The ionic compound must be either molten or dissolved in water so that its ions are free to move. In this state, the compound is called an electrolyte . During electrolysis, two electrodes are placed in the electrolyte and connected to a d.c. power supply. Positively charged ions ( cations ) are attracted to the negatively charged electrode (the cathode ). Negatively charged ions ( anions ) are attracted to the positively charged electrode (the anode ). Key Fact Cations (positive ions) migrate to the cathode (negative electrode). Anions (negative ions) migrate to the anode (positive electrode). Remember: Positive And Negative = PAN — Positive goes to the Anode? No! Positive goes to Negative (cathode). ### 2. [science-ocr-b] Electrolysis and Electrolytes — `/lesson/science-ocr-b/chemistry-paper-2/3`
Triage: Students believe ions move randomly or drift passively, and don't realise that opposite charges are actively pulled toward opposite poles—or that the same ion can reach different electrodes depending on its charge.

> What Is Electrolysis? Electrolysis is the process of using an electric current to break down an ionic compound. The compound must be in a state where its ions are free to move &mdash; either molten (heated until liquid) or dissolved in water. Solid ionic compounds cannot be electrolysed because the ions are held in fixed positions within the lattice and cannot carry the current. The ionic compound used in electrolysis is called the electrolyte . It is placed in a container called an electrolytic cell, and two electrodes are connected to a power supply. The electrode connected to the positive terminal of the supply is the anode , and the electrode connected to the negative terminal is the cathode . ### 3. [separate-sciences-ocr] Electrolysis of Molten and Aqueous Compounds — `/lesson/separate-sciences-ocr/chemistry-paper-2/2`
Triage: Students think ions move randomly or that the power supply 'pushes' ions directly, rather than understanding that the power supply creates an electric field that attracts oppositely charged ions to opposite electrodes, and that the ions complete the circuit by carrying charge through the liquid.

> What Is Electrolysis? Electrolysis is the process of using electricity to decompose an ionic compound into its elements. The compound being broken down is called the electrolyte , and it must be either molten or dissolved in water so that the ions are free to move and carry charge. The electrolyte is placed between two electrodes : the cathode (negative electrode) and the anode (positive electrode). Positive ions (cations) migrate to the cathode; negative ions (anions) migrate to the anode. A useful mnemonic is PANIC : Positive Anode, Negative Is Cathode. Key Fact At the cathode (negative): positive ions gain electrons &mdash; this is reduction . At the anode (positive): negative ions lose electrons &mdash; this is oxidation .
