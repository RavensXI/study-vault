Different outlets covering the same event construct competing versions of reality through selection and combination, rather than simply reporting neutral facts.

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [media-studies-aqa] Selection, Combination and the Construction of Reality — `/lesson/media-studies-aqa/media-language/2`
Triage: Students think media products show reality directly, and that different media outlets covering the same event are simply reporting facts—not constructing competing versions through invisible choices of what to include, exclude, and how to arrange it.

> Media Products Are Always Constructed One of the most important ideas in Media Studies is deceptively simple: media products do not show the world as it is &mdash; they construct a version of it. Every photograph, news broadcast, social media post and music video is the result of thousands of choices made by producers: which camera angle to use, which moment to capture, which words to include in a headline, which voices to interview. The audience sees only the result of those choices, not the choosing itself. The process by which producers make and arrange those choices is called selection and combination . What is selected shapes meaning. What is left out &mdash; exclusion &mdash; shapes meaning just as powerfully, but is invisible to the audience who never sees what was cut. Key Fact Selection = what the producer chooses to include. Combination = how those elements are arranged together. Exclusion = what is deliberately left out.
