# Widget brief: Labelling as a self-reinforcing process

Cluster id: `labelling-theory-identity`  |  serves 2 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

A label isn't just a name given once — it actively reconstructs a person's identity, opportunities and self-concept over time.

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [sociology-aqa] Interactionist Theory of Crime: Becker and Labelling — `/lesson/sociology-aqa/crime-deviance/3`
Triage: Students think labelling is just a name given to someone, rather than a self-reinforcing social process that actively reconstructs a person's identity, opportunities, and sense of self across multiple interactions.

> The Interactionist Challenge Functionalist and structural theories ask why certain social conditions (strain, status frustration, class inequality) lead to crime. Interactionist sociology asks a different question: who decides what counts as criminal or deviant in the first place, and what happens to individuals once they are labelled? This shift of focus &mdash; from the causes of rule-breaking to the social processes of labelling &mdash; produced one of the most influential perspectives in the sociology of crime. Interactionism argues that social reality is constructed through interaction and the meanings people assign to actions. Applied to crime and deviance, this means that an act has no inherent criminal quality &mdash; it becomes deviant only when someone with the power to make the label stick successfully defines it as such. ### 2. [sociology-aqa] Processes Within Schools — `/lesson/sociology-aqa/education/5`
Triage: Students often think self-fulfilling prophecy means a teacher's expectation is magically powerful, rather than understanding the specific chain: label → changed teacher behaviour → pupil internalisation → changed pupil behaviour → confirmation.

> What Happens Inside Schools? Sociologists who focus on the macro level &mdash; class, capitalism, patriarchy &mdash; risk overlooking the daily processes within schools that shape individual outcomes. Interactionist sociologists argue that what happens in classrooms and corridors &mdash; how teachers perceive and label pupils, how pupils are grouped, how peer cultures develop &mdash; has a profound effect on achievement that cannot be explained solely by external factors such as class or home background. Key Fact Processes within schools that affect educational achievement include: streaming , setting , mixed ability teaching, labelling and the self-fulfilling prophecy .
