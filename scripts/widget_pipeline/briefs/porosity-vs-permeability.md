# Widget brief: Porosity is not the same as permeability

Cluster id: `porosity-vs-permeability`  |  serves 2 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

Porosity measures how much space (pores) a rock has, while permeability measures how well those pores are connected to allow fluid flow through them, so high porosity does not guarantee high permeability

> What is an Aquifer? Most of the freshwater people use does not come from rivers or lakes &mdash; it comes from underground. An aquifer is a body of rock that is both porous enough to store significant quantities of water and permeable enough to transmit it. The water table marks the upper boundary of the saturated zone &mdash; all rock below it has pores filled with water; above it, pores contain mainly air. Where the water table meets the surface, a natural spring forms. Wells are simply boreholes drilled down to or below the water table to extract groundwater. Two properties govern whether a rock makes a good aquifer: porosity (how much water it can store) and permeability (how readily water moves through it). > Breaking Rocks Down: Weathering Before sediment can travel anywhere, rock must first be broken apart. Weathering is the in-place breakdown of rock. It happens at or near the Earth&rsquo;s surface where rock is exposed to the atmosphere, water and living organisms. Once weathered material is loosened from the parent rock, erosion picks it up and transport carries it away. Understanding these three linked stages &mdash; weathering, erosion, transport &mdash; is essential for interpreting the sedimentary rock record. Physical weathering breaks rock into smaller fragments without changing its chemistry. Freeze-thaw action is the most important process in cold climates: water in a crack expands by about 9% when it freezes, exerting pressure on the crack walls until the rock fragments.
