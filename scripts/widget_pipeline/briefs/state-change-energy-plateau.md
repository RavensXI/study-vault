# Widget brief: Energy doesn't disappear during a temperature plateau

Cluster id: `state-change-energy-plateau`  |  serves 9 lessons

## The one thing the widget must make concrete

During a change of state, energy continues to be absorbed/released (breaking/forming bonds) even though the thermometer reading stays flat.

## The lessons it will sit in

### 1. [science-aqa] Internal Energy, Specific Heat Capacity and Latent Heat — `/lesson/science-aqa/physics-paper-1/7`
Triage: Students think that during melting or boiling, the energy they add makes particles move faster (raising temperature), when actually it breaks bonds while temperature stays flat.

> What is Internal Energy? All matter is made up of particles that are constantly moving. The internal energy of a substance is the total energy stored by all the particles inside it. This energy comes from two sources: The kinetic energy of particles moving around (vibrating, rotating, or moving freely) The potential energy stored in the forces between particles Key Fact Internal energy increases when a substance is heated and decreases when it cools down. The internal energy determines both the temperature and the state of matter. When you heat a substance, you transfer energy to it. This energy makes the particles move faster and can also change how they’re arranged. The amount of internal energy depends on the substance’s temperature, mass, and what material it’s made from. > The Particle Model All matter is made of tiny particles that are too small to see. The particle model describes the three states of matter in terms of how particles are arranged, how they move, and how strongly they are held together by attractive forces. In a solid , particles are packed closely together in a regular arrangement . Strong forces of attraction hold them in fixed positions. Particles cannot move from place to place &mdash; they only vibrate about their fixed positions. This gives solids a definite shape and a fixed volume. In a liquid , particles are still close together but in an irregular arrangement . The forces of attraction are weaker, allowing particles to slide past each other.
