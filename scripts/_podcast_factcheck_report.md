# Podcast fact-check, preview lessons (2 Oct 2026)

Each podcast transcript (MAI-Transcribe-2) was compared with its lesson text by Claude Opus
(subscription route). Flagged: statements a GCSE examiner would treat as wrong, or that contradict
the lesson. Staff see these as marks in the transcript panel; pupils do not.

## History AQA, Elizabethan England, lesson 2: Key ministers (3 flags, all minor)
- **0:00 and 22:54**: "You cannot run a country of 4 million people single-handedly."
  The lesson says about **three million**. (England was about 3 million in 1558 and about 4 million
  by 1600, so neither is wrong in itself, but the podcast and the lesson disagree.)
- **3:49**: "the historian John Guy calls him the indispensable man of the regime."
  The lesson credits no historian; a pupil repeating a named attribution could cite it wrongly.

## RS AQA, Judaism practices, lesson 1: The Synagogue and Public Worship (1 flag, minor)
- **11:45**: "an upper gallery, which is known as a mechitza".
  A mechitza is the screen or partition between men and women; the upper gallery is not itself
  a mechitza. The lesson has it right.

## Computer Science AQA, Data representation, lesson 2: Character encoding
- No problems found.

## What to decide
- Population: align the lesson and the podcast, or accept both (both are defensible for 1558–1603).
- The mechitza line is a real error a pupil could repeat in an exam; it is worth regenerating that
  podcast or adding a correction note under it.
