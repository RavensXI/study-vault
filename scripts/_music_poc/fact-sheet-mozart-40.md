## Facts I haven't verified to two-source standard — flag for Tom or musicology cross-check

These are claims commonly made in textbooks but I haven't found two open-web sources stating them precisely:

- The second subject in the exposition is in B♭ major (the relative major). Sonata-form *convention* (Wikipedia "Sonata form" article) says minor-key sonatas typically modulate to the relative major, but the Wikipedia article on this specific symphony doesn't state it. **Likely true, common textbook fact, but flagging.**
- The recapitulation's second subject returns in G minor (rather than the more common tonic-major recap). Same caveat.
- The "sigh motif" descending semitone (B♭→A) at the start. Britannica says "plaintive sighs" but doesn't tag the specific interval.
- Specific bar numbers for major events (modulations, recap entry).

**Recommendation**: before this lesson ships, a music-knowledgeable reviewer (or a Grove Music Online lookup) confirms or corrects the four bullets above. I won't write practice questions whose answers depend on them until they're verified.

## Sample practice questions (drafted from verified facts only)

Format matches StudyVault's existing practice-first subjects (Maths, English Language, Languages, Geography Skills). Each question shows: stem, audio segment if relevant, answer, and mark scheme using the StudyVault Mastering / Secure / Developing / Emerging rubric.

### Q1 — 1 mark — Multiple choice
> **Listen to the opening 30 seconds.** What is the home key of this piece?
>
> A) G major
> B) G minor ✓
> C) D minor
> D) B♭ major
>
> *Mark scheme:* 1 mark for B (G minor). Source: composition is widely catalogued as G minor; tempo marking K. 550 in G minor.

### Q2 — 1 mark — Identify
> **Listen to the opening.** Name the family of instruments that plays the accompaniment **before the main theme enters**.
>
> *Answer:* Strings (specifically the lower strings — violas, divided).
> *Mark scheme:* 1 mark for "strings" or any more specific correct answer ("violas", "lower strings", "divided violas"). Reject "violins" (they enter later with the theme), "woodwind", "full orchestra".

### Q3 — 2 marks — Describe
> **Listen to the opening 8 bars.** Describe one feature of the melody that contributes to its mood.
>
> *Answer:* Acceptable answers include: descending semitone / "sigh" motif; falling melodic shape; minor key tonality; Sturm und Drang character; soft (piano) dynamic; agitated string accompaniment underneath.
> *Mark scheme:* 1 mark for identifying a feature, 1 mark for linking it to the "anxious / melancholic / unsettled" mood. Mastering: links a *specific* feature (e.g. "the descending semitone B♭ to A") to mood. Secure: identifies a feature and gives the mood. Developing: feature without mood, or vice versa. Emerging: incorrect or vague.

### Q4 — 3 marks — Explain
> Mozart wrote two versions of this symphony. The original 1788 version was scored without clarinets; in 1791 he added clarinet parts. Identify **two changes** to instrumentation that this revision involved, and explain **one** reason a composer might revise a work in this way.
>
> *Answer:* Changes: addition of two clarinet parts; redistribution of woodwind material between oboes and clarinets so the oboes don't double the new clarinet lines. One reason: a specific upcoming performance with clarinettists available; a desire for a richer or warmer woodwind blend; commercial / patron-driven rescoring.
> *Mark scheme:* 1 mark per identified change (max 2). 1 mark for a coherent reason. Mastering links the reason to a specific historical or musical context (e.g. "Mozart had a specific performance in view and wanted the clarinet timbre to soften the oboe blend"). Secure gives a generic but plausible reason.

### Q5 — 4 marks — Describe
> **Listen to the section between roughly 2:00 and 2:45.** This passage is from the development section. Describe **two features** of how Mozart destabilises the home key here.
>
> *Answer:* Acceptable features: extensive chromatic movement (Mozart writes a passage that uses every note of the chromatic scale except the tonic G); rapid modulation through unrelated keys; sequential repetition of the main motif at different pitch levels; intensification of dynamics; tighter rhythmic activity.
> *Mark scheme:* 2 marks per feature (1 for naming, 1 for describing). Top-band: names the chromatic passage and explains that the deliberate omission of G is what destabilises tonality.
