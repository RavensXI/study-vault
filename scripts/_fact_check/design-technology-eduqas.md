## HIGH Severity Findings

### L19 — Investigating the Work of Designers and Companies
**Claim:** "…the G-Force cyclonic vacuum cleaner was launched in 1983 in Japan before reaching the UK market in 1993 as the DC01."

**Issue:** The G-Force was *designed* in 1983 but was not manufactured or sold in Japan until **March 1986**, produced by Apex Inc. Stating "launched in 1983 in Japan" conflates the design year with the market-launch year by three years. The UK launch date of 1993 as the DC01 is correct (though technically the first UK model was the DA 001, quickly replaced by the DC01 — this simplification is acceptable at GCSE level).

**Fix:** "…the G-Force cyclonic vacuum cleaner was designed in 1983 and launched in Japan in 1986, before Dyson brought the technology to the UK market in 1993 as the DC01."

**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyson_(company)

### L22 — Prototyping, Testing and Iterative Improvement
**Claim:** "Products for children must be tested against requirements such as choking-hazard rules (no component smaller than 45 mm in any direction for products intended for children under 3)."

**Issue:** This is factually wrong on both the dimension and the logic. The EU Toy Safety Directive (2009/48/EC) and its harmonised standard EN 71-1 define small parts using a test cylinder measuring **57.1 mm tall × 31.7 mm diameter**. A "small part" is any component that fits *entirely inside* this cylinder — meaning it is dangerously small. The lesson inverts this: it implies "not smaller than 45 mm" is a pass criterion, which has no basis in any toy safety standard. A student using "45 mm" in an exam answer would be writing incorrect regulation information.

**Fix:** "Products for children must be tested against the small parts requirements of EN 71-1 (under the EU Toy Safety Directive). The small parts test uses a cylinder 57.1 mm tall and 31.7 mm in diameter — any component that fits entirely inside this cylinder is classified as a choking hazard and must not be present in toys intended for children under 3."

**Source:** https://www.tradeaiders.com/toys-for-under-3-years-using-the-small-parts-cylinder-to-prevent-choking-hazards.html

## MEDIUM Severity Findings

### L8 — Programmable Components and Microcontrollers
**Claim:** "A PIC (Programmable Interface Controller) is a popular family of microcontroller chip."

**Issue:** The expansion "Programmable Interface Controller" is incorrect. The PIC family was originally named "Peripheral Interface Controller" by General Instrument. Microchip Technology (the current manufacturer) no longer treats PIC as an acronym, but if expanded it is "Peripheral", not "Programmable". "Programmable Interface Controller" does not appear in any authoritative source and is a fabricated expansion.

**Fix:** "A PIC (Peripheral Interface Controller) is a popular family of microcontroller chip." (Or: note that PIC is no longer officially an acronym but originally stood for Peripheral Interface Controller.)

**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PIC_microcontrollers

### L19 — Investigating the Work of Designers and Companies
**Claim:** "Under Jony Ive as Chief Design Officer, Apple developed a design philosophy built on simplicity, material quality, and the removal of unnecessary elements. Products like the iMac G3 (1998), the iPod (2001), and the iPhone (2007) succeeded…"

**Issue:** The Apple product dates are all correct. However, Jony Ive was not Chief Design Officer during the creation of those products. He held the title **Senior Vice President of Industrial Design** from the late 1990s until **May 2015**, when he was promoted to the newly created CDO role. The iMac G3, iPod, and original iPhone were all designed under his SVP title. Calling him "Chief Design Officer" throughout implies a title that postdates the iMac G3 by 17 years and could mislead students writing about Apple design history.

**Fix:** "Under Jony Ive, initially as Senior Vice President of Industrial Design and later as Chief Design Officer (from 2015), Apple developed a design philosophy built on simplicity…"

**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jony_Ive
