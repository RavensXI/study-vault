## HIGH Severity Issues (require correction before publishing)

### H1 — L7: WADA Prohibited List categories wrong
**Lesson:** Performance-Enhancing Drugs, WADA & Sanctions

**Claim:** "The nine categories on the Prohibited List include: anabolic agents, peptide hormones, **beta blockers**, stimulants, diuretics, narcotics, cannabinoids, glucocorticoids, and alcohol (in certain sports)."

**Issues:**
- The WADA Prohibited List has **10 substance categories** (S0–S9), not nine
- **Beta-2 Agonists (S3)** are omitted entirely — a significant general prohibited category
- **Beta-blockers (P1)** are NOT a universally banned substance; they are a sport-specific category prohibited only in archery, shooting, golf and a small number of other sports. Listing them as a general category is factually wrong and could cause exam errors
- **Hormone and Metabolic Modulators (S4)** are omitted
- **Non-Approved Substances (S0)** are omitted

**Correct category list (S0–S9):** Non-Approved Substances (S0), Anabolic Agents (S1), Peptide Hormones / Growth Factors (S2), Beta-2 Agonists (S3), Hormone and Metabolic Modulators (S4), Diuretics and Masking Agents (S5), Stimulants (S6), Narcotics (S7), Cannabinoids (S8), Glucocorticoids (S9). Plus Prohibited Methods M1–M3 and sport-specific Beta-Blockers (P1).

**Source:** wada-ama.org (2025 Prohibited List, in force January 2025)

### H2 — L5: Paris last hosted Olympics in 1900 — WRONG
**Lesson:** The Olympic & Paralympic Movement

**Claim:** "Paris last hosted the summer Olympics in 1900; it returned in 2024 after 100 years."

**Issue:** Paris hosted the Summer Olympics three times: 1900, **1924**, and 2024. The most recent prior hosting was **Paris 1924**, not 1900. The claim that 1900 was the last hosting before 2024 is factually wrong. The 100-year gap refers to 1924→2024, which is correct in duration but incorrectly attributed to 1900.

**Correct version:** Paris last hosted the Summer Olympics in **1924**, returning in 2024 after 100 years. It was Paris's third time hosting the Summer Games.

**Source:** olympics.com/en/olympic-games/paris-1924

### H3 — L8: Paris 2024 budget figures inaccurate
**Lesson:** Hosting a Major Sporting Event — Pre-Event & Bidding

**Claim:** "The Paris 2024 Games budget grew from an initial estimate of around 3.6 billion euros to approximately 8.8 billion euros by the time the Games opened."

**Issue:** Neither figure is accurate:
- The organizing committee (COJO) final budget was **€4.48 billion** — a surplus of €26.8m was actually recorded
- Total French state expenditure (organizing + infrastructure + security) was **€6.6 billion** (French Court of Auditors, reported 2025)
- The figure of **€8.8 billion** is not supported by any authoritative published total
- The €3.6 billion referred to infrastructure spending alone, not the initial overall budget estimate

**Correct version:** Paris 2024 organizing committee budget: ~€4.48 billion. Total state cost (including infrastructure): ~€6.6 billion. No credible source supports €8.8 billion as a total.

**Source:** france24.com; sportcal.com

### H4 — L6: Hillsborough death toll '97' presented without context (classification note)
**Lesson:** Etiquette, Sportsmanship & Spectator Behaviour

**Claim:** "Where 97 Liverpool supporters lost their lives in a crowd crush."

**Issue:** The figure of 97 is technically the ultimate final total (the 97th victim died in 2021). However:
- At the time of the Taylor Report (1990) only 95 had died
- The pre-2021 widely cited figure was **96**, and the campaign was named "Justice for the 96"
- Students who encounter the Hillsborough memorial, Liverpool FC tributes, or other revision materials will see '96' and may be confused or penalised for writing '97' in some exam contexts
- The lesson should note that the historical campaign figure was 96, with a 97th death confirmed in 2021

This is classified HIGH due to likely exam confusion (not a simple factual error — the figure 97 became correct in 2021 — but the context is essential for student understanding).

**Correct version:** The Hillsborough disaster in 1989 killed 97 people in total. At the time of the Taylor Report (1990) the toll was 95; a 96th victim died in 1993 and a 97th in July 2021. The campaign was historically known as "Justice for the 96". Students should know both figures and the timeline.

**Source:** britannica.com/event/Hillsborough-disaster; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsborough_disaster

### M1 — L3: 'Padel England' described as separate NGB affiliated to LTA
**Lesson:** Popularity of Sport in the UK & Emerging Sports

**Claim:** "Padel England secured recognition as the national governing body and affiliated to the Lawn Tennis Association for development support."

**Issue:** The LTA **is** the national governing body for padel in Britain — confirmed by Sport England, Sport Scotland and Sport Wales. There is no body called 'Padel England' that is separate from and affiliated to the LTA. LTA Padel (formerly British Padel) operates as the padel arm within the LTA. The lesson's description implies two independent organisations with a subordinate relationship, which is wrong.

**Correct version:** The LTA (Lawn Tennis Association) was confirmed as the national governing body for padel in Britain by the UK home country sports councils. LTA Padel (formerly British Padel) operates within the LTA structure.

**Source:** lta.org.uk/news/lta-announced-as-the-national-governing-body-for-padel/

### M2 — L7: Sun Yang ban characterised as 'refusing to cooperate'
**Lesson:** Performance-Enhancing Drugs, WADA & Sanctions

**Claim:** "Swimmer Sun Yang received an eight-year ban (later revised on appeal) for refusing to cooperate with anti-doping testers."

**Issue:** The violation was **tampering with doping control** (a security guard, on Sun Yang's mother's instruction, smashed sample containers with a hammer) — categorically different from 'refusing to cooperate'. The appeal timeline is also compressed: the 8-year ban (Feb 2020) was overturned on procedural grounds by the Swiss Federal Tribunal (Dec 2020), then re-imposed at 4 years and 3 months by a new CAS panel (June 2021).

**Correct version:** Sun Yang received an eight-year ban in February 2020 for tampering with doping control (sample vials were smashed). The ban was overturned procedurally in December 2020, then reinstated at four years and three months by a fresh CAS panel in June 2021.

**Source:** insidethegames.biz/articles/1120097/; cnn.com/2021/06/22/sport/sun-yang-doping-ban-intl-hnk

### M3 — L7: Russian athletes at Paris 2024 described as 'barred'
**Lesson:** Performance-Enhancing Drugs, WADA & Sanctions

**Claim:** "Russia's track and field athletes were barred from competing under the Russian flag at the Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024 Olympics."

**Issue:** The statement is an oversimplification that conflates two distinct situations:
- At **Paris 2024**, Russian athletes in most sports competed as **AIN (Individual Neutral Athletes)** — they were not barred but competed under strict neutrality conditions. However, Russian athletics (track and field) athletes specifically were barred by World Athletics, which maintained a separate suspension independent of the IOC's AIN pathway.
- At **Tokyo 2020**, Russian athletes competed as **ROC (Russian Olympic Committee)** — a different arrangement from AIN, following WADA sanctions.

The lesson implies a blanket Olympic-wide barring which is inaccurate for Paris 2024 (most sports allowed AIN participation).

**Correct version:** At Paris 2024, most Russian athletes could compete as Individual Neutral Athletes (AIN). Russian track and field athletes were specifically barred by World Athletics. At Tokyo 2020 they competed as ROC. Neither was a complete Olympic ban.

**Source:** olympics.com/ioc/news/strict-eligibility-conditions…; washingtonpost.com/sports/olympics/2023/12/19/russia-track-ban-paris-olympics/

### M4 — L8: Paris 2024 opening ceremony '1.5 billion viewers' — unverified
**Lesson:** Hosting a Major Sporting Event — Pre-Event & Bidding

**Claim:** "The opening ceremony along the Seine attracting a global television audience estimated at 1.5 billion viewers."

**Issue:** No authoritative source supports 1.5 billion television viewers for the opening ceremony specifically. Published verified figures: ~24 million in France (domestic record), ~28 million on NBCUniversal platforms (USA). The IOC's overall Paris 2024 cumulative audience across all platforms and all days was described as record-breaking, but a 1.5 billion single-event TV audience is not substantiated.

**Correct version:** The opening ceremony drew a French domestic record of approximately 24 million viewers and around 28 million viewers on US platforms. No credible source supports 1.5 billion as a global TV total for the ceremony.

**Source:** olympics.com/ioc/news/ioc-president-praises-broadcast-operations…; ibc.org opening ceremony ratings article
