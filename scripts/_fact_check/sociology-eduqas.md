## Issues Requiring Fixes

### MEDIUM Issues

#### 1. Divorce peak statistic overstated (Families L06)
- **Lesson:** `fb0d9688-705e-4ecd-ae19-e04b0f0a0b2e` — Divorce: Causes, Consequences and Theoretical Perspectives
- **Claim in lesson:** "By the mid-1990s this had risen to around 175,000 per year"
- **Reality:** ONS data shows the actual peak was **165,018 divorces in 1993** — not 175,000, and the peak year was 1993 (early, not mid, 1990s). The figure is overstated by ~6%.
- **Fix:** Change to "By the early 1990s this had risen to around 165,000 per year, reaching a peak of approximately 165,000 in 1993."

---

#### 2. "Master status" incorrectly attributed to Becker (Education L02)
- **Lesson:** `3a6e0691-fcc5-4da5-b4c9-f4e30a9be73e` — Processes Inside Schools
- **Claim in lesson:** "Becker (Outsiders, 1963) introduced the concept of master status"
- **Reality:** The concept of master status was **coined by Everett Hughes** in his 1945 article "Dilemmas and Contradictions of Status" (American Journal of Sociology). Becker adopted and applied the term in Outsiders (1963) but did not originate it. Saying Becker "introduced" the concept is a misattribution.
- **Fix:** Change to "Becker (Outsiders, 1963) applied the concept of master status — originally developed by Hughes (1945) — to labelling theory" or simply "Becker (Outsiders, 1963) used the concept of master status."
- **Note:** This error also appears in Cultural Transmission L03 (lesson `f1287282-dcd4-45aa-93e5-6da4bc9276cb`) where the key fact box states "Interactionism — Becker (labelling, master status)" without claiming he introduced it — that phrasing is acceptable.

### LOW Issues

#### 3. Walby's first structure named as "paid employment" in L03 (inconsistent with L07)
- **Lesson:** `f1287282-dcd4-45aa-93e5-6da4bc9276cb` — The Four Sociological Perspectives
- **Claim in lesson:** "Walby identified six structures of patriarchy: paid employment, household production, culture, sexuality, violence and the state"
- **Reality:** Walby's direct wording in Theorizing Patriarchy (1990) is **"paid work"** (not "paid employment") and **"housework"** (not "household production"). The dedicated Walby lesson (L07) correctly uses "paid work." Having two different terms across lessons could confuse students who try to memorise the list.
- **Fix:** In L03, change "paid employment" to "paid work" for consistency with L07 and Walby's original.

---

#### 4. Walby's structures listed with non-original qualifiers across lessons
- **Lessons:** `fb2b2fe7` (Stratification L03), `35fd1188` (Stratification L07)
- **Claim:** "paid work, household production, patriarchal culture, sexuality, male violence, and the state"
- **Reality:** Walby's own list is "paid work, housework, culture, sexuality, violence and the state." Adding "patriarchal" before "culture" and "male" before "violence" is common in secondary sources but diverges from Walby's original wording. "Household production" also continues to appear where "housework" is more accurate.
- **Severity:** LOW — not a factual fabrication, but inconsistent terminology that could cost marks in an exam if a student uses "household production" and the mark scheme expects "housework."
- **Fix:** Standardise across all lessons to "paid work, housework, culture, sexuality, violence, and the state."

---

#### 5. Willis study location described as "a West Midlands comprehensive" (minor)
- **Lessons:** `3a6e0691` (Education L02) and `3c369df8` (Education L06)
- **Claim:** Willis studied "twelve working-class boys at a West Midlands comprehensive"
- **Reality:** Willis used the pseudonym "Hammertown" for anonymity. Some sources place the school in Birmingham (West Midlands), others suggest Coventry. Describing it as a "West Midlands comprehensive" is broadly accurate and consistent with most secondary sources.
- **Severity:** LOW — not a factual error, just a minor note.
