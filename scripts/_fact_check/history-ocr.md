## MEDIUM Findings

### 1. Sharpeville death toll — no caveat on revised scholarship
**Lesson:** South Africa L2 — *Sharpeville and the turn to armed struggle* (`ab9bf421`)  
**Claim:** "Sixty-nine people were killed and around 180 wounded."  
**Issue:** The lesson presents the traditional official police figures (69 dead, 180 wounded) as definitive. Research published in *The Conversation* (2023), drawing on hospital records, raises the death toll to at least 91 killed and 281 wounded — roughly one third higher than official figures. The police were shown to have minimised the count. For a GCSE specification that rewards engagement with contested evidence, the lesson should flag this revision.  
**Fix:** Add a sentence: *"The official police figure was 69 killed and approximately 180 wounded; however, recent analysis of contemporary hospital records suggests the true death toll was at least 91 dead and 281 wounded — the police minimised the count by around a third."*  
**Source:** https://theconversation.com/sharpeville-new-research-on-1960-south-african-massacre-shows-the-number-of-dead-and-injured-was-massively-undercounted-217828

### 2. Rolling Thunder bombing comparison overstated
**Lesson:** International Relations L11 — *The Vietnam War 1965–1973* (`070d2bc5`)  
**Claim:** "Operation Rolling Thunder (1965–1968) dropped more bombs on Vietnam than the total dropped in the Second World War."  
**Issue:** Rolling Thunder specifically dropped ~864,000 tons on North Vietnam — more than the Pacific theater of WWII (~503,000 tons), but not more than all of WWII combined across all theaters (~2 million tons). The comparison that stands up is (a) Rolling Thunder > Pacific theater of WWII, or (b) total Vietnam War bombing > total WWII. The lesson attributes the 'more than all of WWII' comparison to Rolling Thunder alone, which overstates its scale and could cause mark-affecting factual errors in student essays.  
**Fix:** Change to: *"During the Vietnam War, the USA dropped more bombs in total than had been dropped across the entire Second World War. Operation Rolling Thunder (1965–1968) alone dropped more tonnage on North Vietnam than the United States had dropped in the whole Pacific theater of WWII."*  
**Source:** https://www.history.navy.mil/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-017/h-017-2.html

## LOW Findings

### 3. Pearl Harbor — 20 naval vessels should be 19 *(FIXED)*
**Lesson:** USA 1919–1948 L9 — *The Second World War and civil rights* (`82724bd2`)  
**Claim:** "destroying or damaging 20 naval vessels and over 300 aircraft"  
**Fix applied:** Changed to "19 naval vessels". The National WWII Museum, US Navy and Britannica consistently cite 19 warships damaged or destroyed at Pearl Harbor. The 2,400 killed figure is confirmed (precise total 2,404).  
**Fixed in Supabase:** 2026-05-09

### 4. Drogheda — 3,500 is upper bound of range, not a single figure
**Lesson:** Personal Rule L6 — *Rump Parliament and the rise of Cromwell 1649–1653* (`5a7dcace`)  
**Claim:** "approximately 3,500 people were killed after the garrison refused surrender"  
**Issue:** Scholarly consensus places the range at approximately 2,500–3,500. Cromwell's own dispatches listed ~2,800 military casualties; civilian deaths account for higher estimates. The 3,500 figure is the upper bound of the range, not an independent consensus figure. The lesson's contextualisation is otherwise excellent.  
**Recommended fix:** Change to "estimates of those killed range from approximately 2,500 to 3,500 people."
