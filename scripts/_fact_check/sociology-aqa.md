## Summary

Sociology AQA is a well-constructed subject with mostly accurate attribution of theorists, study titles, years and arguments. The content is generally reliable for AQA GCSE teaching. Two issues require attention, neither of which is a fabricated claim — they are cases of imprecise dating/framing that could cause mark loss if students reproduce them in exams.

---

## Issues Requiring Action

### MEDIUM — Merton date conflation (Crime & Deviance L2)
**Lesson:** Functionalist and Subcultural Theories: Merton  
**Lesson ID:** `3c09553e-b7ef-4e07-8180-6f0b22c72453`

**The claim:**
> "Robert Merton, writing in Social Theory and Social Structure (1938, enlarged 1968)"

**The issue:**  
The parenthetical `(1938, enlarged 1968)` conflates two separate things. The original essay "Social Structure and Anomie" was published in the **American Sociological Review in 1938**. The book *Social Theory and Social Structure* was first published in **1949** (not 1938), revised in 1957, and enlarged in 1968. Students who memorise '1938' as the book date — a reasonable inference from this framing — may write the wrong year in an exam context. At GCSE level, candidates are expected to cite Merton as a named sociologist; getting the publication year wrong could lose a mark on a "name and describe" question.

**Correct version:**  
> "Robert Merton, in his 1938 essay 'Social Structure and Anomie' (later developed in *Social Theory and Social Structure*, 1949, enlarged 1968)"

Or more simply for GCSE: "Robert Merton (first published 1938; book edition 1949)"

### MEDIUM — Townsend sample size: '6,000 individuals' unverifiable (Social Stratification L4)
**Lesson:** Poverty as a Social Issue: Townsend and Murray  
**Lesson ID:** `b8964038-8182-4be1-97a7-88528619c42f`

**The claim:**
> "Using this index on a sample of over 2,000 households and 6,000 individuals, Townsend concluded that over 22% of the UK population was living in poverty in 1968–69."

**The issue:**  
The 1968–69 survey and the 22% finding are confirmed correct. The household figure of "over 2,000" is accurate (2,052 questionnaires archived at poverty.ac.uk). However, the separate figure of "6,000 individuals" cannot be verified from published sources. It is not cited in the Tutor2u classic texts entry, the poverty.ac.uk archive, or the Townsend Wikipedia entry. It appears to be an agent-generated estimate. Including an unverified number in a text that students are expected to cite from is a risk.

**Correct version:**  
Remove "and 6,000 individuals" or qualify it as approximate. Safe version:  
> "Using this index on a sample of over 2,000 households, Townsend concluded that over 22% of the UK population was living in poverty in 1968–69."

### LOW (informational) — Academies date framing slightly loose (Education L2)
**Lesson:** Types of School and Alternative Provision  
**Lesson ID:** `6a54627c-4aae-4aa6-8f36-0ea40ee646b8`

**The claim:**
> "Academies were introduced under the Labour government in 2000 and significantly expanded by the Coalition government's Academies Act 2010."

**The issue:**  
Academies were enabled by the Learning and Skills Act 2000 as 'city academies' and were formally renamed under the Education Act 2002. Saying "introduced in 2000" is a widely used shorthand and not wrong, but the more precise date for the modern academy framework is 2002. For GCSE teaching this is acceptable; no mark loss risk at this level.

---

### LOW (informational) — Ball's 'Beachside' described as 'fictional' rather than pseudonymous (Education L5)
**Lesson:** Processes Within Schools  
**Lesson ID:** `1c283e69-fca2-4011-a9ea-0c9a4a625856`

**The claim:**
> "a fictional comprehensive school (referred to as 'Beachside')"

**The issue:**  
'Beachside Comprehensive' was a real school given a pseudonym by Ball, not a fictional school. Describing it as 'fictional' is technically inaccurate. Replacing 'fictional' with 'anonymised' or 'pseudonymous' would be more precise.

## Overall Assessment

The content is of high quality for a sociology GCSE course. There are **no fabricated studies, no wrong theorist attributions, no invented quotes, and no seriously wrong dates**. The two medium findings are:

1. A date conflation (Merton 1938 vs 1949) that is confusing but not a fabrication.
2. One unverifiable specific statistic (Townsend's "6,000 individuals") that should simply be removed.

All named studies — Willis, Cohen, Bowles & Gintis, Heidensohn, Carlen, Walby, Townsend, Murray, Halsey, Ball, Oakley, Murdock, Willmott & Young, Becker, Davis & Moore — have correct titles, years, and core arguments.
