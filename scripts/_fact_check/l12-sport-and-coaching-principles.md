## HIGH — Lesson 3: Short-Term Effects of Exercise

**Claim:** During EPOC, lactic acid is converted back into *glycogen* in the liver.

**Issue:** The Cori cycle converts lactate → pyruvate → **glucose** (gluconeogenesis) in the liver. The conversion to glycogen is a separate, subsequent step (glycogenesis) and is not the direct product of lactic acid clearance. At GCSE sport science level, the expected answer is "lactic acid is converted to glucose in the liver." Writing "glycogen" as the direct product is biochemically incorrect and would lose marks.

**Fix:** Replace "convert the accumulated lactic acid back into glycogen (stored energy) in the liver" with "convert the accumulated lactic acid back into glucose in the liver."

## HIGH — Lesson 5 (Health and Fitness L2): Measuring Health and Fitness

**Claim:** "A well-conditioned male aged 15–16 might cover over 2,800 metres; a score below 2,100 metres would be rated poor."

**Issue:** The Cooper 12-minute run norms for males aged 13–19 (Cooper 1968; Topendsports) are:

| Rating    | Distance (m)  |
|-----------|--------------|
| Excellent | > 2,800       |
| Good      | 2,400 – 2,800 |
| Average   | 2,000 – 2,400 |
| Fair      | 1,600 – 2,000 |
| Poor      | < 1,600       |

The "over 2,800 m" figure is correct (Excellent). But "below 2,100 m = poor" is wrong — 2,100 m falls in the *Average* band. The Poor threshold is **< 1,600 m**. Any student scoring 1,700–2,100 m would incorrectly classify themselves as "poor" when they are Fair/Average.

**Fix:** Change "a score below 2,100 metres would be rated poor" to "a score below 1,600 metres would be rated poor."

## MEDIUM — Lesson 1: The Structure of Body Systems

**Claim:** "Short bones (wrist and ankle bones) absorb shock."

**Issue:** The primary function of short bones (carpals/tarsals) is **stability and support with limited movement**, not shock absorption. Shock absorption is the role of articular cartilage and fibrocartilage structures such as menisci. Multiple anatomy references (Kenhub, Visiblebody, OER anatomy texts) consistently describe short bones as providing stability. "Absorb shock" is not the accepted answer at GCSE and would be marked wrong in a bone-classification question.

**Fix:** Change "absorb shock" to "provide stability and support."
