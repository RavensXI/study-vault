# Fact-Check Report: Physical Education — Edexcel

**Subject slug:** `physical-education-edexcel`
**Date:** 2026-05-21
**Lessons checked:** 30 (Unit 1: Fitness and Body Systems L1–L17 + Unit 2: Health and Performance L1–L13)
**Findings:** 2 HIGH, 4 MEDIUM, 9 LOW

---

## HIGH severity

### 1. Harvard Step Test — bench height and heart rate measurement (L13, Fitness Tests)

**Lesson claim:**
> "The Harvard Step Test requires the participant to step up and down on a **30 cm bench** for 5 minutes... **One minute after stopping**, the heart rate is measured."

**Issue (bench height):** The standard Harvard Step Test protocol uses a **50 cm bench for men / 40 cm for women** (original Brouha et al. 1943 protocol). 30 cm is a modified/school-adapted version used for non-athletic or younger populations, not the original standard protocol. Edexcel GCSE PE resources do sometimes reference 30 cm as the school version, but mark schemes vary. The lesson presents this as the definitive Harvard Step Test protocol without noting it is the adapted school version.

**Issue (heart rate timing):** The lesson says heart rate is measured "one minute after stopping". The standard Harvard Step Test protocol measures heart rate across three separate 30-second recovery intervals: 1:00–1:30 min, 2:00–2:30 min, and 3:00–3:30 min post-exercise, summing the counts to calculate a fitness index. Saying "one minute after stopping, the heart rate is measured" suggests a single reading, which is a significant simplification of the actual protocol. Some GCSE-adapted versions do use a single 1-minute post-exercise reading; if this is intentional for the Edexcel teaching context, the lesson should make that explicit.

**Fix:**
- Clarify bench height: "In the school/GCSE version, a 30 cm bench is used; the original protocol uses 50 cm for men / 40 cm for women."
- Either align to Edexcel's simplified version (single reading) with a note that the full protocol uses three recovery pulse readings, or correct to describe the three-interval measurement method.

**Sources:** https://www.topendsports.com/testing/tests/step-harvard.htm | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_step_test

### 2. Illinois Agility Test — cone spacing described as "5 metres apart" (L13, Fitness Tests)

**Lesson claim:**
> "...sprints up and back along a course of cones placed **5 metres apart**, weaving between cones in the middle section."

**Issue:** The "5 metres apart" claim is wrong for the weaving cones. The Illinois Agility Test course is **10 m long × 5 m wide**. The four central weaving cones are spaced **3.3 metres apart** (10 m divided by 3 spaces). The 5-metre figure describes the *width* of the rectangular course (distance between outer lines), not the spacing between the weaving cones. A student revising from this description would learn the wrong cone distance.

**Fix:** Replace "cones placed 5 metres apart" with "cones placed 3.3 metres apart in the central weaving section, with the full course measuring 10 m × 5 m."

**Source:** https://www.topendsports.com/testing/tests/illinois.htm | https://www.physio-pedia.com/Illinois_Agility_Test

## MEDIUM severity

### 3. Vital capacity upper range cited as "3 to 6 litres" (L6, Respiratory System)

**Lesson claim:**
> "Vital capacity typically ranges from **3 to 6 litres** depending on body size, sex and training status."

**Issue:** The standard clinical range cited by StatPearls (NIH), Physiopedia and Wikipedia is **3–5 litres** for healthy adults. The upper figure of 6 litres overstates the typical range. While very tall individuals or elite endurance athletes may occasionally exceed 5 L, citing "up to 6 litres" as the typical upper end is misleading and risks students quoting it incorrectly in exams where 3–5 L is the expected answer.

**Fix:** Change "3 to 6 litres" to "3 to 5 litres". An optional note could add that elite endurance athletes may exceed 5 L.

**Source:** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK541099/

### 4. Beta blockers — snooker not explicitly named in WADA list (L16/L11, PEDs)

**Lesson claim (L16 and L11):**
> "Beta blockers... are used in precision sports such as archery, shooting and **snooker**."

**Issue:** WADA's Prohibited List (P1, Beta-Blockers) names the following sports where beta blockers are banned: Archery (WA), Automobile (FIA), **Billiards — all disciplines (WCBS)**, Darts (WDF), Golf (IGF), Mini-Golf (WMF), Shooting (ISSF, IPC). Snooker is not listed by name; it falls under the **Billiards (WCBS)** category, since the World Confederation of Billiards Sports governs snooker, pool, and carom billiards. So snooker *is* banned in practice, but the lesson says "snooker" while the WADA list says "Billiards". This is technically imprecise.

Additionally, **golf** is banned in the WADA list but is not mentioned in these lessons as a sport where beta blockers are prohibited. The lessons list archery, shooting and snooker/billiards but omit golf, which is also on the prohibited list.

**Fix:** Replace "archery, shooting and snooker" with "archery, shooting, golf and billiards/snooker (covered under the Billiards category on the WADA list)." This adds the missing golf and is more precise about how snooker is covered.

**Source:** https://www.drugs.com/wada/p1-beta-blockers.html

### 5. Head weight cited as "approximately 4–5 kg" (L10, Lever Systems)

**Lesson claim:**
> "The load is the weight of the head — approximately **4–5 kg**"

**Issue:** Most anatomical sources cite the average adult human head as approximately 4.5–5.5 kg (commonly quoted as ~5 kg / 11 lbs). Citing 4–5 kg sits at the lower boundary; for the pedagogical purpose (illustrating a first-class lever for heading a football) this makes no practical difference, but the lower end (4 kg) is slightly below most cited values.

**Fix:** Minor — could update to "approximately 4.5–5.5 kg" for accuracy, though "4–5 kg" is not materially wrong in context.

### 6. EPO — lesson description check across L11 and L16

**L16 (correct):** "EPO is a peptide hormone produced naturally by the kidneys that stimulates bone marrow to produce more red blood cells." — **Accurate.** EPO is primarily produced by the kidneys in adults.

**L11 wording re-checked:** L11 describes EPO as "stimulates bone marrow to produce additional red blood cells" which is also correct (EPO *stimulates* bone marrow; EPO itself is *made* by kidneys). Both lessons are accurate on mechanism. No correction needed.

## LOW severity

### 7. Aerobic ATP yield 36–38 (L7)

The lesson's "around 36–38 ATP molecules per glucose" is the accepted GCSE/textbook figure. Modern biochemistry estimates ~29–30 ATP actual yield. The 36–38 figure is correct for Edexcel mark scheme purposes. No change required.

### 8. Maximum heart rate formula 220 − age (L8, L14)

The 220 − age formula is the Edexcel-specified formula and arithmetically correct. Known to be imprecise (±10–20 bpm), but correct per the specification. No change required.

### 9. Resting stroke volume ~70 ml, resting HR 60–80 bpm, cardiac output ~5 L/min (L5, L8)

All three values are within standard reference ranges. Stroke volume 60–100 mL is normal; 70 mL as a typical resting value is appropriate. HR 60–80 bpm is the standard range. Cardiac output at rest ≈5 L/min is the classic reference value. All accurate.

### 10. Resting tidal volume ~0.5 L; exercise tidal volume ~3 L (L6)

Both values confirmed accurate by StatPearls and Britannica. No change required.

### 11. Resting breathing rate 12–20 breaths per minute (L8)

Confirmed accurate by multiple sources including the American Lung Association. No change required.

### 12. WHO health definition — "emotional" vs "mental" (Unit 2 L1, L2, L12)

The Edexcel GCSE PE specification deliberately uses "emotional" where the WHO original (1948) uses "mental". All instances in the lessons correctly use the Edexcel specification wording. No error from Edexcel's perspective.

### 13. Glycogen stores ~400–500 g (L7)

Lesson states "a typical athlete has around 400–500 g of glycogen available." This is correct for a well-nourished person (~80 kg): muscle glycogen ~400 g, liver ~100 g, total ~500 g. The figure is appropriate.

### 14. Dehydration 2% impairs aerobic performance (Unit 2 L3)

Widely accepted threshold, confirmed by multiple research reviews and ACSM guidance. Accurate for GCSE purposes.

### 15. BMI >25 = overweight, >30 = obese (Unit 2 L2)

Confirmed accurate per WHO classification. No change required.
