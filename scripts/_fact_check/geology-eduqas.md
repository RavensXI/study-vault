## HIGH Severity

### L5 (Origin and Development of Life) — Chicxulub crater diameter wrong
- **Claim:** "the Chicxulub crater in Mexico (approximately 180 km in diameter)"
- **Issue:** The Chicxulub crater is approximately **200 km** in diameter, not 180 km. All major authoritative sources — Wikipedia, Britannica, USGS — consistently give ~200 km (120 miles). The lesson understates it by 20 km.
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicxulub_crater
- **Fix:** Change "approximately 180 km in diameter" → "approximately 200 km in diameter"

### L6 (Geology and Civil Engineering) — St Francis Dam cause description incomplete
- **Claim:** "The 1928 St Francis Dam failure in California killed over 400 people; investigation revealed the dam had been built on a fault zone and across a contact between two incompatible rock types that differentially weakened under reservoir loading."
- **Issue:** The death toll ('over 400') is accurate (~425). However the lesson's geological explanation is incomplete. The primary causes were: (1) a thrust fault on the western abutment; (2) swelling and slaking of Sespe conglomerate on the western side under reservoir water; (3) a landslide in weak schist on the eastern abutment. Framing this simply as 'two incompatible rock types differentially weakening' loses the critical detail about the fault and the conglomerate swelling. Not factually wrong but misleading by omission.
- **Source:** https://damfailures.org/case-study/st-francis-dam-california-1928
- **Fix:** Revise to: "investigation revealed the dam had been built across a thrust fault zone on the western side, with weak conglomerate that swelled when saturated, and unstable schist on the eastern abutment — a combination of geological failures rather than a single cause."

## MEDIUM Severity

### Structures L3 (Unconformities) and Time L1 (Stratigraphic Principles) — Siccar Point Silurian age slightly overstated
- **Claim:** "near-vertical Silurian greywacke beds (around 435 million years old)" (Structures L3)
- **Issue:** The Silurian greywackes at Siccar Point are approximately 425–430 Ma (Llandovery–Wenlock Silurian), not 435 Ma. 435 Ma is within or near the Ordovician–Silurian boundary. Both the Geological Society of London's geosite entry and the Scottish Geology Trust cite values closer to 425 Ma. Minor rounding upward.
- **Source:** https://www.geolsoc.org.uk/science-and-policy/100-great-geosites/historical-scientific/siccar-point/
- **Fix (Structures L3):** Change "around 435 million years old" → "around 425–430 million years old"
- **Note:** The companion mention in Time L1 uses the phrase "tens of millions of years gap" without specifying 435 Ma — no change needed there.

### Geological Time L2 (Zone Fossils) — Graptolite extinction date overstated
- **Claim:** "predominantly from the Cambrian to the end of the Silurian (approximately 520 to 420 million years ago)"
- **Issue:** Graptolites did not go fully extinct at the end of the Silurian. The planktonic graptolites most used as zone fossils largely disappear by end-Silurian/early Devonian, but the group as a whole survived until the Late Devonian (c. 376 Ma). The BGS Graptolites page confirms the broader range. Stating "to the end of the Silurian" as the terminal date is incorrect, even if the most useful zone fossil forms are predominantly Ordovician–Silurian.
- **Source:** https://www.bgs.ac.uk/discovering-geology/fossils-and-geological-time/graptolites/
- **Fix:** Change "predominantly from the Cambrian to the end of the Silurian (approximately 520 to 420 million years ago)" → "predominantly from the Cambrian through the Silurian as zone fossils (approximately 520 to 420 million years ago), with the group finally going extinct in the late Devonian"

### Earth & Solar System L4 (Planetary Geology) — Olympus Mons height overstated; Mauna Kea comparison wrong
- **Claim (1):** "standing about 22 km above the surrounding plains"
- **Issue:** Olympus Mons is 21.1–21.3 km tall. The consistent statement of '22 km' throughout the lesson overestimates by roughly 1 km.
- **Claim (2):** "nearly three times the height of Mauna Kea from its ocean base"
- **Issue:** Mauna Kea is approximately 10.2 km from its ocean base. Olympus Mons at ~21 km is approximately **twice** the height of Mauna Kea from base to summit — not nearly three times. The ratio is ~2:1, not ~3:1.
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympus_Mons
- **Fix (1):** Change "about 22 km" → "about 21 km" (both in the body text and in the key fact box)
- **Fix (2):** Change "nearly three times the height of Mauna Kea from its ocean base" → "approximately twice the height of Mauna Kea from its ocean base"

### Earth & Solar System L5 (Impacts) — Jezero Crater diameter: slight discrepancy
- **Claim:** "approximately 49 km wide"
- **Issue:** Wikipedia gives Jezero Crater as 45.0 km in diameter; the lesson states 49 km. NASA mission pages and some other sources use a slightly larger figure. The discrepancy may reflect different measurement methods (inner vs outer rim). At 49 km vs 45 km this is a medium-severity inconsistency with the most commonly cited figure.
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezero_(crater)
- **Fix:** Change "approximately 49 km wide" → "approximately 45–49 km wide" (to acknowledge the range) or use the Wikipedia/most-cited 45 km figure.
