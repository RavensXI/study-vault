## HIGH Findings

### 1. Margarine vitamins A and D — "required by law" is wrong (Lesson: Food Processing, Production and Technological Developments)

**Exact claim:**
> "Margarine and low-fat spreads are required by law to have vitamins A and D added, because these fat-soluble vitamins are naturally present in butter but lost when dairy fat is replaced with vegetable oil."

**Issue:** The mandatory legal requirement for margarine/spreads to be fortified with vitamins A and D was **removed in December 2013** when Regulation 4 of The Spreadable Fats (Marketing Standards) Regulations was revoked. Most major UK manufacturers continue to fortify voluntarily, but there is no legal requirement. A student who quotes this as current UK law in an exam would be wrong.

**Fix:** Change to: *"Margarine and low-fat spreads are typically fortified with vitamins A and D by manufacturers — though this is now a voluntary industry practice rather than a legal requirement — because these fat-soluble vitamins are naturally present in butter but absent when dairy fat is replaced with vegetable oil."*

**Source:** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/2854/pdfs/uksiem_20132854_en.pdf

## MEDIUM Findings

### 2. Staphylococcus aureus onset window too narrow (Lesson: Bacterial Contamination and Food Poisoning)

**Exact claim:**
> "Staphylococcus aureus toxin can cause symptoms within one to six hours because the toxin is already present in the food."

**Issue:** The CDC and NHS give 30 minutes to 8 hours as the full range (average ~3 hours). The lesson's "one to six hours" floor of 1 hour excludes well-documented 30–60-minute onset cases, and the 6-hour ceiling excludes cases up to 8 hours. A mark scheme aligned to CDC/FSA data would accept "30 minutes to 8 hours."

**Fix:** Change to: *"Staphylococcus aureus toxin can cause symptoms within 30 minutes to 8 hours (typically around 1–6 hours) because the toxin is already present in the food."*

**Source:** https://www.cdc.gov/staph-food-poisoning/about/index.html

### 3. Gelatinisation burst temperature — 80°C should be ~85°C (Lesson: Functional Properties — Proteins and Carbohydrates)

**Exact claim:**
> "Above 80°C they burst, releasing starch molecules that tangle together and thicken the liquid into a gel — this is gelatinisation." (Also in Key Fact box: "burst above 80°C")

**Issue:** IFST Love Food Love Science and ScienceDirect sources state that starch granules reach maximum swelling and burst at approximately **85°C** (not 80°C). Swelling onset is ~60°C, but the burst/main thickening phase is ~85°C. If a student uses the IFST figure of 85°C and is marked against a scheme using that source, the lesson's 80°C would be incorrect.

**Fix:** Change "Above 80°C they burst" and the Key Fact "burst above 80°C" to "Around 85°C the granules burst". The 60°C onset figure in the definition is correct and should stay.

**Source:** https://www.ifst.org/lovefoodlovescience/resources/carbohydrates-gelatinisation

## LOW Findings

### 4. Dextrinisation onset — 110°C is low vs food science consensus (Lesson: Functional Properties — Proteins and Carbohydrates)

**Exact claim:**
> "Dextrinisation happens when starch on the surface of food is heated in dry conditions to above about 110°C."

**Issue:** Food science literature (ScienceInsights, ScienceDirect) places dextrinisation onset at approximately **150–200°C** under dry heat. At 110°C, the surface is still losing residual moisture; meaningful pyrodextrin formation occurs higher. The 110°C figure is widely used in GCSE classroom resources and is not wrong in that context, but it is scientifically low and a student using FSA/IFST-aligned mark schemes citing 150°C would not be marked wrong for using the higher figure.

**Fix (optional):** Consider changing "above about 110°C" to "above approximately 150°C" or noting the GCSE-taught 110°C as an approximation. This is a LOW priority because the 110°C value is embedded in many UK food tech teaching materials and AQA mark schemes may accept it.

**Source:** https://scienceinsights.org/what-is-dextrinization-the-science-of-browning-starch/

### 5. Breakfast cereal fortification — could imply mandatory when it is voluntary (Lesson: Food Processing, Production and Technological Developments)

**Exact claim:**
> "Breakfast cereals are often additionally fortified with folic acid and iron — partly because folic acid is important in reducing the risk of neural tube defects in early pregnancy, and many women consume cereal daily."

**Issue:** Minor framing concern. Placed directly after explaining the mandatory Bread and Flour Regulations, this sentence could imply cereal folic acid/iron fortification is also legally required. It is entirely voluntary. Folic acid becomes mandatory in non-wholemeal wheat flour from December 2026, but that applies to flour, not to breakfast cereals.

**Fix (optional):** Add "voluntarily" — e.g. *"Breakfast cereals are often additionally fortified voluntarily with folic acid and iron..."*

**Source:** https://www.food.gov.uk/safety-hygiene/folic-acid

## Verified Correct (selected key claims)

- Danger zone: 5–63°C — correct per AQA 8585 specification (the FSA's official zone is 8–63°C, but AQA explicitly uses 5–63°C)
- Cooking/reheating temperature: 75°C — confirmed FSA and AQA
- Freezing: −18°C — confirmed FSA
- Chilling: 0 to below 5°C — confirmed FSA
- Pasteurisation: 72°C for 15 seconds — confirmed (The Dairy Products (Hygiene) Regulations 1995)
- UHT: at least 135°C for 1–2 seconds — confirmed (EU/UK regulation minimum is 135°C / ≥1 s; lesson's "1 to 2 seconds" is acceptable)
- Flour fortification (Bread and Flour Regs): calcium, iron, thiamin, niacin — confirmed correct; folic acid added from December 2026
- Free sugars maximum 5% of total energy — confirmed (SACN 2015)
- Protein 15%, fat 35%, carbohydrate 50% energy split — confirmed (COMA 1991 / SACN)
- Saturated fat ≤11% energy — confirmed (COMA/SACN)
- Salt max 6g/day (2.4g sodium) — confirmed NHS
- Fluid intake 6–8 cups / 1.5–2 litres per day — confirmed NHS
- Vitamin A deficiency → night blindness — confirmed
- Vitamin D deficiency → rickets (children) / osteomalacia (adults) — confirmed NHS
- Iron deficiency → anaemia — confirmed NHS
- Vitamin C deficiency → scurvy — confirmed
- B1 (thiamin) deficiency → beriberi — confirmed
- B3 (niacin) deficiency → pellagra — confirmed
- Folic acid / B9 deficiency → megaloblastic anaemia; neural tube defects in pregnancy — confirmed
- B12 deficiency → megaloblastic anaemia and nerve damage; critical for vegans — confirmed
- Campylobacter incubation 2–5 days — confirmed (FSA: typically 2–5 days, range 1–11 days)
- Listeria grows at 0–5°C fridge temperatures — confirmed
- Kwashiorkor: protein deficiency, muscle wasting, swollen abdomen — confirmed NHS
- UK household food waste ~9.5 million tonnes per year — confirmed (WRAP)
- The 14 major allergens are legally required to be declared (lesson 3: Food Labelling) — confirmed FSA
- AQA spec allergens for Food Choice are 6: nuts, egg, milk, wheat, fish, shellfish — confirmed AQA 8585
- Enzymic browning via polyphenol oxidases; controlled by acid (lemon juice) and blanching — confirmed
- Gelatinisation onset ~60°C — confirmed
- Caramelisation onset ~160°C — confirmed (sucrose caramelises at 160–180°C)
