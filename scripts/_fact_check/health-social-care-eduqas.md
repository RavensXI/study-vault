## HIGH Findings

### L4 — Ainsworth attachment type percentages misattributed to original sample
**Lesson:** Social & Emotional Development — Bowlby, Ainsworth & Burke
**Claim:** "Insecure-avoidant attachment (approximately 20%)… Insecure-resistant (ambivalent) attachment (approximately 10%)"

**Issue:** Ainsworth's original 1970 Baltimore Strange Situation study of 100 middle-class American infants produced: Secure ~70%, Insecure-avoidant ~15%, Insecure-resistant ~15%. The lesson attributes 20% avoidant and 10% resistant to Ainsworth's sample — these figures appear to come from broader US population meta-analyses, not Ainsworth's own data. The lesson presents them as figures from Ainsworth's study specifically.

**Fix:** Change to 15% / 15% (Ainsworth's original sample), or remove percentages and describe proportions in qualitative terms (e.g. "avoidant and resistant types were found in smaller proportions").

**Source:** SimplyPsychology.org; tutor2u; Wikipedia Strange situation

## MEDIUM Findings

### L4 — Bowlby 44 Thieves control group description imprecise
**Lesson:** Social & Emotional Development — Bowlby, Ainsworth & Burke
**Claim:** "compared 44 juvenile thieves with a control group"

**Issue:** Not wrong, but imprecise. The control group were 44 emotionally disturbed children from the same clinic who had not committed theft — not a general population sample. Of the 14 thieves with affectionless psychopathy, 12 had experienced early maternal deprivation; the lesson says "a significant proportion" which understates this (12 of 14 = 86%). Low impact for GCSE purposes.

**Fix:** Could add "12 of the 14 affectionless psychopaths had experienced early maternal deprivation." No urgent change required.

### L5 — Marmot Review characterisation slightly narrow
**Lesson:** Life Factors — What Shapes Health Across the Lifespan
**Claim:** "The Marmot Review (2010) highlighted that inequalities in access to services are a major driver of health inequalities."

**Issue:** The 2010 date is correct. The Review (Fair Society, Healthy Lives) is more broadly about social determinants of health across six policy areas — access to services is one element. The characterisation is a reasonable simplification but strips the Review's wider scope.

**Fix:** Low priority — the simplification is defensible at GCSE level. Consider "The Marmot Review (2010) identified social determinants of health — including income, education, housing and access to services — as key drivers of health inequalities."

### L9 — Self-concept factor count inconsistency (12 vs 14 items)
**Lesson:** Factors Shaping Self-Concept & Strategies for Resilience
**Claim:** "The twelve factors affecting self-concept are: [lists 14 items]"

**Issue:** The lesson key-fact box names 14 items but calls them "twelve factors". The spec (1.3.2) lists "gender and sexuality" as a single combined entry. The lesson splits these into separate items ("gender" and "sexuality"), inflating the count to 14. The internal note "some versions of the spec list 12 distinct factors" partially acknowledges this but does not resolve the stated count.

**Fix:** Either combine "gender and sexuality" into one item (matching the spec), or adjust the label to "thirteen or fourteen factors" with an explanatory note. The split of gender/sexuality may actually be pedagogically useful, but the claim of "twelve" while listing fourteen is inconsistent.

### L13 — Change4Life / Better Health attribution
**Lesson:** Health Campaigns, Methods & Current Initiatives
**Claim:** "A series of public health campaigns run by NHS England, initially launched as Change4Life in 2009, rebranded to Better Health in 2020."

**Issue:** Change4Life launched in January 2009, correct. Better Health launched July 2020, correct. However, both campaigns were run by **Public Health England (PHE)**, not NHS England. PHE was a separate agency abolished in 2021. Post-2021 the Better Health programme moved to NHS England/OHID. Attributing the original campaign to "NHS England" is inaccurate at the point of launch.

**Fix:** Change to "initially a Department of Health/PHE campaign" or "a UK government public health campaign". For exam purposes this distinction is unlikely to be tested, but it is factually inaccurate for any lesson text that names the running body.

## Spec Alignment Notes

- **Ainsworth's Strange Situation types**: The spec (1.1.4) specifies only "secure and insecure" (combined), without distinguishing insecure-avoidant from insecure-resistant. The lesson correctly expands to three named types, which matches GCSE-level psychology teaching expectations and is a reasonable elaboration of the spec. However, the spec does not mandate Ainsworth's specific percentages — the HIGH finding above applies to accuracy of attribution, not spec coverage.
- **Bandura**: The lesson includes Bandura's social learning theory (vicarious reinforcement) under adolescence. Bandura does not appear in the spec for this qualification (Piaget, Kohlberg, Bowlby, Ainsworth, Maslow, Erikson are the spec-named theorists). The Bandura section is supplementary content — it is not incorrect, but students should be aware it goes beyond the assessed spec for Eduqas 5249QA. No factual errors in the Bandura content.
- **Burke's theory of personal relationships** (spec 1.1.4 for later adulthood): The lesson mentions "Burke's theory" in passing in the title (Lesson 4 subtitle) but the actual lesson body deals primarily with Bowlby, Ainsworth, and then later adulthood social changes without naming Burke specifically. No errors were introduced.
