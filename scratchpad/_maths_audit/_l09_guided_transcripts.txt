
### OPENER
  [say] A coffee-shop puzzle. No algebra allowed, just look at the two orders.
  [box] A coffee costs £___   => 3   (hint: Compare the orders: the ONLY difference is one extra coffee, and £3 of price.)
  [say] That move you just made, comparing the orders and letting the muffin cancel out, is called <strong>elimination</strong>. You subtracted two equations without noticing.
  [box] And the muffin? £___   => 1   (hint: One coffee (£3) and a muffin cost £4 together.)
  [say] That second move, using the value you know to find the one you don't, is <strong>substitution</strong>. Those two moves are the entire topic. Algebra just writes coffee as \(x\) and muffin as \(y\): \(2x + y = 7\) and \(x + y = 4\).

### TEACH BRONZE — Solve \(3x + y = 10\) and \(x + y = 4\)
  [say] Both equations now have y, the same sign. <strong>Same Signs Subtract.</strong> Take \(x + y = 4\) away from \(3x + y = 10\), term by term:
  [box] 3x − x = ___x   => 2   (hint: Subtract the numbers in front: 3 − 1.)
  [box] y − y = ___   => 0   (hint: They're identical, and anything minus itself is 0.)
        on-correct: Gone. That was the whole point.
  [box] 10 − 4 = ___   => 6   (hint: The right-hand sides get subtracted too, exactly like the left.)
  [say] So 2x = 6.
  [box] x = ___   => 3   (hint: Divide both sides by 2.)
  [say] Now find y. Put x = 3 into \(x + y = 4\):
  [box] 3 + y = 4  →  y = ___   => 1   (hint: Take 3 from both sides.)
  [say] Last thing: check the pair in the other equation:
  [box] 3 × 3 + 1 = ___   => 10   (hint: Work it out. If it doesn't give 10, something slipped.)
        on-correct: It balances, so x = 3, y = 1 is right.
