BRONZE   desc: One equation, values already in the right units. Pick it, substitute and solve.

--- bronze[0] input=single_value calc=True HT=None
display: [SVG]<p style="margin:0.9em 0 0;">A current of 3 A flows through a circuit for 12 seconds. Calculate the charge that passes.</p>
solutions: [36] unit: C accept: None
eq_hint: \(Q = I \times t\)
hint: Charge = current × time. Both are already in amps and seconds, so just multiply.
  mis: inverse_error expect= 0.25 :: Q = I × t, so multiply: 3 × 12 = 36 C. Dividing (3 ÷ 12 = 0.25) is the slip here.

--- bronze[1] input=single_value calc=True HT=None
display: [SVG]<p style="margin:0.9em 0 0;">A resistor has 9 V across it and 0.3 A through it. Calculate its resistance.</p>
solutions: [30] unit: Ω accept: None
eq_hint: \(R = V \div I\)
hint: Rearrange V = IR to R = V ÷ I, then divide.
  mis: inverse_error expect= 2.7 :: R = V ÷ I = 9 ÷ 0.3 = 30 Ω. Multiplying (9 × 0.3 = 2.7) is the slip: divide instead.

--- bronze[2] input=single_value calc=True HT=None

  mis: inverse_error expect= 0.025 :: V = I × R = 0.5 × 20 = 10 V. Dividing (0.5 ÷ 20 = 0.025) is the slip: multiply here.

--- bronze[3] input=single_value calc=True HT=None

GOLD   desc: Work backwards from the total resistance, or handle a parallel branch, over several steps.

--- gold[0] input=single_value calc=True HT=None
display: [SVG]<p style="margin:0.9em 0 0;">A 12 V battery drives a current of 0.2 A through a series circuit of three resistors (10 Ω, 20 Ω and R). Calculate R.</p>
solutions: [30] unit: Ω accept: None
eq_hint: None
hint: Find the total resistance from V ÷ I first, then subtract the two known resistors.
  mis: forgot_step expect= 60 :: 60 Ω is the total resistance (V ÷ I). The question wants only R, so subtract the known resistors: 60 − 10 − 20 = 30 Ω.
  mis: wrong_rearrange expect= None :: Find the total resistance with R = V ÷ I = 12 ÷ 0.2 = 60 Ω, then take away the two known resistors: R = 60 − 10 − 20 = 30 Ω.

--- gold[1] input=single_value calc=True HT=None
display: [SVG]<p style="margin:0.9em 0 0;">A thermistor in series with a 100 Ω fixed resistor is connected to a 6 V supply. The current is 0.03 A. Calculate the resistance of the thermistor.</p>
solutions: [100] unit: Ω accept: None
eq_hint: None
hint: Total resistance is V ÷ I; the thermistor is the total minus the fixed resistor.
  mis: forgot_step expect= 200 :: 200 Ω is the total resistance (V ÷ I). The thermistor is the total minus the fixed resistor: 200 − 100 = 100 Ω.
  mis: inverse_error expect= None :: Total R = V ÷ I = 6 ÷ 0.03 = 200 Ω. Thermistor = total − fixed = 200 − 100 = 100 Ω.

--- gold[2] input=single_value calc=True HT=None
display: [SVG]<p style="margin:0.9em 0 0;">A 9 V battery drives current through two parallel branches. Branch 1 has a 30 Ω resistor. Branch 2 has a 45 Ω resistor. Calculate the current through branch 1 in amperes.</p>
solutions: [0.3] unit: A accept: None
eq_hint: None
hint: In parallel each branch has the full supply pd across it: I = V ÷ R for that branch.
  mis: series_mistake expect= 0.12 :: The branches are in parallel, not series. Treating them as series gives 30 + 45 = 75 Ω and 9 ÷ 75 = 0.12 A, which is wrong. Each branch has the full 9 V, so I₁ = 9 ÷ 30 = 0.3 A.
  mis: wrong_equation expect= 0.2 :: Use branch 1's own resistance: I₁ = 9 ÷ 30 = 0.3 A. Using 45 Ω (9 ÷ 45 = 0.2 A) gives branch 2, not branch 1.

--- gold[3] input=single_value calc=True HT=None

solutions: [60] unit: V accept: None
eq_hint: None
hint: Find the current from Q ÷ t first, then use V = I × R.
  mis: forgot_step expect= 12 :: 12 A is the current (Q ÷ t). The question asks for pd, so keep going: V = I × R = 12 × 5 = 60 V.
  mis: wrong_equation expect= 12000 :: First find the current: I = Q ÷ t = 2400 ÷ 200 = 12 A. Then V = I × R = 12 × 5 = 60 V. Multiplying charge by resistance (2400 × 5) is not a real step.
