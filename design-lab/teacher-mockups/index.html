Three directions

Three ways the class screen could work, all in the student dashboard’s paper-and-ink language: 4px corners, hairline borders, rust as the only accent. Every figure on every page is illustrative — a Year 11 History class with 24 of 28 joined — but every figure is one the platform can actually produce today, or could with the strength model moved server-side. The reasoning is in TEACHER_DASHBOARD_BRIEF.md.

A

The Monday briefing recommended

Opens with a dated sentence saying what to do, then three panels — reteach this, going cold, have a word — with everything the current screen shows kept below a divider.

Passes the five-second test and throws nothing away. Two of the three panels are a re-render of data the endpoint already returns.

B

The markbook

One grid: pupils down, units across, a strength band in each cell, in the same three words the pupils see. Click a cell for that pupil on that unit.

Teachers read grids without being taught how, and it prints for a department meeting — but a grid recommends nothing, and it invites being read as a grading tool.

Open B →grid + open drawer
C

The lesson starter

Not a report — teaching material. The three questions this class got most wrong, one to a screen in display type, with the wrong answer they actually chose hidden until you show it.

Targets the real bottleneck, which is making the do-now. Too narrow to be the front door, so in direction A it is a button on the reteach panel.
