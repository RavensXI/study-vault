The road to war, 1900–1914

By 1900 the great powers of Europe had bound themselves into two armed camps. A web of secret treaties meant that a quarrel between any two nations could pull the whole continent into conflict, and each government raced to out-build the others' armies and fleets.

This is the paragraph currently being read aloud. The light should run around its edge — quick along the long top and bottom, easing as it turns each corner — instead of the old gold bar down the left-hand side.

Key fact

The Schlieffen Plan committed Germany to fighting France first, sweeping through Belgium, before turning east to face Russia. Its rigid timetable left almost no room for diplomacy once mobilisation began.

When Archduke Franz Ferdinand was assassinated in Sarajevo in June 1914, the alliance system did exactly what its critics had feared, and within six weeks the major powers were at war.

Exam tip: Always link a cause back to the alliance system — examiners reward analysis of why a local crisis became a world war.
