History

AQA Elizabethan England, c1568–1603

Lesson 2 — Government, the Royal Court & Elizabeth's ministers

William Cecil served Elizabeth as Secretary of State from 1558 and as Lord Treasurer from 1572 until his death in 1598.

Cecil = Elizabeth's main minister for 40 years. Started day one. 1558 (start) and 1598 (death).

Cecil was a moderate Protestant who shared Elizabeth's caution about religious extremes.

Cecil matched Elizabeth's religious view — probably why the Religious Settlement ended up middle-way. Not just her idea.

The Privy Council numbered around nineteen members and met three or four times a week to advise the Queen on policy.

~19 members, met 3–4 times a week. More than I'd guessed, but they met often so decisions happened fast.

Patronage allowed Elizabeth to reward loyalty without spending Crown money — granting lands, titles, monopolies and offices in return for service.

Crown was broke when she came in (£300k debt from Mary). Patronage was political tool AND financial workaround.

Sir Francis Walsingham led the spy network and uncovered the Babington Plot of 1586.

Why Mary Queen of Scots was executed in 1587 — Walsingham's spying gave Elizabeth the proof.

Elizabeth held over twenty Royal Progresses during her reign, visiting nobles' country estates for weeks at a time.

20+ progresses. Saved the Queen money + showed off royal power + kept nobles in line. Three things at once.

Robert Dudley, Earl of Leicester, was Elizabeth's favourite — granted significant lands and the Order of the Garter despite being unable to marry her.

Dudley got gifts and a title but never real political power. Elizabeth kept feelings and politics separate.

Parliament's role under Elizabeth was largely to approve taxation and pass laws that she requested.

If Parliament was so weak, why did Elizabeth argue with them so often about marriage and the succession? Ask Mr Shaun.
