Elizabeth's Government & Court — Cecil and the Council

Name: Year 11 student (sample) Date: 15 May 2026

History

AQA Elizabethan England, c1568–1603

Lesson 2 — Government, the Royal Court & Elizabeth's ministers

Key facts

William Cecil served Elizabeth as Secretary of State from 1558 and as Lord Treasurer from 1572 until his death in 1598.

Cecil = Elizabeth's main minister for 40 years. Started day one. Need to remember 1558 (start) and 1598 (death).

The Privy Council numbered around nineteen members and met three or four times a week to advise the Queen on policy.

~19 members, met 3–4 times a week. More people than I thought — but they met a lot so decisions happened fast.

Sir Francis Walsingham led the spy network and uncovered the Babington Plot of 1586.

Walsingham = spymaster. Babington Plot 1586. Worth memorising — this is how they finally caught Mary Queen of Scots.

Elizabeth held over twenty Royal Progresses during her reign, visiting nobles' country estates for weeks at a time.

20+ progresses. She stayed at nobles' houses, sometimes for weeks. Big number to drop into an essay.

Why

Cecil was a moderate Protestant who shared Elizabeth's caution about religious extremes.

Cecil matched Elizabeth's religious view — probably why the Religious Settlement ended up middle-way. Not just her idea (will need this for L3).

Patronage allowed Elizabeth to reward loyalty without spending Crown money — granting lands, titles, monopolies and offices in return for service.

Reason patronage existed: Elizabeth inherited a broke Crown from Mary. So she paid people in land and titles instead of money. Clever.

So what

Robert Dudley, Earl of Leicester, was Elizabeth's favourite — granted significant lands and the Order of the Garter despite being unable to marry her.

Dudley got the gifts and the title but never any real power. Elizabeth liked him but didn't let her feelings change politics. Good example of how she ruled.

Elizabeth held over twenty Royal Progresses during her reign, visiting nobles' country estates for weeks at a time.

Progresses = saving the Crown money AND showing off royal power AND keeping nobles in line. Three things at once. Use this in a 16-marker.

Sir Francis Walsingham led the spy network and uncovered the Babington Plot of 1586.

Without Walsingham, Mary Queen of Scots probably doesn't get executed in 1587. His intelligence is what gave Elizabeth the proof she needed.

Questions

Parliament's role under Elizabeth was largely to approve taxation and pass laws that she requested.

If Parliament was so weak, why did Elizabeth argue with them so often about her marriage and the succession? Ask Mr Shaun — check L7.

Patronage allowed Elizabeth to reward loyalty without spending Crown money — granting lands, titles, monopolies and offices in return for service.

If patronage was so clever, what eventually went wrong with it? Lesson says court got divided into factions later but doesn't really explain why.
