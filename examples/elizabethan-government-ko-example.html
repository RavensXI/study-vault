Elizabeth's Government & Court — Cecil and the Council

Name: Year 11 student (sample) Date: 14 May 2026

History — AQA Elizabethan England, c1568–1603

Lesson 2 — Government, the Royal Court & Elizabeth's ministers

Key facts

William Cecil served Elizabeth as Secretary of State from 1558 and as Lord Treasurer from 1572 until his death in 1598.

Day-one minister, forty years loyal. Single most important name in any "Elizabeth's government" essay.

The Privy Council numbered around nineteen members and met three or four times a week to advise the Queen on policy.

Small + frequent = efficient. Compare Mary I's larger, divided council.

Elizabeth held over twenty Royal Progresses during her reign, visiting nobles' country estates for weeks at a time.

Free accommodation for the Queen, ruinous expense for the host. Quiet power move.

Why

Cecil was a moderate Protestant who shared Elizabeth's caution about religious extremes.

Cecil's moderation is half the explanation for why the Religious Settlement was middle-way — not just Elizabeth's idea.

Sir Francis Walsingham developed an intelligence network reaching across Europe, uncovering the Babington Plot of 1586.

Why Mary Queen of Scots was finally executed — Walsingham's spying gave Elizabeth the proof she needed.

So what

Patronage allowed Elizabeth to reward loyalty without spending Crown money — granting lands, titles, monopolies and offices in return for service.

Crown was broke when she came in (inherited £300k debt). Patronage was political tool AND financial workaround — but caused court factionalism later (Essex vs the Cecils, L11).

Robert Dudley, Earl of Leicester, was Elizabeth's favourite — granted significant lands and the Order of the Garter despite being unable to marry her.

Patronage in action. Dudley got symbolic rewards, never real political power. Elizabeth kept feelings and politics separate.

Questions

Parliament's role under Elizabeth was largely to approve taxation and pass laws that she requested.

If Parliament was so weak, why did Elizabeth argue with them over marriage, succession, monopolies? Ask Mr Shaun — also check L7.
