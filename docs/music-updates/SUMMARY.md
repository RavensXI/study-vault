## Lesson 1: AoS1 L1 — Binary and Ternary Form
**ID:** `ff98d11e-2db8-4a5c-ab06-6a9155e68e67`
**File:** `lesson-musical-forms-and-devices-L1.html`
**Existing narration IDs:** n1–n42
**New narration IDs:** n43–n51

**Added:** "Minuet and Trio" section appended after the existing "Applying This to Composition" collapsible.
- Origin as a Classical-era dance in 3/4 time, moderate tempo
- Structure: Minuet (A, often binary internally) → Trio (B, contrasting key/texture) → Minuet da capo (A)
- Why it is a special case of ternary form (ABA where A=Minuet, B=Trio)
- Typical features: elegant balanced phrases, found in Haydn/Mozart symphonies and string quartets
- Key fact box: how to identify minuet and trio vs plain ternary in the exam
- Exam tip collapsible: look for dance character in 3/4 + contrasting trio section

1. **"Repetition and Contrast" (n33–n37)**
   - Repetition: restating a musical idea to create unity and familiarity
   - Contrast: introducing different material for variety
   - How they work together; balance between the two
   - Key fact box

2. **"Broken Chord, Arpeggio and Alberti Bass" (n38–n43)**
   - Broken chord: notes of a chord played separately
   - Arpeggio: broken chord played in order (low to high or vice versa)
   - Alberti bass: bottom-top-middle-top pattern, named after Domenico Alberti
   - Common in Mozart/Haydn piano sonatas
   - Key fact box with Alberti bass pattern clearly described

3. **"Regular Phrasing and Motifs" (n44–n49)**
   - Regular phrasing: balanced 4-bar or 8-bar phrases, antecedent-consequent pairs
   - Melodic motif: short distinctive melodic idea as building block
   - Rhythmic motif: short distinctive rhythmic pattern
   - How motifs are developed (repetition, sequence, inversion, variation)
   - Beethoven's 5th Symphony example
   - Key fact box

4. **"Devices Covered in Other Lessons" collapsible (n50)**

## Lesson 6: Musical Elements L4 — Harmony and Tonality
**ID:** `d89096a0-8121-46f4-9260-8ef65d85748c`
**File:** `lesson-musical-elements-L4.html`
**Existing narration IDs:** n1–n39
**New narration IDs:** n40–n53

**Added:** 2 new H2 sections inserted after the "Harmony in Africa" collapsible and before "Other Tonality Terms You Need to Know."

1. **"Cadences" (n40–n47)**
   - Definition: musical equivalent of punctuation at phrase endings
   - Perfect cadence (V→I): finished, like a full stop
   - Imperfect cadence (any→V): unfinished, like a comma
   - Plagal cadence (IV→I): the "Amen" cadence, gentle and complete
   - Interrupted cadence (V→vi): the surprise
   - Key fact box with all 4 cadences summarised
   - Collapsible: how to identify cadences in the exam

2. **"Modulation in Detail" (n48–n53)**
   - Modulation to the dominant: home key → key 5 notes above (e.g. C major → G major), brighter feel
   - Modulation to the relative minor: major key → its relative minor (e.g. C major → A minor), darker mood
   - How to spot modulation: new accidentals, new tonal centre, cadences in the new key
   - Key fact box
