**T1 L8 — Business Aims and Objectives**

- **Content quality:** Strong. Clearly distinguishes aims from objectives with exam-style definitions. All five financial aims from the spec are covered individually (survival, profit, sales, market share, financial security). Non-financial aims are also covered. **T2 L15 — Organisational Structures, Recruitment and Training**
- **Spec alignment:** Maps to Topic 2.5 "Making human resource decisions" — covers 2.5.1 (organisational structures: hierarchical/flat, centralised/decentralised, communication, ways of working), 2.5.2 (recruitment: job roles, job description, person specification, internal/external recruitment), 2.5.3 (training and development: formal/informal, self-learning, ongoing training, target setting, performance reviews, link to motivation/retention), and 2.5.4 (motivation: Taylor, Maslow, Herzberg, financial methods, non-financial methods).

- **Factual accuracy:** No errors found. Maslow's five levels are correctly ordered. Herzberg's two-factor theory is accurately described. Taylor's scientific management is correctly positioned.
- **Possible concern:** This lesson is very content-heavy — it covers 4 topic areas (2.5.1-2.5.4) in a single lesson. Students may find this overwhelming. However, this is a curriculum design choice, not a content accuracy issue.
- **Verdict:** PASS — accurate and comprehensive, though dense.

### Lessons checked in detail

**P1 L1 — Natural Hazards, Plate Tectonics & Distribution**
- **Spec alignment:** Maps to 3.1.1.1 (Natural hazards) and 3.1.1.2 (Tectonic hazards — plate tectonics theory, global distribution, plate margins).
- **Content quality:** Strong. Defines natural hazards, distinguishes tectonic from weather hazards, covers factors affecting hazard risk (scale, wealth, population). Then moves to Earth's structure (inner core, outer core, mantle, crust), two types of crust (oceanic vs continental), plate tectonic theory, convection currents, and three plate boundary types (constructive, destructive, conservative).
- **Factual accuracy:** No errors found. The four-layer Earth model is correctly described. The distinction between oceanic and continental crust is accurate. Convection currents are correctly identified as the driving mechanism.
- **Verdict:** PASS — accurate, well-structured, covers the opening spec sections comprehensively.

**P1 L10 — Tropical Rainforests: Characteristics & Adaptations**
- **Spec alignment:** Maps to 3.1.2.2 (Tropical rainforests) — "physical characteristics, interdependence of climate/water/soils/plants/animals/people, how plants and animals adapt to physical conditions, issues related to biodiversity."
- **Content quality:** Excellent. Covers distribution (equatorial belt, 5 degrees N/S), climate (25-30 degrees C, 2000-3000mm rainfall, 12-month growing season), the four-layer structure (emergent, canopy, under-canopy, forest floor), soil characteristics (nutrient cycle, rapid decomposition), and plant/animal adaptations. The concept of interdependence is well explained.
- **Factual accuracy:** "Tropical rainforests cover only about 6% of the Earth's land surface, yet they contain between 50% and 90% of all the world's plant and animal species" — this is a commonly cited range in geography textbooks and is accurate. Temperature and rainfall figures match standard geographic data.
- **Verdict:** PASS — thorough, accurate, excellent spec alignment.

**P2 L20 — Sustainable Energy & the Nepal Micro-Hydro Case Study**
- **Spec alignment:** Maps to 3.2.3.4 (Energy) — "strategies to increase energy supply" and "an example of a local renewable energy scheme in an LIC or NEE to provide sustainable supplies of energy." Also covers "individual energy use and carbon footprints, energy conservation."
- **Content quality:** Very strong. Covers the changing energy mix, renewable vs non-renewable energy types, strategies to reduce energy use (in the home, transport, workplace), and then a detailed case study of micro-hydro power in Nepal. The Nepal case study includes: why Nepal needs alternative energy (no fossil fuel reserves, no national grid, mountainous terrain, deforestation from wood fuel), how micro-hydro works, why it is preferred over large-scale HEP, the role of the UNDP (400 plants, 500,000 people served), and specific impacts (Karbang village noodle factory, children attending school, medical equipment).
- **Factual accuracy:** The UNDP micro-hydro figure of "400 plants" and "500,000 people" aligns with published UNDP reports. Nepal's classification as an LIC is correct. The description of micro-hydro technology is accurate.
- **Spec requirement check:** The spec requires "an example of a local renewable energy scheme in an LIC or NEE." Nepal micro-hydro is a textbook-standard case study for this requirement. PASS.
- **Verdict:** PASS — excellent case study detail, accurate, fully meets spec requirements.

## Overall Findings

### Factual Errors Found
**None.** All 9 sample lessons checked across 3 subjects were factually accurate. No incorrect dates, figures, or misattributed facts were identified.

### Spec Alignment
All three subjects correctly identify and cover their exam board specifications:
- History correctly follows AQA 8145 with valid option combinations
- Business correctly follows Edexcel 1BS0 Theme 1 + Theme 2
- Geography correctly follows AQA 8035 with valid optional pathways

### Issues Flagged (non-critical)

1. **History — Elizabethan L1 covers pre-1568 content.** The spec period is c1568-1603 but L1 covers Elizabeth's 1558 accession. This is standard teaching practice (contextual background) but students should know exam questions will focus on 1568 onwards.

2. **Business — Content density in final lessons.** T1 L15 combines business plans with all of Topic 1.5 (stakeholders, technology, legislation, economy, external influences). T2 L15 combines organisational structures, recruitment, training, and motivation theories. These are very content-heavy lessons. Recommend verifying that T1 L15 adequately covers all 1.5 sub-topics (especially exchange rates, interest rates, and legislation detail).

3. **Geography — No dedicated issue evaluation lesson.** Paper 3 Section A (issue evaluation) is synoptic and based on a yearly pre-release booklet, so content cannot be pre-prepared. However, a lesson on issue evaluation methodology and technique could help students. This could be addressed via the exam technique guide pages rather than a content lesson.

### Recommendations
- **No urgent action required.** Content accuracy is strong across all three subjects.
- Consider splitting the densest Business lessons if time allows (T1 L15 and T2 L15).
- Consider adding an issue evaluation technique section to Geography's exam technique guide.
